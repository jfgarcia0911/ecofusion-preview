# Course 101: Introduction to Sustainable Food Systems

## Course Overview

**Course Description:**
This foundational course provides a comprehensive introduction to sustainable food systems, exploring the interconnections between agriculture, environment, economy, and society. Learners will understand why sustainable food production matters, examine current food system challenges, and discover how aquaponics and controlled environment agriculture offer innovative solutions for feeding a growing global population.

**Learning Outcomes:**
Upon successful completion, learners will be able to:
1. Explain the components and interconnections of modern food systems
2. Identify sustainability challenges in conventional agriculture
3. Describe the principles of sustainable food production
4. Compare traditional and sustainable agricultural methods
5. Articulate the role of urban agriculture in food security
6. Evaluate the environmental, social, and economic impacts of food choices

**Duration:** 8 hours total
- Online: 8 self-paced modules (1 hour each)
- Live: 2 sessions × 4 hours
- Hybrid: 4 hours online + 4 hours live

**Maximum Class Size:** 30 (online), 20 (live)

**Key Concepts:**

**Definition:**
Aquaponics is the integration of aquaculture (fish farming) and hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation) in a symbiotic environment where fish waste provides nutrients for plants, and plants filter water for fish.

**Historical Origins:**
- Ancient origins: Aztec chinampas, Asian rice-fish systems
- Modern development: 1970s research at various universities
- Commercial growth: 2000s-present

**The Symbiotic Relationship:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                         │
│     FISH                                    PLANTS      │
│       │                                        │        │
│       │ Produce                    Absorb      │        │
│       ▼                                        │        │
│    AMMONIA ──► BACTERIA ──► NITRATE ──────────►│        │
│       │           │            │                        │
│       │     Nitrosomonas    Nitrobacter                │
│       │     (NH3→NO2)       (NO2→NO3)                  │
│       │                                                 │
│       └────────────────── CLEAN WATER ◄────────────────┘
│                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Benefits of Aquaponics:**
| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Water efficiency | Uses 90% less water than traditional agriculture |
| Space efficiency | Vertical integration, small footprint |
| Dual harvest | Fish protein + plant produce |
| No synthetic fertilizers | Fish provide all nutrients |
| Year-round production | Controlled environment capability |
| Local food production | Urban-friendly systems |

### Module 2: The Nitrogen Cycle Deep Dive (1 hour)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Trace nitrogen through the aquaponic system
- Identify the bacteria responsible for nitrification
- Explain cycling a new system

**Content Outline:**

| Time | Activity | Type | Materials |
|------|----------|------|-----------|
| 0:00-0:05 | Review: Quick quiz on Module 1 | Assessment | Quiz |
| 0:05-0:25 | Lecture: The nitrogen cycle explained | Presentation | Slides 16-35 |
| 0:25-0:35 | Animation: Watch the cycle in action | Media | Animation |
| 0:35-0:45 | Hands-on: Water testing demonstration | Lab | Test kit |
| 0:45-0:55 | Worksheet: Cycle diagram completion | Activity | Handout |
| 0:55-1:00 | Summary & key takeaways | Wrap-up | Review |

**Key Concepts:**

**The Nitrogen Cycle in Aquaponics:**

**Stage 1: Fish Produce Ammonia**
- Fish excrete ammonia (NH3) through gills and waste
- Ammonia is toxic to fish at levels above 0.5 ppm
- This is why traditional aquaculture requires frequent water changes

**Stage 2: Nitrosomonas Bacteria Convert Ammonia to Nitrite**
- Nitrosomonas bacteria colonize biofilter media
- NH3 + O2 → NO2⁻ (nitrite)
- Nitrite is also toxic to fish (above 1 ppm)

**Stage 3: Nitrobacter Bacteria Convert Nitrite to Nitrate**
- Nitrobacter bacteria complete the conversion
- NO2⁻ + O2 → NO3⁻ (nitrate)
- Nitrate is much less toxic and is plant food

**Stage 4: Plants Absorb Nitrate**
- Plants uptake nitrate through roots
- Nitrate is converted to amino acids and proteins
- Water is cleaned and returned to fish tank

**Water Parameter Targets:**

| Parameter | Target Range | Danger Level |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Ammonia (NH3) | 0 ppm | >0.5 ppm |
| Nitrite (NO2) | 0 ppm | >1 ppm |
| Nitrate (NO3) | 20-60 ppm | >150 ppm |
| pH | 6.8-7.2 | <6 or >8 |
| Temperature | 68-86°F | Species-dependent |
| Dissolved Oxygen | >5 ppm | <4 ppm |

**Cycling a New System:**

| Week | What's Happening | What to Monitor |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| 1-2 | Ammonia rises as bacteria establish | Ammonia levels |
| 2-3 | Nitrite rises as Nitrosomonas work | Ammonia + Nitrite |
| 3-4 | Nitrate rises as Nitrobacter establish | All three |
| 4-6 | System stabilizes | All parameters |

**Hands-On Activity:**
Water Testing Lab - Learners practice using API Master Test Kit to measure ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH. Record results and interpret findings.

             └──────────┘
```
- **Best for:** Leafy greens, commercial
- **Pros:** Lightweight, efficient, scalable
- **Cons:** Requires separate biofilter

```
┌──────────────┐     ┌───────────────────────┐

             └──────────┘

| Bright coloration | Faded color |

**Stocking Density Guidelines:**

**Rule of Thumb:** 1 lb of fish per 5-10 gallons of tank water

**Detailed Calculation:**
```
Tank Volume (gallons) × Stocking Rate (lb/gal) = Maximum Fish Weight

Example:
300 gallon tank × 0.1 lb/gal = 30 lbs of fish
If target harvest weight is 1.5 lbs:
30 lbs ÷ 1.5 lbs = 20 fish maximum
```

**Feeding Guidelines:**
- Feed 1-2% of body weight daily
- Feed 2-3 times per day
- Remove uneaten food after 5 minutes
- Adjust for temperature (less in cold)

**Key Concepts:**

**Design Principles:**

1. **Balance:** Fish waste production must match plant uptake capacity
2. **Redundancy:** Backup aeration, multiple pumps for larger systems
3. **Accessibility:** Easy access for maintenance, harvesting
4. **Efficiency:** Minimize energy use, maximize production
5. **Scalability:** Design for future expansion

**Critical Ratios:**

| Ratio | Guideline | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Fish tank : Grow bed | 1:1 to 1:2 | For media bed systems |
| Grow bed depth | 12 inches | Standard for media beds |
| Fish stocking | 1 lb per 5-10 gal | Adjust for species |
| Feed rate | 40-50g feed per m² grow area | Daily maximum |
| Water turnover | 1-2× per hour | Minimum circulation |

**Component Sizing Calculations:**

**Example: Design a system for a 4×8 grow bed**

Step 1: Calculate grow bed volume
- 4 ft × 8 ft × 1 ft = 32 cubic feet = 239 gallons

Step 2: Calculate fish tank size (1:1 ratio)
- 239 gallons fish tank

Step 3: Calculate fish capacity (0.1 lb/gal)
- 239 × 0.1 = 23.9 lbs of fish

Step 4: Calculate pump flow rate (2× turnover/hour)
- 239 gal × 2 = 478 GPH minimum pump

Step 5: Calculate daily feed
- 23.9 lbs × 16 oz × 0.015 = 5.7 oz feed/day

1. Design a home-scale system appropriate for their space
2. Source and prepare all necessary materials
3. Construct a functional system
4. Cycle and stock their system
5. Operate and maintain their system successfully

**Duration:** 16 hours total
- Online: 8 hours instructional + ongoing support
- Live: 2 full-day workshops (8 hours each)
- Hybrid: 8 hours online + 8 hours hands-on workshop
