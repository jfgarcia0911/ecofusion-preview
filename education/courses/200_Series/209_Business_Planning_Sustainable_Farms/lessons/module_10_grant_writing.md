## Lesson Content

### 10.1 Understanding Grants

#### Grant Basics

**What Funders Want:**
- **Innovation:** New approaches, pilot programs
- **Impact:** Measurable outcomes
- **Sustainability:** Long-term viability beyond grant
- **Alignment:** Fits funder's mission
- **Feasibility:** Realistic, achievable plan

**Success Rates:**
- Federal grants: 10-30%
- State grants: 20-40%
- Private foundations: 15-35%
- Local grants: 30-50%

**Strategy:** Apply to 5-10 grants to secure 1-3

### 10.3 Grant Proposal Components

#### Standard Sections

**1. Executive Summary (1 page)**
- Project overview
- Funding request
- Key outcomes
- **Write this LAST**

**2. Statement of Need (1-2 pages)**
- Problem/opportunity
- Supporting data/statistics
- Why this matters
- Why now

**3. Project Description (3-5 pages)**
- Goals and objectives
- Methods/approach
- Timeline
- Staffing
- Innovation

**4. Evaluation Plan (1-2 pages)**
- How you'll measure success
- Metrics and indicators
- Data collection methods
- Reporting timeline

**5. Budget (1-2 pages)**
- Itemized costs
- Budget justification
- Matching funds
- In-kind contributions

**6. Organizational Capacity (1 page)**
- Your qualifications
- Track record
- Team expertise
- Resources/infrastructure

**7. Sustainability Plan (1 page)**
- How project continues after grant
- Revenue generation
- Long-term impact

**8. Appendices**
- Letters of support
- Financial statements
- Certifications
- Resumes

### 10.4 Writing the Narrative

#### Statement of Need Example

**Weak:**
"Local food is important. We need more local farms. Our community doesn't have enough fresh vegetables."

**Strong:**
"Northside County faces a critical food access gap. Of the county's 85,000 residents, 22,000 (26%) live in USDA-designated food deserts, with no grocery store within 3 miles. The nearest source of fresh produce requires a 25-minute drive—inaccessible for the 18% of households without vehicles. This contributes to our county's obesity rate of 34%, well above the state average of 28%. Meanwhile, consumption of fresh vegetables is 40% below USDA recommendations.

While four conventional farms operate within the county, none provide year-round production or direct community access. A 2024 community needs assessment revealed that 78% of residents would purchase locally-grown produce if available, and 62% would pay a premium for pesticide-free options. Our proposed aquaponics facility will bridge this gap, providing fresh, affordable vegetables 52 weeks per year within 2 miles of the county's largest food desert."

**Why It's Strong:**
- Specific data and statistics
- Clear problem statement
- Shows demand evidence
- Connects to funder priorities (food access, health)
- Demonstrates community need

---

#### Project Description Tips

**Use SMART Goals:**
- **S**pecific
- **M**easurable
- **A**chievable
- **R**elevant
- **T**ime-bound

**Example:**

**Vague:** "Increase local food production"

**SMART:** "Establish a 2,000 sq ft aquaponics facility producing 15,000 lbs of leafy greens and 2,000 lbs of tilapia annually by December 2026, serving 200 low-income families through subsidized CSA shares and providing nutrition education to 500 community members."

---

**Show, Don't Tell:**

| Telling | Showing |
|---------|---------|
| "We're experienced" | "Our team has operated three successful urban farms over 15 years, training 200+ new farmers" |
| "The project will help the community" | "Based on pilot results, participating families increased vegetable consumption by 45% and reduced grocery costs by $80/month" |
| "We'll educate people" | "Monthly workshops will train 30 participants in aquaponics basics, with 85% reporting they grew food at home within 6 months (pilot data)" |

#### Budget Justification

**For each line item, explain:**
- Why it's necessary
- How cost was determined
- How it supports project goals

**Example:**

**Farm Manager (0.5 FTE, $25,000):**
"The Farm Manager will oversee daily aquaponics operations, ensuring system health and optimal production. This half-time position is essential for maintaining the 2,000 sq ft system, conducting water quality testing, managing fish and plant health, coordinating volunteer work parties, and supervising educational tours. Salary is based on regional agricultural manager rates ($50,000 full-time) as documented by USDA agricultural wage survey for our region."

### 10.6 Demonstrating Impact

#### Measuring Outcomes

**Input → Output → Outcome → Impact**

**Example:**

| Level | Metric | Target |
|-------|--------|--------|
| **Input** | Workshop hours delivered | 120 hours/year |
| **Output** | Participants trained | 200 people/year |
| **Outcome** | Participants who start growing food | 150 people (75%) |
| **Impact** | Increased food security & nutrition | 30% increase in vegetable consumption among participants |

### 10.7 Letters of Support

#### Who to Ask

- **Customers:** Restaurants, CSA members
- **Partners:** Food hubs, distributors
- **Community Leaders:** City council, nonprofit directors
- **Technical Experts:** Extension agents, researchers
- **Other Stakeholders:** Schools, food banks

#### Letter Components

**Strong Letter Includes:**
1. Who they are and their connection to project
2. Specific ways they'll support/benefit
3. Why project is needed
4. Endorsement of your capability
5. Specific commitment (if applicable)

**Example Excerpt:**

"As Executive Director of the Northside Food Bank serving 2,500 families monthly, I enthusiastically support GreenCycle Farm's grant application. Access to fresh, locally-grown produce is our greatest unmet need—currently, 85% of our distributed food is shelf-stable. GreenCycle has committed to providing 200 lbs of fresh greens weekly at a subsidized rate, directly addressing this gap. Furthermore, their proposed nutrition education workshops will be offered at our facility, serving our clients. We commit to providing space for bi-weekly workshops and promoting the program through our newsletter reaching 5,000 families."

### 10.8 Grant Writing Tips

#### Do's and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Follow instructions exactly
- Meet all deadlines (submit early)
- Answer every question asked
- Use clear, concise language
- Include specific, measurable goals
- Provide supporting data
- Have others review before submission
- Keep under page limits
- Use requested formatting

**DON'T:**
- Use jargon or technical language
- Make unsupported claims
- Submit late or incomplete applications
- Exceed page limits
- Ignore evaluation criteria
- Forget to proofread
- Assume reviewers know your organization
- Leave sections blank
- Submit without reading it aloud

---

#### Common Mistakes

**1. Not Following Instructions**
- Instant disqualification in many cases
- Use a checklist for requirements

**2. Weak Statement of Need**
- Vague, no data
- **Fix:** Use specific statistics and evidence

**3. Unrealistic Goals**
- Overpromising
- **Fix:** Set achievable, measurable targets

**4. Poor Budget**
- Incomplete or unjustified
- **Fix:** Detail every line item, provide justification

**5. Lack of Evaluation Plan**
- No way to measure success
- **Fix:** Include specific metrics and methods

**6. Missing Sustainability**
- No plan beyond grant period
- **Fix:** Show how project continues

### 10.9 Submission & Follow-Up

#### Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Completed all required sections
- [ ] Met page limits
- [ ] Included all attachments
- [ ] Letters of support signed and dated
- [ ] Budget adds up correctly
- [ ] Proofread for errors
- [ ] Reviewed by 2+ people
- [ ] Formatted correctly
- [ ] Submitted by deadline
- [ ] Confirmation received

#### After Submission

**If Funded:**
- Thank the funder
- Comply with all reporting requirements
- Document everything
- Deliver on promises
- Maintain relationship
- Report success stories

**If Not Funded:**
- Request reviewer feedback
- Revise and resubmit (if allowed)
- Apply to other sources
- Learn from the process
- Don't take it personally
- Keep improving
