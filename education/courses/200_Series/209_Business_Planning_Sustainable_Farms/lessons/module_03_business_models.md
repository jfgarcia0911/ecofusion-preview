## Lesson Content

### 3.1 Common Farm Business Models

#### Revenue Model Comparison

| Model | Description | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|-------|-------------|------|------|----------|
| **Wholesale** | Sell bulk to distributors/retailers | Volume sales, predictable | Low margins, quality demands | Scale operations |
| **Direct-to-Consumer** | Farmers markets, farm stand, online | Higher margins, customer connection | Time intensive, smaller volume | Small-medium farms |
| **Restaurant/Food Service** | Supply to restaurants, caterers | Premium pricing, relationships | Demanding specs, reliability critical | Quality-focused farms |
| **CSA (Community Supported Agriculture)** | Pre-sold subscription shares | Cash upfront, guaranteed sales | Limits flexibility, member management | Community-oriented farms |
| **Value-Added Products** | Process into jams, sauces, dried goods | Higher margins, extended shelf life | Regulations, processing costs | Farms with excess or B-grade |
| **Agritourism** | Farm tours, events, education | Additional revenue, marketing | Liability, seasonality | Farms near population centers |
| **Hybrid/Multiple Channels** | Combination of above | Risk diversification, maximized revenue | Complex operations | Most commercial farms |

#### 1. Customer Segments

**Who are you creating value for?**

**Example Segments for Aquaponics Farm:**
- Health-conscious home consumers (age 28-55, income $75K+)
- Farm-to-table restaurants (upscale, local-focused)
- Natural food stores (Whole Foods, local co-ops)
- Corporate cafeterias (sustainability programs)

**Characteristics to Define:**
- Demographics
- Geographic location
- Psychographics (values, lifestyle)
- Purchasing behaviors
- Pain points/needs

---

#### 2. Value Proposition

**What unique value do you deliver?**

**Framework: [Product] helps [Customer Segment] [Benefit] by [Differentiation]**

**Examples:**

"UrbanGreens delivers pesticide-free leafy greens and fresh tilapia to Portland restaurants within 24 hours of harvest, enabling chefs to offer the freshest, most sustainable local ingredients year-round."

"FreshHarvest CSA provides busy families with a weekly box of organic vegetables grown using aquaponics, eliminating grocery shopping while supporting local, sustainable food production."

**Value Proposition Components:**
- Newness (innovative product/service)
- Performance (superior quality)
- Customization (tailored to customer)
- Design (aesthetics, presentation)
- Brand/Status (values alignment)
- Price (competitive or premium justified)
- Convenience (saves time/effort)
- Risk Reduction (guarantees, consistency)

### 3.3 Revenue Stream Design

#### Diversification Strategy

**Why Diversify Revenue?**
- Reduce dependency on single customer/channel
- Smooth seasonal fluctuations
- Maximize use of capacity
- Reach different customer segments

**Recommended Mix:**
- Primary revenue: 40-60% of total
- Secondary revenue: 25-35% of total
- Tertiary revenue: 10-20% of total
- Experimental/emerging: 5-10% of total

---

#### Pricing Strategies

**1. Cost-Plus Pricing**
```
Production Cost per Unit: $6.00
+ Desired Margin (40%): $2.40
= Selling Price: $8.40
```

**2. Value-Based Pricing**
```
Customer Value Perceived: High freshness, local, pesticide-free
Willingness to Pay: $14/lb
Competitor Price: $10/lb
Your Price: $12/lb (premium justified by value)
```

**3. Competitive Pricing**
```
Competitor A: $10/lb
Competitor B: $8/lb
Market Average: $9/lb
Your Price: $9.50/lb (slight premium for superior freshness)
```

**4. Tiered Pricing**

| Customer Type | Price/lb | Rationale |
|---------------|----------|-----------|
| Wholesale (bulk) | $7 | Lower margin, high volume |
| Restaurant | $10 | Regular orders, relationship |
| Retail/Farmers Market | $14 | Full retail, small quantities |
| CSA (subscription) | $8 effective | Prepaid, guaranteed, some variety risk |

### 3.4 Case Study: Complete Business Model

**Example: GreenCycle Aquaponics**

**Customer Segments:**
1. Farm-to-table restaurants (30% of revenue)
2. CSA members (40% of revenue)
3. Farmers market shoppers (20% of revenue)
4. Educational workshops (10% of revenue)

**Value Proposition:**
"Fresh, pesticide-free leafy greens and herbs delivered within 24 hours of harvest, grown sustainably using aquaponics that uses 90% less water than conventional farming."

**Channels:**
- Direct sales calls (restaurants)
- Website with online ordering (CSA)
- Weekly farmers market presence (retail)
- Social media marketing (all segments)
- Delivery van (all segments)

**Customer Relationships:**
- Dedicated account manager for restaurants
- Monthly CSA member events and farm tours
- Personal interaction at farmers market
- Email newsletter to all customers
- Social media community building

**Revenue Streams:**
- Restaurant sales: $90,000/year
- CSA subscriptions (50 members): $120,000/year
- Farmers market: $60,000/year
- Workshop fees: $30,000/year
- **Total Revenue: $300,000/year**

**Key Activities:**
- Daily system monitoring and maintenance
- 3x weekly harvesting
- CSA box preparation and delivery
- Weekly farmers market attendance
- Restaurant relationship management
- Monthly educational workshops

**Key Resources:**
- 2,000 sq ft greenhouse with aquaponics system
- Delivery van
- 500 sq ft packing facility
- Owner + 1.5 FTE employees
- 20 years combined aquaponics expertise
- Established customer base and reputation

**Key Partners:**
- Local fish hatchery (fingerling supplier)
- Seed company (certified organic seeds)
- Food hub (backup distribution)
- University extension (technical support)
- Other local farms (co-marketing)

**Cost Structure:**
- Total annual costs: $240,000
- Key costs: Labor (38%), Utilities (15%), Feed/Seeds (12%)
- **Net Profit: $60,000 (20% margin)**

### 3.5 Business Model Testing & Iteration

#### Assumptions to Test

Every business model contains assumptions. Test them before full commitment:

**Critical Assumptions:**
- [ ] Customers will pay $X for our product
- [ ] We can produce Y volume with our system
- [ ] Restaurants will commit to regular weekly orders
- [ ] CSA members will renew at Z% rate
- [ ] Our cost structure is accurate
- [ ] Distribution method is viable
- [ ] We can achieve target quality consistently

**How to Test:**
- Pilot programs
- Pre-sales campaigns
- Small-scale trials
- Customer interviews
- Competitive analysis

---

#### Pivot Indicators

**When to Consider Pivoting Your Business Model:**

- Customer acquisition cost > customer lifetime value
- Churn rate exceeds 40% annually
- Unable to achieve target margins
- Production capacity misaligned with demand
- Better opportunity emerges from customer feedback
- Competitive landscape changes dramatically

**Types of Pivots:**
- **Customer Segment** - Target different customers
- **Value Proposition** - Emphasize different benefits
- **Channel** - Change how you reach customers
- **Revenue Model** - Alter pricing/sales approach
- **Product** - Add/remove products based on demand
