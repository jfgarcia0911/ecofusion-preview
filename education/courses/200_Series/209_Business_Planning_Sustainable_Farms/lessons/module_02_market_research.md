### 2.2 Types of Market Research

#### Primary Research (You Collect Data)

**Qualitative Methods:**

**1. Customer Interviews**
- One-on-one conversations
- Deep understanding of needs
- Explore motivations and behaviors
- Sample size: 15-30 interviews

**Interview Guide Template:**
```
CUSTOMER INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Opening:
- Tell me about how you currently buy [product category]
- What are your biggest frustrations with [current solution]?

Needs Discovery:
- What characteristics are most important to you?
- How do you define quality in [product]?
- What would make you switch to a new supplier?

Willingness to Pay:
- What do you currently pay for [product]?
- How much value do you place on [differentiator]?
- Would you pay a premium for [benefit]? How much?

Purchasing Behavior:
- How often do you purchase [product]?
- What quantity do you typically order?
- Who makes the purchasing decision?
```

**2. Focus Groups**
- 6-10 people discuss product/concept
- Observe group dynamics
- Generate ideas and feedback
- Useful for product development

**3. Surveys**
- Quantify preferences and behaviors
- Larger sample sizes (100+)
- Statistical validation
- Online tools make it easy

**Quantitative Methods:**

**4. Experiments**
- Test pricing at farmers market
- A/B test product offerings
- Trial sales periods
- Pre-order campaigns

**5. Observation**
- Watch competitors' customer flow
- Analyze purchasing patterns
- Study farmers market dynamics
- Note what sells out first

### 2.4 Market Size Analysis

#### Total Addressable Market (TAM)

**TAM** = Total revenue opportunity if you had 100% market share

**Example Calculation: Salad Greens in Portland Metro**
```
Population: 2,500,000
  × % who eat salad regularly (65%): 1,625,000
  × Average consumption per person per year: 12 lbs
  = Total market: 19,500,000 lbs/year
  × Average price per lb: $8
  = TAM: $156,000,000/year
```

#### Serviceable Available Market (SAM)

**SAM** = Portion of TAM you can realistically reach

**Refining the Portland Example:**
```
TAM: $156,000,000
  × % within your distribution area (20%): $31,200,000
  × % who buy premium/local (30%): $9,360,000
  = SAM: $9,360,000/year
```

#### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)

**SOM** = Realistic market share you can capture in near term

**Being Conservative:**
```
SAM: $9,360,000
  × Realistic market share Year 1 (0.5%): $46,800
  × Realistic market share Year 3 (2%): $187,200
  × Realistic market share Year 5 (5%): $468,000
  = SOM Projections
```

### 2.5 Competitive Analysis

#### Identifying Competitors

**Direct Competitors:**
- Sell same products to same customers
- Example: Other local hydroponic farms

**Indirect Competitors:**
- Sell different products that satisfy same need
- Example: Conventional farms, imported greens

**Substitute Products:**
- Alternative solutions to customer problem
- Example: Frozen vegetables, meal kits

---

#### Competitive Analysis Framework

| Competitor | Products | Price Point | Strengths | Weaknesses | Market Share |

### 2.6 Customer Needs Assessment

#### The Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

Customers "hire" your product to accomplish specific jobs:

**Functional Jobs:**
- Feed family nutritious meals
- Stock restaurant kitchen
- Have fresh salad ingredients

**Emotional Jobs:**
- Feel good about supporting local
- Express environmental values
- Provide best for family

**Social Jobs:**
- Impress dinner guests
- Appear health-conscious
- Support community

---

#### Pain Points & Gain Creators

**Common Customer Pain Points:**

For Home Consumers:
- Grocery store produce not fresh
- Don't know where food comes from
- Pesticide concerns
- Limited local options in winter
- Inconsistent quality

For Restaurant Customers:
- Unreliable suppliers
- Inconsistent product specifications
- Limited local sourcing options
- Price fluctuations
- Need year-round availability

**Your Gain Creators:**
- Harvest-to-delivery in 24 hours
- Full transparency and farm tours
- Pesticide-free growing method
- 52-week production
- Consistent sizing and quality
- Stable, predictable pricing
- Local sourcing story for menus

### 2.7 Market Trends & Opportunities

#### Major Food Industry Trends (2025)

**1. Local Food Movement**
- 42% of consumers prioritize local sourcing
- Willingness to pay 20-30% premium
- "Food miles" becoming purchase factor

**2. Year-Round Local**
- Gap in winter months for local produce
- CEA enables local production 365 days
- Growing demand for consistency

**3. Transparency & Traceability**
- Farm-to-table tracking
- QR codes linking to farm information
- Video tours and social media engagement

**4. Health & Wellness**
- Organic and pesticide-free preference
- Nutrient density awareness
- Functional foods and superfoods

**5. Sustainability**
- Water conservation matters to consumers
- Carbon footprint consciousness
- Regenerative agriculture interest

**6. Convenience**
- Pre-washed, ready-to-eat
- Subscription models (CSA 2.0)
- Online ordering and delivery

### 2.8 Validating Demand

#### Pre-Sales Strategy

**Don't build until you've sold!**

**Validation Approaches:**

**1. Pre-Orders/Crowdfunding**
- Launch Kickstarter for CSA subscriptions
- Take deposits for first season
- Validates willingness to pay
- Generates startup capital

**2. Letter of Intent**
- Get restaurants to commit to purchasing
- Specify quantities and pricing
- Not legally binding but shows interest
- Helps with loan applications

**3. Pilot Program**
- Start with small production
- Test with select customers
- Refine product-market fit
- Build testimonials and case studies

**4. Farmers Market Testing**
- Sell at established market
- Test pricing and products
- Gather real customer feedback
- Build email list for future sales

---

#### Market Validation Checklist

Before significant investment, can you answer YES to:

- [ ] I have interviewed at least 20 potential customers
- [ ] I have identified 3+ customer segments willing to buy
- [ ] I have validated pricing through actual test sales
- [ ] I have letters of intent or pre-orders for first 3 months
- [ ] I understand exactly why customers will choose me over alternatives
- [ ] I have confirmed my value proposition solves real pain points
- [ ] I know my customer acquisition cost and channels
- [ ] I have evidence of market size sufficient for my goals

**If you can't check most of these boxes, do more research before proceeding!**
