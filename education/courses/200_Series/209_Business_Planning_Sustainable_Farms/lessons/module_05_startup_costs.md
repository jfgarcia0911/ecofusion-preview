### 5.2 Working Capital Requirements

#### What is Working Capital?

**Working Capital** = Money needed to operate before revenue covers expenses

**Components:**
- Cash for operating expenses during ramp-up
- Inventory (seeds, supplies)
- Accounts receivable (customer payment terms)
- Buffer for unexpected expenses

**Calculation:**

```
Monthly Operating Expenses: $22,700
× Months Until Break-Even: 18 months
= Total Operating Burn: $408,600

Revenue During Ramp-Up (18 months): $180,000
Cash Needed from Operations: $228,600

Add:
  Inventory Buffer: $15,000
  AR Buffer (30-day terms): $20,000
  Contingency (15%): $39,000
  ───────────────────────
Working Capital Needed: $302,600
```

**Rule of Thumb:** Plan for 6-18 months of operating expenses as working capital

### 5.4 Reducing Startup Costs

#### Cost Reduction Strategies

**1. Start Smaller**
- Build 1,000 sq ft instead of 2,000
- Reduce initial costs by 40-50%
- Scale up after proving concept

**2. Phased Build-Out**
- Phase 1: Core system (6-12 months)
- Phase 2: Expansion (Year 2)
- Phase 3: Optimization (Year 3)

**3. Used/Refurbished Equipment**
- Used greenhouses: Save 30-50%
- Refurbished vehicles: Save 40-60%
- Surplus equipment auctions

**4. DIY Where Appropriate**
- Build some components yourself
- Site prep work
- Simple construction
- **Caution:** Don't DIY critical systems without expertise

**5. Lease vs. Buy**

| Item | Buy | Lease | Savings |
|------|-----|-------|---------|
| Delivery Vehicle | $30,000 | $450/mo | Lower upfront |
| Facility | $300,000 | $3,500/mo | $300,000 upfront |
| Equipment | $50,000 | $1,200/mo | Flexibility |

**6. Negotiate Payment Terms**
- Extended payment terms with suppliers
- Supplier financing for equipment
- Phased payments for construction

**7. Partner/Share Resources**
- Shared processing facility
- Co-op purchasing for discounts
- Shared distribution

**8. Focus on Revenue-Generating Assets First**
- Delay non-essential upgrades
- Minimum viable product approach
- Invest in what directly generates revenue

### 5.5 Contingency Planning

#### Why Contingencies Matter

**Common Unexpected Costs:**
- Construction delays and overruns (average 15-25%)
- Equipment failures
- Regulatory compliance additions
- Learning curve mistakes
- Market delays (slower customer acquisition)

**Recommended Contingency:**
- Construction/Equipment: Add 15-20%
- Working Capital: Add 20-30%
- Overall Budget: Include 10-15% contingency line item

### 5.7 Example Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Bootstrap Startup (Minimal Capital)

**Total Budget: $75,000**

**Strategy:**
- Backyard/small rented space
- 500 sq ft system
- Used greenhouse
- DIY where possible
- Owner does all labor initially
- Focus on farmers markets and CSA
- No employees first year

**Trade-offs:**
- Slower growth
- More personal labor
- Limited capacity
- Revenue potential: $40,000-$60,000/year

---

#### Scenario 2: Small Business Loan

**Total Budget: $250,000**

**Strategy:**
- 1,000 sq ft commercial space
- New greenhouse + systems
- Some automation
- 1 part-time employee
- Multi-channel sales
- Professional marketing

**Trade-offs:**
- Debt service obligations
- Moderate growth
- Revenue potential: $120,000-$180,000/year
- Break-even: Year 2-3

---

#### Scenario 3: Investor-Backed

**Total Budget: $750,000**

**Strategy:**
- 2,500 sq ft state-of-the-art facility
- Full automation
- 3-4 employees from start
- Aggressive marketing
- Multi-year runway

**Trade-offs:**
- Investor expectations
- Equity dilution
- Higher fixed costs
- Revenue potential: $350,000-$500,000/year
- Break-even: Year 2-3
- Exit pressure
