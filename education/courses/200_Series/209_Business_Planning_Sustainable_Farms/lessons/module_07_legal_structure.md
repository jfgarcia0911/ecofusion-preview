### 7.2 Sole Proprietorship

**Description:** You and the business are legally the same entity

**Pros:**
- Simplest to form
- Lowest cost ($50-$200)
- Complete control
- Easy tax filing (Schedule C)
- No separate business tax return

**Cons:**
- **Unlimited personal liability** (biggest risk!)
- Personal assets at risk
- Harder to raise capital
- Ends if you die/become disabled
- Less credible to partners/customers

**Tax Treatment:**
- Business income/losses on personal tax return
- Self-employment tax (15.3%) on all profit
- No salary - all income is business profit

**Formation Steps:**
1. Choose business name (DBA if different from your name)
2. Obtain EIN from IRS
3. Register with state/local government
4. Get necessary licenses

**Typical Costs:** $50-$500

**Best For:**
- Testing business concept
- Very small operations
- Temporary/side businesses
- When transitioning to LLC later

### 7.3 Partnership

#### Types of Partnerships

**General Partnership (GP)**
- All partners share liability
- Simple formation
- Pass-through taxation
- **Risk:** Each partner liable for others' actions

**Limited Partnership (LP)**
- General partners (unlimited liability)
- Limited partners (liability limited to investment)
- Common for investor arrangements

**Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)**
- All partners have limited liability
- Common for professional services
- Not available in all states for farms

---

**Partnership Pros:**
- Easy to form
- Combines resources/skills
- Shared risk and workload
- Pass-through taxation

**Partnership Cons:**
- Unlimited liability (GP)
- Joint and several liability
- Potential for disputes
- Partnership agreement essential

**Tax Treatment:**
- Pass-through to partners
- Form 1065 partnership return
- Partners receive K-1 forms
- Self-employment tax applies

**Critical Document:** Partnership Agreement
- Ownership percentages
- Profit/loss allocation
- Management responsibilities
- Dispute resolution
- Exit/buyout provisions

**Best For:**
- Family farms
- Co-founder situations
- Joint ventures
- When trust is very high

### 7.4 Limited Liability Company (LLC)

**Description:** Hybrid structure combining liability protection of corporation with tax flexibility

**Why LLCs Are Popular for Farms:**
1. Personal asset protection
2. Flexible taxation
3. Less formality than corporation
4. Credibility with lenders/partners
5. Flexible ownership structure

**LLC Pros:**
- Limited liability protection
- Pass-through taxation (default)
- Can elect S-Corp or C-Corp taxation
- Flexible management structure
- Flexible ownership (members)
- Minimal formalities
- Can have single member or multiple

**LLC Cons:**
- More complex than sole proprietorship
- State filing fees ($100-$800)
- Annual fees/taxes in some states
- Self-employment tax on all earnings (unless S-Corp election)
- Operating agreement recommended

**Formation Steps:**
1. Choose business name (check availability)
2. File Articles of Organization with state
3. Create Operating Agreement
4. Obtain EIN
5. Register for state taxes
6. Get business licenses
7. Annual filings/fees

**Typical Costs:**
- Formation: $100-$800
- Attorney (optional): $500-$2,000
- Annual fees: $0-$800/year

**Tax Options for LLCs:**

**Single-Member LLC:**
- Default: Disregarded entity (Schedule C)
- Option: S-Corp election
- Option: C-Corp election

**Multi-Member LLC:**
- Default: Partnership taxation (Form 1065)
- Option: S-Corp election
- Option: C-Corp election

**Best For:**
- Most small to medium farms
- 1-5 member operations
- When liability protection needed
- Flexible growth plans

### 7.5 S-Corporation

**Description:** Corporate structure with pass-through taxation

**S-Corp Pros:**
- Limited liability
- Pass-through taxation
- **Tax savings:** Only salary subject to self-employment tax
- Credibility with lenders
- Perpetual existence
- Clear ownership structure (shares)

**S-Corp Cons:**
- More formalities (board meetings, minutes)
- Restrictions: Max 100 shareholders, one class of stock
- Must pay yourself "reasonable salary"
- More complex accounting
- State filing fees
- Annual compliance requirements

**Tax Advantage Example:**

**LLC (Default) Taxation:**
```
Business Profit: $100,000
Self-Employment Tax (15.3%): $15,300
Income Tax (22% bracket): $22,000
Total Tax: $37,300
```

**S-Corp Taxation:**
```
Salary: $60,000
  Self-Employment Tax: $9,180
  Income Tax: $13,200

Distribution: $40,000
  Self-Employment Tax: $0
  Income Tax: $8,800

Total Tax: $31,180
Savings: $6,120/year
```

**Formation Steps:**
1. File Articles of Incorporation
2. Create corporate bylaws
3. Issue stock
4. Hold organizational meeting
5. Elect S-Corp status (Form 2553)
6. Obtain EIN
7. Register for payroll taxes

**Best For:**
- Profitable farms ($50K+ net income)
- Multiple employees
- Clear ownership structure desired
- Tax savings justify added complexity

### 7.6 C-Corporation

**Description:** Traditional corporation with double taxation

**C-Corp Pros:**
- Limited liability
- Unlimited shareholders
- Multiple share classes
- Easier to raise venture capital
- Attractive to investors
- Can retain earnings
- More fringe benefit options

**C-Corp Cons:**
- **Double taxation** (corporate + personal)
- Most complex structure
- Highest compliance costs
- Significant formalities
- Most expensive to form/maintain

**Tax Treatment:**
```
Corporate Profit: $100,000
Corporate Tax (21%): $21,000
After-tax Profit: $79,000

Dividend Distribution: $79,000
Personal Tax (20% qualified dividend): $15,800

Total Tax: $36,800 (vs $31,180 for S-Corp)
```

**Best For:**
- Investor-backed ventures
- Planning for acquisition/IPO
- Retaining significant earnings
- Complex ownership structures
- Not typical for most farms

---

### 7.7 Cooperative

**Description:** Member-owned business where members share profits

**Types:**
- Producer Co-ops (farmers sell together)
- Consumer Co-ops (buyers own collectively)
- Worker Co-ops (employees own business)

**Co-op Pros:**
- Democratic control (one member, one vote)
- Shared resources and risk
- Tax advantages (single taxation)
- Community alignment
- Grant opportunities

**Co-op Cons:**
- Complex formation
- Requires multiple committed members
- Slower decision-making
- Specialized legal requirements
- Limited individual control

**Best For:**
- Groups of farmers working together
- Community-supported models
- Shared processing/distribution
- Mission-driven organizations

---

### 7.8 Nonprofit (501(c)(3))

**Description:** Tax-exempt organization with charitable/educational mission

**Nonprofit Pros:**
- Tax-exempt status
- Grant eligibility
- Donation acceptance
- Mission-driven
- Property tax exemptions (often)

**Nonprofit Cons:**
- Cannot distribute profits to individuals
- Extensive reporting requirements
- IRS oversight
- Compensation restrictions
- Mission constraints on activities

**Best For:**
- Educational farms
- Community gardens
- Food access programs
- Research farms
- Not for profit-driven farms

### 7.9 Decision Framework

#### Choosing Your Structure

**Ask These Questions:**

**1. Liability Risk?**
- High risk (employees, customers, land): LLC, Corp
- Low risk (small, solo): Sole proprietorship okay initially

**2. Number of Owners?**
- Solo: Sole proprietorship or LLC
- 2-5: LLC or Partnership
- Many: Corporation or Cooperative

**3. Tax Situation?**
- Profit $0-$40K: Sole proprietorship or LLC
- Profit $40K-$100K: LLC with S-Corp election
- Profit $100K+: S-Corp
- Investors: C-Corp

**4. Growth Plans?**
- Testing concept: Sole proprietorship
- Small stable: LLC
- Growing/scaling: S-Corp
- Venture-backed: C-Corp

**5. Complexity Tolerance?**
- Minimum: Sole proprietorship
- Moderate: LLC
- Higher: S-Corp
- Maximum: C-Corp

```
WEEK 1-2: PLANNING

├─ Choose business name
├─ Research requirements
└─ Consult attorney/accountant

├─ Create operating documents
```
