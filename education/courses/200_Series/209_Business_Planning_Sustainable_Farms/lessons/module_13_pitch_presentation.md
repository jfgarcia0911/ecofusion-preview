## Lesson Content

### 13.1 Types of Pitches

#### Elevator Pitch (30-60 seconds)

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (5 sec): Attention-grabbing statement
2. **Problem** (10 sec): What problem exists
3. **Solution** (15 sec): How you solve it
4. **Traction** (10 sec): Proof it works
5. **Ask** (10 sec): What you need

**Example:**

"Most Portland restaurants want to serve local greens year-round, but local farms shut down every winter. We're GreenCycle Aquaponics, and we produce pesticide-free leafy greens 365 days a year using 90% less water than conventional farming. We've already secured commitments from 8 farm-to-table restaurants representing $85,000 in annual revenue. We're raising $250,000 to build our facility and capture this $450 million local food market. Can we schedule 20 minutes next week to show you our full plan?"

---

### 13.3 Presentation Skills

#### Delivery Tips

**Before the Presentation:**
- **Practice 10+ times** (out loud)
- Time yourself (stay under limit)
- Record yourself and review
- Practice with audience feedback
- Prepare for technical issues (have backup)
- Arrive early to set up and test
- Bring printed copies of deck

**During the Presentation:**

**Body Language:**
- Stand tall, confident posture
- Make eye contact with everyone
- Use natural gestures
- Move purposefully (don't pace)
- Face the audience, not the screen
- Smile genuinely

**Voice:**
- Speak clearly and at moderate pace
- Vary tone and volume for emphasis
- Pause for effect
- Project confidence
- Avoid filler words ("um," "like," "so")

**Engagement:**
- Start with a hook
- Tell stories, not just facts
- Ask rhetorical questions
- Read the room and adjust
- Show passion for your mission
- Connect authentically

### 13.4 Handling Questions

#### Common Questions & Answers

**Q: "What happens if a competitor enters the market?"**

**Weak:** "Um, we haven't really thought about that..."

**Strong:** "Great question. Competition validates market demand. Our barriers to entry include: First, our 8 restaurant commitments with 2-year contracts provide stability. Second, our team's 8 years of aquaponics experience creates a knowledge advantage new entrants won't have. Third, we're building brand loyalty through our CSA and community engagement. Finally, our focus is execution—we plan to be so good at what we do that customers won't consider switching. That said, we monitor the competitive landscape quarterly and can pivot our strategy if needed."

---

**Q: "Why won't restaurants just buy cheaper conventional greens?"**

**Weak:** "Because local is better."

**Strong:** "We've validated this directly with our 8 committed restaurants. They told us three things matter more than price: First, freshness—our 24-hour harvest-to-delivery provides measurably better quality and shelf life than 5-7 day conventional. Second, story—their customers want to support local and sustainable, and our greens give them that story. Third, consistency—conventional supply and quality varies; we provide the same high quality year-round. We're priced 15-25% above conventional, which they've accepted because the value justifies it. Our pilot demonstrated 98% retention, proving customers stick with us."

---

**Q: "What if you lose your key person (CEO)?"**

**Weak:** "Well, that would be bad..."

**Strong:** "Smart risk to address. We've mitigated this several ways: First, we have key person insurance ($500K policy). Second, our COO James is cross-trained on all aquaponics operations and can step in. Third, we have documented SOPs for every critical process. Fourth, our advisor Dr. Lee has committed to providing technical backup. Finally, we're building a team culture, not a one-person operation—by Year 2, we'll have two experienced technicians who can maintain operations. But yes, it's a risk we take seriously."

---

#### Q&A Best Practices

**DO:**
- Listen completely before answering
- Pause to think (it's okay!)
- Repeat/rephrase question for clarity
- Answer directly and concisely
- Admit when you don't know (offer to follow up)
- Stay calm and professional
- Thank them for tough questions

**DON'T:**
- Get defensive
- Argue with questioner
- Ramble or go off-topic
- Make up answers
- Dismiss concerns
- Be arrogant
- Show frustration

**If Stumped:**
"That's an excellent question I want to answer thoughtfully. Can I research that and get back to you tomorrow?"

### 13.5 Adapting for Audience

#### For Lenders

**Emphasize:**
- Stable, predictable cash flow
- Collateral and personal assets
- Conservative projections
- Risk mitigation
- Market validation
- Experience and character

**Tone:** Professional, conservative, risk-aware

**Key Slide Adjustments:**
- Show debt service coverage ratio
- Highlight personal financial strength
- Detail collateral
- Conservative growth assumptions

---

#### For Investors

**Emphasize:**
- Large market opportunity
- High growth potential
- Competitive moats
- Scalability
- Team capabilities
- Return on investment

**Tone:** Exciting, ambitious, confident

**Key Slide Adjustments:**
- Market size prominent
- Growth trajectory emphasized
- Exit strategy included
- Comparable valuations shown

---

#### For Grants

**Emphasize:**
- Mission alignment
- Community impact
- Innovation
- Education/knowledge sharing
- Sustainability
- Measurable outcomes

**Tone:** Impact-focused, educational, collaborative

**Key Slide Adjustments:**
- Impact metrics featured
- Community partners highlighted
- Educational components emphasized
- Long-term sustainability shown

### 13.6 Virtual Presentation Tips

#### Technical Setup

**Equipment:**
- Good webcam (or phone camera)
- External microphone
- Proper lighting (ring light or natural)
- Stable internet connection
- Backup device ready

**Environment:**
- Quiet, professional background
- Minimal distractions
- Good lighting (face clearly visible)
- Professional appearance (full outfit, not just top!)

**Platform Preparation:**
- Test audio/video beforehand
- Practice screen sharing
- Have tech support contact ready
- Close unnecessary programs
- Silence notifications

---

#### Engagement Strategies

**Stay Engaged:**
- Look at camera (not screen) when speaking
- Use hand gestures (visible in frame)
- Check chat for questions
- Call participants by name
- Use polls/questions for interaction

**Managing Virtual Q&A:**
- Monitor chat during presentation
- Designate moderator if possible
- Take questions at designated times
- Repeat questions before answering
- Use "raise hand" feature
