### 9.2 Debt Financing

#### Types of Loans

**1. SBA Loans (Small Business Administration)**

**SBA 7(a) Loan**
- Amount: Up to $5 million
- Terms: 10-25 years
- Use: Working capital, equipment, real estate
- Interest: Prime + 2.25-4.75%
- Down Payment: 10-20%
- Guarantee: SBA guarantees 75-85%

**SBA Microloan**
- Amount: Up to $50,000
- Terms: Up to 6 years
- Use: Working capital, equipment
- Interest: 8-13%
- Easier to qualify than 7(a)

**2. USDA Loans**

**Farm Service Agency (FSA) Loans:**
- Beginning Farmer Loans
- Farm Ownership Loans (up to $600,000)
- Operating Loans (up to $400,000)
- Favorable terms for new farmers
- Lower interest rates

**Rural Development Loans:**
- Business & Industry Loan Guarantee
- Up to $25 million
- For rural area businesses
- Job creation emphasis

**3. Traditional Bank Loans**

**Commercial Term Loan**
- Amount: $25,000-$5M+
- Terms: 1-20 years
- Collateral: Usually required
- Interest: 5-10%
- Best for: Established businesses with good credit

**Equipment Financing**
- Amount: Equipment purchase price
- Terms: 3-7 years
- Collateral: The equipment itself
- Interest: 5-12%
- Down Payment: 10-20%

**Line of Credit**
- Amount: $10,000-$500,000
- Revolving credit
- Pay interest only on amount used
- For: Working capital, seasonal cash flow

**4. Alternative Lenders**

**Online Lenders (Kabbage, OnDeck, etc.)**
- Faster approval (days vs. months)
- Less stringent requirements
- Higher interest rates (12-30%)
- Shorter terms (3-36 months)

**Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs)**
- Mission-driven lenders
- Serve underserved communities
- More flexible terms
- Technical assistance often included

### 9.3 Equity Financing

#### Giving Up Ownership for Capital

**Pros of Equity:**
- No monthly payments
- No personal guarantee (usually)
- Partners bring expertise
- Shared risk

**Cons of Equity:**
- Give up ownership %
- Loss of control
- Profit sharing
- Exit expectations

---

#### Types of Equity Investors

**1. Friends & Family**
- Amount: $5,000-$100,000
- Equity: 5-25%
- Pros: Easier to raise, flexible terms
- Cons: Relationship risk

**Structure Options:**
- Convertible note (debt that converts to equity)
- Simple equity purchase
- Revenue share agreement

**2. Angel Investors**
- Amount: $25,000-$500,000
- Equity: 10-30%
- Typical: $50,000-$150,000
- Individuals investing personal funds
- Often provide mentorship

**Where to Find:**
- Angel investment networks
- Industry conferences
- LinkedIn
- Referrals from other entrepreneurs

**3. Venture Capital**
- Amount: $500,000-$10M+
- Equity: 20-40%
- High growth expectations
- Eventual exit required (acquisition or IPO)
- Rare for traditional farms
- More common for ag-tech

**4. Impact Investors**
- Focus on social/environmental impact + financial return
- Patient capital (longer timeline)
- Lower return expectations than VC
- Aligned with sustainable ag values

**Examples:**
- Slow Money
- RSF Social Finance
- Local food investment funds

### 9.4 Grants

#### Federal Grants

**USDA Value-Added Producer Grants (VAPG)**
- Amount: Up to $250,000
- Use: Processing, marketing value-added products
- Match: 1:1 required (can be in-kind)
- Competitive: ~30% success rate
- Deadline: Typically spring

**USDA Sustainable Agriculture Research & Education (SARE)**
- Amount: $5,000-$30,000
- Use: Research, education, innovation
- Farmer-led projects encouraged
- Multiple deadlines per year

**NRCS Environmental Quality Incentives Program (EQIP)**
- Amount: Varies
- Use: Conservation practices, water conservation
- Cost-share: 50-90% of project
- Annual signup periods

**National Institute of Food and Agriculture (NIFA)**
- Various grant programs
- Often require university partnership
- Research and education focus

---

#### State & Local Grants

**State Department of Agriculture Grants**
- Vary by state
- Specialty crop grants
- Beginning farmer programs
- Marketing and promotion
- Equipment assistance

**Economic Development Grants**
- Job creation incentives
- Rural development funds
- Community development block grants (CDBG)

**Local/Regional Grants:**
- Community foundation grants
- Food policy council grants
- Watershed protection grants

**Example State Programs:**
- CA: Specialty Crop Block Grant Program
- NY: FarmNet Grant Program
- OR: Agricultural Water Quality Grants
- (Research your specific state programs)

---

#### Private Foundation Grants

### 9.5 Creative Financing

#### Alternative Funding Methods

**1. Crowdfunding**

**Platforms:**
- **Barnraiser:** Farm-specific crowdfunding
- **Kickstarter:** Rewards-based (all or nothing)
- **Indiegogo:** Flexible or fixed funding
- **GoFundMe:** Donation-based

**Success Tips:**
- Compelling story/video
- Clear rewards/perks
- $10,000-$50,000 realistic goal
- Active promotion required
- Build email list first

**2. Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) Pre-Sales**
- Sell shares before season
- Immediate working capital
- Builds customer base
- Reduces marketing costs

**Example:**
50 members × $600/year = $30,000 upfront capital

**3. Revenue-Based Financing**
- Repay based on % of monthly revenue
- No equity given up
- Flexible payments (high months = higher payment)
- 1.3-2.5x repayment multiple
- Growing in popularity

**4. Vendor Financing**
- Equipment suppliers offer financing
- Net 30/60/90 payment terms with suppliers
- Deferred payment arrangements

**5. Lease Arrangements**
- Lease land instead of buying
- Lease equipment instead of buying
- Reduces upfront capital needs

**6. Partnerships**
- Partner owns land, you operate
- Revenue share arrangement
- Sweat equity contribution

### 9.6 Capital Raising Strategy

#### Developing Your Funding Mix

**Example Capital Stack: $400,000 Needed**

```
FUNDING SOURCE              AMOUNT        % OF TOTAL
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Personal Savings           $ 50,000         12.5%
Friends & Family Loan      $ 30,000          7.5%
SBA 7(a) Loan             $200,000         50.0%
Equipment Financing        $ 60,000         15.0%
USDA VAPG Grant           $ 40,000         10.0%
CSA Pre-Sales             $ 20,000          5.0%
                          ─────────        ──────
TOTAL                     $400,000         100%

Debt: $290,000 (72.5%)
Equity: $50,000 (12.5%)
Grants: $40,000 (10%)
Revenue: $20,000 (5%)
```

#### Timeline for Fundraising

```
MONTH 1-2: PREPARATION
├─ Finalize business plan
├─ Create financial projections
├─ Develop pitch deck
├─ Organize financial documents
└─ Research funding sources

MONTH 3-4: APPLICATIONS
├─ Apply for grants
├─ Submit loan applications
├─ Reach out to potential investors
├─ Launch crowdfunding (if applicable)
└─ Secure pre-sales

MONTH 5-6: FOLLOW-UP & NEGOTIATION
├─ Respond to due diligence requests
├─ Negotiate terms
├─ Finalize agreements
└─ Close funding

Total Timeline: 3-6 months typical
(Some grants may take 6-12 months)
```

### 9.7 Preparing for Funders

#### Required Documents

**For Lenders:**
- [ ] Complete business plan
- [ ] 3-5 year financial projections
- [ ] Personal financial statement
- [ ] Personal credit report (700+ preferred)
- [ ] Business credit report (if existing)
- [ ] Tax returns (3 years personal, business if existing)
- [ ] Bank statements (6 months)
- [ ] Collateral documentation
- [ ] Legal formation documents

**For Investors:**
- [ ] Executive summary (2 pages)
- [ ] Pitch deck (10-15 slides)
- [ ] Financial model (detailed)
- [ ] Market research data
- [ ] Competitive analysis
- [ ] Team backgrounds
- [ ] Use of funds breakdown
- [ ] Exit strategy

**For Grants:**
- [ ] Project narrative
- [ ] Budget and budget justification
- [ ] Timeline/milestones
- [ ] Evaluation plan
- [ ] Organizational background
- [ ] Letters of support
- [ ] Matching fund documentation
