## Lesson Content

### 12.1 Business Plan Overview

#### Why You Need a Business Plan

**For You:**
- Clarifies your vision and strategy
- Identifies gaps and challenges
- Forces realistic financial planning
- Provides roadmap for execution
- Helps measure progress

**For Others:**
- Demonstrates viability to lenders
- Shows preparation to investors
- Proves capacity to grant funders
- Attracts partners and employees

---

```

   ├─ Product Descriptions

   └─ Future Development

VI. MARKETING & SALES (8-12 pages)
    ├─ Marketing Strategy
    ├─ Sales Strategy
    ├─ Distribution Channels
    ├─ Customer Acquisition

VII. OPERATIONS PLAN (8-12 pages)
     ├─ Production Process
     ├─ Facilities & Equipment
     ├─ Supply Chain
     ├─ Quality Control
     ├─ Technology
     └─ Regulatory Compliance

VIII. FINANCIAL PLAN (10-15 pages)
      ├─ Startup Costs
      ├─ Revenue Projections
      ├─ Expense Budget
      ├─ Pro Forma Statements
      ├─ Cash Flow Analysis
      ├─ Break-Even Analysis
      ├─ Funding Request

    ├─ Resumes
    ├─ Letters of Support
    ├─ Market Research Data
    ├─ Technical Specifications
    ├─ Legal Documents
    └─ Financial Assumptions

TOTAL LENGTH: 40-70 pages + appendices
```

### 12.2 Executive Summary

#### The Most Important Section

**Why It Matters:**
- Read first (often only section read initially)
- Determines if reader continues
- Must stand alone
- Convince reader of viability in 2 pages

**What to Include:**

**1. Business Concept (2-3 paragraphs)**
- What you do
- Who you serve
- How you're different

**2. Market Opportunity (1-2 paragraphs)**
- Market size
- Growth trends
- Gap you're filling

**3. Competitive Advantage (1 paragraph)**
- Your unique strengths
- Why customers will choose you

**4. Business Model (1 paragraph)**
- How you make money
- Revenue streams
- Unit economics

**5. Team (1 paragraph)**
- Key people and expertise
- Advisory support

**6. Financial Highlights (1 paragraph)**
- Startup costs
- Revenue projections (3-5 year)
- Profitability timeline
- Key metrics

**7. Funding Request (1 paragraph - if applicable)**
- Amount seeking
- Use of funds
- Proposed terms

---

#### Example Executive Summary

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
GreenCycle Aquaponics - Portland, OR

THE OPPORTUNITY

Portland-area consumers increasingly demand fresh, local,
sustainably-grown produce year-round, yet local supply disappears
each winter. Meanwhile, restaurants struggle to source consistent,
high-quality greens from local farms. The Portland metro local food
market is valued at $450 million annually and growing 15% per year,
yet fewer than 5% of greens consumed are grown locally.

GreenCycle Aquaponics will fill this gap by operating a 3,000 sq ft
indoor aquaponics facility producing 25,000 lbs of pesticide-free
leafy greens and 3,000 lbs of tilapia annually, 365 days per year.

THE SOLUTION

Our commercial aquaponics system combines fish farming with
hydroponic vegetable production in a symbiotic, closed-loop system
that uses 90% less water than conventional farming and produces
two revenue streams (vegetables and fish) from one integrated
operation. Located within 5 miles of downtown Portland, we can
deliver harvest-to-customer in under 24 hours, providing freshness
conventional suppliers cannot match.

COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE

GreenCycle is the only year-round local producer of pesticide-free
leafy greens in the Portland metro area. Our competitive advantages
include: (1) Ultra-fresh product (24-hour harvest-to-delivery vs.
5-7 days conventional), (2) Pesticide-free growing without organic
certification costs, (3) Year-round consistency eliminating seasonal
gaps, (4) Dual revenue streams (greens + fish), and (5) Compelling
sustainability story (90% less water, zero pesticides, local).

BUSINESS MODEL

Revenue will be generated through four channels:
• Farm-to-table restaurants (45% of revenue): $10-11/lb greens,
  $8/lb fish, weekly standing orders
• CSA subscriptions (30%): $35-50/week boxes, 80 members
• Farmers markets (15%): $12-14/lb retail pricing
• Educational workshops (10%): $35/person, monthly events

Unit economics are strong with gross margins of 55% on greens
and 48% on fish.

MANAGEMENT TEAM

Founder Maria Chen brings 8 years of commercial aquaponics
experience, having managed a 5,000 sq ft facility in Seattle that
achieved profitability in Year 2. Co-founder James Park contributed
15 years in restaurant management and has secured commitments from
8 Portland restaurants. Our advisory board includes Dr. Susan Lee
(Oregon State Extension, aquaponics specialist) and Tom Wilson
(founder of three profitable urban farms).

FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS

Total startup investment of $380,000 will fund facility build-out,
equipment, and 12 months working capital. Pro forma financials
project:
• Year 1: $145,000 revenue, ($62,000) net loss
• Year 2: $265,000 revenue, $18,000 net income (7% margin)
• Year 3: $310,000 revenue, $52,000 net income (17% margin)
• Year 5: $385,000 revenue, $89,000 net income (23% margin)

Break-even is projected in Month 18.

FUNDING REQUEST

We seek $250,000 in debt financing (SBA 7(a) loan) and $130,000
in equity investment. Funds will be used for: facility and
infrastructure ($200,000), equipment ($105,000), working capital
($60,000), and contingency ($15,000). We have secured $130,000 in
equity commitments from founders and angel investors, and have
identified property for lease at $3,500/month.

With Portland's growing demand for local food, proven aquaponics
technology, an experienced team, and strong unit economics,
GreenCycle is positioned to become the leading year-round local
greens supplier in the Portland market.
```

### 12.8 Tailoring for Different Audiences

#### For Lenders (Banks, SBA)

**Emphasize:**
- Cash flow and debt service coverage
- Collateral available
- Personal financial strength
- Conservative projections
- Risk mitigation

**Include:**
- Personal financial statement
- Personal and business credit reports
- Detailed collateral list
- Debt service coverage ratio calculation

**Tone:** Conservative, risk-aware

---

#### For Investors (Angels, VC)

**Emphasize:**
- Market opportunity and growth potential
- Competitive advantages
- Scalability
- Team expertise
- Return on investment
- Exit strategy

**Include:**
- Pitch deck (separate document)
- Use of funds emphasizing growth
- Equity offering terms
- Cap table and dilution model
- Comparable exits/valuations

**Tone:** Growth-focused, opportunity-driven

---

#### For Grants

**Emphasize:**
- Mission alignment with funder
- Community impact
- Innovation
- Educational component
- Sustainability beyond grant
- Measurable outcomes

**Include:**
- Logic model
- Evaluation plan
- Letters of support
- Organizational capacity
- Budget justification

**Tone:** Impact-focused, mission-driven

### 12.9 Writing Tips

#### Do's

**DO:**
- Use clear, concise language
- Back claims with data
- Include visuals (charts, graphs, photos)
- Tell a compelling story
- Address risks honestly
- Proofread multiple times
- Get external review
- Update regularly

**Use Active Voice:**
- "We will achieve $250K revenue" vs. "Revenue will be achieved"

**Show, Don't Just Tell:**
- "Our team has started and scaled three profitable urban farms" vs. "Our team is experienced"

**Quantify Everything:**
- "Reduce customer acquisition cost by 40%" vs. "Lower acquisition costs"

---

#### Don'ts

**DON'T:**
- Use jargon or unexplained acronyms
- Make unsupported claims
- Ignore competition
- Present unrealistic projections
- Exceed reasonable length (70 pages max)
- Use complex formatting
- Include irrelevant information
- Forget page numbers and table of contents

### 12.10 Final Checklist

**Before Submission:**

**Completeness:**
- [ ] All sections included
- [ ] Consistent formatting
- [ ] Page numbers throughout
- [ ] Table of contents accurate
- [ ] All appendices attached
- [ ] Executive summary complete

**Quality:**
- [ ] Proofread for typos/grammar
- [ ] Numbers add up correctly
- [ ] Consistent voice/tone
- [ ] Professional appearance
- [ ] Charts/tables readable
- [ ] Sources cited

**Content:**
- [ ] Clear value proposition
- [ ] Market opportunity demonstrated
- [ ] Realistic financials
- [ ] Risks addressed
- [ ] Team capability shown
- [ ] Competitive advantage clear
- [ ] Implementation plan detailed

**Audience:**
- [ ] Tailored to specific reader
- [ ] Asks clearly stated
- [ ] Addresses likely questions
- [ ] Professional but accessible

**Review:**
- [ ] Reviewed by mentor/advisor
- [ ] Reviewed by accountant
- [ ] Reviewed by attorney (if needed)
- [ ] Feedback incorporated
