## Lesson Content

### 6.1 Pricing Fundamentals

#### Three Pricing Approaches

**1. Cost-Plus Pricing**
```
Total Cost per Unit
+ Desired Margin
= Selling Price

Example: Lettuce Head
Production Cost: $2.50
+ 60% Markup: $1.50
= Price: $4.00
```

**2. Competitive Pricing**
```
Analyze competitor prices
Position relative to market
Justify premium or discount

Market Range: $3.50-$5.50
Your Price: $4.75 (premium positioning)
```

**3. Value-Based Pricing**
```
Customer perceived value
Willingness to pay
Differentiation value

Customer Value: Pesticide-free, ultra-fresh, local
Justified Premium: 30-40% above conventional
Price: $6.00/head
```

### 6.5 Pricing Psychology

#### Strategies That Work

**1. Charm Pricing**
- $9.99 vs. $10.00
- Perceived as significantly less
- Best for price-sensitive segments

**2. Prestige Pricing**
- $12.00 vs. $11.99
- Round numbers convey quality
- Best for premium positioning

**3. Bundling**
- Mixed greens box: $35
- Individual items would cost: $42
- Creates perceived value

**4. Tiered Pricing**
- Small CSA Box: $25/week
- Medium CSA Box: $40/week (best value!)
- Large CSA Box: $60/week

**5. Anchor Pricing**
- Show original price crossed out
- Display savings percentage
- "Was $50, Now $40 (20% off!)"

### 6.6 Dynamic Pricing Strategies

#### Seasonal Adjustments

**Winter (High Demand, Limited Local Supply):**
- Premium pricing: +15-25%
- Customers value local in winter
- Less competition

**Summer (High Supply, More Competition):**
- Competitive pricing: baseline
- Focus on differentiation (freshness, consistency)
- Farmers markets saturated

**Example:**
| Season | Price/lb | Strategy |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Jan-Mar | $11.00 | Winter premium |
| Apr-Jun | $9.00 | Spring baseline |
| Jul-Sep | $8.50 | Summer competitive |
| Oct-Dec | $10.00 | Fall demand increase |

### 6.8 Pricing Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Pricing Too Low**
- Can't cover costs
- Devalues product
- Attracts wrong customers
- Hard to raise later

**2. Pricing Based Only on Costs**
- Ignores customer value perception
- Misses revenue opportunities
- Doesn't account for differentiation

**3. Not Differentiating by Channel**
- Same price everywhere
- Leaves money on table
- Doesn't account for different service levels

**4. Fear of Premium Pricing**
- Underestimating customer willingness to pay
- Not communicating value effectively
- Competing on price instead of value

**5. Inconsistent Pricing**
- Confuses customers
- Damages credibility
- Hard to manage

**6. Ignoring Competitor Changes**
- Market shifts around you
- Get priced out or underpriced
- Lose competitive position

### 6.9 Price Testing & Optimization

#### A/B Testing Approach

**Test 1: Farmers Market Pricing**
- Week 1-2: $10/lb
- Week 3-4: $12/lb
- Measure: Units sold, total revenue, customer feedback

**Results Example:**
| Price | Units Sold | Revenue | Feedback |
|-------|------------|---------|----------|
| $10/lb | 75 lbs | $750 | "Good value" |
| $12/lb | 55 lbs | $660 | "A bit high" |

**Decision:** $10/lb optimizes revenue

---

**Test 2: CSA Pricing**
- Option A: $35/week
- Option B: $40/week (10% larger box)
- Measure: Sign-up rate, retention

**Results Example:**
| Option | Sign-ups | Annual Value | Retention |
|--------|----------|--------------|-----------|
| $35/week | 65 members | $118,300 | 78% |
| $40/week | 45 members | $93,600 | 85% |

**Decision:** $35 option generates more total revenue
