## Questions

### Question 1
**What are the three main categories of startup costs?**

A) Equipment, labor, and marketing

B) Capital expenditures, working capital, and pre-opening expenses ✓

C) Fixed costs, variable costs, and overhead

D) Infrastructure, inventory, and insurance

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Startup costs are divided into: Capital Expenditures (one-time purchases), Working Capital (operating expenses until cash-positive), and Pre-Opening Expenses (professional fees, permits, initial inventory).*

---

### Question 2
**What percentage buffer should you add to your initial startup cost estimate?**

A) 5-10%

B) 10-15%

C) 15-25% ✓

D) 30-40%

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: A 15-25% contingency buffer accounts for unexpected costs, price increases, and underestimated expenses during startup.*

---

### Question 3
**Working capital should cover operating expenses for how many months until the business reaches break-even?**

A) 1-3 months

B) 3-6 months

C) 6-12 months

D) 12-24 months ✓

**Correct Answer: D**

*Explanation: Most farms take 12-24 months to reach break-even, so working capital must cover operating expenses during this entire ramp-up period.*

---

### Question 4
**Which of these is considered a Capital Expenditure (CapEx)?**

A) Monthly rent payments

B) Employee salaries

C) Greenhouse construction ✓

D) Marketing expenses

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Capital Expenditures are one-time investments in long-term assets like facilities, equipment, and infrastructure. Operating expenses like rent and salaries are not CapEx.*

---

### Question 5
**What is the typical useful life for depreciating greenhouse equipment?**

A) 3-5 years

B) 5-7 years ✓

C) 10-15 years

D) 20-30 years

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Greenhouse equipment typically has a 5-7 year useful life for depreciation purposes, though the actual structure may last longer.*

---

### Question 6
**What is bootstrapping?**

A) Self-funding the business with minimal external capital ✓

B) Securing large venture capital investments

C) Taking out maximum bank loans

D) Running the business without any capital

**Correct Answer: A**

*Explanation: Bootstrapping means starting and growing the business using personal savings, revenue, and minimal external funding.*

---

### Question 7
**A typical small-scale aquaponics startup (2,000 sq ft) requires approximately how much total startup capital?**

A) $50,000-$100,000

B) $150,000-$300,000 ✓

C) $400,000-$600,000

D) $700,000-$1,000,000

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Small-scale operations typically need $150,000-$300,000 including equipment, facility improvements, working capital, and pre-opening expenses.*

---

### Question 8
**What percentage of total startup costs should typically be allocated to working capital?**

A) 10-15%

B) 20-25%

C) 30-40% ✓

D) 50-60%

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Working capital typically represents 30-40% of total startup costs to ensure adequate cash runway during the ramp-up phase.*

---

### Question 9
**Which startup cost is often underestimated by new farm entrepreneurs?**

A) Equipment costs

B) Facility costs

C) Professional fees and permits ✓

D) Marketing costs

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Professional fees (legal, accounting, consultants), permits, licenses, and regulatory compliance costs are frequently underestimated by new entrepreneurs.*

---

### Question 10
**What is the "burn rate" in financial terms?**

A) The rate at which you generate revenue

B) The rate at which you consume cash ✓

C) The depreciation rate of equipment

D) The interest rate on loans

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Burn rate is the rate at which a business consumes its cash reserves, typically expressed as monthly cash outflow minus inflow.*
