# Business Planning Glossary
## Course 209: Business Planning for Sustainable Farms

---

## A

**Accounts Payable (AP)**
Money owed to suppliers and vendors for goods/services received. Current liability on balance sheet.

**Accounts Receivable (AR)**
Money owed to you by customers for goods/services delivered. Current asset on balance sheet.

**Accrual Accounting**
Accounting method that records revenues when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of cash movement.

**Angel Investor**
Individual who invests personal funds in early-stage businesses, typically $25K-$500K, in exchange for equity.

**Aquaponics**
Integrated farming system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation).

---

## B

**Balance Sheet**
Financial statement showing assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time. Snapshot of financial position.

**Break-Even Point**
Sales level at which total revenue equals total costs (fixed + variable), resulting in zero profit or loss.

**Business Model Canvas**
One-page strategic tool visualizing business model through 9 building blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.

**Business Plan**
Comprehensive written document describing business concept, market opportunity, strategy, operations, management, and financial projections.

---

## C

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**
Total sales and marketing costs divided by number of new customers acquired. Key metric for evaluating marketing efficiency.

**Capital Expenditure (CapEx)**
Funds used to acquire, upgrade, or maintain physical assets like buildings, equipment, or facilities.

**Cash Burn Rate**
Rate at which business uses cash, typically expressed as monthly cash outflow exceeding inflow.

**Cash Flow Statement**
Financial statement tracking cash inflows and outflows from operating, investing, and financing activities over a period.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Food production in structures like greenhouses or vertical farms where environmental conditions are controlled.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)**
Direct costs of producing goods sold, including materials, direct labor, and direct overhead.

**Competitive Advantage**
Unique attributes or capabilities that allow business to outperform competitors.

**CSA (Community Supported Agriculture)**
Business model where customers buy shares/subscriptions upfront, receiving regular deliveries throughout season.

**Current Ratio**
Current assets divided by current liabilities. Measures ability to pay short-term obligations. Target >1.5.

---

## D

**Debt Financing**
Raising capital by borrowing money that must be repaid with interest.

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)**
Net operating income divided by total debt payments. Measures ability to service debt. Lenders typically require >1.25.

**Depreciation**
Allocation of asset cost over its useful life. Non-cash expense reducing taxable income.

**Direct-to-Consumer (DTC)**
Business model selling directly to end consumers without intermediaries (farmers markets, online, farm stand).

**Due Diligence**
Comprehensive investigation of business by potential investors or lenders before committing capital.

---

## E

**EBITDA**
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. Measure of operating profitability.

**Equity Financing**
Raising capital by selling ownership shares in business.

**Exit Strategy**
Plan for how investors will realize return on investment (acquisition, IPO, buyback).

---

## F

**Feasibility Study**
Analysis determining whether business idea is viable before significant investment.

**Fixed Costs**
Expenses that don't vary with production level (rent, insurance, base salaries).

**Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA)**
Federal law requiring produce farms >$25K sales to implement food safety practices.

**FSMA**
See Food Safety Modernization Act.

**FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)**
Standardized measure of employees. 2 half-time workers = 1 FTE.

---

## G

**GAP (Good Agricultural Practices) Certification**
USDA voluntary food safety audit program often required by wholesale buyers.

**Gross Margin**
(Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100. Percentage of revenue remaining after direct costs.

**Grant**
Non-repayable funds provided by government, foundation, or organization for specific purpose.

---

## H

**Hydroponics**
Method of growing plants without soil, using nutrient-rich water solutions.

---

## I

**Income Statement (P&L)**
Financial statement showing revenues, expenses, and net income over a period.

**Investor Pitch**
Formal presentation to potential investors explaining business opportunity and requesting funding.

---

## K

**Key Performance Indicator (KPI)**
Measurable value demonstrating how effectively business achieves key objectives.

**Key Resources**
Most important assets required to make business model work (physical, intellectual, human, financial).

---

## L

**Lean Startup**
Methodology emphasizing starting small, testing assumptions, gathering customer feedback, and iterating quickly.

**Letter of Intent (LOI)**
Non-binding document expressing serious interest in purchasing from business, helpful for validating demand.

**Liability**
Financial obligation or debt owed to another party.

**Limited Liability Company (LLC)**
Legal business structure providing liability protection with pass-through taxation and flexible management.

**Line of Credit**
Revolving credit facility allowing business to borrow up to certain limit, paying interest only on amount used.

**Liquidity**
Ability to convert assets to cash quickly or meet short-term financial obligations.

**LTV (Lifetime Value)**
Total revenue expected from customer over entire relationship. Key metric: LTV/CAC >3:1.

---

## M

**Market Segmentation**
Dividing broad market into distinct customer groups with different needs or characteristics.

**Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**
Simplest version of product/service that can be launched to test market demand.

**Mission Statement**
Brief statement of organization's purpose and primary objectives.

---

## N

**Net Income**
Total revenue minus all expenses, taxes, and costs. "Bottom line" profit or loss.

**Net Profit Margin**
Net income divided by revenue × 100. Percentage of revenue remaining as profit.

---

## O

**Operating Expenses (OpEx)**
Ongoing costs of running business (salaries, utilities, marketing, rent).

**Organic Certification**
USDA certification verifying farm meets organic production standards.

---

## P

**Partnership**
Legal business structure where two or more people share ownership, profits, and liabilities.

**Persona (Customer Persona)**
Detailed fictional profile representing target customer segment based on research.

**Pitch Deck**
Visual presentation (typically 10-20 slides) used to pitch business to investors or lenders.

**Pre-Sales**
Selling products/services before officially launching to validate demand and generate working capital.

**Pro Forma**
Financial statements based on projections rather than actual historical data.

**Product Liability Insurance**
Coverage protecting against claims of injury or illness caused by products sold.

---

## Q

**Quick Ratio (Acid Test)**
(Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities. Stricter liquidity measure than current ratio.

---

## R

**Revenue**
Total income generated from sales of products or services before expenses.

**Revenue Stream**
Source from which business generates income (product sales, subscriptions, licensing, etc.).

**ROI (Return on Investment)**
(Net Profit / Total Investment) × 100. Percentage return earned on invested capital.

**Runway**
Length of time business can operate before running out of cash, given current burn rate.

---

## S

**S-Corporation**
Corporate structure providing liability protection with pass-through taxation, allowing tax savings on self-employment.

**SAM (Serviceable Available Market)**
Portion of total market you can realistically reach given your geography, channels, and product.

**SARE (Sustainable Agriculture Research & Education)**
USDA grant program funding farmer-led research and education projects ($5K-$30K).

**SBA (Small Business Administration)**
Federal agency providing loan guarantees, counseling, and support to small businesses.

**Scalability**
Ability to grow business without proportional increase in costs.

**Scenario Analysis**
Modeling best-case, base-case, and worst-case projections to assess range of possible outcomes.

**Sensitivity Analysis**
Testing how changes in key variables impact financial outcomes.

**Sole Proprietorship**
Simplest business structure where individual and business are legally the same entity. No liability protection.

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**
Realistic portion of SAM you can capture in near term given resources and competition.

**Startup Costs**
One-time expenses required to launch business (equipment, buildout, initial inventory, professional fees).

---

## T

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**
Total revenue opportunity if you captured 100% of market.

**Target Market**
Specific group of customers business aims to serve.

**Traction**
Evidence of market demand and business progress (sales, customers, growth, partnerships).

---

## U

**USDA (United States Department of Agriculture)**
Federal department overseeing agriculture, food safety, rural development, and related programs.

**Utilization Rate**
Actual production divided by maximum capacity × 100. Measures how fully you're using resources.

---

## V

**Value Proposition**
Clear statement of unique value delivered to customers, solving their problem or meeting their need.

**Value-Added Producer Grant (VAPG)**
USDA grant program up to $250K for value-added agricultural product marketing and processing.

**Variable Costs**
Expenses that change in proportion to production volume (materials, packaging, some labor).

**Venture Capital (VC)**
Investment firms providing large capital ($500K+) to high-growth startups in exchange for equity.

**Vision Statement**
Aspirational description of what organization aims to achieve in future.

---

## W

**Wholesale**
Selling in bulk to retailers or distributors at lower prices for resale.

**Working Capital**
Current assets minus current liabilities. Cash available for day-to-day operations.

**Workers' Compensation Insurance**
Required insurance covering medical costs and lost wages for employees injured on job.

---

## Y

**Yield**
Amount of product harvested or produced, often expressed per unit area or time.

---

## Z

**Zoning**
Local regulations governing how land can be used. Important for farm location selection.

---

## FINANCIAL RATIOS QUICK REFERENCE

| Ratio | Formula | Target |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Gross Margin | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | 40-60% |
| Operating Margin | EBITDA / Revenue | 15-25% |
| Net Margin | Net Income / Revenue | 5-15% |
| Current Ratio | Current Assets / Current Liabilities | >1.5 |
| DSCR | Operating Income / Debt Payments | >1.25 |
| LTV/CAC | Lifetime Value / Acquisition Cost | >3:1 |
