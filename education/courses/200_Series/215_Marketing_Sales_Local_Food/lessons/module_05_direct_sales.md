## Introduction

Direct-to-consumer (D2C) sales offer the highest profit margins and strongest customer relationships for local food businesses. By eliminating middlemen, you capture full retail value while building direct connections with the people who eat your food.

This module covers the major D2C sales models and how to implement them successfully.

## CSA (Community Supported Agriculture) Programs

### CSA Fundamentals

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                          CSA MODEL OVERVIEW                                ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                            ║
║  CONCEPT:                                                                  ║
║  Customers purchase "shares" of your harvest upfront, receiving weekly    ║
║  or bi-weekly boxes of seasonal produce throughout the growing season.    ║
║                                                                            ║
║  BENEFITS FOR FARMER:                                                      ║
║  • Guaranteed sales and cash flow                                         ║
║  • Upfront capital for spring expenses                                    ║
║  • Shared risk (customers understand crop failures)                       ║
║  • Loyal, committed customer base                                         ║
║  • Reduced marketing costs per sale                                       ║
║                                                                            ║
║  BENEFITS FOR CUSTOMER:                                                    ║
║  • Lower cost per box than farmers market                                 ║
║  • Guaranteed weekly fresh produce                                        ║
║  • Connection to farm and farmer                                          ║
║  • Support local agriculture                                              ║
║  • Exposure to new vegetables and varieties                               ║
║                                                                            ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### CSA Pricing and Structure

**Standard Share Sizing:**

| Share Size | Serves | Weekly Value | Season Price (20 weeks) | Weekly Cost |
|------------|--------|--------------|-------------------------|-------------|
| **Half Share** | 1-2 people | $20-25 | $400-500 | $20-25 |
| **Full Share** | 2-4 people | $30-40 | $600-800 | $30-40 |
| **Family Share** | 4-6 people | $45-60 | $900-1,200 | $45-60 |

**Pricing Calculation Example:**

```
Target: 50 full shares at $700 each
Season length: 20 weeks
Revenue: 50 × $700 = $35,000

Value per box: $700 / 20 weeks = $35/week
Equivalent retail value: $45-50/week (20-30% discount)

Production cost target: $15-20/box
Margin: $15-20/box × 20 weeks × 50 shares = $15,000-$20,000
```

### Subscription Box Models

**Benefits:**
- **Predictable revenue** - Know sales in advance
- **Customer retention** - Automatic recurring orders
- **Higher lifetime value** - Customers stay longer
- **Reduced marketing costs** - Acquire once, sell repeatedly

### Key Takeaways

4. **Subscription models create predictable revenue** - Recurring customers are most valuable
