## The Restaurant Market Opportunity

### Why Restaurants Buy Local

**Chef Motivations:**
- Menu differentiation and story
- Superior quality and freshness
- Seasonal menu flexibility
- Marketing advantage ("farm-to-table")
- Customer demand for local sourcing

**Market Size:**
- 20% of local food sales go to restaurants
- Average restaurant account: $200-800/week
- Year-round potential (vs. seasonal farmers markets)
- Premium wholesale pricing (40-100% above commodity)

## Identifying Target Restaurants

### Restaurant Types and Fit

| Restaurant Type | Local Food Fit | Price Point | Volume | Relationship |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| **Fine Dining** | Excellent | Premium | Low-Medium | High-touch |
| **Farm-to-Table** | Excellent | Premium | Medium | Partnership |
| **Casual Upscale** | Good | Moderate | Medium-High | Professional |
| **Fast Casual** | Moderate | Competitive | High | Efficiency-focused |
| **Chains** | Poor | Low | High | Difficult |

### Qualification Criteria

**Ideal Restaurant Partners:**
- Commitment to local sourcing (stated values)
- Menu changes seasonally
- Chef-owner or empowered chef
- Price point supports premium ingredients ($20+ entrees)
- Volume matches your capacity
- Reasonable payment terms (Net 15-30)

**Research Questions:**
- Does the menu list farm names?
- Do they promote local sourcing?
- What's the chef's reputation and tenure?
- How often does the menu change?
- What are online reviews saying about food quality?

## Chef Outreach Strategy

### Initial Contact Methods

**1. In-Person Visit (Most Effective)**
```
Best Time: Tuesday-Thursday, 2-4pm (between lunch and dinner service)
Bring: Product samples, farm info sheet, price list
Approach: "Hi, I'm [name] from [farm]. We grow [products] and
          specialize in [unique selling point]. I'd love to share
          some samples and see if there's a fit for your menu."
```

**2. Email Introduction**
```
Subject: Local [Product] for [Restaurant Name]

Hi Chef [Name],

I'm a local farmer growing [products] just [X miles] from your
restaurant. We specialize in [unique varieties/methods] and supply
several area restaurants including [name-drop if possible].

I'd love to drop off some samples for you to try and discuss how
we might support your menu. Are you available for a brief visit
this Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon?

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Farm Name]
[Contact Info]
```

**3. Farmers Market Introduction**
- Invite chefs to visit your booth
- Offer chef-only early shopping
- Provide business card for follow-up

### The Sales Meeting

**Preparation:**
- Know their menu and concept
- Bring your best samples (peak quality)
- Have price list ready
- Research what they currently buy

**Meeting Structure:**
1. **Introduction** (2 min) - Your farm story, brief
2. **Discovery** (5 min) - Ask about their needs, current suppliers, challenges
3. **Presentation** (5 min) - Showcase products, emphasize unique value
4. **Sampling** (5 min) - Let the product sell itself
5. **Next Steps** (3 min) - Trial order, delivery logistics, follow-up

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- "What local products are you currently using?"
- "What challenges do you have with current suppliers?"
- "How do you typically like to receive orders?"
- "What's your lead time for planning seasonal menus?"

## Pricing for Restaurants

### Restaurant Pricing Strategy

**Price Positioning:**
```
Commodity Wholesale:          $1.50/lb (distributor price)
Your Premium Wholesale:       $2.50-3.50/lb (67-133% premium)
Farmers Market Retail:        $4.50-5.00/lb
Restaurant Menu Price:        $14-18 (salad with your greens)

Chef's Food Cost Target: 28-32%
Your greens in salad: $2.50 (within target)
```

**Pricing Framework:**
- **Floor:** Cost + 30% minimum
- **Target:** 50-100% above commodity wholesale
- **Ceiling:** 60-70% of retail (farmers market) price

### Volume Discounts and Minimums

```
Pricing Tiers:
Small orders (5-20 lbs/week):    $3.50/lb
Medium orders (20-50 lbs/week):  $3.00/lb
Large orders (50+ lbs/week):     $2.75/lb

Or:
Minimum order: $50
Free delivery: $100+
```

## Logistics and Operations

### Ordering Systems

**Options:**
1. **Standing Orders** - Same items/quantities weekly (easiest)
2. **Email/Text Orders** - Chef texts needs by cutoff (flexible)
3. **Online Platform** - Local Line, Barn2Door (professional)

**Best Practice:**
- Order cutoff: 48 hours before delivery
- Confirm order 24 hours before delivery
- Text when departing for delivery

### Delivery

**Scheduling:**
- Deliver early morning (6-10am) before service
- Tuesday-Friday typically best (avoid Monday deliveries)
- Consistent schedule (same day/time weekly)

**Packaging:**
- Restaurant-ready (washed, trimmed as needed)
- Sturdy boxes/crates (reusable)
- Clear labeling (contents, weight, farm name)
- Invoices included

### Payment Terms

**Standard Terms:**
- Net 15 or Net 30 (15-30 days to pay)
- Invoicing via email
- Payment by check or ACH

**Managing Cash Flow:**
- Require payment before next delivery if overdue
- Offer 2% discount for Net 7 (early payment)
- Deposit for new accounts (first month prepaid)

## Product Selection for Restaurants

### What Chefs Want

**Top Restaurant Products:**

| Product | Why Chefs Love It | Price Premium |
|---------|------------------|---------------|
| **Salad Greens** | Daily use, quality visible | 100-150% |
| **Herbs** | High-value, differentiation | 150-200% |
| **Heirloom Tomatoes** | Menu highlight, summer star | 100-200% |
| **Specialty Items** | Unique menu offerings | 200-300% |
| **Microgreens** | Garnish, high-value | 200-400% |

**Chef Preferences:**
- Consistent quality and sizing
- Unusual/heirloom varieties (menu story)
- Peak ripeness (vs. shipping-ready firmness)
- Seasonal specialties
- Reliability over variety

### Menu Collaboration

**Seasonal Menu Planning:**
- Share harvest forecast 4-6 weeks ahead
- Collaborate on specialty plantings
- Offer first access to new varieties
- Invite chef to farm for menu inspiration

**Example:**
"Chef, we're planting in two weeks for June harvests. If you'd like me to grow specific heirloom tomatoes or unique herbs for your summer menu, let me know by Friday."

## Maintaining Restaurant Relationships

### Communication Best Practices

**Weekly:**
- Send harvest availability list
- Confirm standing orders
- Provide delivery updates

**Monthly:**
- Check in on quality/satisfaction
- Share upcoming seasonal changes
- Invite feedback

**Quarterly:**
- In-person visit to maintain relationship
- Discuss menu planning for next season
- Address any issues proactively

### Problem Prevention

**Common Issues and Solutions:**

| Problem | Prevention | Solution |
|---------|------------|----------|
| **Quality Complaints** | Consistent quality control, proper handling | Replace immediately, investigate root cause |
| **Late Deliveries** | Buffer time, communicate delays | Text updates, discount for inconvenience |
| **Short Orders** | Harvest buffer, communicate shortages early | Offer substitutions, pro-rate invoice |
| **Payment Delays** | Clear terms, regular invoicing | Friendly reminder, hold delivery if needed |

### Growing the Account

**Upsell Strategies:**
- "We have beautiful heirloom tomatoes this week"
- "I could add herbs to your standing order"
- "Spring peas just came in—want to try them?"

**Expanding Volume:**
- Prove reliability with small orders first
- Gradually increase as trust builds
- Offer volume discounts as incentive
- Suggest additional menu applications

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Target the right restaurants** - Fine dining and farm-to-table concepts are best fits
