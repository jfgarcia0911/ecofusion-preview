## Why Retention Matters

### The Economics of Retention

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                    CUSTOMER ACQUISITION VS. RETENTION                      ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                            ║
║  ACQUISITION COSTS:                                                        ║
║  • Farmers market booth fee: $35                                          ║
║  • Labor: $90                                                              ║
║  • First-time buyer conversion: 30%                                       ║
║  • Cost per new customer: $417                                            ║
║                                                                            ║
║  RETENTION VALUE:                                                          ║
║  • One-time customer: $35 average purchase                                ║
║  • Monthly customer: $140/month × 6 months = $840                         ║
║  • Loyal customer (2 years): $140 × 24 = $3,360                           ║
║                                                                            ║
║  KEY INSIGHT:                                                              ║
║  Increasing retention from 30% to 40% doubles lifetime value             ║
║  5% increase in retention = 25-95% increase in profits                    ║
║                                                                            ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

**Calculation:**
```
CLV = (Average Purchase) × (Purchases per Year) × (Customer Lifespan)

Example:
Average purchase: $35
Frequency: 2x per month = 24x per year
Lifespan: 3 years

CLV = $35 × 24 × 3 = $2,520
```

**Strategic Implication:** You can spend up to $2,520 to acquire a customer and break even (but aim for 20% CAC/CLV ratio = $504 max acquisition cost)

## Measuring Retention

### Key Retention Metrics

**1. Repeat Purchase Rate**
```
Formula: (Customers who bought 2+ times) / (Total customers) × 100

Example: 150 repeat customers / 500 total customers = 30%

Benchmark: 20-40% for farmers markets, 60-80% for CSA
```

**2. Customer Churn Rate**
```
Formula: (Customers lost) / (Total customers at period start) × 100

Example: Lost 25 customers / Started with 200 = 12.5% monthly churn

Goal: <5% monthly churn for subscription models
```

**3. Purchase Frequency**
```
How often do customers buy?

Segments:
- Weekly: High-value, target for VIP
- Bi-weekly: Regular, maintain with communication
- Monthly: Occasional, nurture with offers
- Quarterly: At-risk, re-engage campaigns
```

## Loyalty Program Design

### Loyalty Program Types

**1. Points/Punch Card**
```
"Earn 1 point per $10 spent. 100 points = $10 discount."

Or simpler:
"Buy 9 bunches of greens, get 10th free"

Pros:
- Easy to understand
- Tangible progress
- Encourages repeat visits

Cons:
- Can erode margins if too generous
- Administrative tracking
```

**2. Tiered VIP Program**
```
Silver (0-$500/year): 5% discount
Gold ($500-$1,000/year): 10% discount + early shopping
Platinum ($1,000+/year): 15% discount + free delivery + farm events

Pros:
- Motivates increased spending
- Creates status and exclusivity
- Rewards best customers

Cons:
- Complex to manage
- May alienate occasional customers
```

**3. Referral Program**
```
"Refer a friend who joins CSA, both get 1 free week"

Or:
"Share your unique code. Friend gets 20% off first order,
you get $10 credit when they buy"

Pros:
- Drives new customer acquisition
- Rewards existing customers
- Word-of-mouth marketing

Cons:
- Requires tracking system
- Cost of incentives
```

**4. Subscription/CSA as Loyalty**
```
CSA membership itself is a loyalty program:
- Upfront commitment
- Weekly engagement
- Shared risk and reward

Enhance with:
- Member-only events
- Exclusive products
- Priority access to seasonal items
```

## Exceptional Customer Service

### Service Principles for Farms

**1. Accessibility**
- Respond to emails within 24 hours
- Answer questions at market warmly
- Provide multiple contact methods
- Make it easy to give feedback

**2. Education**
- Teach customers how to store produce
- Share recipe ideas
- Explain varieties and uses
- Help customers succeed with your products

**3. Flexibility**
- CSA vacation holds
- Substitutions when possible
- Accommodate special requests
- Delivery adjustments

**4. Problem Resolution**
- Replace unsatisfactory products immediately
- No-questions-asked quality guarantee
- Over-deliver on solutions (free extra item)
- Follow up to ensure satisfaction

### Service Recovery Framework

```
When things go wrong:

1. ACKNOWLEDGE quickly
   "I'm sorry the lettuce wilted. That's not our standard."

2. EMPATHIZE
   "I understand how frustrating that is."

3. APOLOGIZE genuinely
   "This was our mistake, and I apologize."

4. ACT immediately
   "Let me replace it right now, plus here's extra for the
   inconvenience."

5. FOLLOW UP
   "How was the replacement? We've updated our cooling
   process to prevent this."

Result: Customer feels heard, problem solved, loyalty strengthened
```

## Engagement and Communication

### Stay-in-Touch Strategy

**Weekly:**
- Email harvest forecast
- Social media posts (4-7x)
- Instagram Stories daily

**Monthly:**
- Newsletter with farm updates, recipes, upcoming events
- Special offer or promotion
- Feature customer of the month

**Quarterly:**
- Survey for feedback
- Seasonal preview (what's coming)
- Farm event or tour invitation

**Annually:**
- Thank you note to top customers
- Year-end review and next year preview
- Early access to CSA sign-up

### Personalization Tactics

**Low-Tech:**
- Remember names and faces
- Note preferences ("You love the heirloom tomatoes!")
- Ask about previous purchases ("How'd the basil work out?")
- Handwritten thank-you notes to VIP customers

**High-Tech:**
- CRM system tracking purchase history
- Segmented email lists (CSA members, market-only, online)
- Personalized recommendations based on past purchases
- Birthday emails with special offer

### Creating Belonging

**Farm Events:**

**Customer Connection:**

- Environmental impact reporting

## Gathering and Acting on Feedback

### Feedback Collection Methods

**Informal:**
- Conversations at farmers market
- Social media comments and DMs
- Email replies

**Formal:**
- Annual customer survey
- CSA mid-season check-in
- End-of-season evaluation
- Product-specific feedback requests

### Sample Customer Survey

```
1. How satisfied are you with [Farm Name]? (1-10 scale)

2. How likely are you to recommend us to a friend? (1-10 scale)
   [Net Promoter Score]

3. What do you love most about our farm?

4. What could we improve?

5. What products would you like to see us grow?

6. How do you prefer to buy from us?
   □ Farmers market  □ CSA  □ Online  □ Delivery

7. How often do you purchase? __________

8. Any other feedback?
```

### Acting on Feedback

**Prioritize Based On:**
1. Frequency of mention
2. Impact on customer experience
3. Feasibility and cost
4. Alignment with farm values

**Communicate Changes:**
- "You asked, we listened: Now offering half-shares!"
- "Based on your feedback, we're extending market hours"
- Show customers their voice matters

## Win-Back Campaigns

### Re-Engaging Lost Customers

**Identify:**
- Haven't purchased in 60+ days (market customers)
- Didn't renew CSA
- Unsubscribed from email

**Reach Out:**
```
Email Subject: "We miss you at [Farm Name]!"

Hi [Name],

We noticed you haven't been by in a while and wanted to check in.
Is there anything we could do better?

We'd love to see you back at the market. Here's 20% off your
next purchase as a welcome back gift.

[Promo Code]

Hope to see you soon!
[Your Name]
```

**Special Offers:**
- Discount for returning customers
- Free item with purchase
- Exclusive invitation to event
- Early access to CSA sign-up
