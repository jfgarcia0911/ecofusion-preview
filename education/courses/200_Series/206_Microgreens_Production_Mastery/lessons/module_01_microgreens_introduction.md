## Introduction

Microgreens represent one of the most accessible and profitable segments of controlled environment agriculture. With minimal startup costs, short production cycles (7-21 days), and strong market demand, microgreens have become the entry point for countless successful urban farming ventures. This module provides a comprehensive overview of the microgreens industry and helps you understand the opportunity.

### The Greens Spectrum

Understanding where microgreens fit in the spectrum of young greens:

| Type | Age | Size | Harvest Method | Price Point |
|------|-----|------|----------------|-------------|
| **Sprouts** | 2-7 days | Seeds + roots | Whole plant | $8-15/lb |
| **Microgreens** | 7-21 days | 1-3 inches | Cut above media | $25-50/lb |
| **Baby Greens** | 21-35 days | 3-6 inches | Cut or whole | $8-15/lb |
| **Mature Greens** | 40-70 days | Full size | Whole head | $3-8/lb |

### Market Segments

**1. Fine Dining / Restaurant Trade**
- Highest volume segment
- Consistent weekly orders
- Price sensitivity: Low
- Typical pricing: $30-40/lb
- Order size: 1-10 lbs/week per account

**2. Retail / Grocery Stores**
- Growing segment
- Requires packaging and shelf life
- Price sensitivity: Moderate
- Typical pricing: $25-35/lb
- Order size: 5-50 lbs/week per account

**3. Farmers Markets / Direct Sales**
- Highest margins
- Best for startup operations
- Price sensitivity: Low (educated consumers)
- Typical pricing: $40-60/lb
- Volume: Variable, seasonal

**4. Home Delivery / CSA**
- Emerging segment
- Subscription-based revenue
- Price sensitivity: Low
- Typical pricing: $35-45/lb
- Consistent weekly volume

### Why Microgreens Are Beginner-Friendly

**Low Barriers to Entry:**
- Minimal equipment requirements
- Can start in spare room, basement, garage
- No specialized HVAC or climate control
- Standard shelving and lighting works well
- Small scale testing before investing

**Fast Feedback Loop:**
- 7-21 day crop cycles
- Rapid learning and iteration
- Quick market validation
- Immediate cash flow potential
- Mistakes have low cost

**Scalable Model:**
- Start small (1-2 racks)
- Add capacity incrementally
- Low per-unit expansion costs
- Proven systems replicate easily
- Can remain small-scale and profitable

## Production Overview

### The Microgreens Production Cycle

```
TYPICAL 10-14 DAY PRODUCTION CYCLE

Day 0-1: SEEDING
├─ Pre-soak seeds (if needed)
├─ Prepare growing media
├─ Seed trays at proper density
└─ Water and cover

Day 1-4: GERMINATION (Blackout)
├─ Stack trays with weight
├─ Daily moisture check
├─ Temperature: 68-75°F
└─ Complete darkness

Day 4-5: TRANSITION
├─ Remove from blackout
├─ Expose to light gradually
├─ Begin air circulation
└─ Monitor moisture

Day 5-10: GROWING
├─ 12-16 hours light daily
├─ Bottom watering as needed
├─ Monitor growth rate
└─ Watch for pests/disease

Day 10-14: HARVEST
├─ Harvest at peak quality
├─ Wash (if needed)
├─ Package immediately
└─ Refrigerate
```

## Success Factors

### What Makes Microgreens Growers Successful

**1. Consistency Over Variety**
- Master 3-5 core varieties before expanding
- Produce reliable quality every harvest
- Maintain steady production schedule
- Build systems and SOPs early

**2. Strong Customer Relationships**
- Weekly deliveries build loyalty
- Responsive to feedback and requests
- Samples and new variety trials
- Reliability is paramount

**3. Quality Standards**
- Fresh, clean product every time
- Proper harvest timing
- Excellent post-harvest handling
- Professional packaging

**4. Efficient Systems**
- Staggered seeding schedules
- Organized growing area
- Streamlined harvest/packaging
- Good record keeping

**5. Market Positioning**
- Clear value proposition
- Target right customers
- Premium positioning justified
- Story and branding matter

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         COMMON FAILURE PATTERNS                          │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ GROWING TOO MANY VARIETIES                                            │
│    → Focus on 3-5 that sell well instead of 20+ varieties                │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ POOR HARVEST TIMING                                                   │
│    → Harvesting too early (low yield) or too late (quality loss)         │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ INADEQUATE SANITIZATION                                               │
│    → Mold and contamination issues destroy product and reputation        │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ OVERPRODUCTION                                                        │
│    → Growing without confirmed buyers leads to waste                     │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ PRICING TOO LOW                                                       │
│    → Undervaluing product makes operation unsustainable                  │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ NEGLECTING CASH FLOW                                                  │
│    → Payment terms and timing can make or break small operations         │
│                                                                          │
│  ✗ INCONSISTENT PRODUCTION                                               │
│    → Missing deliveries loses customers permanently                      │
│                                                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
