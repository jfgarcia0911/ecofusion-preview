## The Starter Portfolio

### Recommended First Five Varieties

For new growers, focus on these proven performers:

**1. Sunflower (Black Oil, Hulled)**
```
✓ Pros:
  • Highest yield (10-12 oz per 10×20 tray)
  • Universal appeal (nutty, crunchy)
  • Forgiving for beginners
  • Strong consistent demand
  • 10-12 day cycle

✗ Cons:
  • Requires hulled seeds (more expensive)
  • Needs pre-soak (8-12 hours)
  • Prone to mold if over-watered
  • Medium price point

Seed Cost: $6-8/lb | Yield: 3-4 lbs per lb seed | ROI: ★★★★
```

**2. Pea Shoots (Speckled Pea)**
```
✓ Pros:
  • High yield (10-12 oz per tray)
  • Sweet flavor, broad appeal
  • Sturdy product, good shelf life
  • Reliable germination
  • 12-16 day cycle

✗ Cons:
  • Requires pre-soak (8-12 hours)
  • Seeds can be expensive
  • Needs good air circulation
  • Can develop powdery mildew

Seed Cost: $3-5/lb | Yield: 2-3 lbs per lb seed | ROI: ★★★★★
```

**3. Radish (Red, Purple, or Mix)**
```
✓ Pros:
  • Fast cycle (7-10 days)
  • Spicy flavor, colorful
  • High market demand
  • No pre-soak needed
  • Easy for beginners

✗ Cons:
  • Lower yield (6-8 oz per tray)
  • Delicate stems, careful harvest
  • Short shelf life
  • Can be stringy if overgrown

Seed Cost: $8-12/lb | Yield: 8-12 lbs per lb seed | ROI: ★★★★
```

**4. Broccoli (Waltham, Calabrese)**
```
✓ Pros:
  • Health halo, nutritious
  • Strong restaurant demand
  • Mild flavor, versatile
  • Consistent performance
  • 12-14 day cycle

✗ Cons:
  • Tiny seeds, tricky seeding
  • Lower yield (4-6 oz per tray)
  • Needs gentle watering
  • Premium seed cost

Seed Cost: $20-30/lb | Yield: 15-20 lbs per lb seed | ROI: ★★★★
```

**5. Arugula/Rocket**
```
✓ Pros:
  • Peppery flavor, familiar
  • Fast cycle (8-10 days)
  • Good for mixed blends
  • Easy to grow
  • No pre-soak

✗ Cons:
  • Moderate yield (4-5 oz per tray)
  • Can bolt quickly
  • Needs consistent moisture
  • Delicate handling

Seed Cost: $12-18/lb | Yield: 10-15 lbs per lb seed | ROI: ★★★
```

### Seed Specifications to Request

When ordering from suppliers, request:

| Specification | Why It Matters |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Germination Rate** | Affects yield and consistency |
| **Lot Number** | Traceability for food safety |
| **Test Date** | Germination decreases over time |
| **Purity %** | Less chaff = better value |
| **Treatment Status** | Must be untreated for food use |
| **Pathogen Testing** | Critical for food safety |
| **Storage Recommendations** | Maintains seed viability |

## Seed Testing and Evaluation

### Conducting Germination Tests

**Simple Germination Test Protocol:**

```
1. SETUP
   ├─ Count 100 seeds
   ├─ Prepare small tray with moist media
   ├─ Seed at normal density
   └─ Label with variety, date, lot number

2. INCUBATION
   ├─ Follow normal germination protocol
   ├─ Maintain proper moisture
   ├─ Appropriate temperature (68-75°F)
   └─ Darkness/light as variety requires

3. EVALUATION (Day 3-5)
   ├─ Count emerged seedlings
   ├─ Calculate germination %
   ├─ Assess uniformity and vigor
   └─ Look for disease or abnormalities

4. DECISION
   ├─ >90%: Excellent, proceed
   ├─ 85-90%: Good, may need higher seeding density
   ├─ 80-85%: Marginal, contact supplier
   └─ <80%: Reject or return

5. DOCUMENTATION
   └─ Record results for future reference
```

### Variety Trial Process

When testing new varieties:

**Week 1-2: Initial Trial**
- Grow 2-4 trays
- Follow recommended protocols
- Document germination, growth rate, yield
- Taste and evaluate quality

**Week 3-4: Market Testing**
- Provide samples to key customers
- Gather feedback on flavor, appearance
- Test pricing acceptance
- Assess demand level

**Week 5-6: Economic Evaluation**
- Calculate actual yield vs. seed cost
- Compare to existing varieties
- Assess fit in production schedule
- Determine if worth adding to portfolio

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                          │
│  TEMPERATURE                                                             │
│  ───────────                                                             │
│  • Ideal: 40-50°F (refrigeration)                                        │

│                                                                          │
│  HUMIDITY                                                                │

| Radish, Arugula | 2 years | 3-4 years | >4 years |
| Pea Shoots | 2 years | 3 years | >3 years |

| Basil, Herbs | 1 year | 2 years | >2 years |

**Note:** Germination rates decrease over time even with proper storage.

## Calculating Seed Needs

### Production Planning Formula

```
SEED REQUIREMENT CALCULATION

Weekly Production Target: X trays
─────────────────────────────────

Step 1: Determine seeding rate per tray
Example (Sunflower): 3.5 oz per 10×20 tray

Step 2: Calculate weekly seed needs
X trays × 3.5 oz = Y oz per week

Step 3: Add 10% buffer for testing/waste
Y × 1.10 = Z oz per week

Step 4: Convert to pounds and scale up
Z oz ÷ 16 = pounds per week
Pounds/week × 52 = annual seed needs

Step 5: Determine order quantities
Balance: bulk savings vs. storage limits
Consider: shelf life and germination decline
```

```
EXAMPLE: 50 TRAYS/WEEK OPERATION

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Start with 3-5 proven varieties** (Sunflower, Pea Shoots, Radish, Broccoli, Arugula) before expanding

2. **Source from reputable microgreens seed suppliers** ensuring food-safe, pathogen-tested seeds

3. **Test germination rates** before committing to large quantities; maintain >85% germination

4. **Buy in bulk to maximize margins** but balance with storage limits and seed viability

5. **Proper storage extends seed life** - cool, dry, dark conditions in sealed containers
