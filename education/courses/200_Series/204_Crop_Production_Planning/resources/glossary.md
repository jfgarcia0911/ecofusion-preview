# Glossary: Crop Production Planning
## Course 204

---

## A

**Annual Production Plan**: A comprehensive document outlining crop selection, production schedules, resource requirements, and financial projections for a full year of operations.

**Average Daily Light Integral (DLI)**: The cumulative amount of photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) received by plants during a 24-hour period, measured in mol/m²/day.

## B

**Batch**: A group of plants seeded at the same time that moves through the production cycle together.

**Buffer Stock**: Additional inventory maintained above committed orders to handle unexpected demand or production losses.

**Break-even Analysis**: Financial calculation determining the production volume at which total revenue equals total costs.

## C

**Crop Cycle**: The complete duration from seeding to harvest for a particular crop, measured in days.

**Crop Mix**: The combination of different crops grown in a facility, typically expressed as percentages of total production area.

**Crop Rotation**: The practice of alternating different crops in the same growing space to manage pests, diseases, and nutrient balance.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)**: Direct costs associated with producing crops, including seeds, nutrients, packaging, and direct labor.

## D

**Days to Harvest (DTH)**: The number of days from seeding until a crop reaches harvest maturity.

**Days to Transplant (DTT)**: The number of days seedlings remain in propagation before being moved to production systems.

**Demand Forecasting**: The process of estimating future customer demand based on historical data, market trends, and seasonal patterns.

## E

**Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)**: The optimal order quantity that minimizes total inventory costs including ordering and holding costs.

## F

**First-In-First-Out (FIFO)**: Inventory management method where the oldest stock is sold or used first to ensure freshness.

**Fixed Costs**: Expenses that remain constant regardless of production volume, such as rent, insurance, and equipment depreciation.

**Forecast Accuracy**: A measure of how closely predicted yields or demand match actual results, typically expressed as a percentage.

## G

**Germination Rate**: The percentage of seeds that successfully sprout and develop into viable seedlings.

**Growing Area**: The total square footage dedicated to crop production, excluding propagation, processing, and storage areas.

**Gross Margin**: Revenue minus cost of goods sold, expressed as a percentage of revenue.

## H

**Harvest Window**: The optimal time period during which a crop should be harvested for best quality and shelf life.

**Head Count**: The number of individual plants (particularly lettuce) rather than weight-based measurement.

## I

**Input Costs**: Expenses for materials directly used in production, including seeds, nutrients, growing media, and packaging.

## J

**Just-In-Time (JIT)**: Production approach where crops are grown to match customer demand with minimal inventory.

## L

**Lead Time**: The time between placing an order (for supplies) or starting production and receiving/completing the product.

**Loss Rate**: The percentage of planted crops that fail to reach harvestable maturity due to germination failure, disease, pests, or quality issues.

## M

**Market Channel**: A specific sales outlet such as farmers markets, restaurants, grocery stores, or CSA programs.

**Market Window**: A period when demand is high and/or prices are elevated for specific products.

## O

**Overseed Rate**: The percentage of extra seeds planted above the target quantity to compensate for expected germination losses.

## P

**Production Capacity**: The maximum output a facility can achieve given its growing space, equipment, and labor resources.

**Production Schedule**: A detailed timeline showing when specific activities (seeding, transplanting, harvesting) occur for each crop batch.

## R

**Resource Allocation**: The distribution of limited resources (space, labor, capital) among competing uses to maximize productivity.

**Revenue per Square Foot**: A key performance indicator calculated by dividing total revenue by production area.

## S

**Seasonality**: Predictable patterns of demand and pricing that occur at specific times of year.

**Seeding Rate**: The number of seeds planted per tray, cell, or square foot.

**SKU (Stock Keeping Unit)**: A unique identifier for each distinct product variant.

**Space Utilization**: The percentage of available growing area that is actively in production at any given time.

**Succession Planting**: The practice of planting crops at regular intervals to ensure continuous harvest throughout the year.

**Succession Interval**: The number of days between successive plantings of the same crop.

## T

**Throughput**: The rate at which products move through the production system, typically measured in pounds per week or units per day.

**Transplant**: The act of moving seedlings from propagation trays to production growing systems.

**Turn Time**: The period between harvesting one crop and having the next crop ready for harvest in the same space.

**Turns per Year**: The number of complete crop cycles that can be completed in a growing space annually.

## V

**Variable Costs**: Expenses that change in proportion to production volume, such as seeds, packaging, and hourly labor.

**Variety Trial**: A systematic evaluation of different crop varieties to compare performance under specific conditions.

## W

**Weekly Production Target**: The planned harvest volume for each week, typically expressed in pounds or units.

## Y

**Yield**: The harvestable output from a given area or number of plants, typically measured in pounds per square foot or ounces per plant.

**Yield per Square Foot**: A productivity metric measuring the weight of harvested product from one square foot of growing area per crop cycle.
