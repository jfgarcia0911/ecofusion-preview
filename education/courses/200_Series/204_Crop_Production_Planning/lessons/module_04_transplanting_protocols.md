## Introduction

Transplanting is a critical moment in the production cycle. Poor transplanting technique or timing can result in stunted growth, disease problems, and lower yields—even if propagation was perfect. Conversely, excellent transplanting protocols ensure rapid establishment, minimal losses, and crops that reach their full potential.

This module provides detailed protocols for successful transplanting across various crops and production systems.

## Transplanting Procedures

### Standard Operating Procedure

```
TRANSPLANTING SOP
═════════════════

MATERIALS NEEDED:
• Seedling trays with ready transplants
• Clean net pots or grow sites
• Growing media (if using) - clay pebbles, etc.
• Labels and markers
• Clean gloves
• Batch tracking sheet

STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS:

1. PRE-TRANSPLANT PREPARATION (30 min before)
   □ Water seedling trays thoroughly
   □ Allows plugs to be fully hydrated
   □ Easier removal, less root damage
   □ Verify system is ready (water quality, spacing)

2. SEEDLING SELECTION
   □ Inspect each seedling
   □ SELECT: Uniform size, dark green, vigorous
   □ CULL: Diseased, stunted, damaged, discolored
   □ Typical selection rate: 90-95%

3. PLUG REMOVAL
   □ Support plug from bottom
   □ Gently push from below
   □ Never pull on stem or leaves if possible
   □ Plug should come out intact with roots holding it together

4. PLACEMENT
   □ Position plug at correct depth:
      • Top of plug level with growing media surface
      • NOT buried (crown rot risk)
      • NOT elevated (drying out risk)
   □ Ensure plug makes contact with water/nutrient source
   □ For net pots: fill around plug with clay pebbles

5. SECURING
   □ Gently firm media around plug
   □ Ensure stability (shouldn't wobble)
   □ Verify water is reaching plug bottom
   □ Check that roots will access solution

6. LABELING
   □ Label section with:
      • Crop variety
      • Batch number
      • Transplant date
      • Expected harvest date

7. INITIAL MONITORING (First hour)
   □ Walk system after 30-60 minutes
   □ Check for tilted or fallen plants
   □ Verify all plugs are wet
   □ Adjust any issues immediately
```

### Density Trade-offs

```
SPACING DENSITY ANALYSIS
════════════════════════

SCENARIO: Butterhead Lettuce

OPTION 1: STANDARD SPACING (8" × 8" = 2.25 plants/sqft)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
✓ Optimal head size (10-12 oz)
✓ Good air circulation (less disease)
✓ Premium quality
✓ Higher price point
✗ Lower plant count per area

Revenue/sqft: 2.25 × $3.50 = $7.88 per harvest


OPTION 2: DENSE SPACING (6" × 6" = 4.00 plants/sqft)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
✓ More plants per area
✗ Smaller heads (6-8 oz)
✗ Disease pressure higher
✗ Lower price due to size
✗ May not meet customer specs

Revenue/sqft: 4.00 × $2.25 = $9.00 per harvest

CAUTION: 14% revenue increase BUT quality issues often outweigh gains


RECOMMENDATION:
═══════════════
Use standard spacing for quality and reliability
Dense spacing only for specific market demands (baby lettuce, etc.)
```

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                    TRANSPLANT SHOCK FACTORS                            ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                        ║
║  WHAT IS TRANSPLANT SHOCK?                                             ║
║  ═════════════════════════                                             ║
║  Temporary growth interruption caused by:                              ║
║  • Root disturbance                                                    ║
║  • Water stress                                                        ║
║  • Environmental change                                                ║
║  • Handling damage                                                     ║
║                                                                        ║
║  SYMPTOMS:                                                             ║
║  • Wilting (most common)                                               ║
║  • Leaf droop                                                          ║
║  • Slowed growth                                                       ║
║  • Color change                                                        ║
║  • Leaf drop (severe cases)                                            ║
║                                                                        ║
║  MINIMIZATION STRATEGIES:                                              ║
║                                                                        ║
║  1. TIMING                                                             ║
║     • Transplant at optimal seedling age                               ║
║     • Choose cooler times of day (morning/evening)                     ║
║     • Avoid extreme weather periods                                    ║
║                                                                        ║
║  2. PREPARATION                                                        ║
║     • Water seedlings before transplanting                             ║
║     • Prepare system completely first                                  ║
║     • Minimize time out of media                                       ║
║                                                                        ║
║  3. HANDLING                                                           ║
║     • Gentle removal from trays                                        ║
║     • Handle by plug, not stem                                         ║
║     • Minimize root exposure to air                                    ║
║                                                                        ║
║  4. ENVIRONMENT                                                        ║
║     • Reduce light temporarily                                         ║
║     • Increase humidity                                                ║
║     • Moderate temperature                                             ║
║     • Reduce air movement                                              ║
║                                                                        ║
║  5. WATER/NUTRIENTS                                                    ║
║     • Ensure immediate water contact                                   ║
║     • Use reduced nutrient strength                                    ║
║     • Maintain optimal pH                                              ║
║                                                                        ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Performance Tracking

### Key Metrics

```
TRANSPLANT SUCCESS METRICS
══════════════════════════

1. TRANSPLANT SURVIVAL RATE
   Formula: (Surviving plants at day 7 ÷ Total transplanted) × 100
   Target: >95%
   Benchmark: 90-95% acceptable, <90% investigate

2. ESTABLISHMENT TIME
   Measurement: Days until visible new growth
   Target: 3-5 days for most crops
   Indicator of transplant quality and conditions

3. QUALITY SCORE (at day 14)
   5 = Excellent (uniform, vigorous, dark green)
   4 = Good (mostly uniform, healthy)
   3 = Fair (some variation, acceptable)
   2 = Poor (significant issues, slow)
   1 = Fail (diseased, stunted, replant needed)

   Target: Average score >4.0

4. TIME TO HARVEST (vs. planned)
   Track: Actual DTM vs. expected DTM
   Target: Within 3 days of planned
   Indicates transplant quality impact on growth
```

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Symptoms | Cause | Solution |
|---------|----------|-------|----------|
| **Severe wilting** | Drooping, not recovering | Dry plugs, air pockets, root damage | Verify water contact, increase humidity, check for gaps |
| **Slow establishment** | No new growth after 7 days | Transplant shock, nutrient issues, temperature | Check environment, verify nutrient strength, patience |
| **Tipping over** | Plants falling sideways | Poor support, too little media, plug not secure | Add media support, re-secure plants |
| **Yellowing leaves** | Chlorosis after transplant | Nutrient deficiency, pH issue | Increase nutrients gradually, check pH |
| **Brown leaf edges** | Leaf margins browning | Salt stress, nutrient burn | Reduce EC, flush with clean water |
| **Disease outbreak** | Spots, mold, damping off | Contaminated plugs, poor sanitation, stress | Remove affected plants, improve sanitation, reduce stress |

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Timing is critical** - Transplant within the optimal window when roots are established but not root-bound

2. **System preparation matters** - Clean systems, optimal water quality, and proper environmental conditions set transplants up for success

3. **Gentle handling minimizes shock** - Proper technique during removal and placement prevents damage and stress

4. **Spacing affects quality and yield** - Use proven spacing for each crop; dense spacing often reduces quality

5. **First week care is essential** - Reduced stress conditions for 3-7 days ensure successful establishment

6. **Track performance** - Monitor survival rates, establishment time, and quality scores to continuously improve

7. **Troubleshoot quickly** - Address problems within first 24-48 hours to prevent losses

8. **Practice improves efficiency** - Developed techniques and team coordination significantly increase transplanting speed
