## Introduction

Crop selection is the foundation of successful production planning. While it's tempting to grow what you find personally interesting or what seems trendy, commercial success requires systematic analysis of multiple factors: market demand, profitability, system compatibility, labor efficiency, and resource utilization.

This module provides a framework for making strategic crop selection decisions that maximize profitability while maintaining operational efficiency and customer satisfaction.

## Factor 1: Market Demand Analysis

### Understanding Your Market

Before committing growing space to any crop, you must understand market demand in your specific region and sales channels.

**Key Questions:**
- Who will buy this crop? (restaurants, retail, direct consumers)
- How much will they purchase regularly?
- What quality standards do they expect?
- What's their price sensitivity?
- Are there seasonal demand fluctuations?

Most successful operations use a portfolio approach:

## Factor 2: Profitability Analysis

### Key Profitability Metrics

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                    CROP PROFITABILITY METRICS                          ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                        ║
║  METRIC 1: GROSS MARGIN PER UNIT                                       ║
║  ═══════════════════════════                                           ║
║  Formula: (Selling Price - Direct Costs) ÷ Selling Price × 100        ║
║                                                                        ║
║  Example - Butterhead Lettuce:                                         ║
║    Selling price:        $3.50/head                                    ║
║    Direct costs:         $1.20/head                                    ║
║    Gross margin:         ($3.50 - $1.20) ÷ $3.50 = 66%                ║
║                                                                        ║
║  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────      ║
║                                                                        ║
║  METRIC 2: REVENUE PER SQUARE FOOT PER YEAR                            ║
║  ═══════════════════════════════════════════                           ║
║  Formula: (Units/sq ft × Turns/year × Price/unit)                     ║
║                                                                        ║
║  Example - Loose Leaf Lettuce:                                         ║
║    Density:              2.25 plants/sq ft (8" spacing)                ║
║    Annual turns:         10.4 (5-week crop cycle)                     ║
║    Price:                $2.50/head                                    ║
║    Revenue/sq ft/year:   2.25 × 10.4 × $2.50 = $58.50                 ║
║                                                                        ║
║  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────      ║
║                                                                        ║
║  METRIC 3: CONTRIBUTION MARGIN PER SQUARE FOOT                         ║
║  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════                   ║
║  Formula: Revenue/sq ft - Variable costs/sq ft                        ║
║                                                                        ║
║  Example - Basil:                                                      ║
║    Revenue/sq ft/year:   $42                                           ║
║    Variable costs/year:  $18                                           ║
║    Contribution margin:  $24/sq ft/year                                ║
║                                                                        ║
║  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────      ║
║                                                                        ║
║  METRIC 4: LABOR COST PER POUND                                        ║
║  ═══════════════════════════════                                       ║
║  Formula: Total labor hours × wage ÷ pounds produced                  ║
║                                                                        ║
║  Target: <$1.50/lb for profitability                                  ║
║                                                                        ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Factor 5: Space Efficiency

### Space Utilization Calculations

Understanding space efficiency helps maximize facility profitability.

```
SPACE EFFICIENCY FORMULA
════════════════════════

Annual Production per Square Foot =
    (Plants per sq ft) × (Annual turns) × (Yield per plant)

Annual Revenue per Square Foot =
    Annual Production × Price per Unit


EXAMPLE: LOOSE LEAF LETTUCE
═══════════════════════════

Spacing:            8" × 8" = 2.25 plants/sq ft
Crop Cycle:         35 days = 10.4 turns/year
Yield:              0.25 lb/head
Price:              $2.50/head

Annual Production:  2.25 × 10.4 × 0.25 = 5.85 lb/sq ft/year
Annual Revenue:     2.25 × 10.4 × $2.50 = $58.50/sq ft/year


COMPARISON TABLE
════════════════
```

### The 80/20 Rule of Crop Selection

In most profitable operations:
- **20% of crops** generate **80% of revenue**
- **20% of growing space** produces **80% of profit**

**Actionable Strategy:**
1. Identify your top revenue crops (usually 3-5 items)
2. Allocate majority of space to these proven performers
3. Reserve 15-20% of space for specialty/trial crops
4. Continuously test and evaluate new options
5. Replace underperformers ruthlessly

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Use a systematic framework** - Evaluate crops across five factors: market demand, profitability, system compatibility, labor efficiency, and space efficiency

2. **Market demand comes first** - Growing what sells is more profitable than growing what's interesting

3. **Profitability requires calculation** - Use metrics like gross margin, revenue per square foot, and contribution margin to compare options

4. **System compatibility is non-negotiable** - Match crops to your infrastructure and environmental capabilities

5. **Labor costs matter** - Factor in time, skill requirements, and wage costs when evaluating profitability

6. **Space is your most valuable resource** - Maximize revenue per square foot through strategic crop selection

7. **Build a balanced portfolio** - Combine core crops (reliability), rotation crops (diversity), and trial crops (innovation)

8. **Avoid common mistakes** - Don't grow everything, don't select before validating demand, don't ignore labor costs
