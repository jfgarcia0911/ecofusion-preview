## Introduction

Climate management is the single most critical factor determining success in greenhouse production. While nutrients, water, and light are essential, the environment in which plants grow dictates how effectively they can utilize these resources. A greenhouse is essentially a climate modification tool—its purpose is to create conditions that optimize plant growth regardless of external weather.

This module establishes the fundamental principles of greenhouse climate that will guide all subsequent modules.

### 1. Temperature Management

Temperature is the master controller of plant metabolism:

**Air Temperature:**
- Controls photosynthesis rate
- Affects respiration
- Influences transpiration
- Determines growth speed
- Triggers developmental stages (flowering, fruiting)

**Root Zone Temperature:**
- Affects nutrient uptake rate
- Influences water absorption
- Impacts beneficial microorganisms
- Controls root growth

### 2. Humidity and VPD

Humidity affects transpiration, which drives nutrient uptake and cooling:

**Relative Humidity (RH):**
- Percentage of moisture in air relative to maximum possible
- Temperature-dependent
- Easy to measure but incomplete picture

**Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD):**
- The "drying power" of air
- What plants actually experience
- Combines temperature and humidity
- Critical for optimizing growth

**The VPD Concept:**
```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                                       ║
║                  UNDERSTANDING VAPOR PRESSURE DEFICIT                 ║
║                                                                       ║
║   VPD = Saturation Vapor Pressure - Actual Vapor Pressure            ║
║                                                                       ║
║   Think of VPD as the "thirst" of the air:                           ║
║                                                                       ║
║   LOW VPD (< 0.4 kPa)      OPTIMAL VPD (0.8-1.2 kPa)                ║
║   ═════════════════         ══════════════════════                    ║
║   • Air is "full"           • Balanced conditions                    ║
║   • Little transpiration    • Active transpiration                   ║
║   • Disease risk ↑          • Strong nutrient flow                   ║
║   • Slow growth             • Maximum growth                         ║
║                                                                       ║
║   HIGH VPD (> 1.6 kPa)                                               ║
║   ══════════════════                                                  ║
║   • Air is "thirsty"                                                 ║
║   • Excessive transpiration                                          ║
║   • Stomata close (stress)                                           ║
║   • Wilting risk                                                     ║
║                                                                       ║
║   FORMULA:  VPD = SVP × (1 - RH/100)                                 ║
║             where SVP = saturation vapor pressure at leaf temp       ║
║                                                                       ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### 3. Air Movement

Airflow serves multiple critical functions:

**Functions of Air Movement:**
- Temperature distribution and uniformity
- Humidity distribution
- CO₂ distribution to leaf surfaces
- Transpiration regulation
- Stem strengthening (thigmomorphogenesis)
- Disease prevention (reduces surface moisture)
- Heat removal from leaves

**Air Movement Targets:**

### 4. Light Integration

While light has its own dedicated module (Module 8), it interacts critically with climate:

**Light-Climate Interactions:**
- High light intensity → increased temperature → cooling required
- High light → higher photosynthesis → more CO₂ needed
- High light → increased transpiration → VPD management critical
- Photoperiod affects temperature requirements (day/night differential)

### Developmental Triggers

Climate affects more than just growth rate:

| Climate Factor | Developmental Response |
|----------------|----------------------|

## Calculating Basic Climate Metrics

### VPD Calculation

**Step 1:** Determine Saturation Vapor Pressure (SVP)

Using the approximation formula:
```
SVP (kPa) = 0.6108 × e^(17.27 × T/(T + 237.3))

Where T = temperature in °C
```

Or use lookup table:
- 20°C (68°F): SVP = 2.34 kPa
- 25°C (77°F): SVP = 3.17 kPa
- 30°C (86°F): SVP = 4.24 kPa

**Step 2:** Calculate VPD

```
VPD = SVP × (1 - RH/100)

Example:
Temperature: 25°C (77°F)
Relative Humidity: 65%

SVP at 25°C = 3.17 kPa
VPD = 3.17 × (1 - 65/100)
VPD = 3.17 × 0.35
VPD = 1.11 kPa  ← Optimal range!
```

### DIF Calculation

```
DIF = Day Temperature - Night Temperature

Example:
Day temperature: 78°F
Night temperature: 65°F
DIF = 78 - 65 = +13°F

POSITIVE DIF → More stem elongation (stretching)
NEGATIVE DIF → More compact growth
ZERO DIF → Neutral
```

### Heat Accumulation (Growing Degree Days)

```
GDD = ((Max Temp + Min Temp) / 2) - Base Temperature

Example for tomatoes (base temp 50°F):
Day high: 85°F
Day low: 65°F

GDD = ((85 + 65) / 2) - 50
GDD = 75 - 50
GDD = 25 degree days accumulated

Useful for predicting flowering, fruiting timing
```

## Common Climate Challenges

### Challenge 1: Temperature Uniformity

```
PROBLEM: Hot spots and cold zones

CAUSES:
• Poor air circulation
• Inadequate mixing
• Heat source placement
• Structure design (dead zones)

SOLUTIONS:
→ Add HAF fans
→ Improve air distribution
→ Multiple temperature sensors
→ Zone-based control
```

### Challenge 2: High Humidity/Low VPD

```
PROBLEM: Condensation, disease pressure

CAUSES:
• Inadequate ventilation
• Overwatering
• Cold surfaces
• High plant density

SOLUTIONS:
→ Increase air exchange
→ Improve heating uniformity
→ Dehumidification
→ Better spacing
```

### Challenge 3: Dry Air/High VPD

```
PROBLEM: Plant stress, stunted growth

CAUSES:
• Excessive heating
• Dry climate
• Over-ventilation
• Low irrigation

SOLUTIONS:
→ Humidification systems
→ Reduce ventilation when possible
→ Evaporative cooling
→ Shade to reduce temperature
```

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Four pillars of climate control** - Temperature, humidity/VPD, air movement, and light integration must be managed together

2. **VPD is the critical metric** - Better than relative humidity alone for understanding plant water relations

3. **Plants respond to climate** - Through photosynthesis, respiration, transpiration, and developmental triggers

4. **Proper monitoring is essential** - Multiple sensors at representative locations provide accurate data

5. **Control systems create feedback loops** - Sensors, controllers, and actuators work together continuously

6. **Setpoints should be crop-specific** - Different crops require different optimal conditions
