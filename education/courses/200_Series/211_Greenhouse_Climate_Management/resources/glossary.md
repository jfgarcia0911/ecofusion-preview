# Climate Control Glossary
## Course 211: Greenhouse Climate Management

---

## A

**Absolute Humidity**
The actual amount of water vapor in air, measured in grams per cubic meter (g/m³) or grains per pound. Unlike relative humidity, absolute humidity doesn't change with temperature.

**ACH (Air Changes per Hour)**
The number of times the entire volume of air in a greenhouse is replaced in one hour. Typical targets: 15-30 ACH minimum, 60-120 ACH for summer cooling.

**Actuator**
A device that carries out control actions, such as opening vents, turning on fans, or activating heaters, in response to controller commands.

**Anemometer**
An instrument for measuring air velocity, typically in feet per minute (ft/min) or meters per second (m/s).

**Aspiration**
The movement of air over a sensor, required for accurate humidity and temperature measurements. Aspirated sensors have fans to ensure consistent airflow.

---

## B

**Blackout Curtain**
An opaque curtain system used to block all light, enabling photoperiod control for day-neutral or short-day plants.

**BTU (British Thermal Unit)**
A unit of heat energy. One BTU is the amount of energy needed to raise one pound of water by 1°F. Used to rate heating equipment capacity.

---

## C

**Canopy**
The uppermost layer of plant foliage. Sensors should be placed at canopy height to measure the conditions plants actually experience.

**CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute)**
A measure of volumetric airflow, used to rate fan capacity. Greenhouse cooling fans typically rated in thousands of CFM.

**CO₂ Enrichment**
The practice of increasing carbon dioxide concentration above ambient (400 ppm) to enhance photosynthesis, typically to 800-1200 ppm.

**Condensation**
The change of water vapor to liquid water when air temperature drops to or below the dew point. Causes dripping and disease risk in greenhouses.

**Controller**
The electronic device (computer or dedicated unit) that reads sensors, compares to setpoints, and operates actuators to maintain desired climate conditions.

**Convection**
Heat transfer by the movement of air or fluids. Natural convection occurs due to density differences (hot air rises); forced convection uses fans.

---

## D

**Deadband**
The temperature range around a setpoint where no control action occurs. For example, with a 70°F setpoint and 2°F deadband, heating starts at 68°F and cooling at 72°F. Prevents excessive cycling.

**Dehumidification**
The removal of moisture from air, accomplished through condensation (cooling below dew point), ventilation (replacing with drier air), or desiccant materials.

**Design Temperature**
The coldest outdoor temperature expected for a location, used to calculate maximum heating requirements. Typically based on 99% winter design temperature (colder than this 1% of winter hours).

**Desiccant Dehumidifier**
A dehumidification system using moisture-absorbing materials. Works at any temperature but adds heat and has higher operating cost than refrigerant types.

**Destratification**
The process of mixing air layers to eliminate temperature stratification (hot air at top, cold at bottom). Accomplished with HAF fans or vertical mixing fans.

**Dew Point**
The temperature at which air becomes saturated (100% RH) and water vapor begins to condense into liquid. Any surface below the dew point will collect condensation.

**DIF (Day-Night Temperature Differential)**
The difference between day and night temperatures. Positive DIF (day warmer) promotes stem elongation; negative DIF (night warmer) produces compact growth.

**DLI (Daily Light Integral)**
The total amount of photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) received over 24 hours, measured in moles per square meter per day (mol/m²/day). Critical for crop planning.

---

## E

**Edema**
A physiological disorder caused by cells taking up water faster than it can transpire, resulting in water-soaked lesions or blisters on leaves. Occurs when humidity is too high and VPD too low.

**Enthalpy**
The total heat content of air, including both sensible heat (temperature) and latent heat (moisture). Measured in BTU per pound or kJ per kg.

**Evaporative Cooling**
Cooling method that uses water evaporation to remove heat from air. Most effective in dry climates. Common types: pad-and-fan systems, fog systems.

**Evapotranspiration (ET)**
The combined water loss from evaporation (soil/surfaces) and transpiration (plants). Increases with temperature, light, and VPD.

---

## F

**Feedback Loop**
The cycle of sensing conditions, comparing to setpoints, taking control action, and sensing the results. Basis of automatic climate control.

**Forced Air Heating**
Heating system that uses fans to blow air over a heat exchanger and distribute warm air through the greenhouse. Fast response but can create uneven temperatures.

---

## G

**Glazing**
The transparent or translucent covering material of a greenhouse (glass, polyethylene film, polycarbonate, etc.). Properties affect light transmission, insulation, and durability.

**Growing Degree Days (GDD)**
Accumulated heat units above a base temperature, used to predict crop development. Calculated daily as: ((Max temp + Min temp) / 2) - Base temp.

---

## H

**HAF (Horizontal Air Flow)**
A circulation fan system creating a horizontal air pattern throughout the greenhouse. Provides uniform temperature and humidity without creating drafts.

**Heat Pump**
A device that moves heat from one location to another. Can heat (extracting heat from outside air) or cool (moving heat outside). Efficiency measured as COP (Coefficient of Performance).

**Humidity Ratio**
The mass of water vapor per unit mass of dry air, typically measured in grains per pound or grams per kilogram. Shown on the vertical axis of psychrometric charts.

**HVAC**
Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning. The integrated systems that control temperature and air quality.

**Hysteresis**
See Deadband. The difference between the temperature that turns equipment on versus off, preventing rapid cycling.

---

## I

**Infiltration**
Uncontrolled air leakage into or out of a greenhouse through cracks, gaps, and openings. Increases heating load and makes climate control more difficult.

**Infrared Thermometer**
A non-contact thermometer that measures surface temperature using infrared radiation. Useful for detecting cold spots on glazing or plant canopy temperature.

**Insect Screen**
Fine mesh screening installed over vents to exclude insects while allowing airflow. Reduces effective ventilation area and must be accounted for in fan sizing.

**Internode**
The stem segment between two nodes (leaf attachment points). Internode length controlled by DIF and light intensity.

---

## L

**Latent Heat**
Energy absorbed or released during a phase change (water to vapor or vice versa) without changing temperature. Important in evaporative cooling and dehumidification.

**Lux**
A measure of visible light intensity (lumens per square meter). Not ideal for plant applications—use PAR instead.

---

## M

**Microclimate**
Small-scale climate conditions that differ from the general greenhouse environment, such as within dense plant canopy or near heating/cooling sources.

**Modulating Control**
Proportional control that varies equipment output smoothly rather than simple on/off. For example, a modulating heater adjusts flame size based on distance from setpoint.

---

## N

**Natural Ventilation**
Ventilation relying on wind and thermal buoyancy (stack effect) rather than fans. Energy-free but less controllable than mechanical ventilation.

**NDIR (Non-Dispersive Infrared)**
Technology used in accurate CO₂ sensors. Measures infrared absorption by CO₂ molecules.

---

## O

**Offset**
The consistent difference between setpoint and actual achieved value, often due to controller proportional band or system limitations.

---

## P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)**
Light in the 400-700 nm wavelength range that plants use for photosynthesis. Measured in μmol/m²/s (micromoles per square meter per second).

**Photoperiod**
Day length, measured in hours. Affects flowering in many crops (short-day, long-day, or day-neutral responses).

**Photosynthesis**
The process by which plants convert light energy, CO₂, and water into sugars and oxygen. Optimized by proper temperature, light, CO₂, and water availability.

**PID Controller**
Proportional-Integral-Derivative controller. Advanced control algorithm providing smooth, accurate control by considering error magnitude, duration, and rate of change.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**
See PAR. The preferred term for light intensity as experienced by plants.

**Proportional Band**
The temperature range over which a proportional controller modulates equipment from 0% to 100% output. Example: 2°F band with 70°F setpoint means 0% at 71°F, 100% at 69°F.

**Psychrometric Chart**
A graphical representation of the properties of moist air, showing relationships between temperature, humidity, dew point, enthalpy, and other parameters.

---

## Q

**Quantum Sensor**
A sensor that measures PAR (photosynthetically active radiation) in μmol/m²/s. Essential for light management in greenhouses.

---

## R

**Radiant Heating**
Heating that warms objects and surfaces directly through infrared radiation rather than heating air. Examples: hot water pipes, infrared heaters. Provides even, gentle heat.

**Relative Humidity (RH)**
The percentage of water vapor in air compared to the maximum it could hold at that temperature. Changes with temperature even if moisture content stays constant.

**Respiration**
The process by which plants break down sugars to release energy for growth and metabolism. Produces CO₂ and heat. Increases exponentially with temperature.

---

## S

**Saturation Vapor Pressure (SVP)**
The maximum amount of water vapor air can hold at a given temperature. Used to calculate VPD. Increases exponentially with temperature.

**Sensible Heat**
Heat that causes a temperature change (as opposed to latent heat). The heat you feel and thermometers measure.

**Setpoint**
The target value for a climate parameter (e.g., 72°F temperature setpoint). Controller acts to maintain actual conditions at setpoint.

**Stack Effect**
Natural convection creating airflow due to temperature difference between inside and outside. Warm air rises and exits through high vents; cool air enters low vents.

**Static Pressure**
Resistance to airflow created by ductwork, screens, or other restrictions. Fans must overcome static pressure; high pressure reduces CFM output.

**Stomata**
Microscopic pores on leaf surfaces that regulate gas exchange (CO₂ in, water vapor and oxygen out). Open wider when VPD is optimal; close when VPD too high or too low.

**Stratification**
Layers of different temperature air in a greenhouse, with warmest at the top. Indicates poor air circulation. Solved with HAF fans.

---

## T

**Thermal Curtain**
Retractable curtain system that reduces heat loss at night by creating an insulating air gap. Can reduce heating costs 30-60%. Also called energy curtain or shade curtain if used for light control.

**Thermal Mass**
Materials that store heat (water, concrete, rock). High thermal mass moderates temperature swings by absorbing heat when warm and releasing when cool.

**Thermistor**
Type of temperature sensor using a semiconductor whose resistance changes predictably with temperature. Common in greenhouse controllers.

**Thigmomorphogenesis**
Plant responses to mechanical stimulation (touch, wind, vibration). Air movement from fans creates shorter, sturdier stems.

**Throttling Range**
See Proportional Band.

**Transpiration**
Water loss from plants through stomata. Drives nutrient uptake and cools leaves. Controlled by VPD, temperature, light, and air movement.

---

## U

**U-Value**
Heat transfer coefficient measuring how well a material insulates. Units: BTU/hr·ft²·°F. Lower values indicate better insulation. Inverse of R-value.

---

## V

**Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)**
The difference between actual water vapor pressure and saturation vapor pressure, measured in kilopascals (kPa). Represents the "drying power" of air and drives transpiration. Optimal range for most crops: 0.8-1.2 kPa.

**Venting**
Opening vents or operating fans to exchange inside air with outside air. Used for temperature control, humidity management, and CO₂ replenishment.

**VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)**
Electronic controller that varies fan motor speed by adjusting electrical frequency. Provides energy-efficient variable ventilation.

---

## W

**Wet Bulb Temperature**
Temperature measured by a thermometer with a wet wick in moving air. Always equal to or lower than dry bulb (air) temperature. Indicates evaporative cooling potential.

---

## Z

**Zone**
A section of greenhouse with separate climate control. Multi-zone systems allow different crops with different requirements in the same structure.
