## Questions

**1. What percentage of greenhouse operating costs typically goes to heating in cold climates?**
- A) 10-15%
- B) 20-30%
- C) 40-60%
- D) 80-90%

**2. What is the first step in improving greenhouse energy efficiency?**
- A) Install solar panels
- B) Replace all equipment with new models
- C) Conduct an energy audit to identify losses
- D) Switch to a different fuel source

**3. What is the R-value of a material?**
- A) Rate of heat transfer
- B) Resistance to heat flow (higher = better insulation)
- C) Reflectivity of light
- D) Required ventilation rate

**4. Which glazing material provides the best insulation?**
- A) Single-layer glass
- B) Double-layer polyethylene
- C) Triple-wall polycarbonate
- D) Single-layer fiberglass

**5. A thermal curtain with 60% energy savings potential is used 12 hours per night. Annual heating cost is $20,000. Curtain system costs $15,000 installed. What is the simple payback period?**
- A) 1.25 years
- B) 2.5 years
- C) 5 years
- D) 7.5 years

**6. What is a major benefit of thermal curtains besides energy savings?**
- A) They increase light transmission
- B) They eliminate the need for heating systems
- C) They reduce temperature stratification and create smaller heated volume at night
- D) They prevent all pest entry

**7. Air infiltration in greenhouses causes energy loss primarily by:**
- A) Allowing heated/cooled air to escape
- B) Blocking sunlight
- C) Damaging equipment
- D) Increasing structural load

**8. What is the recommended maintenance interval for cleaning greenhouse glazing to maintain light transmission?**
- A) Once per year
- B) Every 5 years
- C) At least annually, preferably 2-4 times per year
- D) Only when visibly dirty

**9. TRUE or FALSE: Investing in a high-efficiency boiler is always the best first step for reducing energy costs.**
- A) True
- B) False

**10. Which renewable energy option is most commonly viable for greenhouses?**
- A) Wind turbines
- B) Biomass boilers or geothermal heating
- C) Tidal power
- D) Small-scale nuclear

---

## Answer Key

1. **C** - In cold climates, heating typically represents 40-60% of operating costs, making it the largest expense
2. **C** - An energy audit identifies where energy is being wasted; fix the biggest losses before investing in new equipment
3. **B** - R-value measures thermal resistance; higher R-value means better insulation (less heat transfer)
4. **C** - Triple-wall polycarbonate has the highest R-value (around R-2.0 to R-2.5) of common greenhouse materials
5. **B** - Annual savings = $20,000 × 0.60 × (12/24) = $6,000; Payback = $15,000 / $6,000 = 2.5 years
6. **C** - Curtains create a smaller heated volume at night and reduce stratification (hot air at roof); also provide light control
7. **A** - Infiltration allows conditioned air to escape and outdoor air to enter, forcing heating/cooling systems to work harder
8. **C** - Clean glazing at least annually, ideally 2-4 times per year to maintain optimal light transmission (dirty glazing can reduce transmission by 20%+)
9. **B** - False. Fixing envelope losses (insulation, sealing) often has better ROI than equipment upgrades; reduce load first, then upgrade equipment
10. **B** - Biomass (wood chips, pellets) and geothermal are most practical; solar thermal is also viable; wind typically not cost-effective for heating needs
