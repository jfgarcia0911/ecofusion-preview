# Educational Garden Program Glossary
## Course 214: Community & School Garden Programs

---

## A

**Accessibility** - Design features that ensure spaces and programs can be used by people of all abilities, including those with physical, sensory, or cognitive disabilities.

**Active Learning** - Educational approach where students engage directly with material through hands-on activities rather than passive reception of information.

**ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)** - Federal law requiring accessibility accommodations; gardens should follow ADA guidelines for pathways, bed heights, and facilities.

**Age-Appropriate** - Activities and expectations matched to students' developmental stage and abilities.

**Asset Mapping** - Process of identifying existing resources, strengths, and capabilities in a community that can support a program.

---

## B

**Backward Design** - Curriculum planning approach that begins with desired learning outcomes and works backward to design activities and assessments.

**Beneficial Insects** - Insects that provide services like pollination, pest control, or decomposition (e.g., ladybugs, bees, lacewings).

**Budget Narrative** - Written explanation accompanying a budget that justifies each line item and explains costs.

**Burnout** - State of physical, emotional, or mental exhaustion often caused by prolonged stress or overwork; common risk for garden coordinators.

---

## C

**Capstone Project** - Culminating project that synthesizes learning from an entire course or program.

**Carbon Sequestration** - Process by which plants absorb CO2 from atmosphere and convert it to biomass, helping mitigate climate change.

**Cold Frame** - Low structure with transparent top used to extend growing season by protecting plants from cold while allowing sunlight.

**Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)** - Model where consumers purchase "shares" of a farm's harvest in advance, sharing in risks and bounty.

**Companion Planting** - Growing certain plants together for mutual benefit (pest control, nutrient sharing, or growth enhancement).

**Compost** - Decomposed organic matter used as soil amendment; also the process of creating this material.

**Consensus** - General agreement among group members, even if not unanimous.

**Continuous Improvement** - Ongoing cycle of evaluation, reflection, and enhancement of program quality.

**Cross-Curricular** - Educational approach integrating multiple subject areas through common themes or projects.

**Curriculum Integration** - Incorporating garden-based learning into core academic curriculum rather than treating as separate/extra.

---

## D

**Demonstration Bed** - Garden area designed for instructor to model techniques or showcase plants to groups.

**Developmental Appropriateness** - Matching content, methods, and expectations to learners' developmental stage.

**Differentiation** - Adapting instruction to meet diverse student needs, abilities, interests, and learning styles.

**Diversified Funding** - Financial strategy using multiple revenue sources to reduce dependence on any single funder.

**Drip Irrigation** - Water delivery system that slowly drips water directly to plant roots, conserving water.

---

## E

**Earned Income** - Revenue generated through program activities like plant sales, workshops, or produce sales.

**Ecosystem** - Community of living organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment.

**Edible Schoolyard** - Program model featuring integrated garden and kitchen education; originated in Berkeley, CA.

**Engagement Spectrum** - Range of stakeholder participation from inform (low) to empower (high).

**Environmental Literacy** - Understanding of environmental systems and how to make informed decisions about environment.

**Evaluation** - Systematic assessment of program processes, outputs, and outcomes to determine effectiveness.

**Experiential Learning** - Learning through doing and reflection on those experiences; core pedagogy for garden education.

**Extension** - Cooperative Extension Service - university-based educational outreach providing research-based information on agriculture, horticulture, and youth development.

---

## F

**Food Desert** - Area with limited access to affordable, nutritious food, typically due to absence of grocery stores.

**Food Justice** - Movement ensuring equitable access to healthy, culturally appropriate food produced through sustainable methods.

**Food Literacy** - Knowledge and skills to make informed food choices including nutrition, food systems, and preparation.

**Food Miles** - Distance food travels from production to consumption; indicator of environmental impact.

**Food Security** - State of having reliable access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food.

**Food Systems** - All processes involved in feeding a population: production, processing, distribution, consumption, waste.

**Food Web** - Complex network of feeding relationships in an ecosystem showing energy flow between organisms.

**Formative Assessment** - Ongoing evaluation during learning process to inform instruction and provide feedback.

**FoodCorps** - AmeriCorps program placing service members in schools to provide nutrition education and garden programming.

---

## G

**Garden-Based Learning (GBL)** - Educational approach using gardens as outdoor classrooms to support academic and developmental goals.

**Garden Coordinator** - Dedicated staff person (paid or volunteer) managing garden program operations and education.

**Germination** - Process of seed sprouting and beginning growth.

**Grant** - Financial award from foundation, government, or corporation to support specific project or program.

**Greenhouse** - Structure with transparent walls and roof for growing plants in controlled environment.

---

## H

**Hardscape** - Non-living elements of landscape design (paths, walls, structures) as opposed to plants.

**Hoop House** - Tunnel-shaped greenhouse structure typically covered with plastic for season extension.

**High Tunnel** - Walk-in hoop house tall enough for people to work inside comfortably.

---

## I

**Implementation** - Process of putting a plan into action.

**In-Kind Donation** - Non-monetary contribution of goods, services, or time that has economic value.

**Inclusive** - Welcoming and accessible to people of all backgrounds, abilities, and identities.

**Infrastructure** - Basic physical systems and structures needed for garden operation (water, paths, beds, storage).

**Institutional Integration** - Embedding program into organization's core curriculum, budget, policies, and culture.

**Integrated Pest Management (IPM)** - Ecosystem-based pest control strategy using multiple tactics minimizing pesticide use.

---

## J

**Junior Master Gardener** - Youth development program using gardening to teach life skills and science.

---

## K

**KidsGardening** - National nonprofit providing grants, resources, and information for youth garden programs.

**Kolb's Learning Cycle** - Model of experiential learning: experience, reflection, conceptualization, experimentation.

---

## L

**LEAP Method** - Approach to addressing concerns: Listen, Empathize, Ask, Propose.

**Life Cycle** - Series of changes organism goes through from birth to death.

**Life Lab** - Science education program using gardens to teach standards-aligned science.

**Local Food** - Food produced within relatively close distance to where it's consumed (often defined as within 100 miles).

**Logic Model** - Visual representation showing program's intended relationships between inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, and impact.

---

## M

**Master Gardener** - Trained volunteer educator providing research-based horticultural information through Extension programs.

**Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** - Written agreement outlining terms of partnership between organizations.

**Microclimate** - Local conditions (temperature, moisture, wind) in small area that differ from surrounding area.

**Mulch** - Material (organic or inorganic) spread on soil surface to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, regulate temperature.

---

## N

**Native Plant** - Plant species that evolved and occurs naturally in particular region.

**Needs Assessment** - Systematic process to identify gaps between current and desired conditions to inform program planning.

**Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)** - K-12 science education standards emphasizing three-dimensional learning.

**Nutrient Cycling** - Movement of nutrients through ecosystem via processes like decomposition and nutrient uptake.

---

## O

**Outdoor Classroom** - Natural space designed and used for teaching; in this context, the garden as learning environment.

**Outputs** - Direct products of program activities (number of lessons taught, students served, etc.).

**Outcomes** - Changes resulting from program (knowledge gained, behaviors changed, skills developed).

---

## P

**Partnership** - Formal collaboration between organizations for mutual benefit.

**Pathway** - Routes through garden for access; should be accessible, safe, and appropriate width.

**Perennial** - Plant that lives for more than two years, typically producing growth season after season.

**Permaculture** - Design system for sustainable human settlements mimicking patterns found in nature.

**Photosynthesis** - Process by which plants convert sunlight, water, and CO2 into energy and oxygen.

**Pilot Program** - Small-scale trial implementation to test feasibility before full rollout.

**Pollinator** - Animal (insect, bird, mammal) that moves pollen between flowers, enabling plant reproduction.

**Power-Interest Grid** - Stakeholder analysis tool mapping stakeholders by their power/influence and interest/engagement level.

---

## R

**Raised Bed** - Above-ground planting area contained within frame, improving drainage and accessibility.

**Readiness Assessment** - Evaluation of conditions necessary for successful program implementation.

**Replication** - Adapting successful program model to new context or location.

**Retention** - Keeping volunteers, participants, or stakeholders engaged and committed over time.

**Rubric** - Scoring guide with criteria and performance levels for assessing student work.

---

## S

**Scaffolding** - Educational support provided to help students achieve what they couldn't do alone.

**Season Extension** - Techniques to grow plants outside normal season (cold frames, row covers, greenhouses).

**Sensory Garden** - Garden designed to engage multiple senses (touch, smell, taste, sight, sound).

**SMART Goals** - Goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

**Square Foot Gardening** - Method dividing growing area into small square sections with specified plant quantities.

**Stakeholder** - Individual or group with interest in or influence over a program.

**Stakeholder Engagement** - Process of involving stakeholders in program decisions and activities.

**Standards Alignment** - Connecting activities to academic learning standards (state or national).

**Stewardship** - Responsible care and management; also cultivation of relationships with donors/volunteers.

**Succession Planting** - Planting crops at intervals to ensure continuous harvest rather than all at once.

**Succession Planning** - Preparing for leadership transitions through documentation and pipeline development.

**Summative Assessment** - Evaluation at end of instructional unit to measure overall achievement.

**Sustainability** - Ability of program to continue effectively over time; also environmental concept of meeting present needs without compromising future.

**Sustainable Agriculture** - Farming practices that are environmentally sound, economically viable, and socially responsible.

---

## T

**Theory of Change** - Explanation of how program activities will lead to desired short-term and long-term outcomes.

**Three Sisters** - Indigenous companion planting method growing corn, beans, and squash together.

**Transplanting** - Moving plant from one growing location to another.

---

## U

**Universal Design** - Design approach creating spaces and materials usable by people of all abilities without adaptation.

**Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** - Educational framework providing multiple means of representation, engagement, and expression.

**Urban Agriculture** - Growing food in urban areas; includes community gardens, rooftop farms, vertical gardens.

---

## V

**Vermicomposting** - Composting using worms (typically red wigglers) to break down organic matter.

**Vertical Gardening** - Growing plants upward on structures to maximize space.

**Volunteer** - Person contributing time and skills without financial compensation.

**Volunteer Management** - Coordinating recruitment, training, scheduling, and retention of volunteers.

---

## W

**Water-Wise Gardening** - Practices that conserve water (mulching, drip irrigation, appropriate plant selection).

**Weatherization** - Preparing garden for winter or extreme weather.

**Weed** - Plant growing where not wanted; can be subjective based on garden goals.

---

## Y

**Yield** - Amount of crop produced, typically measured by weight or volume.

**Youth Development** - Process supporting young people's social, emotional, cognitive, and physical growth.

---

## Z

**Zone** - Distinct area within garden serving specific purpose (pollinator zone, production zone, teaching zone).

**Zoning Regulations** - Local government rules governing land use that may affect garden projects.

---
