# Quiz 10: Program Design Project
## Course 214: Community & School Garden Programs

**Module 10** | 10 Questions | 15 minutes | 80% to pass

## Questions

**1. A comprehensive program proposal should include:**
- A) Only a budget
- B) Context, stakeholders, design, curriculum, operations, budget, evaluation ✓
- C) Just the garden layout
- D) Only good intentions

**2. SMART goals in your proposal must be:**
- A) Vague and unmeasurable
- B) Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound ✓
- C) Impossible dreams
- D) Optional

**3. A garden design should be drawn:**
- A) Freehand with no measurements
- B) To scale with measurements ✓
- C) Not at all
- D) Only in your imagination

**4. Sample lesson plans in the proposal should demonstrate:**
- A) Random activities
- B) Age-appropriate, standards-aligned instruction ✓
- C) Impossibly complex content
- D) No educational value

**5. An implementation timeline should cover:**
- A) Only the first week
- B) 12-24 months with major milestones ✓
- C) 50 years
- D) No specific timeframe

**6. The logic model connects:**
- A) Random unrelated concepts
- B) Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact ✓
- C) Only financial information
- D) Students to lunch

**7. A realistic startup budget for a small school garden might be:**
- A) $500
- B) $10,000-$20,000 ✓
- C) $500,000
- D) Free with no costs

**8. Professional quality proposals include:**
- A) Many spelling and grammar errors
- B) Poor organization
- C) Clear writing, good organization, polished presentation ✓
- D) Handwritten notes only

**9. Phased development means:**
- A) Building everything immediately
- B) Year 1 infrastructure, Year 2 expansion, Year 3 sustainability ✓
- C) Never starting
- D) Random approach

**10. After completing your proposal, the next step is:**
- A) Put it in a drawer and forget it
- B) Share with stakeholders, build support, secure funding, implement ✓
- C) Start over from scratch
- D) Wait indefinitely

**Answers: 1-B, 2-B, 3-B, 4-B, 5-B, 6-B, 7-B, 8-C, 9-B, 10-B**
