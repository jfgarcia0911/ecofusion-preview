### 2.1 The Importance of Needs Assessment

#### Why Start with Assessment?

**Assessment prevents common mistakes:**
- Building programs no one wants or uses
- Missing critical stakeholder buy-in
- Overlooking existing resources
- Setting unrealistic goals
- Ignoring sustainability factors

### 2.2 Stakeholder Identification & Analysis

#### Key Stakeholder Categories

**For School-Based Programs:**

| Stakeholder Group | Key Individuals | Importance | Influence Level |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------|-----------------|
| **Decision Makers** | Principal, superintendent, school board | Critical | High |
| **Direct Users** | Teachers, students, families | Critical | Medium-High |
| **Support Staff** | Custodians, cafeteria, maintenance | High | Medium |
| **Funding Sources** | PTA, district, grants | High | Medium-High |
| **Community Partners** | Extension, nonprofits, businesses | Medium | Low-Medium |
| **Neighbors** | Adjacent residents | Medium | Low-Medium |

**For Community Gardens:**

| Stakeholder Group | Key Individuals | Importance | Influence Level |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------|-----------------|
| **Decision Makers** | Land owner, city officials, board | Critical | High |
| **Gardeners** | Current and potential plot holders | Critical | Medium |
| **Neighbors** | Adjacent residents, businesses | High | Medium |
| **Partners** | Extension, food banks, schools | Medium-High | Medium |
| **Supporters** | Volunteers, donors | Medium | Low-Medium |

#### Stakeholder Mapping Exercise

**Power-Interest Grid:**

```
        HIGH POWER/INFLUENCE
              │
    KEEP      │      MANAGE
  SATISFIED   │     CLOSELY
              │
──────────────┼──────────────────
              │
   MONITOR    │     KEEP
              │    INFORMED
              │
         LOW INTEREST ──→ HIGH INTEREST
```

**Mapping Your Stakeholders:**
- **Manage Closely** (High power, high interest): Key partners, must engage deeply
- **Keep Satisfied** (High power, low interest): Keep informed, don't burden
- **Keep Informed** (Low power, high interest): Communicate regularly
- **Monitor** (Low power, low interest): General awareness

### 2.3 Information Gathering Methods

#### Assessment Tools & Techniques

**Surveys:**
- Student/participant interest surveys
- Teacher readiness assessments
- Family/community needs surveys
- Online or paper-based

**Focus Groups:**
- Small group discussions (6-10 people)
- Targeted stakeholder groups
- Facilitated conversations
- 60-90 minutes

**Interviews:**
- One-on-one conversations
- Key decision makers
- 30-45 minutes
- In-depth understanding

**Direct Observation:**
- Site visits to existing programs
- Space assessment
- Activity observation

**Document Review:**
- Strategic plans
- Curriculum documents
- Budget information
- Existing program data

#### Sample Survey Questions

**Student Interest Survey (Elementary):**
1. Have you ever grown a plant or vegetable? (Yes/No)
2. What vegetables do you like to eat?
3. Would you like to learn about growing food? (Yes/No)
4. What would you want to grow in a school garden?
5. Have you ever tasted tomatoes/lettuce/carrots from a garden?

**Teacher Readiness Survey:**
1. How comfortable are you teaching outdoors? (1-5 scale)
2. What subjects could you connect to garden lessons?
3. How often would you use a school garden? (frequency)
4. What support would you need to use the garden?
5. What concerns do you have about garden-based learning?

**Community Needs Survey:**
1. Does your household need access to garden space?
2. What would you like to grow?
3. What skills/knowledge do you want to learn?
4. How much time could you commit?
5. What barriers exist to participating?

### 2.5 Organizational Readiness Assessment

#### Readiness Dimensions

**1. Leadership Support**

| Indicator | Low Readiness | Medium Readiness | High Readiness |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Administrative buy-in | Resistant | Neutral/permissive | Enthusiastic champion |
| Resource commitment | None allocated | Some flexibility | Dedicated budget |
| Priority level | Low | Medium | Strategic priority |

**2. Staff/Volunteer Capacity**

| Indicator | Low Readiness | Medium Readiness | High Readiness |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Champion identified | No champion | Interested person | Dedicated coordinator |
| Staff knowledge | Limited experience | Some experience | Expertise available |
| Time available | Overextended | Tight but possible | Adequate capacity |

**3. Community Support**

| Indicator | Low Readiness | Medium Readiness | High Readiness |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Interest level | Low awareness | Some interest | Strong demand |
| Volunteer pool | Very limited | Small group | Active base |
| Partnership potential | Few connections | Some partners | Network exists |

**4. Physical Resources**

| Indicator | Low Readiness | Medium Readiness | High Readiness |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Space available | No suitable space | Space with limitations | Ideal location |
| Infrastructure | Lacks basics | Some infrastructure | Ready to use |
| Materials/tools | None | Some items | Well-equipped |

**5. Financial Resources**

| Indicator | Low Readiness | Medium Readiness | High Readiness |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Funding identified | No funding | Potential sources | Secured funds |
| Budget allocation | None | Small/uncertain | Dedicated line item |
| Sustainability plan | No plan | Initial ideas | Multi-year funding |

#### Readiness Scoring

**Scoring Guide:**
- Low Readiness (1 point): Major barriers exist
- Medium Readiness (2 points): Possible with effort
- High Readiness (3 points): Strong foundation

**Total Score Interpretation:**
- 13-15 points: Ready to launch substantial program
- 10-12 points: Ready for pilot/modest beginning
- 7-9 points: Focus on building readiness first
- 5-6 points: Significant preparation needed

### 2.6 Setting Program Goals

#### SMART Goals Framework

**SMART Criteria:**
- **S**pecific - Clear and well-defined
- **M**easurable - Quantifiable outcomes
- **A**chievable - Realistic given resources
- **R**elevant - Aligned with needs and mission
- **T**ime-bound - Specific deadline or timeframe

#### Example Goals

**Weak Goal:**
"Start a school garden"

**SMART Goal:**
"Establish four 4x8 raised bed gardens by August 2026, serving 120 third-grade students in weekly garden-based science lessons aligned to state standards."

---

**Weak Goal:**
"Improve nutrition"

**SMART Goal:**
"Increase the percentage of students who can identify and willingness to taste three vegetables from 45% to 70% by June 2026, as measured by pre/post surveys."

---

**Weak Goal:**
"Get community involved"

**SMART Goal:**
"Recruit and train 15 community volunteers who contribute a minimum of 4 hours per month to garden maintenance and education activities by September 2026."

### 2.7 Priority Setting

#### Balancing Multiple Goals

**Priority Matrix:**

```
    URGENT & IMPORTANT  │  IMPORTANT, NOT URGENT
                        │
    Do First            │  Schedule & Plan
    ────────────────────┼────────────────────────
                        │
    URGENT, NOT         │  NEITHER URGENT NOR
    IMPORTANT           │  IMPORTANT
                        │
    Delegate/Minimize   │  Eliminate
```

**Common Priority Areas:**

| Priority Area | Year 1 Focus | Year 2-3 Expansion |
|---------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Infrastructure** | Basic beds, water, fence | Additional space, shed, systems |
| **Programming** | Single grade/group | Multiple grades, expanded offerings |
| **Community** | Core team, key partners | Broader engagement, events |
| **Curriculum** | Pilot lessons | Full integration, assessment |
| **Sustainability** | Initial funding, volunteers | Diversified funding, succession |

### 2.8 Theory of Change

#### Developing Your Logic Model

**Logic Model Components:**

```
INPUTS → ACTIVITIES → OUTPUTS → SHORT-TERM → LONG-TERM
                                 OUTCOMES     IMPACT

What we     What we    What we    What        What
invest      do         produce    changes     ultimately
                                 (1-2 yrs)   changes
                                             (3-5 yrs)
```

**Example: Elementary School Garden**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Inputs** | Garden space, coordinator (10 hrs/wk), $5,000 budget, teacher training, curriculum materials |
| **Activities** | Weekly garden lessons for grades 3-5, teacher professional development, family garden nights |
| **Outputs** | 120 students participate in 20 lessons/year, 10 teachers trained, 4 family events held |
| **Short-term Outcomes** | Students increase plant knowledge, try new vegetables, demonstrate science concepts |
| **Long-term Impact** | Improved science achievement, healthier eating habits, environmental stewardship |

### 2.9 Planning Timeline

#### Year-One Implementation Timeline

**Example Timeline for School Garden:**

| Timeframe | Major Milestones | Key Activities |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|
| **Summer (Year 0)** | Planning & Preparation | Needs assessment, stakeholder engagement, site selection |
| **August-Sept** | Site Development | Build beds, install water, prepare soil, tool purchase |
| **October-Nov** | Fall Season | Fall planting, initial lessons, volunteer recruitment |
| **December-Feb** | Winter Planning | Curriculum development, teacher training, fundraising |
| **March-April** | Spring Season | Main planting season, peak programming |
| **May-June** | Harvest & Celebration | Harvest activities, end-of-year event, evaluation |
| **July-August** | Summer Maintenance | Care plan, summer program (if any), prepare for Year 2 |

#### Phased Approach

**Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-6)**
- Complete needs assessment
- Secure approvals and support
- Form core team
- Develop initial plans
- Identify funding

**Phase 2: Pilot (Months 7-12)**
- Build initial garden space
- Launch with one or two groups
- Test curriculum and activities
- Recruit and train volunteers
- Document and learn

**Phase 3: Expansion (Year 2)**
- Expand to additional participants
- Enhance programming
- Strengthen partnerships
- Diversify funding
- Refine based on evaluation

**Phase 4: Sustainability (Year 3+)**
- Institutionalize program
- Develop succession plans
- Achieve financial sustainability
- Scale and replicate
- Continuous improvement
