### 4.1 Experiential Learning Theory

#### Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle

```
LEARNING CYCLE IN THE GARDEN

        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │   CONCRETE EXPERIENCE   │
        │  (Doing/Experiencing)   │
        │  Plant seeds, observe   │
        └──────────┬──────────────┘
                   │
                   ▼
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │ REFLECTIVE OBSERVATION  │
        │  (Reviewing/Reflecting) │
        │  Journal, discuss       │
        └──────────┬──────────────┘
                   │
                   ▼
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │ ABSTRACT CONCEPTUALIZATION│
        │ (Concluding/Learning)   │
        │ Connect to concepts     │
        └──────────┬──────────────┘
                   │
                   ▼
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │  ACTIVE EXPERIMENTATION │
        │ (Planning/Testing)      │
        │ Apply to new situation  │
        └──────────┬──────────────┘
                   │
                   └──────(cycle continues)
```

**Example Application: Seed Germination**

1. **Concrete Experience:** Plant bean seeds in different conditions (light/dark, wet/dry)
2. **Reflective Observation:** Observe and record which sprout first, compare results
3. **Abstract Conceptualization:** Understand plants need water and light; discuss germination
4. **Active Experimentation:** Predict what would happen with different seeds or conditions

### 4.3 Developmental Appropriateness

#### Age-Based Design Considerations

**K-2 (Ages 5-7):**

| Developmental Stage | Implications for Garden Curriculum |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Concrete thinkers** | Use tangible, hands-on experiences |
| **Short attention spans** | 15-20 minute activities, movement breaks |
| **Sensory learners** | Emphasize touch, smell, taste, observation |
| **Emerging fine motor** | Simple tasks: poking holes, dropping seeds |
| **Literal language** | Clear, simple instructions |
| **Learning social skills** | Partner activities, sharing tasks |

**Curriculum Focus:**
- Sensory exploration (touching soil, smelling herbs)
- Basic plant needs (water, sun, soil)
- Simple observation and description
- Care and responsibility
- Wonder and curiosity

---

**Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-10):**

| Developmental Stage | Implications for Garden Curriculum |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Developing abstract thinking** | Introduce concepts like photosynthesis |
| **Longer focus** | 30-45 minute lessons possible |
| **Improving literacy** | Reading, writing, research integration |
| **More complex motor skills** | Use real garden tools with supervision |
| **Interest in how things work** | Life cycles, ecosystems, processes |
| **Peer cooperation** | Small group projects and investigations |

**Curriculum Focus:**
- Life cycles and plant reproduction
- Ecosystems and food webs
- Scientific measurement and data
- Connecting garden to larger food systems
- Problem-solving (pests, weather challenges)

### 4.4 Curriculum Design Framework

#### Backward Design Process

```
1. IDENTIFY DESIRED RESULTS
   What should students know, understand, be able to do?

2. DETERMINE ACCEPTABLE EVIDENCE
   How will we know students achieved results?

3. PLAN LEARNING EXPERIENCES
   What activities will lead to desired results?
```

**Example: 3rd Grade Plant Life Cycle Unit**

**Step 1: Desired Results**
- **Know:** Stages of plant life cycle, parts of a flower
- **Understand:** Plants reproduce through seeds
- **Able to do:** Diagram life cycle, grow plant from seed to seed

**Step 2: Assessment Evidence**
- **Performance:** Grow fast-growing plant (radish) through complete cycle
- **Product:** Labeled life cycle diagram with drawings from observation
- **Written:** Journal entries documenting each stage

**Step 3: Learning Activities**
- Week 1: Plant seeds, predict what will happen
- Week 2-3: Observe and measure seedlings, draw stages
- Week 4-5: Observe flowering and pollination
- Week 6: Harvest seeds, complete life cycle diagram
- Week 7: Reflection and presentation

### 4.5 Integrated Curriculum Examples

#### Multi-Subject Lesson: "Square Foot Garden Math & Science"

**Grade Level:** 4th

**Standards Addressed:**
- Math: Area, multiplication, division
- Science: Plant needs and growth
- ELA: Informational reading, technical writing

**Lesson Sequence:**

```
DAY 1: INTRODUCTION & PLANNING
├── Read: "Square Foot Gardening" excerpt
├── Math: Calculate area of 4'x4' bed (16 sq ft)
└── Planning: Decide what to plant where

DAY 2: GARDEN DESIGN
├── Math: Division - plants per square based on spacing
│   (16 carrots, 4 lettuce, 1 tomato, etc.)
├── Science: Research plant needs
└── Design: Create scale drawing of garden plan

DAY 3: IMPLEMENTATION
├── Math: Measure and mark squares
├── Science: Plant according to needs
└── Writing: Label diagram with plant names, spacing

DAY 4-8: OBSERVATION & CARE
├── Science: Daily observations, measurements
├── Math: Graph growth data
└── Writing: Observation journal entries

DAY 9: ANALYSIS & REFLECTION
├── Math: Calculate germination rates
├── Science: Analyze which plants thrived
└── Writing: Recommendations for next planting
```

**Assessment:**
- Accurate area calculations
- Appropriate plant spacing based on research
- Complete observation data
- Thoughtful analysis of results

### 4.7 Assessment Strategies

#### Types of Garden-Based Assessment

**Formative Assessment (During Learning):**

| Method | Purpose | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Observation** | Monitor engagement, skills | Use checklist while students work |
| **Questioning** | Check understanding | "Why do you think the plant grew taller?" |
| **Journals** | Document thinking over time | Weekly garden observations |
| **Quick writes** | Gauge concept understanding | "List three things plants need" |
| **Peer discussion** | Reveal thinking processes | Partner talks about their findings |

**Summative Assessment (End of Learning):**

| Method | Purpose | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Projects** | Demonstrate mastery | Complete life cycle diagram |
| **Presentations** | Communicate learning | Share experiment results |
| **Written tests** | Assess knowledge | Quiz on plant parts and functions |
| **Performance tasks** | Apply skills | Successfully transplant seedlings |
| **Portfolios** | Show growth over time | Collection of garden work |

#### Assessment Rubric Example

**4th Grade: Plant Growth Investigation**

| Criteria | Developing (1-2) | Proficient (3) | Advanced (4) |
|----------|------------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Data Collection** | Missing data, incomplete | Consistent measurements recorded | Detailed, systematic data with extras |
| **Graph Creation** | Incorrect scale or labels | Accurate graph with proper labels | Clear, creative visualization |
| **Analysis** | States observations only | Identifies patterns, offers explanation | Connects to broader concepts |
| **Conclusion** | Incomplete or unsupported | Clear conclusion supported by data | Insightful conclusion, considers limitations |

### 4.8 Curriculum Sequences & Scope

#### Year-Long Scope & Sequence Example

**3rd Grade Garden Science Integration**

| Season | Theme | Key Concepts | Activities |
|--------|-------|--------------|------------|
| **Fall** (Sept-Nov) | Seeds & Germination | Seed structure, conditions for sprouting | Fall planting, seed dissection |
| **Winter** (Dec-Feb) | Planning & Indoor Growing | Plant needs, planning | Seed catalogs, indoor starts |
| **Spring** (Mar-May) | Growth & Life Cycles | Photosynthesis, reproduction | Transplanting, observation |
| **Late Spring** (May-Jun) | Ecosystems & Harvest | Food webs, interdependence | Harvest, composting |

#### Unit Planning

**Unit Structure:**
- 6-8 weeks per unit
- 1-2 lessons per week
- 30-45 minutes per lesson
- Balance between instruction and hands-on

**Sample Unit: "From Seed to Table"**

```
WEEK 1: INTRODUCTION
└── Lesson: Seed exploration & planting

WEEK 2: GERMINATION
└── Lesson: Observing sprouts, germination needs

WEEK 3: GROWTH
└── Lesson: Measuring growth, plant parts

WEEK 4: CARE
└── Lesson: Watering, weeding, problem-solving

WEEK 5: FLOWERING
└── Lesson: Flower anatomy, pollination

WEEK 6: HARVEST & EATING
└── Lesson: Harvesting, food preparation

WEEK 7: COMPOSTING
└── Lesson: Plant life after harvest, decomposition

WEEK 8: REFLECTION
└── Lesson: Seed saving, documenting learning
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Experiential Learning** | Learning through reflection on direct experience |
| **Standards Alignment** | Connecting activities to academic learning standards |
| **Backward Design** | Planning process starting with desired outcomes |

| **Integrated Curriculum** | Connecting multiple subject areas through common themes |
