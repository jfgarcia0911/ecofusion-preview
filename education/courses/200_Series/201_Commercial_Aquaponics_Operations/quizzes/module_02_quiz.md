## Questions

**1. What is the primary design philosophy for commercial aquaponics facilities?**
   - a) Maximize aesthetics and visual appeal
   - b) Linear workflow with materials flowing in one direction
   - c) Circular design for space efficiency
   - d) Vertical stacking for maximum density

**2. What percentage of total facility space should typically be allocated to the growing area?**
   - a) 30-40%
   - b) 40-50%
   - c) 50-60%
   - d) 70-80%

**3. What is the recommended minimum width for main aisles that need cart and equipment access?**
   - a) 2-3 feet
   - b) 3-4 feet
   - c) 4-6 feet
   - d) 6-8 feet

**4. Which facility zone should be physically separated from the harvest/packing area for food safety?**
   - a) Propagation area
   - b) Fish production area
   - c) Office space
   - d) Cold storage

**5. What is the recommended temperature range for walk-in cooler storage?**
   - a) 28-32°F
   - b) 34-38°F
   - c) 40-45°F
   - d) 45-50°F

**6. Which principle ensures that every tank, bed, and equipment piece can be easily maintained?**
   - a) Linear flow
   - b) Zone separation
   - c) Accessibility
   - d) Scalability

**7. In a circular/central fish tank arrangement, what is the primary advantage?**
   - a) Better visual inspection
   - b) Efficient plumbing and central access
   - c) Easier cohort management
   - d) Lower construction cost

**8. What design feature is most critical for cold climate facilities?**
   - a) Shading and cooling systems
   - b) Insulation priority and heating capacity
   - c) Dehumidification systems
   - d) Natural ventilation

**9. What is the rule of thumb for maximum distance from any point in grow beds to an aisle?**
   - a) No more than 2 feet
   - b) No more than 3 feet
   - c) No more than 5 feet
   - d) No more than 8 feet

**10. Which of these is NOT a core design principle for commercial facilities?**
   - a) Linear flow from inputs to outputs
   - b) Maximum automation regardless of cost
   - c) Zone separation for biosecurity
   - d) Building infrastructure for future expansion

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) Linear workflow with materials flowing in one direction** - Good design features linear flow from inputs to shipping, preventing cross-contamination and wasted movement.

2. **c) 50-60%** - Typical space distribution allocates 50-60% to growing area to maximize productive capacity.

3. **c) 4-6 feet** - Main aisles require 4-6 feet width to accommodate carts and equipment movement.

4. **b) Fish production area** - Fish area should be separated from harvest/packing with physical barriers or airlocks to prevent contamination.

5. **b) 34-38°F** - Walk-in coolers should maintain 34-38°F for optimal produce storage and shelf life.

6. **c) Accessibility** - Every tank, bed, and equipment should be accessible for daily observation, maintenance, emergency repairs, and harvesting.

7. **b) Efficient plumbing and central access** - Circular/central arrangements provide efficient plumbing with shorter pipe runs and central maintenance access.

8. **b) Insulation priority and heating capacity** - Northern/cold climates require prioritizing insulation, heating capacity, and thermal mass.

9. **b) No more than 3 feet** - The rule of thumb is that no point in grow beds should be more than 3 feet from an aisle for accessibility.

10. **b) Maximum automation regardless of cost** - While automation is valuable, it must be economically justified. Not all automation is cost-effective at all scales.
