## 5.1 Production Capacity Calculations

### Understanding Your Production Potential

Production capacity determines revenue potential. Accurate calculations are essential for business planning.

**Key Variables:**
- **System area:** Total sq ft of growing space
- **Plant spacing:** Distance between plants
- **Growth cycle:** Days from transplant to harvest
- **Turnover rate:** Harvests per year
- **Average yield:** Weight per plant or per sq ft
- **Success rate:** Percentage of plants that reach market quality

### Capacity Calculation Framework

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                    PRODUCTION CAPACITY FORMULA                             ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                            ║
║  Annual Production (lbs) =                                                 ║
║                                                                            ║
║    Growing Area (sq ft)                                                    ║
║    × Plants per sq ft                                                      ║
║    × Harvests per year (365 ÷ growth cycle days)                          ║
║    × Average weight per plant (lbs)                                        ║
║    × Success rate (0.90-0.95)                                              ║
║                                                                            ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### Example Calculations

**Example 1: Lettuce in DWC System**

```
Facility: 5,000 sq ft DWC rafts

Growing Area: 5,000 sq ft
Plant Spacing: 6 inches on center = 4 plants per sq ft
Growth Cycle: 35 days (transplant to harvest)
Harvests per Year: 365 ÷ 35 = 10.4 cycles
Average Weight: 6 oz per head = 0.375 lbs
Success Rate: 92% = 0.92

Calculation:
5,000 sq ft × 4 plants/sq ft × 10.4 cycles × 0.375 lbs × 0.92
= 71,160 lbs annual production

Revenue Projection:
71,160 lbs × $3.50/lb wholesale = $249,060 annual revenue
```

**Example 2: Basil in NFT System**

```
Facility: 1,000 sq ft NFT channels

Growing Area: 1,000 sq ft
Plant Spacing: 4 inches = 9 plants per sq ft
Growth Cycle: 28 days
Harvests per Year: 365 ÷ 28 = 13 cycles
Average Weight: 2 oz per plant = 0.125 lbs
Success Rate: 90% = 0.90

Calculation:
1,000 sq ft × 9 plants/sq ft × 13 cycles × 0.125 lbs × 0.90
= 13,163 lbs annual production

Revenue Projection:
13,163 lbs × $6.00/lb = $78,978 annual revenue
```

**Example 3: Tomatoes in Media Beds**

```
Facility: 1,500 sq ft media beds

Growing Area: 1,500 sq ft
Plant Spacing: 2 feet = 0.25 plants per sq ft
Growth Cycle: 120 days (transplant to first harvest + 90 day harvest period)
Yield: 15 lbs per plant over harvest period
Success Rate: 85% = 0.85

Calculation:
1,500 sq ft × 0.25 plants/sq ft × 15 lbs × 0.85
= 4,781 lbs annual production

Revenue Projection:
4,781 lbs × $4.00/lb = $19,124 annual revenue
```

## 5.2 Crop Selection for Commercial Success

### Market-Driven Crop Selection

**Crop Categories by Commercial Viability:**

**Tier 1: Proven Commercial Crops (Core Revenue)**
- Lettuce (butterhead, romaine, oakleaf)
- Basil (Genovese, Thai, purple)
- Bok choy and Asian greens
- Kale and Swiss chard

**Tier 2: Good Potential (Specialty/Premium)**
- Specialty lettuces (red varieties, butter blends)
- Herbs (cilantro, parsley, dill, mint)
- Arugula and watercress
- Cherry tomatoes

**Tier 3: Experimental (Test Small)**
- Fruiting crops (peppers, cucumbers)
- Microgreens
- Edible flowers
- Exotic herbs

### Crop Characteristics for Selection

| Crop | Growth Days | Price/lb | Difficulty | Space Efficiency | Market Size |
|------|-------------|----------|------------|------------------|-------------|
| **Butterhead Lettuce** | 32-38 | $3-5 | Easy | High | Large |
| **Basil** | 25-30 | $6-10 | Easy | High | Large |
| **Bok Choy** | 35-42 | $3-4 | Easy | High | Growing |
| **Kale** | 40-50 | $3-5 | Easy | Moderate | Large |
| **Arugula** | 30-35 | $4-6 | Easy | High | Moderate |
| **Cherry Tomatoes** | 90-120 | $4-6 | Moderate | Low | Large |
| **Herbs (mixed)** | 25-35 | $6-12 | Easy-Mod | High | Moderate |
| **Microgreens** | 7-14 | $20-40 | Easy | Very High | Growing |

### Recommended Starter Crop Mix

**For First Year Operations (De-risk Strategy):**

- **60% Lettuce varieties** (proven, reliable, fast turnover)
- **25% Basil** (high value, strong demand)
- **10% Bok choy/Asian greens** (niche market, good margins)
- **5% Experimental** (test market response, learn growing)

**Rationale:**
- Focus on proven crops with established markets
- Fast turnovers improve cash flow
- Diversification reduces risk
- Small experimental allocation for learning

## 5.3 Succession Planting Schedules

### The Continuous Harvest Model

Commercial operations must provide consistent supply to customers. Succession planting ensures continuous harvest.

**Succession Planting Principles:**

1. **Plant every week** (or at minimum every 2 weeks)
2. **Plant in batches** matching customer demand
3. **Maintain pipeline** of plants at all growth stages
4. **Buffer production** for quality issues or market fluctuations

### Succession Planning Example

**Scenario: Lettuce Production for Weekly Restaurant Deliveries**

```
Goal: Harvest 500 heads per week consistently

Growth cycle: 35 days
Plant spacing: 6 inches = 4 per sq ft
Success rate: 90%

Weekly planting needed:
500 heads ÷ 0.90 = 556 plants to start
556 ÷ 4 plants per sq ft = 139 sq ft planted per week

Total production space needed:
139 sq ft × 5 weeks in pipeline = 695 sq ft

Add 20% buffer: 695 × 1.2 = 834 sq ft minimum
```

**Weekly Planting Calendar:**

```
Week 1: Plant 556 lettuce seedlings (Batch A)
Week 2: Plant 556 lettuce seedlings (Batch B), Batch A @ 1 week
Week 3: Plant 556 lettuce seedlings (Batch C), Batch B @ 1 week, Batch A @ 2 weeks
Week 4: Plant 556 lettuce seedlings (Batch D), ongoing...
Week 5: Plant 556 lettuce seedlings (Batch E)
Week 6: HARVEST Batch A (500+ heads), Plant Batch F

Continuous cycle established!
```

### Multi-Crop Succession Planning

**Managing Multiple Crops Simultaneously:**

```
WEEKLY PLANTING SCHEDULE (Example)

Monday:
- Transplant 400 butterhead lettuce seedlings
- Transplant 200 basil seedlings
- Start 600 lettuce seeds in germination

Tuesday:
- Transplant 300 bok choy seedlings
- Quality check week-old transplants
- Harvest mature lettuce (previous planting)

Wednesday:
- Transplant 250 kale seedlings
- Start 400 basil seeds
- Clean and prep harvested areas

Thursday:
- Transplant 150 arugula seedlings
- Monitor all growth stages
- Adjust nutrient supplementation

Friday:
- Catch-up and system maintenance
- Prep for next week's plantings
- Inventory and order supplies
```

**Benefits of Structured Schedule:**
- Predictable workflow
- Consistent harvests
- Even distribution of labor
- Easier to manage with staff

## 5.4 Nursery and Transplant Management

### The Nursery Operation

Professional commercial operations run dedicated nursery areas for seedling production.

**Nursery Requirements:**

```
╔════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                        NURSERY SPECIFICATIONS                              ║
╠════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                            ║
║  SPACE ALLOCATION:                                                         ║
║  • Nursery should be 15-25% of total production area                       ║
║  • Example: 5,000 sq ft production = 750-1,250 sq ft nursery               ║
║                                                                            ║
║  GERMINATION AREA:                                                         ║
║  • Temperature: 70-80°F (heating mat recommended)                          ║
║  • Humidity: 70-90% (dome covers)                                          ║
║  • Lighting: Not critical (dark germination acceptable for most)           ║
║  • Duration: 2-5 days                                                      ║
║                                                                            ║
║  SEEDLING GROWTH AREA:                                                     ║
║  • Lighting: 12-16 hrs per day, 200-400 μmol/m²/s                          ║
║  • Temperature: 65-75°F                                                    ║
║  • Airflow: Gentle circulation for strong stems                            ║
║  • Nutrients: Half-strength aquaponics water or seedling fertilizer        ║
║  • Duration: 10-21 days to transplant size                                 ║
║                                                                            ║
╚════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### Transplant Timing Guidelines

| Crop | Days to Germination | Days to Transplant | Total Nursery Time |
|------|---------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Lettuce | 2-3 | 10-14 | 12-17 days |
| Basil | 5-7 | 14-21 | 19-28 days |
| Bok Choy | 3-5 | 12-16 | 15-21 days |
| Kale | 3-5 | 21-28 | 24-33 days |
| Tomatoes | 5-7 | 28-35 | 33-42 days |
| Peppers | 7-10 | 35-42 | 42-52 days |

**Transplant Size Indicator:**
- 2-4 true leaves developed (beyond cotyledons)
- Strong root system visible at bottom of plug
- Stem sturdy enough to handle without damage
- Height: 2-4 inches typical

## 5.5 Harvest Planning and Execution

### Quality Standards for Harvest

Commercial operations must maintain consistent quality standards.

**Lettuce Quality Standards:**
- Head size: 6-10 oz (varies by variety)
- Color: Vibrant, variety-appropriate
- Texture: Crisp, turgid leaves
- Defects: <5% damaged or diseased leaves
- Shelf life: 7-14 days refrigerated

**Harvest Timing:**
- **Too early:** Small size, lower revenue
- **Optimal:** Market size, peak quality
- **Too late:** Bolting risk, bitter flavor

### Harvest Workflow

**Pre-Harvest Preparation:**
1. Review customer orders
2. Prep harvest bins/packaging
3. Clean and sanitize harvest tools
4. Schedule harvest crew
5. Ensure cooler space available

**Harvest Execution:**
1. Harvest in cool part of day (early morning ideal)
2. Cut cleanly at base
3. Remove damaged outer leaves
4. Place in clean bins
5. Immediate cooling (within 30 minutes)

**Post-Harvest Handling:**
1. Wash/rinse if needed (or dry harvest for longer storage)
2. Spin dry if washed
3. Grade by size/quality
4. Package according to customer specs
5. Chill to 36-40°F
6. Record harvest data (count, weight, quality notes)

### Harvest Labor Efficiency

**Efficiency Targets:**
- Lettuce: 100-150 heads per hour (experienced harvester)
- Basil: 50-75 plants per hour (cut and bunch)
- Mixed crops: 40-60 lbs per hour

**Improving Harvest Efficiency:**
- Dedicated harvest crew (vs. multi-tasking)
- Optimized layout (minimal walking)
- Proper tools (sharp knives, comfortable bins)
- Standard procedures (consistent quality decisions)
- Pre-assigned zones (each person has area)

## 5.6 Crop Mix Strategy

### Financial Optimization

Different crops contribute differently to profitability. Strategic mix optimization is critical.

**Crop Profitability Analysis:**

```
CROP PROFITABILITY COMPARISON (per 100 sq ft, annual)

BUTTERHEAD LETTUCE:
- Production: 3,650 heads (10 cycles × 365 heads/cycle @ 0.92 success)
- Revenue: 3,650 × 0.375 lbs × $3.50 = $4,797
- Variable costs (seed, labor): $800
- Contribution margin: $3,997
- Margin %: 83%

BASIL:
- Production: 1,170 lbs (13 cycles × 90 plants/cycle × 0.125 lbs × 0.90)
- Revenue: 1,170 lbs × $6.00 = $7,020
- Variable costs: $1,200
- Contribution margin: $5,820
- Margin %: 83%

BOK CHOY:
- Production: 850 heads (10 cycles × 85 heads/cycle)
- Revenue: 850 × 0.5 lbs × $3.25 = $1,381
- Variable costs: $350
- Contribution margin: $1,031
- Margin %: 75%
```

**Analysis:**
- Basil generates highest revenue per sq ft
- Lettuce provides reliable, consistent income
- Bok choy fills niche but lower revenue density
- Consider market demand, not just revenue potential

### Seasonal Mix Adjustments

**Spring/Summer Strategy:**
- Increase heat-tolerant varieties
- Add fruiting crops (tomatoes, peppers)
- Reduce cold-weather crops
- Emphasize fresh, light greens

**Fall/Winter Strategy:**
- Increase hearty greens (kale, chard)
- Maximize lettuce production (holiday demand)
- Reduce basil (lower demand)
- Add Asian greens for soup/stir-fry demand

**Example Seasonal Rotation:**

```
SPRING (March-May):
- 50% Lettuce (spring mix varieties)
- 30% Basil (increasing for summer)
- 15% Arugula and specialty greens
- 5% Early tomatoes (transplanted)

SUMMER (June-August):
- 40% Lettuce (heat-tolerant varieties)
- 35% Basil (peak demand)
- 15% Tomatoes (fruiting)
- 10% Summer herbs (cilantro, parsley)

FALL (September-November):
- 55% Lettuce (holiday demand building)
- 20% Kale and chard
- 15% Basil (declining)
- 10% Asian greens

WINTER (December-February):
- 60% Lettuce (holiday and New Year demand)
- 20% Kale and chard
- 15% Bok choy and Asian greens
- 5% Microgreens (testing)
```

**Daily/Weekly Tracking:**

## 5.8 Customer Demand Planning

### Aligning Production with Sales

**The Cardinal Rule:** Grow what you can sell, not what you want to grow.

**Demand Forecasting Process:**

1. **Establish baseline orders:**
   - Survey existing customers for weekly needs
   - Identify seasonal patterns
   - Build order pipeline 4-6 weeks out

2. **Add growth projections:**
   - New customer acquisition rate
   - Existing customer growth
   - Conservative estimates (better to sell out than have waste)

3. **Calculate production needs:**
   - Customer demand + 15-20% buffer
   - Account for quality culls
   - Plan for sampling/demos

4. **Schedule plantings:**
   - Work backwards from delivery dates
   - Add buffer time for delays
   - Stagger plantings for continuous supply

**Example Demand-Driven Planning:**

```
Customer: Local Restaurant Chain (3 locations)
Weekly Order: 200 heads lettuce, 50 bunches basil

Production Plan:
- Plant 230 lettuce/week (15% buffer)
- Plant 60 basil/week (20% buffer for bushy plants)
- Delivery every Wednesday
- Plant on Mondays 5 weeks prior (lettuce)
- Plant on Mondays 4 weeks prior (basil)

Result: Consistent supply, customer satisfaction, minimized waste
```
