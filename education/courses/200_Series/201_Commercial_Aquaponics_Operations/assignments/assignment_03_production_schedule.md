## Purpose

Consistent weekly harvests are the foundation of commercial success. Customers expect reliability—restaurants need their basil every Tuesday, retailers want fresh lettuce every Thursday. This assignment teaches you to develop detailed production schedules that ensure continuous, predictable harvests while maximizing your growing space and revenue.

---

## Scenario

You are planning production for a 4,000 square foot growing area using NFT channels and DWC rafts. Your primary customers are:
- **2 high-end restaurants** (weekly orders, ~$800/week total)
- **1 food co-op** (bi-weekly deliveries, ~$1,200 every 2 weeks)
- **Farmers market** (Saturday mornings, variable ~$300-500/week)

They want a consistent mix of lettuces, herbs, and greens year-round.

## Part 2: Succession Planting Schedule (12 points)

### Step 3: Calculate Seeding Frequency

For continuous weekly harvests, you need to seed new plants regularly. Calculate how often to seed each crop.

**Formula:**
- **Seeding interval = 7 days** (for weekly harvests)
- **Number of batches in rotation = Days to harvest ÷ 7** (round up)

**Example:** Basil takes 35 days. 35 ÷ 7 = 5 batches needed.
So you need 5 separate batches at different growth stages, seeding a new batch every 7 days.

## Tips for Success

### Math Helpers
- Round up when calculating batches (can't have 4.5 batches!)
- If grow time is 38 days, round to 40 for easier math
- It's okay to harvest slightly more/less than exactly weekly

### Strategic Thinking
- **Faster crops (28-35 days) = quicker cash flow** but may be lower value
- **Herbs = higher $/lb** but weigh less per plant
- **Lettuces = steady demand** make good foundation crops
- **Diversity = risk management** if one crop has issues

### Common Pitfalls
- **Too much variety:** Focus is better than trying to grow everything
- **Not enough buffer:** Things go wrong—build in 10-15% extra capacity
- **Ignoring customer preferences:** Ask your customers what they actually want!
- **Forgetting propagation time:** Count days from seed, not transplant

### Real-World Application
If you're planning an actual business:
- Use YOUR actual customer commitments instead of the scenario
- Adjust for YOUR specific growing area and systems
- Research YOUR local market prices
- This becomes your actual production plan!
