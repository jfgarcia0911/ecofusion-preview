## Purpose

Numbers tell the story of business viability. Can your aquaponics operation actually make money? How long until you break even? What happens if yields are 20% lower than projected? This assignment requires you to build a comprehensive financial model that demonstrates you understand the economics of commercial aquaponics and can project realistic scenarios.

---

## Assignment Requirements

Build a financial model for a commercial aquaponics operation that includes:

1. **Revenue Projections** (monthly for Year 1, annually for Years 2-3)
2. **Operating Expense Projections** (monthly for Year 1, annually for Years 2-3)
3. **Profit & Loss Statement** (P&L)
4. **Cash Flow Forecast** (monthly for Year 1)
5. **Break-Even Analysis**
6. **Key Assumptions Documentation**

## Tips for Success

### Finding Realistic Numbers
- **Revenue:** Use conservative yield estimates (80% of theoretical max)
- **Prices:** Research YOUR local market—don't use national averages
- **Labor:** Don't forget payroll taxes (add 15-20% to wages)
- **Utilities:** Electricity is usually largest expense—calculate carefully
- **Contingency:** Add 10-15% to expense estimates for "other"

### Common Mistakes
- **Too optimistic on yields:** Things go wrong—plan for it
- **Forgetting ramp-up:** You won't be at 100% capacity on Day 1
- **Underestimating labor:** You'll work more hours than you think
- **No seasonality:** Most markets have seasonal price/demand variations

### Excel Tips
- Use separate tabs for Revenue, Expenses, P&L, Cash Flow
- Use formulas, not hard-coded numbers
- Color-code: Blue = input, Black = formula, Green = important result
- Include a Summary tab with key metrics
