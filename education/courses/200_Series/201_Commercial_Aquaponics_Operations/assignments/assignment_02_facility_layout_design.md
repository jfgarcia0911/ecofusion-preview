## Purpose

Facility layout is one of the most critical decisions in commercial aquaponics. A well-designed layout minimizes labor, prevents cross-contamination, improves efficiency, and accommodates future growth. A poor layout creates daily frustration, wastes time and money, and limits your operation's potential. This exercise helps you apply commercial design principles to create an efficient, scalable facility.

---

## Scenario

You are designing a commercial aquaponics facility with the following parameters:

### Given Information

**Facility:**
- **Total building size:** 10,000 square feet (100 ft × 100 ft)
- **Ceiling height:** 16 feet (suitable for vertical growth)
- **Type:** Retrofit warehouse or new pole barn construction
- **Access:** One overhead door (14 ft wide), one personnel door
- **Utilities:** All available (water, power, gas, internet)

**Production Goals:**
- **Primary products:** Leafy greens and herbs
- **Secondary product:** Tilapia (for nutrient production primarily)
- **Target:** 500-750 lbs produce/week at full capacity
- **System type:** Combination of DWC and NFT channels
- **Fish system:** ~2,000 gallon total fish tank volume

**Business Model:**
- Wholesale to restaurants and retail stores (requires post-harvest processing area)
- Some direct sales (small farm stand or pickup area)
- Educational tours monthly (requires safe visitor pathway)

## Design Requirements

Your layout must address these critical factors:

### Workflow Efficiency
- **Minimize walking distances** between frequently connected activities
- **Logical flow:** Germination → Growing → Harvesting → Processing → Cooler → Shipping
- **Reduce backtracking** and crossed paths
- **Accessibility** for equipment (carts, hand trucks)

### Food Safety
- **Separation** of clean and dirty areas
- **Designated pathways** preventing cross-contamination
- **Handwashing stations** strategically located
- **Controlled access** to production areas

### Operations Management
- **Visual oversight:** Ability to see most production from central points
- **Climate zones:** Group areas with similar environmental needs
- **Service access:** Equipment can be maintained/replaced
- **Emergency exits:** Multiple escape routes, clearly marked

### Scalability
- **Expansion capacity:** Room to add more production
- **Flexible design:** Can reconfigure without major renovation
- **Infrastructure:** Utilities positioned for growth
- **Modular approach:** Can grow in logical phases

## Tips for Success

### Before You Start
- Review Module 2 materials on facility design principles
- Study the case study examples provided in the course
- Sketch rough ideas before committing to final layout

### During Design
- Start with the biggest zones first (plant production)
- Think about daily worker movements—walk through the day mentally
- Consider seasonal changes (expansion for peak season)
- Don't forget aisles and circulation—these add up!

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Too much production space, not enough support:** Don't underestimate processing, storage, and workspace needs
- **Poor flow:** Requiring workers to constantly cross the facility
- **No expansion room:** Filling every inch leaves no growth capacity
- **Unsafe tour paths:** Visitors should see but not interfere with operations
- **Forgotten zones:** Restrooms, break areas, chemical storage often overlooked

### Space Allocation Guidelines

Based on industry norms for this size facility:
- **Production (fish + plants):** 50-60% of total
- **Post-harvest/processing:** 8-12%
- **Storage (dry + cold):** 8-12%
- **Support areas (office, maintenance, restroom):** 10-15%
- **Circulation (aisles, pathways):** 10-15%
- **Other (entrance, education):** 5-10%

## Example Workflow Considerations

### Daily Harvest Flow
Harvest cart → Wash station → Quality check → Packaging → Cooler → Staging for delivery
- **Design implication:** These stations should be adjacent or in sequence

### Seeding/Transplanting Flow
Seed storage → Germination trays → Transplant to system → Growing area
- **Design implication:** Propagation near both storage and production areas

### Fish Feeding Flow
Feed storage → Mix feeding cart → Fish tanks → Return cart → Clean cart
- **Design implication:** Feed storage near fish area but in dry location

### Visitor Tour Flow
Entrance → Safe viewing area → See fish tanks → See plant production → Exit through entrance
- **Design implication:** Designated pathway that doesn't cross production workflows

## Bonus Considerations (Optional)

If you want to go above and beyond, consider these additional elements:
- **Climate zoning:** Group areas by temperature/humidity needs to optimize HVAC
- **Lighting zones:** Arrange for efficient LED placement and electrical
- **Biosecurity:** Include footbaths, changing areas, controlled access
- **3D thinking:** Show vertical growing racks or stacked production
- **Rainwater:** Include collection and storage infrastructure
- **Solar:** Space/orientation for solar panels
