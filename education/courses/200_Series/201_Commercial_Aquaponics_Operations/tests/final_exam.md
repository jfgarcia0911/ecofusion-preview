### Section C: Short Answer

**26.** Any four of: Capital, Experience, Market, Location, Time, Team, Resilience

**27.** Linear flow prevents cross-contamination between dirty/clean areas, reduces wasted movement, improves efficiency, and supports food safety compliance by ensuring materials and products move in one direction from inputs to shipping.

**28.** Any three of: Start with proven crops, secure customers before building, build for reliability not innovation, control costs aggressively, develop multiple revenue streams, invest in automation early, focus on consistent quality over maximum quantity

**29.** Gross margin = (Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Revenue. It measures profitability after direct costs. Net margin = Net Profit ÷ Revenue. It measures profitability after ALL costs including operating expenses, and represents actual bottom-line profit.

**30.** Labor is the largest operating cost (35-45%), automation reduces variability and increases consistency, 24/7 monitoring prevents catastrophic losses, and early investment builds efficiency into operations from the start rather than retrofitting later.

### Section E: Case Analysis

**34a.** Red flags (any three):
- Insufficient experience (6 months hobby, need 2+ years)
- Inadequate market validation (only 1 interested restaurant)
- Unrealistic profitability timeline (8 months vs. 18-36 months typical)
- Overbuilding (15,000 sq ft before proving market)
- Limited customer base in rural location
- No team beyond owners

**34b.** Recommendations:
- Gain more experience (operate hobby system for another 12-18 months)
- Validate market by securing commitments from multiple customers before building
- Start smaller (5,000 sq ft) to prove concept, then expand
- Develop relationships with more potential customers
- Extend profitability expectations to 24-36 months
- Create financial cushion for extended startup period
- Connect with existing commercial operators for mentorship

**34c.** No, not realistic. Typical timeline to profitability is 18-36 months. Their 8-month target doesn't account for system cycling (1-2 months), learning curve, customer development time, and normal ramp-up period. Setting unrealistic expectations increases risk of premature failure.
