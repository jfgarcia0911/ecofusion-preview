## Introduction

**Why Use a Decision Matrix?**

Most beginners choose species based on emotion ("I like trout!") or single factors ("Barramundi has the best price!"). This leads to failures when reality doesn't match expectations.

A decision matrix forces you to:
- Consider ALL important factors objectively
- Weight criteria by importance
- Score each species honestly
- Let math reveal the best choice

**The Result:** You'll choose the species most likely to succeed in YOUR climate, with YOUR resources, for YOUR market.

---

## Step 1: Define Your Selection Criteria

### Core Criteria (Use These)

| Criterion | Weight (×) | Why This Weight |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Legal in Your State** | ×5 | Go/no-go factor - illegal = impossible |
| **Temperature Match** | ×4 | Can you maintain optimal temps year-round? |
| **Market Demand** | ×3 | Can you sell what you produce? |
| **Initial Cost** | ×2 | Affects cash flow and risk |
| **Growth Rate** | ×2 | Faster = quicker return on investment |
| **Difficulty Level** | ×2 | Match to your experience level |
| **Feed Availability** | ×1 | Can you get quality feed locally? |

**Total Possible Points:** Add up (Weight × 5 points max) = 95 points maximum

### Optional Additional Criteria

Add these if relevant to your situation:

| Criterion | Suggested Weight | When to Use |
|-----------|------------------|-------------|
| **Water Quality Tolerance** | ×2 | If you have challenging water (hard/soft, pH issues) |
| **Space Efficiency** | ×2 | If you have limited space |
| **Breeding Potential** | ×2 | If you want to produce your own fingerlings |
| **Market Price** | ×2 | If maximizing revenue per fish is critical |
| **Processing Difficulty** | ×1 | If you plan to process/fillet fish yourself |

---

## Step 2: Rate Each Species (1-5 Points)

**Rating Scale:**
- **5 = Excellent** - Best possible outcome
- **4 = Good** - Above average, minor concerns
- **3 = Acceptable** - Meets minimum requirements
- **2 = Poor** - Significant challenges
- **1 = Very Poor** - Major obstacle or dealbreaker

## Step 3: Complete Your Matrix

### Example Decision Matrix (Midwest Location)

| Criterion | Weight | Tilapia | Catfish | Trout | Barramundi | Goldfish |
|-----------|--------|---------|---------|-------|------------|----------|
| **Legal** | ×5 | 3 (restricted) | 5 (legal everywhere) | 5 (legal) | 5 (legal) | 5 (legal) |
| **Temperature Match** | ×4 | 3 (need heating) | 5 (perfect match) | 2 (need cooling) | 2 (need heating) | 5 (hardy) |
| **Market Demand** | ×3 | 5 (high demand) | 4 (good demand) | 5 (premium market) | 3 (emerging) | 3 (niche) |
| **Initial Cost** | ×2 | 4 ($0.75/fingerling) | 5 ($0.50/fingerling) | 4 ($0.75/fingerling) | 2 ($3/fingerling) | 5 ($0.25/fish) |
| **Growth Rate** | ×2 | 4 (6-9 months) | 3 (8-12 months) | 4 (6-8 months) | 5 (5-6 months) | 3 (4-6 months) |
| **Difficulty** | ×2 | 5 (easy) | 5 (easy) | 2 (advanced) | 3 (intermediate) | 5 (easy) |
| **Feed Availability** | ×1 | 5 (widely available) | 5 (widely available) | 4 (available) | 3 (specialty) | 5 (widely available) |

**Calculate Weighted Scores:**

| Species | Calculation | Total Score |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Tilapia** | (3×5)+(3×4)+(5×3)+(4×2)+(4×2)+(5×2)+(5×1) | **76/95** |
| **Catfish** | (5×5)+(5×4)+(4×3)+(5×2)+(3×2)+(5×2)+(5×1) | **92/95** |
| **Trout** | (5×5)+(2×4)+(5×3)+(4×2)+(4×2)+(2×2)+(4×1) | **72/95** |
| **Barramundi** | (5×5)+(2×4)+(3×3)+(2×2)+(5×2)+(3×2)+(3×1) | **65/95** |
| **Goldfish** | (5×5)+(5×4)+(3×3)+(5×2)+(3×2)+(5×2)+(5×1) | **84/95** |

**Winner for This Scenario:** Catfish (92 points)
**Runner-Up:** Goldfish (84 points)

**Interpretation:** Even though barramundi has the fastest growth and tilapia has highest market demand, catfish wins because it matches the Midwest climate perfectly, is legal everywhere, and is beginner-friendly. The matrix reveals the best OVERALL choice.

## Step 5: Reality Check Questions

Before finalizing your decision, honestly answer:

### Legal Reality
- [ ] I have verified this species is legal in my state
- [ ] I have checked local city/county ordinances
- [ ] I have confirmed my HOA allows aquaculture (if applicable)
- [ ] I understand permit requirements (if any)

**If ANY are unchecked, STOP and verify before proceeding.**

### Temperature Reality
- [ ] I can maintain optimal temperature year-round
- [ ] I have calculated heating/cooling costs
- [ ] I have backup heating/cooling systems planned
- [ ] Temperature control is financially sustainable

**If more than one is unchecked, consider a hardier species.**

### Market Reality
- [ ] I have confirmed demand with actual buyers
- [ ] I know realistic selling prices (not internet inflated prices)
- [ ] I have 2-3 potential sales channels identified
- [ ] I understand processing/delivery requirements

**If more than one is unchecked, do more market research first.**

### Financial Reality
- [ ] I can afford fingerlings for initial stocking
- [ ] I can afford 6+ months of feed before revenue
- [ ] I have emergency fund for equipment failure
- [ ] I have realistic profit expectations (not get-rich-quick)

**If more than one is unchecked, start smaller or delay start.**

## Common Decision Matrix Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Warm Climate (South/Southwest US)
**Top Scorers:** Tilapia (if legal), Catfish, Barramundi, Prawns
**Why:** No heating costs, year-round growing season
**Watch Out:** Cooling may be needed in peak summer

### Scenario 2: Cold Climate (North/Mountain States)
**Top Scorers:** Trout (if you can cool), Goldfish, Catfish (seasonal)
**Why:** Natural cooling, hardier species survive winters
**Watch Out:** Heating costs for warmwater species prohibitive

### Scenario 3: Limited Budget
**Top Scorers:** Goldfish, Catfish, Tilapia (where legal)
**Why:** Cheap fingerlings, affordable feed, forgiving
**Watch Out:** May sacrifice premium pricing for affordability

### Scenario 4: Maximum Profit Focus
**Top Scorers:** Barramundi, Trout, Koi (if skilled), Prawns
**Why:** Premium pricing justifies higher input costs
**Watch Out:** Higher risk, need precise management

### Scenario 5: Complete Beginner
**Top Scorers:** Goldfish, Catfish, Tilapia (where legal)
**Why:** Forgiving, widely available resources, proven markets
**Watch Out:** Don't start with advanced species (trout, prawns)

## Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake #1: Choosing Based on Single Factor
**Example:** "Barramundi has the best price, so I'll raise barramundi"
**Problem:** Ignores temperature needs, difficulty, fingerling cost
**Solution:** Use the full matrix, consider ALL factors

### Mistake #2: Ignoring Climate Reality
**Example:** "I'll raise trout in Texas by cooling the water"
**Problem:** Cooling costs make operation unprofitable
**Solution:** Weight temperature match heavily (×4 or ×5)

### Mistake #3: Assuming You Can Change Laws
**Example:** "I'll raise tilapia even though it's illegal - nobody will know"
**Problem:** Fines, shutdown, legal consequences
**Solution:** Legal status should automatically disqualify species (score = 0)

### Mistake #4: Overestimating Your Skill
**Example:** Beginner starts with prawns or trout
**Problem:** Advanced species require expertise you don't have yet
**Solution:** Be honest about difficulty level, start beginner-friendly

### Mistake #5: No Market Research
**Example:** "I'll raise it and they will come"
**Problem:** No buyers = no business
**Solution:** Confirm markets BEFORE selecting species

## Next Steps After Completing Matrix

1. **Verify Legal Status** (if you haven't already)
   - Contact state fish & wildlife department
   - Check local ordinances
   - Confirm permit requirements

2. **Find Fingerling Suppliers**
   - Locate 2-3 suppliers for your chosen species
   - Get quotes on fingerlings
   - Verify shipping options and costs

3. **Source Feed**
   - Find local feed suppliers
   - Get pricing on quality feed (correct protein %)
   - Verify availability year-round

4. **Finalize System Design**
   - Now that you know your species, optimize system for them
   - Calculate exact stocking density
   - Plan temperature control specifics

5. **Create Production Schedule**
   - Map out stocking dates
   - Calculate harvest dates
   - Plan batch rotation (continuous production)

6. **Develop Market Plan**
   - Contact potential buyers
   - Get commitments (if possible)
   - Plan processing/packaging
