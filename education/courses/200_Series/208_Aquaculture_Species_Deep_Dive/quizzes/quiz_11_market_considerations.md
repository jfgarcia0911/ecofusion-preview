## Questions

### Question 1
**Which sales channel typically offers the HIGHEST price per pound?**

A) Wholesale to grocery stores

B) Wholesale to restaurants

C) Direct to consumer ✓

D) Food distributors

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Direct to consumer channels (farmers markets, farm store, online direct sales, CSA) command the highest prices - often 2-3x wholesale. You keep all the margin but must handle marketing, packaging, and customer service yourself.*

---

### Question 2
**What price positioning do restaurants typically expect (wholesale)?**

A) Same as retail

B) Higher than retail

C) Lower than retail ✓

D) Varies randomly

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Restaurants buy wholesale at prices lower than retail (typically 40-60% of retail price). They need margin to prepare, serve, and profit. While per-pound price is lower, restaurants offer consistent volume and no individual packaging.*

---

### Question 3
**What does CSA stand for in marketing?**

A) Certified Sustainable Aquaculture

B) Community Supported Agriculture/Aquaculture ✓

C) Consumer Sales Agreement

D) Commercial Supply Alliance

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: CSA (Community Supported Agriculture/Aquaculture) is a model where customers pay upfront for a season's share of harvest. They receive regular deliveries (weekly/monthly). Benefits: guaranteed sales, upfront cash flow, loyal customer base.*

---

### Question 4
**What is the primary advantage of a CSA model?**

A) Highest price per pound

B) Upfront payment and guaranteed sales ✓

C) No packaging required

D) Lowest labor requirement

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: CSA's biggest advantage is upfront payment (cash flow to fund operations) and guaranteed sales (reduced risk). Customers share the risk - they pay before harvest. This is especially valuable for small-scale producers with limited capital.*

---

### Question 5
**What should small aquaculture operations NOT compete on?**

A) Quality

B) Freshness

C) Local production

D) Price with imports ✓

**Correct Answer: D**

*Explanation: Never compete on price with imports (Asian tilapia at $3-4/lb). You'll lose. Instead, compete on: freshness (hours vs. days old), local/traceable, superior quality, sustainability story, and customer relationship. Justify premium pricing.*

---

### Question 6
**What should small-scale producers emphasize to justify premium pricing?**

A) Price matching imports

B) Fresh, Local, Quality, Story ✓

C) Volume discounts

D) Convenience only

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Your competitive advantages are: Fresh (never frozen, harvested to order), Local (know your farmer, support local), Quality (superior to imports), and Story (sustainable, traceable, passion). Market these, not price.*

---

### Question 7
**How do value-added products affect profitability?**

A) Decrease profits

B) No effect

C) Increase profit margins ✓

D) Only increase costs

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Value-added products (smoked fish, fish tacos, prepared fillets, fish dip, etc.) command higher margins than whole fish. A $7/lb whole tilapia becomes $15-20/lb as smoked fillets or $8-10 as fish tacos. Processing adds value customers will pay for.*

---

### Question 8
**What is an advantage of selling at farmers markets?**

A) Lowest prices

B) No customer interaction needed

C) Higher prices and community building ✓

D) Bulk volume sales

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Farmers markets offer premium direct-to-consumer pricing AND build community connections. You tell your story, educate customers, get feedback, and create loyal customers who return weekly. Great for building a customer base.*

---

### Question 9
**What formula do restaurants use to set menu prices from wholesale cost?**

A) Wholesale cost × 2

B) Wholesale cost ÷ 0.30 (30% food cost) ✓

C) Wholesale cost + $5

D) Wholesale cost × 1.5

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Restaurants typically target 30% food cost, so menu price = wholesale cost ÷ 0.30. If you sell fish at $9/lb wholesale, expect it on menu at $30/lb ($9 ÷ 0.30 = $30). This helps you understand restaurant economics when negotiating.*

---

### Question 10
**What is the best scaling strategy for aquaculture operations?**

A) Build everything at once

B) Start at maximum capacity

C) Grow incrementally, don't skip stages ✓

D) Never scale up

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Start small, master the process, then scale incrementally. Don't jump from 100 fish to 10,000 fish - go 100 → 500 → 1,000 → 5,000. Each stage teaches lessons that prevent costly mistakes at larger scales. Slow growth = sustainable growth.*
