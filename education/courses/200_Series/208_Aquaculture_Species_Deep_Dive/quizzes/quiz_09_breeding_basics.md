## Questions

### Question 1
**Which fish species is easiest to breed in aquaponics?**

A) Trout (requires precise conditions)

B) Tilapia ✓

C) Barramundi (requires hormones)

D) Perch (slow, complex)

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Tilapia are the easiest fish to breed - they reproduce naturally every 4-6 weeks without hormones or special conditions. Males and females readily spawn at 6+ months old, with females mouthbrooding eggs. Some say tilapia will breed "in a bucket!"*

---

### Question 2
**How frequently do tilapia breed under optimal conditions?**

A) Once per year

B) Every 6 months

C) Every 2-3 months

D) Every 4-6 weeks ✓

**Correct Answer: D**

*Explanation: Tilapia can breed every 4-6 weeks year-round in optimal conditions (78-82°F). This frequent breeding is an advantage for fingerling production but a challenge in grow-out systems where you don't want reproduction (use monosex culture).*

---

### Question 3
**How long do goldfish eggs take to hatch?**

A) 1-2 days

B) 4-7 days ✓

C) 10-14 days

D) 3-4 weeks

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Goldfish eggs hatch in 4-7 days depending on temperature. Warmer water (70-75°F) = faster hatching (4-5 days). Cooler water (60-65°F) = slower hatching (6-7 days). Eggs are sticky and attach to surfaces.*

---

### Question 4
**What is the most critical factor during the first week for fish fry?**

A) Tank size

B) Water temperature

C) Proper food size ✓

D) pH level

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Proper food size is critical - fry mouths are tiny and can only eat microscopic food. Start with infusoria (microscopic organisms) or liquid fry food, then transition to baby brine shrimp. Using food too large results in starvation despite abundant feed.*

---

### Question 5
**What is infusoria?**

A) A type of fish disease

B) Microscopic organisms for first feeding ✓

C) A vitamin supplement

D) A type of fish egg

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Infusoria are microscopic organisms (protozoa, algae, etc.) that serve as first food for newly hatched fry. You can culture infusoria by leaving lettuce or hay in water in sunlight. It's essential for goldfish, koi, and other species with tiny fry.*

---

### Question 6
**What should fry eat immediately after absorbing their yolk sac?**

A) Regular fish pellets (too large)

B) Infusoria or liquid fry food ✓

C) Adult brine shrimp (too large)

D) Commercial flakes (too large)

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: After the yolk sac is absorbed (2-3 days post-hatch for most species), fry need microscopic food: infusoria or commercial liquid fry food. After 5-7 days, graduate to newly hatched baby brine shrimp, then fine crumbles.*

---

### Question 7
**What environmental triggers initiate breeding in temperate species like goldfish?**

A) Food quantity only

B) Water changes only

C) Temperature increase + photoperiod lengthening ✓

D) Moon phases

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Temperate species breed in response to spring signals: rising temperature (to 60-65°F+) and lengthening daylight (photoperiod). In controlled environments, you can simulate these conditions to breed year-round by manipulating temperature and lighting.*

---

### Question 8
**What is milt in fish breeding terminology?**

A) Fish eggs

B) Fish sperm ✓

C) Breeding tank water

D) Fry food

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Milt is fish sperm. In artificial breeding, milt is collected from males and mixed with eggs from females for fertilization. The term is used across all fish species.*

---

### Question 9
**What does "gravid" mean when describing a female fish?**

A) Sick or diseased

B) Carrying eggs/pregnant ✓

C) Aggressive

D) Molting

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: A gravid female is carrying eggs and ready to spawn. Visible signs include swollen abdomen and, in some species, a protruding ovipositor. Identifying gravid females is important for timing breeding activities.*

---

### Question 10
**When should beginners attempt breeding fish?**

A) Immediately when starting

B) After mastering grow-out ✓

C) Only after 5+ years

D) Never - always buy fingerlings

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Master grow-out (raising fish to market size) before attempting breeding. Breeding adds complexity: managing fry, sizing food, preventing cannibalism, and culling. Once you're profitable with grow-out, breeding can reduce fingerling costs.*
