## Questions

### Question 1
**Feed typically represents what percentage of total operating costs in aquaculture?**

A) 20-30% (too low)

B) 35-45% (still too low)

C) 50-70% ✓

D) 75-90% (too high)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Feed is the single largest operating expense at 50-70% of total costs. This is why optimizing Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) and avoiding waste is critical to profitability. Even small improvements in FCR significantly impact the bottom line.*

---

### Question 2
**What is the formula for calculating FCR (Feed Conversion Ratio)?**

A) Weight Gain ÷ Feed Fed

B) Feed Fed ÷ Weight Gain ✓

C) Total Fish Weight ÷ Feed Cost

D) Feed Cost ÷ Revenue

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: FCR = Feed Fed (kg) ÷ Weight Gain (kg). For example, if you fed 140 kg of feed and fish gained 100 kg, FCR = 140÷100 = 1.4. Lower FCR is better - it means less feed needed per pound of fish produced.*

---

### Question 3
**Which species has the BEST (lowest) FCR?**

A) Tilapia (1.4-1.6)

B) Catfish (1.5-1.8)

C) Rainbow trout (1.1-1.3) ✓

D) Prawns (2.0-2.5)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Rainbow trout have the best FCR at 1.1-1.3, meaning they convert feed to body weight most efficiently. This helps offset their higher feed costs. Barramundi (1.2-1.5) are close second among warmwater fish.*

---

### Question 4
**What protein percentage do carnivorous fish (like barramundi and trout) require?**

A) 20-28% (too low, for herbivores)

B) 28-35% (omnivores like tilapia)

C) 40-50% ✓

D) 60-70% (unnecessarily high)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Carnivorous fish need 40-50% protein. Trout require 40-45%, barramundi 42-48%. In contrast, omnivores like tilapia and catfish need only 28-32%. Higher protein feeds cost more but are essential for carnivore growth.*

---

### Question 5
**What protein percentage do omnivorous fish (like tilapia and catfish) require?**

A) 15-20% (too low)

B) 28-35% ✓

C) 40-45% (too high, wasteful)

D) 50-60% (way too high)

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Omnivores like tilapia and catfish need 28-35% protein, with 28-32% being optimal for most production. Using higher protein feeds (40%+) on omnivores wastes money without improving growth significantly.*

---

### Question 6
**When using visual feeding, when should you stop feeding?**

A) After exactly 5 minutes

B) When fish slow down consumption ✓

C) After 30 minutes

D) When all feed is gone

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Stop visual feeding when fish slow down or stop actively feeding, typically 10-15 minutes. Continuing to feed after this wastes feed and pollutes water. This is why "feed to satiation" means watching fish behavior, not a time limit.*

---

### Question 7
**What is the maximum recommended storage time for commercial fish feed?**

A) 3 months

B) 6 months ✓

C) 12 months

D) 24 months

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Store feed for maximum 6 months. After this, vitamins degrade, fats go rancid, and protein quality declines. Always check manufacture date, store in cool dry place, and use oldest stock first (FIFO - First In, First Out).*

---

### Question 8
**What are signs of bad/spoiled feed?**

A) Uniform color

B) Fresh smell

C) Mold, rancid smell, discoloration ✓

D) Firm pellets

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Bad feed shows mold growth, rancid/off smell, discoloration, or crumbling pellets. Never use spoiled feed - it causes poor growth, illness, and water quality problems. Proper storage prevents spoilage.*

---

### Question 9
**Should catfish receive floating or sinking feed?**

A) Floating only

B) Sinking ✓

C) Either works equally well

D) Slow-sinking only

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Catfish are bottom feeders and should receive sinking pellets. They naturally feed from the bottom and don't compete well for floating feed. In contrast, tilapia and barramundi do well with floating pellets.*

---

### Question 10
**How does temperature affect feeding frequency (up to the optimal temperature)?**

A) No effect on feeding

B) Lower temperature = more frequent feeding

C) Higher temperature = more frequent feeding ✓

D) Temperature only affects amount, not frequency

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Within the optimal range, higher temperatures increase metabolism, requiring more frequent feeding. For example, tilapia at 80°F eat 2-3 times daily, but at 70°F might only need 1-2 times daily. Above optimal temperature, reduce feeding due to stress.*
