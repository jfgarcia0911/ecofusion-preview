## Questions

### Question 1
**What is the number one cause of fish disease outbreaks?**

A) Parasites

B) Bacteria

C) Poor water quality ✓

D) Viruses

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Poor water quality (high ammonia/nitrite, low DO, wrong pH/temperature) is the root cause of 90%+ of disease problems. Stressed fish have compromised immune systems and succumb to pathogens that healthy fish easily resist. Fix water quality FIRST.*

---

### Question 2
**What is the recommended minimum quarantine period for new fish?**

A) 7 days

B) 14 days

C) 30 days ✓

D) 60 days

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Quarantine new fish for minimum 30 days in a completely separate system (no shared water). Many diseases have incubation periods of 2-3 weeks. This 30-day period allows time for symptoms to appear before introducing fish to your main population.*

---

### Question 3
**How long is the complete life cycle of Ich (white spot disease)?**

A) 1-2 days

B) 3-7 days ✓

C) 2-3 weeks

D) 1 month

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Ich completes its life cycle in 3-7 days (temperature dependent). Understanding this is crucial for treatment - you must treat for the full cycle to catch parasites when they're vulnerable (free-swimming stage). Partial treatment allows survivors to reinfect.*

---

### Question 4
**What are effective treatments for Ich?**

A) Antibiotics

B) Water changes only

C) Salt + heat, formalin, or copper ✓

D) Vitamin supplements

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Ich treatments include: 1) Salt (3-5 ppt) + raised temperature (accelerates life cycle), 2) Formalin (kills free-swimming stage), or 3) Copper sulfate. Treatment must continue for full life cycle. Antibiotics don't work - Ich is a parasite, not bacteria.*

---

### Question 5
**What are the primary symptoms of Columnaris?**

A) Red spots on fins

B) White/gray patches, cotton-like appearance ✓

C) Bloated abdomen

D) Black spots

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Columnaris (bacterial) appears as white or gray patches with a cotton-like texture, often around the mouth, fins, or gills. It spreads rapidly in warm water and poor conditions. Treatment includes antibacterials and immediate water quality improvement.*

---

### Question 6
**What are symptoms of Streptococcus in fish?**

A) White spots

B) Erratic swimming, spiraling, bulging eyes ✓

C) Clamped fins only

D) Faded colors

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Streptococcus causes neurological symptoms: erratic swimming, spiraling/whirling, bulging eyes (exophthalmia), and hemorrhages. It's a serious bacterial infection requiring antibiotics (if legal) or culling. Prevention through stress reduction is key.*

---

### Question 7
**How much cheaper is disease prevention compared to treatment?**

A) 2-5 times cheaper

B) 10-20 times cheaper

C) 50-75 times cheaper

D) 100+ times cheaper ✓

**Correct Answer: D**

*Explanation: Prevention is literally 100+ times cheaper than treatment when you factor in: medications, labor, mortality losses, reduced growth, market delays, and system downtime. The saying "an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure" severely understates the reality in aquaculture.*

---

### Question 8
**Which treatments are FDA-approved for food fish?**

A) Any medications from the pet store

B) All antibiotics

C) Salt, formalin, hydrogen peroxide, certain antibiotics ✓

D) Homeopathic remedies only

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Only specific treatments are FDA-approved for food fish: salt (sodium chloride), formalin, hydrogen peroxide (35%), and certain antibiotics with withdrawal periods (like oxytetracycline). Using non-approved drugs on food fish is a federal offense.*

---

### Question 9
**What is the withdrawal period for oxytetracycline antibiotic in food fish?**

A) 7 days

B) 14 days

C) 21 days ✓

D) 30 days

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Oxytetracycline requires a 21-day withdrawal period - fish cannot be harvested for food within 21 days of treatment. This allows the drug to clear from fish tissues. Always follow label withdrawal periods exactly to ensure food safety.*

---

### Question 10
**What is the best time to perform daily health checks?**

A) Early morning before sunrise

B) Feeding time ✓

C) Late at night

D) Randomly throughout the day

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Feeding time is ideal for health checks because fish are active and visible. Observe: feeding response (appetite), swimming behavior, color, breathing rate, and any abnormalities. Reduced feeding is often the first sign of problems.*
