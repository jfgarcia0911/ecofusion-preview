## Questions

### Question 1
**What is the purpose of a species decision matrix?**

A) To list all available species

B) Objective species evaluation using weighted criteria ✓

C) To calculate feed costs only

D) To design tank systems

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: A decision matrix provides objective species evaluation by scoring each species against weighted criteria (legal status ×5, temperature match ×4, market demand ×3, etc.). This removes emotion and ensures you choose the species best suited to YOUR situation.*

---

### Question 2
**Which criterion should receive the HIGHEST weight in a species selection matrix?**

A) Market price (×3)

B) Growth rate (×2)

C) Legal status (×5) ✓

D) Personal preference (×1)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Legal status must be weighted highest (×5) because it's a go/no-go factor. You literally cannot raise illegal species regardless of profitability. Temperature match (×4) and market demand (×3) follow, as these are also critical success factors.*

---

### Question 3
**A complete species project must include which components?**

A) Species selection only

B) Production plan only

C) Financial analysis only

D) All: selection, production, financial, and market plan ✓

**Correct Answer: D**

*Explanation: A complete project integrates all aspects: 1) Species selection (why this species), 2) Production plan (how to raise it), 3) Financial analysis (costs, revenue, profit), and 4) Market plan (how to sell it). Each component depends on the others.*

---

### Question 4
**What does break-even analysis show?**

A) Total revenue potential

B) Time to profitability ✓

C) Feed conversion ratio

D) Market share

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Break-even analysis shows when total revenue equals total costs - the point where you start making profit. It answers: "How many batches/months until I'm profitable?" This is critical for planning cash flow and investment decisions.*

---

### Question 5
**What is the purpose of a stocking schedule in your project?**

A) Record past harvests

B) Plan batch rotation for continuous harvest ✓

C) Track daily feeding

D) Monitor water quality

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: A stocking schedule plans when to add new batches so you have continuous harvest. Example: stock 100 fish monthly. After 6 months, harvest 100 monthly while restocking 100, creating steady cash flow instead of feast-or-famine.*

---

### Question 6
**What is a common mistake to avoid in project planning?**

A) Being too conservative

B) Unrealistic projections (overly optimistic) ✓

C) Including contingency funds

D) Researching market prices

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: The most common mistake is unrealistic projections: assuming 100% survival, best-case FCR, top-market prices, and no problems. Reality includes losses, setbacks, and learning curves. Use conservative estimates, especially for first projects.*

---

### Question 7
**When should market research be conducted?**

A) After building the system

B) After raising the first batch

C) Before species selection and production ✓

D) Only if sales are slow

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Market research MUST come before species selection and system design. There's no point efficiently producing fish nobody wants to buy. Research: who will buy, at what price, what quantities, what form (whole/filleted), before investing in production.*

---

### Question 8
**What contingency should be added to cost estimates?**

A) 5-10%

B) 15-20%

C) 30% ✓

D) 50%+

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Add 30% contingency to cost estimates for beginners. Things WILL go wrong: equipment failures, disease losses, learning curves, unexpected expenses. Experienced operators might use 15-20%, but beginners should budget conservatively at 30%+ buffer.*

---

### Question 9
**What is the FIRST step in species selection for a project?**

A) Calculate potential revenue

B) Design the system

C) Verify legal status ✓

D) Find fingerling suppliers

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Step 1 is ALWAYS verify legal status in your state/locality. All other planning is wasted if the species is prohibited. Check state fish & wildlife regulations, local ordinances, and HOA rules before proceeding.*

---

### Question 10
**What should the project conclusion assess?**

A) Historical fish prices only

B) Viability and outline next steps ✓

C) Other people's projects

D) Alternative business ideas

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: The conclusion should honestly assess: Is this project viable (profitable, legal, achievable)? What are the risks? What are next steps (permits, equipment, training)? Should you proceed, modify the plan, or choose a different species? Be objective.*
