# Water Quality Guide by Species
## Course 208: Aquaculture Species Deep Dive - Quick Reference

---

## Critical Parameters Summary

```
WATER QUALITY HIERARCHY
(In order of importance)

1. TEMPERATURE (species-specific)
   ↓
2. DISSOLVED OXYGEN (>4-6 mg/L minimum)
   ↓
3. AMMONIA (0 mg/L ideal, <0.5 mg/L max)
   ↓
4. NITRITE (0 mg/L ideal, <1 mg/L max)
   ↓
5. pH (7.0-8.0 for most species)
   ↓
6. NITRATE (20-100 mg/L optimal)
```

## Temperature Guide

### Warmwater Species (75-86°F)

**TILAPIA (Nile)**
```
CRITICAL TEMPS:
├─ <50°F → DEATH (within hours)
├─ 50-60°F → Survival only, no feeding, high stress
├─ 60-70°F → Slow growth, poor FCR
├─ 70-75°F → Fair growth
├─ 75-82°F → OPTIMAL ★★★
├─ 82-86°F → Good (watch DO closely)
├─ 86-90°F → Stress, reduce feeding
└─ >90°F → High stress, mortality risk

Target: 78-82°F
Backup heating: CRITICAL (redundancy required)
```

**BARRAMUNDI**
```
CRITICAL TEMPS:
├─ <65°F → Severe stress
├─ 65-75°F → Poor growth
├─ 75-80°F → Fair growth
├─ 80-86°F → OPTIMAL ★★★
├─ 86-90°F → Acceptable (monitor DO)
└─ >95°F → Critical stress

Target: 82-84°F
Notes: Prefers HIGHER temps than tilapia
```

**CHANNEL CATFISH**
```
CRITICAL TEMPS:
├─ <55°F → Minimal growth, reduced feeding
├─ 55-70°F → Slow growth (OK for overwintering)
├─ 70-75°F → Fair growth
├─ 75-85°F → OPTIMAL ★★★
├─ 85-90°F → Acceptable
└─ >90°F → Stress

Target: 78-82°F
Advantage: Survives cold better than tilapia
```

### Coldwater Species (45-65°F)

**RAINBOW TROUT**
```
CRITICAL TEMPS:
├─ <40°F → Slow growth
├─ 40-50°F → Fair growth
├─ 50-60°F → OPTIMAL ★★★
├─ 60-65°F → Acceptable (upper limit)
├─ 65-70°F → STRESS, disease risk
├─ 70-75°F → CRITICAL, stop feeding
└─ >75°F → LETHAL (death within hours)

Target: 55-60°F
Backup chilling: CRITICAL
Emergency plan: Mandatory
```

### Wide-Tolerance Species

**GOLDFISH/KOI**
```
SURVIVAL RANGE: 40-85°F
OPTIMAL: 65-75°F

Temperature Response:
├─ <50°F → Dormant, no feeding
├─ 50-60°F → Slow metabolism
├─ 60-70°F → Good growth
├─ 70-80°F → Peak growth
└─ >80°F → Reduce feeding

Advantage: Very forgiving!
```

## Dissolved Oxygen Requirements

| Species | Minimum Survival | Acceptable | Optimal | Notes |
|---------|------------------|------------|---------|-------|
| **Rainbow Trout** | 5 mg/L | 6 mg/L | 8+ mg/L | VERY sensitive |
| **Barramundi** | 4 mg/L | 5 mg/L | 6+ mg/L | High demand |
| **Tilapia** | 3 mg/L | 4 mg/L | 5+ mg/L | Fairly tolerant |
| **Channel Catfish** | 2 mg/L | 3 mg/L | 5+ mg/L | Most tolerant |
| **Prawns** | 3 mg/L | 4 mg/L | 5+ mg/L | Critical during molt |
| **Goldfish** | 2 mg/L | 3 mg/L | 5+ mg/L | Very tolerant |

**DO and Temperature Relationship:**
```
Water Temp    Max DO Capacity
──────────────────────────────
40°F          12.8 mg/L
50°F          11.3 mg/L
60°F          10.1 mg/L  ← Trout range
70°F           9.1 mg/L
80°F           8.3 mg/L  ← Tilapia range
90°F           7.6 mg/L

KEY INSIGHT: Warmwater fish in warm water = lower DO available
            This is why aeration is MORE critical in summer!
```

## Ammonia Toxicity

### pH Effect on Ammonia Toxicity

```
AMMONIA TOXICITY INCREASES WITH pH

pH    % Un-ionized NH3 (toxic form)
─────────────────────────────────────
6.0         0.3%    (mostly safe NH4+)
6.5         1.0%
7.0         2.7%
7.5         5.4%
8.0        10.5%    (significant toxicity)
8.5        19.0%    (very toxic)
9.0        32.0%    (extremely toxic)

RULE: Same total ammonia reading is MORE toxic at higher pH
```

### Species Ammonia Tolerance

| Species | Safe Level | Action Level | Lethal |
|---------|------------|--------------|--------|
| **Trout** | <0.1 mg/L | >0.5 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L |
| **Barramundi** | <0.25 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L | >2.0 mg/L |
| **Tilapia** | <0.5 mg/L | >2.0 mg/L | >5.0 mg/L |
| **Catfish** | <0.5 mg/L | >2.0 mg/L | >5.0 mg/L |

**Emergency Response for Ammonia Spike:**
1. STOP FEEDING immediately
2. Perform 25-50% water change with dechlorinated water
3. Increase aeration (add air stones)
4. Test every 4-6 hours
5. Don't resume feeding until <0.5 mg/L

## Nitrite Toxicity

### "Brown Blood Disease"

```
HOW NITRITE KILLS FISH:

Nitrite (NO2-) → Enters bloodstream
                 ↓
              Converts hemoglobin to methemoglobin
                 ↓
              Cannot carry oxygen
                 ↓
              Fish suffocates (even in high DO water!)
                 ↓
              Brown/gray gills (not red)
```

### Species Nitrite Tolerance

| Species | Safe Level | Maximum Acceptable | Dangerous |
|---------|------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Trout** | 0 mg/L | <0.1 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L |
| **Tilapia** | 0 mg/L | <1.0 mg/L | >5.0 mg/L |
| **Catfish** | 0 mg/L | <1.0 mg/L | >5.0 mg/L |
| **Barramundi** | 0 mg/L | <1.0 mg/L | >3.0 mg/L |

**Salt as Nitrite Protection:**
- Adding salt (1-3 ppt) blocks nitrite uptake
- Chloride (Cl-) competes with nitrite (NO2-) for gill absorption
- Emergency measure during cycling or spikes

## pH Management

### Optimal Ranges by Species

| Species | Optimal | Acceptable | Stress Zones |
|---------|---------|------------|--------------|
| **Tilapia** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 |
| **Catfish** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 |
| **Trout** | 7.0-7.5 | 6.5-8.0 | <6.0 or >8.5 |
| **Barramundi** | 7.5-8.5 | 7.0-9.0 | <6.5 or >9.5 |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 |

### pH Adjustment Guidelines

```
TO RAISE pH:
• Add potassium hydroxide (KOH) - slowly!
• Add potassium carbonate (K2CO3)
• Add calcium carbonate (CaCO3) - slow but safe
• Rule: Adjust max 0.2 pH units per day

TO LOWER pH:
• Let plants/biofilter work (natural acidification)
• Add weak acid (phosphoric preferred in aquaponics)
• Increase fish load (more CO2 = lower pH)
• Rule: Adjust max 0.2 pH units per day

CAUTION: Rapid pH swings kill fish!
         Slow and steady wins the race
```

## Hardness & Alkalinity

### Why It Matters

**Hardness (GH)** = Calcium + Magnesium
- Fish need calcium for bones, scales
- **Prawns/crayfish need HIGH calcium for shells**
- Plants need calcium and magnesium

**Alkalinity (KH)** = Buffering capacity
- Prevents pH crashes
- Stable system needs adequate alkalinity

### Requirements by Species

| Species | Hardness (GH) | Alkalinity (KH) | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Tilapia** | 50-300 mg/L | 80-150 mg/L | Flexible |
| **Catfish** | 50-300 mg/L | 80-150 mg/L | Flexible |
| **Trout** | 60-150 mg/L | 50-120 mg/L | Moderate |
| **Barramundi** | 50-200 mg/L | 60-120 mg/L | Moderate |
| **Prawns/Crayfish** | 100-300 mg/L | 100-200 mg/L | HIGH calcium essential! |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 50-300 mg/L | 80-150 mg/L | Very flexible |

**To Increase Hardness/Alkalinity:**
- Add calcium carbonate (limestone chips in filter)
- Add calcium hydroxide (carefully!)
- Use harder source water
- Add cuttlebone (especially for prawns)

## Emergency Action Levels

```
IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED IF:

🚨 Temperature:
   • Tilapia <55°F or >90°F
   • Trout >70°F or <40°F
   • Any species: >10°F change in 24 hours

🚨 Dissolved Oxygen:
   • Any species <3 mg/L
   • Trout <5 mg/L
   • Fish gasping at surface

🚨 Ammonia:
   • >2.0 mg/L (any species)
   • >0.5 mg/L (trout)

🚨 Nitrite:
   • >5.0 mg/L (any species)
   • >1.0 mg/L (trout)

🚨 pH:
   • <6.0 or >9.0 (any species)
   • Change >0.5 units in 24 hours

ACTION: Stop feeding, water change, increase aeration, identify cause
```

## Quick Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Action |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| Fish gasping at surface | Low DO | Increase aeration, check temp, reduce feeding |
| Fish lethargic | Ammonia/nitrite spike | Test, water change, stop feeding |
| Sudden deaths | Ammonia, temperature shock, disease | Test all parameters, check heater/chiller |
| Refusing food | Temp too high/low, poor water quality | Test parameters, adjust as needed |
| Red/inflamed gills | Ammonia burn, nitrite poisoning | Emergency water change |
| Brown/gray gills | Nitrite poisoning | Salt treatment, water change |
