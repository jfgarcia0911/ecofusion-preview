## Complete Water Quality Matrix

### Temperature Requirements

| Species | Survival Range | Optimal Growth | Critical Limits | Notes |
|---------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Nile Tilapia** | 60-90°F | 78-82°F | <50°F = DEATH | Die quickly below 50°F |
| **Blue Tilapia** | 50-90°F | 78-82°F | <40°F = DEATH | More cold-tolerant |
| **Channel Catfish** | 60-90°F | 75-85°F | <50°F = dormant | Very hardy |
| **Rainbow Trout** | 40-70°F | 55-60°F | >75°F = DEATH | Must keep cool |
| **Barramundi** | 70-95°F | 80-86°F | <65°F = stress | Needs warmth |
| **Yellow Perch** | 50-80°F | 68-72°F | >80°F = stress | Moderate temps |
| **Largemouth Bass** | 60-90°F | 75-80°F | >90°F = stress | Warmwater species |
| **Goldfish** | 40-85°F | 65-75°F | >95°F = stress | Extremely hardy |
| **Koi** | 40-88°F | 65-75°F | >90°F = stress | Very adaptable |
| **Freshwater Prawns** | 75-95°F | 82-86°F | <70°F = no growth | Tropical species |
| **Red Claw Crayfish** | 65-90°F | 75-82°F | >95°F = stress | Wide tolerance |

### pH Requirements

| Species | Optimal pH | Acceptable Range | Critical Levels | Tolerance |
|---------|-----------|------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Tilapia** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-9.0 | <5.5 or >10 | HIGH tolerance |
| **Catfish** | 6.5-8.0 | 6.0-9.0 | <5.0 or >10 | HIGH tolerance |
| **Trout** | 6.5-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 | LOW tolerance |
| **Barramundi** | 7.5-8.5 | 7.0-9.0 | <6.5 or >9.5 | MEDIUM tolerance |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.5 | HIGH tolerance |
| **Prawns** | 7.0-8.5 | 6.5-9.0 | <6.0 or >9.5 | MEDIUM tolerance |
| **Crayfish** | 7.0-8.5 | 6.5-9.0 | <6.0 or >9.5 | MEDIUM tolerance |

**Key Insight:** Most aquaponics systems naturally run 6.5-7.5 pH (compromise between fish and plants). Hardy species (tilapia, catfish, goldfish) thrive here. Trout prefer 7.0+.

### Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Requirements

| Species | Minimum DO | Optimal DO | Critical Level | Relative Need |
|---------|-----------|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| **Tilapia** | >3 mg/L | >5 mg/L | <2 mg/L = stress | MODERATE |
| **Catfish** | >2 mg/L | >4 mg/L | <1 mg/L = death | LOW (very tolerant) |
| **Trout** | >6 mg/L | >7 mg/L | <5 mg/L = stress | VERY HIGH |
| **Barramundi** | >5 mg/L | >6 mg/L | <4 mg/L = stress | HIGH |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | >4 mg/L | >6 mg/L | <2 mg/L = stress | MODERATE |
| **Prawns** | >4 mg/L | >5 mg/L | <3 mg/L = stress | MODERATE-HIGH |
| **Crayfish** | >3 mg/L | >5 mg/L | <2 mg/L = stress | MODERATE |

**Temperature Effect:** Warm water holds LESS oxygen
- 60°F water: ~10 mg/L max saturation
- 80°F water: ~8 mg/L max saturation
- Warm species (tilapia, barramundi) evolved to need less because warm water has less

### Ammonia (TAN - Total Ammonia Nitrogen) Tolerance

| Species | Ideal | Maximum Safe | Danger Level | Relative Tolerance |
|---------|-------|--------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Tilapia** | 0 mg/L | <1.0 mg/L | >2 mg/L | HIGH |
| **Catfish** | 0 mg/L | <0.5 mg/L | >1.5 mg/L | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| **Trout** | 0 mg/L | <0.1 mg/L | >0.5 mg/L | VERY LOW |
| **Barramundi** | 0 mg/L | <0.5 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L | MEDIUM |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 0 mg/L | <1.0 mg/L | >2 mg/L | HIGH |
| **Prawns** | 0 mg/L | <0.5 mg/L | >1.0 mg/L | MEDIUM |
| **Crayfish** | 0 mg/L | <0.5 mg/L | >1.5 mg/L | MEDIUM-HIGH |

**pH Effect:** Ammonia toxicity increases dramatically with pH
- pH 7.0: Ammonia mostly NH4+ (less toxic)
- pH 8.0: More NH3 (toxic form) present
- pH 9.0: Mostly NH3 (highly toxic)

Formula: % toxic NH3 = f(pH, temperature) - use ammonia calculator

### Nitrite (NO2) Tolerance

| Species | Ideal | Maximum Safe | Danger Level | Relative Tolerance |
|---------|-------|--------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Tilapia** | 0 mg/L | <5 mg/L | >10 mg/L | HIGH |
| **Catfish** | 0 mg/L | <5 mg/L | >10 mg/L | HIGH |
| **Trout** | 0 mg/L | <0.1 mg/L | >0.5 mg/L | VERY LOW |
| **Barramundi** | 0 mg/L | <1 mg/L | >3 mg/L | MEDIUM |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 0 mg/L | <5 mg/L | >10 mg/L | HIGH |
| **Prawns** | 0 mg/L | <1 mg/L | >3 mg/L | MEDIUM |
| **Crayfish** | 0 mg/L | <3 mg/L | >5 mg/L | MEDIUM-HIGH |

**Salt Treatment:** Nitrite toxicity can be reduced by adding salt (1-3 ppt). Chloride blocks nitrite uptake through gills.

### Nitrate (NO3) - "Good Nitrogen" for Plants

| Species | Ideal Range | Maximum Safe | Notes |
|---------|------------|--------------|-------|
| **All Fish** | 20-150 mg/L | <300 mg/L | Plants need 50-150 mg/L |
| **Trout** | 20-100 mg/L | <200 mg/L | Slightly more sensitive |
| **Prawns/Crayfish** | 20-150 mg/L | <300 mg/L | Same as fish |

**Aquaponics Sweet Spot:** 50-150 mg/L nitrate
- Enough for plants (they love nitrate!)
- Safe for all fish
- Indicates healthy nitrogen cycle

### Water Hardness (GH - General Hardness)

| Species | Optimal (mg/L CaCO3) | Acceptable Range | Special Needs |
|---------|---------------------|------------------|---------------|
| **Tilapia** | 50-300 | 20-500+ | Very flexible |
| **Catfish** | 50-300 | 20-500+ | Very flexible |
| **Trout** | 100-300 | 50-400 | Moderate hardness |
| **Barramundi** | 100-300 | 50-400 | Moderate hardness |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | 100-300 | 50-500+ | Flexible |
| **Prawns** | 100-300 | 50-400 | Need Ca for shells |
| **Crayfish** | 100-400 | 75-500+ | HIGH need for Ca |

**Crustacean Alert:** Prawns and crayfish NEED calcium (hardness 75+ mg/L minimum) for:
- Exoskeleton formation
- Successful molting
- Shell hardening

Add crushed coral, cuttlebone, or calcium carbonate if hardness <75 mg/L.

### Salinity Tolerance

| Species | Native | Freshwater OK? | Salinity Tolerance | Notes |
|---------|--------|---------------|-------------------|-------|
| **Tilapia** | Freshwater | YES | 0-15 ppt (some to 20 ppt) | Some salinity tolerance |
| **Catfish** | Freshwater | YES | 0-5 ppt max | Strictly freshwater |
| **Trout** | Freshwater | YES | 0-5 ppt max | Strictly freshwater |
| **Barramundi** | Euryhaline | YES | 0-35 ppt (full range!) | Can live in fresh or salt |
| **Goldfish/Koi** | Freshwater | YES | 0-8 ppt | Some tolerance |
| **Prawns** | Freshwater (adults) | YES | 0-5 ppt (adults), 12-16 ppt (larvae) | Larvae need brackish! |
| **Crayfish** | Freshwater | YES | 0-5 ppt | Strictly freshwater |

**Aquaponics Note:** Most systems run 0 ppt (pure freshwater) because plants don't tolerate salt. Low salinity (1-3 ppt) can be used for disease treatment if plants can handle it temporarily.

## Quick Reference: Species Hardiness Ranking

### MOST HARDY (Beginner-Friendly)
1. **Channel Catfish** - Tolerates poor DO, wide temp range, hardy
2. **Goldfish** - Extremely wide temperature range, very forgiving
3. **Tilapia** - High ammonia/nitrite tolerance (BUT temperature sensitive)
4. **Koi** - Wide temperature range, adaptable

### MODERATE HARDINESS
5. **Barramundi** - Good once temperature is stable
6. **Crayfish** - Forgiving but need calcium
7. **Blue Tilapia** - More cold-tolerant than Nile tilapia

### SENSITIVE (Advanced)
8. **Prawns** - Precise temperature, vulnerable during molting
9. **Rainbow Trout** - Low ammonia tolerance, must keep cool, high DO needs
10. **Yellow Perch** - Precise conditions, slow growth

## Critical Water Quality by Life Stage

### Fry/Fingerlings (All Species)
- **More sensitive** than adults to EVERYTHING
- Keep ammonia/nitrite at ZERO
- Maintain optimal temperature precisely (±2°F)
- Higher DO requirements
- Stable pH (no swings)

### Juveniles/Grow-out
- Standard requirements apply
- Most forgiving life stage
- Can tolerate short-term stress

### Spawning/Breeding
- Need OPTIMAL conditions
- Any stress reduces breeding success
- Precise temperature control
- Zero ammonia/nitrite
- High DO

### During/After Handling
- Stress = immune suppression
- Maintain perfect water quality for 48-72 hours post-handling
- Watch for disease outbreaks

## Emergency Action Levels

### Immediate Action Required (Next 1-2 hours):

| Parameter | Action Level | What to Do |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **DO** | <3 mg/L | Add emergency aeration, reduce feeding, check for system failure |
| **Temperature** | Outside survival range | Heat/cool immediately, reduce density if needed |
| **Ammonia** | >2 mg/L (most species) | 50% water change, stop feeding, check biofilter |
| **pH** | <6.0 or >9.0 | Gradual adjustment with buffers, investigate cause |

### Take Action Today (Next 12-24 hours):

| Parameter | Action Level | What to Do |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **DO** | 3-5 mg/L | Increase aeration, reduce feeding, check temperature |
| **Ammonia** | 0.5-2 mg/L | Water change, reduce feeding, check biofilter function |
| **Nitrite** | >1 mg/L | Water change, add salt (1-3 ppt), reduce feeding |
| **Temperature** | Suboptimal but survivable | Adjust heating/cooling, increase monitoring |

## Species-Specific Water Quality Checklists

### Tilapia System Checklist
- [ ] Temperature: 78-82°F
- [ ] pH: 6.5-8.5
- [ ] DO: >4 mg/L (>5 ideal)
- [ ] Ammonia: <1 mg/L (0 ideal)
- [ ] Nitrite: <5 mg/L (0 ideal)

### Trout System Checklist
- [ ] Temperature: 55-60°F (CRITICAL - never >70°F)
- [ ] pH: 6.5-8.0
- [ ] DO: >6 mg/L (>7 ideal)
- [ ] Ammonia: <0.1 mg/L (must be near zero!)
- [ ] Nitrite: <0.1 mg/L (must be near zero!)

### Barramundi System Checklist
- [ ] Temperature: 80-86°F (higher than tilapia!)
- [ ] pH: 7.5-8.5
- [ ] DO: >5 mg/L (>6 ideal)
- [ ] Ammonia: <0.5 mg/L (0 ideal)
- [ ] Nitrite: <1 mg/L (0 ideal)

### Prawn/Crayfish System Checklist
- [ ] Temperature: 80-86°F (prawns), 75-82°F (crayfish)
- [ ] pH: 7.0-8.5
- [ ] DO: >4 mg/L (>5 ideal)
- [ ] Hardness: >75 mg/L (>100 ideal) - CRITICAL!
- [ ] Ammonia: <0.5 mg/L (0 ideal)
- [ ] Nitrite: <1 mg/L (0 ideal)

## Testing Frequency Recommendations

### Daily (Critical Parameters)
- Temperature (check 2-3x daily)
- Dissolved Oxygen (especially morning, when lowest)
- Visual fish health check

### 2-3 Times Per Week
- pH
- Ammonia (if system <6 months old or stocked heavily)

### Weekly
- Nitrite
- Nitrate
- General fish behavior and appetite

### Monthly
- Hardness (GH/KH)
- Full water chemistry panel
- Calibrate instruments

### During Critical Periods (test daily):
- New system cycling
- After stocking new fish
- After disease treatment
- After equipment failure
- When fish show stress signs

## Water Quality Problem Solver

### Symptom: Fish gasping at surface
**Likely Cause:** Low dissolved oxygen
**Test:** DO, temperature, ammonia
**Solution:** Increase aeration, reduce feeding, check for system issues

### Symptom: Fish hanging at bottom, lethargic
**Likely Cause:** Ammonia or nitrite toxicity
**Test:** Ammonia, nitrite, pH
**Solution:** Water change, reduce feeding, check biofilter

### Symptom: Erratic swimming, flashing
**Likely Cause:** Parasites OR water quality stress
**Test:** Full panel (ammonia, nitrite, pH, DO)
**Solution:** Fix water quality first, then assess for parasites

### Symptom: Loss of appetite
**Likely Cause:** Temperature wrong, water quality, or disease
**Test:** Temperature, full chemistry panel
**Solution:** Correct temperature and water quality before treating disease

## Key Reminders

**Temperature is King:**
- Most critical parameter
- Affects: metabolism, DO solubility, ammonia toxicity, disease resistance
- Use heaters with thermostats and backup systems

**DO is Queen:**
- Second most critical
- Prevents most problems before they start
- More aeration = almost never wrong

**Ammonia/Nitrite = Death:**
- Keep at zero through proper biofilration
- Spike = emergency water change
- New systems need 4-6 weeks to cycle

**pH Stability > pH Perfection:**
- Stable pH of 7.0 better than swinging 6.5-8.5
- Gradual changes only (0.2 per day max)
- Most species adapt to non-optimal but stable pH

**Match Species to Your Water:**
- Don't fight your natural water chemistry
- If you have hard water (high GH), great for goldfish/koi/prawns
- If you have soft water (low GH), fine for catfish/tilapia
- Adjusting water chemistry = constant battle
