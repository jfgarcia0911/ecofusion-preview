## Lesson Content

### 4.1 Introduction to Coldwater Aquaponics

**The Premium Niche:**

Trout represent the **opposite** end of the spectrum from tilapia:
- Require COLD water (55-65°F optimal)
- Demand HIGH dissolved oxygen (>6 mg/L)
- Need EXCELLENT water quality (very sensitive)
- Command PREMIUM prices ($10-15/lb)

```
COLDWATER VS. WARMWATER COMPARISON

Parameter          TROUT              TILAPIA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Temp Optimum      55-65°F ❄️         78-86°F 🔥
DO Requirement    >6 mg/L            >4 mg/L
Water Quality     Pristine ✓✓✓       Tolerant ✓
Ammonia Tolerance VERY LOW           High
Growth Rate       FAST               Fast
Market Price      PREMIUM $$$        Standard $$
Difficulty        ADVANCED           Beginner
Climate           Cold/Controlled    Warm/Controlled
```

**Why Grow Trout?**
- Highest market price of common food fish
- Fast growth (6-8 months to 1 lb)
- Excellent eating quality
- Strong market recognition
- Low competition in aquaponics (few growers)

### 4.3 Critical Water Parameters

#### Temperature Management

```
TROUT TEMPERATURE TOLERANCE

  Growth
  Rate
    ^
    │        ╱──────╲
100%│       ╱        ╲
    │      ╱          ╲
 75%│    ╱              ╲
    │   ╱                 ╲

    │                                    ╲___[LETHAL]
  0%└┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────> Temp (°F)
    40   45   50   55   60   65   70   75   80

    SLOW    GOOD   OPTIMAL  STRESS  DANGER  DEATH
```

| Temperature | Response | Management |
|------------|----------|------------|
| **Below 40°F** | Slow growth, reduced feeding | Acceptable winter temps |
| **40-50°F** | Fair growth | Suboptimal |
| **50-60°F** | OPTIMAL GROWTH | Target range! |
| **60-65°F** | Acceptable | Upper limit for sustained growth |
| **65-70°F** | STRESS, reduced feeding | Emergency - cool water |
| **70-75°F** | High stress, disease risk | CRITICAL - immediate cooling |
| **Above 75°F** | LETHAL within hours | EMERGENCY |

**Critical:** Trout are OPPOSITE of tilapia:
- Tilapia die when it's too COLD
- Trout die when it's too WARM

---

#### Complete Water Quality Requirements

| Parameter | Optimal | Acceptable | Danger Zone |
|-----------|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 55-60°F | 45-65°F | >70°F or <40°F |
| **pH** | 7.0-7.5 | 6.5-8.0 | <6.0 or >8.5 |
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | >8 mg/L | >6 mg/L | <5 mg/L |
| **Ammonia (TAN)** | 0 mg/L | <0.1 mg/L | >0.5 mg/L |
| **Nitrite** | 0 mg/L | <0.1 mg/L | >1 mg/L |
| **Nitrate** | 20-80 mg/L | <150 mg/L | >200 mg/L |
| **Alkalinity** | 60-120 mg/L | 40-180 mg/L | <30 mg/L |

**Key Differences from Warmwater Fish:**
- ZERO tolerance for ammonia/nitrite spikes
- Require HIGH dissolved oxygen (cold water holds more O2)
- Sensitive to sudden changes
- Need excellent filtration

### 4.4 Water Chilling Systems

**The biggest challenge: Keeping water COLD**

#### Chilling Options

```
WATER COOLING METHODS

Method               Cost    Efficiency  Climate Limits
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Natural/Groundwater  $       Best        Cold climates only
                     (well)              (year-round cold)

Evaporative Cooling  $$      Good        Dry climates
```

**Chiller Costs:**
- 1/2 HP chiller: $1500-2500
- 1 HP chiller: $2500-4000
- 3 HP chiller: $5000-8000
- Operating cost: $50-150/month electricity (varies widely)

**Aeration Strategies for Trout:**

| Method | DO Added | Cost | Best For |
|--------|----------|------|----------|
| **Air stones** | Low | $ | Supplemental only |
| **Venturi injectors** | Medium | $$ | Good baseline |
| **Oxygen cones** | High | $$$ | Commercial systems |
| **Pure O2 injection** | Highest | $$$$ | Intensive production |
| **Water turbulence** | Medium | $ | Waterfalls, weirs |

**Recommended:** Combination approach
- Venturi on pump return
- Air stones as backup
- Water turbulence (waterfall effect)
- Pure O2 for high-density systems

### 4.6 Feeding & Nutrition

#### Trout Feed Requirements

**Trout are CARNIVORES:**
- Need 40-50% protein
- Require fishmeal-based diets
- Fast metabolism = frequent feeding
- Excellent FCR (1.1-1.3 - best of any fish!)

```
FEEDING SCHEDULE BY TEMPERATURE

Water Temp    Feeding Rate        Frequency
────────────────────────────────────────────────
40-45°F       0.5% body wt/day   1x daily
45-50°F       1.0% body wt/day   2x daily
50-55°F       1.5% body wt/day   2-3x daily
55-60°F       2.0% body wt/day   3x daily
60-65°F       1.5% body wt/day   2-3x daily
>65°F         Reduce/stop        Survival only

Note: Trout STOP FEEDING above 68-70°F (stress response)
```

**Feed by Life Stage:**

| Stage | Size | Protein | Feed Size | Rate (%BW/day) |
|-------|------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **Fry** | <2" | 50% | Starter crumble | 5-8% |
| **Fingerling** | 2-4" | 45% | 2mm pellet | 3-5% |
| **Grower** | 4-8" | 42% | 4mm pellet | 2-3% |
| **Finisher** | 8-12" | 40% | 5mm pellet | 1.5-2% |

**Trout Feed Brands:**
- Skretting (premium, expensive)
- Zeigler Bros (good quality)
- Rangen (Pacific Northwest standard)
- Silver Cup (economical)

### 4.8 Other Coldwater Species

#### Brook Trout (Salvelinus fontinalis)

```
BROOK TROUT PROFILE

Temperature:    45-60°F (even colder than rainbow)
Growth:         Slower than rainbow
Price:          Premium ($12-18/lb)
Market:         Niche, gourmet
Difficulty:     Higher than rainbow

Advantages:
• Beautiful coloration (mottled with red spots)
• Excellent flavor (many prefer to rainbow)
• Native to eastern US
• Tolerate even colder water

Disadvantages:
• Slower growth (12-18 months to market)
• More sensitive to warm temps (>65°F lethal)
• Smaller market recognition
• Harder to source fingerlings
```

#### Arctic Char (Salvelinus alpinus)

| Characteristic | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Temperature** | 50-60°F optimal |
| **Growth** | Moderate (similar to brook trout) |
| **Market** | Growing (Nordic cuisine trend) |
| **Price** | Very premium ($15-20/lb) |
| **Color** | Pink/orange flesh (like salmon) |
| **Advantage** | Salt-tolerant (can use saltwater) |

#### Atlantic Salmon (Salmo salar)

**NOT recommended for aquaponics:**
- Requires very large systems
- Anadromous (complex life cycle)
- High oxygen demands
- Regulatory challenges
- Better suited for ocean net pens

### 4.9 Health Management

#### Common Trout Diseases

| Disease | Cause | Symptoms | Treatment | Prevention |
|---------|-------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Whirling Disease** | Parasite | Spinning, deformed spine | None (cull) | Source certified stock |
| **Furunculosis** | Bacteria | Boils, ulcers | Antibiotics | Water quality, vaccines |
| **Ich** | Parasite | White spots | Formalin, salt | Temperature control |
| **Bacterial Gill Disease** | Bacteria | Gasping, gill damage | Antibiotics | DO, reduce density |
| **Enteric Redmouth** | Bacteria | Red mouth, hemorrhages | Antibiotics | Vaccines available |

```
TROUT HEALTH MONITORING

HEALTHY TROUT                 SICK TROUT
─────────────                ────────────

✓ Vigorous swimming          ✗ Lethargic, "tailing"
✓ Aggressive feeding         ✗ Refuses food
✓ Uniform school behavior    ✗ Isolated individuals
✓ Bright, silvery color      ✗ Dark, blotchy
✓ Clear eyes                 ✗ Cloudy, sunken eyes
✓ Smooth body                ✗ Lesions, hemorrhages
✓ Rapid response to stimuli  ✗ Slow reactions
✓ Pink/red gills             ✗ Pale or dark gills
```

**Trout Health Principles:**
- Prevention >>> Treatment
- Biosecurity is critical
- Source from certified disease-free hatcheries
- Quarantine all new fish (30 days minimum)
- Never mix stocks from different sources
- Vaccines available for some diseases

### 4.10 Economics of Trout Production

#### Cost Analysis

```
TROUT PRODUCTION COSTS (per 100 fish to 1.25 lb)

Input                Cost        Notes
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Fingerlings          $75         $0.75 each (3-4 inch)
Feed (175 lbs @      $140        FCR 1.4, high protein
$0.80/lb)
Chiller electricity  $180        $30/mo × 6 months
Backup O2            $50         Emergency system
Water testing        $25         Test kits
Miscellaneous        $30         Nets, meds, etc.
                     ─────
TOTAL COSTS:         $500

REVENUE:
100 fish × 1.25 lbs = 125 lbs
125 lbs × $12/lb = $1500

PROFIT: $1000 per batch (6-8 months)

BUT: Requires $3000-5000 infrastructure investment!
```

**Break-Even Analysis:**

| Scale | Infrastructure | Annual Production | Break-Even Time |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Small (500 gal)** | $5,000 | $2,000 profit/yr | 2.5 years |
| **Medium (2000 gal)** | $15,000 | $8,000 profit/yr | 2 years |
| **Commercial (10,000 gal)** | $75,000 | $40,000 profit/yr | 2 years |

**Key Economic Considerations:**
- HIGH initial investment (chilling equipment)
- HIGH operating costs (electricity)
- PREMIUM prices offset costs
- Niche market = limited volume
- Best for: Direct sales, restaurants, specialty markets
