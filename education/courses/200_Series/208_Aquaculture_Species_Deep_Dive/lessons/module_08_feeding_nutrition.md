### 8.3 Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)

**The Most Important Metric**

```
FCR CALCULATION

FCR = Total Feed Fed (dry weight) ÷ Weight Gain

Example:
Started with: 100 fish @ 50g each = 5 kg
Ended with: 100 fish @ 500g each = 50 kg
Weight gain: 45 kg
Feed used: 63 kg

FCR = 63 kg ÷ 45 kg = 1.4

Interpretation: 1.4 kg of feed produces 1 kg of fish
```

**Target FCRs:**

| Species | Excellent | Good | Poor | World Record |
|---------|-----------|------|------|--------------|
| **Trout** | <1.2 | 1.2-1.4 | >1.5 | 0.9 |
| **Barramundi** | <1.3 | 1.3-1.5 | >1.6 | 1.0 |
| **Tilapia** | <1.4 | 1.4-1.7 | >1.8 | 1.2 |
| **Catfish** | <1.5 | 1.5-1.9 | >2.0 | 1.3 |
| **Prawns** | <2.0 | 2.0-2.5 | >2.8 | 1.7 |

### 8.4 Feeding Schedules

#### Frequency by Species & Size

```
FEEDING FREQUENCY MATRIX

Life Stage       Tilapia    Catfish    Trout      Barramundi
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Fry             4-6x       4-6x       6-8x       4-6x
Fingerlings     3-4x       3-4x       4-6x       3-4x
Juveniles       2-3x       2-3x       3-4x       2-3x
Grow-out        2x         2x         2-3x       2x
Adults          1-2x       1-2x       2x         1-2x

Temperature Rule: Higher temp = more frequent feeding
                  (up to optimal range)
```

#### Feeding Rate Calculation

```
DAILY FEEDING RATE FORMULA

Daily Feed = (Biomass × Feeding Rate %)

Example: 1000 fish × 300g each = 300 kg biomass
         Feeding rate for juveniles = 3% body weight/day
         Daily feed = 300 kg × 0.03 = 9 kg/day

Divide across feedings:
Morning (40%): 3.6 kg
Midday (30%): 2.7 kg
Evening (30%): 2.7 kg
```

### 8.7 Feeding Management Best Practices

#### Visual Feeding Method

```
OBSERVE & ADJUST TECHNIQUE

Step 1: Start with calculated amount
        ↓
Step 2: Feed slowly, observe
        ↓
Watch for:
✓ Aggressive feeding = good
✓ Competition for food = good
✗ Slow pickup = overfeeding
✗ Food sinking uneaten = stop!
        ↓
Step 3: Stop when feeding slows
        ↓
Step 4: Record amount actually fed
        ↓
Step 5: Adjust tomorrow's ration
```

#### Common Feeding Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Overfeeding** | Poor water quality, wasted money | Feed to satiation, stop when slow |
| **Underfeeding** | Slow growth, cannibalism | Monitor growth rates |
| **Inconsistent schedule** | Stressed fish, poor FCR | Feed same times daily |
| **Wrong feed type** | Poor growth, disease | Match species requirements |
| **Old/spoiled feed** | Illness, poor FCR | Use within 6 months, store properly |

### 8.8 Feed Storage & Handling

```
PROPER FEED STORAGE

DO:                              DON'T:
✓ Cool, dry location            ✗ Direct sunlight
✓ Sealed containers             ✗ Open bags
✓ Elevated off floor            ✗ On damp concrete
✓ First in, first out           ✗ Mixing old + new
✓ Check for mold regularly      ✗ Use moldy feed
✓ Use within 6 months           ✗ Keep >12 months

SIGNS OF BAD FEED:
• Musty smell
• Discoloration
• Mold/fungus
• Insects
• Clumping
• Rancid odor (fats oxidized)
```

### 8.9 Troubleshooting Feeding Problems

```
PROBLEM SOLVING GUIDE

SYMPTOM: Fish not eating
Possible Causes:
→ Water quality issue (check ammonia, nitrite, DO)
→ Temperature stress (too hot or cold)
→ Disease (observe for other symptoms)
→ Overfeeding previously (skip 1-2 feedings)
→ Spawning behavior (seasonal)

SYMPTOM: Poor growth despite feeding
Possible Causes:
→ Wrong feed type (check protein %)
→ Old/degraded feed (vitamins lost)
→ Overcrowding (increase space or reduce stock)
→ Temperature suboptimal (adjust heater/chiller)
→ Disease (check health indicators)

SYMPTOM: High FCR (>2.0)
Possible Causes:
→ Overfeeding (reduce rations)
→ Low-quality feed (upgrade feed)
→ Temperature too low (increase temp)
→ Sick fish (treat disease)
→ Feeding wrong size pellets (adjust size)
```

### 8.10 Economic Analysis of Feed Choices

```
FEED COST vs. REVENUE ANALYSIS
(Example: Growing 100 tilapia to 1 lb)

SCENARIO A: Premium Feed ($1.20/lb)
Feed needed: 140 lbs (FCR 1.4)
Feed cost: $168
Growth time: 7 months
Fish value: $700 (100 lbs @ $7/lb)
Feed cost %: 24%

SCENARIO B: Economy Feed ($0.60/lb)
Feed needed: 180 lbs (FCR 1.8 - worse)
Feed cost: $108
Growth time: 9 months (slower)
Fish value: $700
Feed cost %: 15%

ANALYSIS:
Scenario A: 2 extra months to market = 2 extra months of expenses
           Higher FCR = wasted feed
           Sometimes "cheap" feed is expensive!

LESSON: Balance feed cost with FCR and time to market
```
