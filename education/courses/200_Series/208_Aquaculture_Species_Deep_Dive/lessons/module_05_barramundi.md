```

        Dorsal Fin (spiny front, soft rear)

            ┌┴──────────┐
           ╱             ╲
          ╱  ●            ╲═══════════╗

Physical Characteristics:
• Silver-gray body, darker back
• Large, upturned mouth (predatory)
• Deeply forked tail
• Can grow HUGE (100+ lbs in wild)
• Typical aquaponics harvest: 1-2 lbs
```

**Unique Characteristics:**
- **Protandrous hermaphrodite** (start as males, become females!)
  - Males: <2-3 years
  - Females: >3-4 years
  - All aquaponics fish are harvested as males
- Native to Indo-Pacific region (Australia, Southeast Asia)
- Tolerates both fresh and salt water (euryhaline)
- Highly predatory (carnivorous)
- Excellent swimmers (strong, fast)

#### Temperature - Critical Control Point

```

  Growth
  Rate

  0%│ ╱                                        ╲___
    └┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────┴────> Temp (°F)
    65   70   75   80   85   90   95  100  105  110

    STRESS  OK    GOOD  OPTIMAL GOOD  STRESS DANGER
```

| Temperature | Response | Management |
|------------|----------|------------|
| **Below 65°F** | Severe stress, immune suppression | Emergency heating needed |
| **65-75°F** | Poor growth, reduced feeding | Suboptimal |
| **75-80°F** | Good growth | Acceptable |
| **80-86°F** | OPTIMAL GROWTH | Target range! |
| **86-90°F** | Good growth, watch DO | Acceptable short-term |
| **90-95°F** | Stress, reduced appetite | Cooling recommended |
| **Above 95°F** | High stress, mortality risk | Emergency cooling |

**Critical Difference from Tilapia:**
- Barramundi prefer HIGHER temps (80-86°F vs. 78-82°F)
- Less cold-tolerant than tilapia
- More sensitive to temperature swings
- Require precise temperature control

---

#### Complete Water Parameters

| Parameter | Optimal | Acceptable | Action Level |
|-----------|---------|------------|--------------|
| **Temperature** | 80-86°F | 75-90°F | <70°F or >95°F |
| **pH** | 7.5-8.5 | 7.0-9.0 | <6.5 or >9.5 |
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | >6 mg/L | >5 mg/L | <4 mg/L |
| **Ammonia (TAN)** | 0 mg/L | <0.5 mg/L | >1 mg/L |
| **Nitrite** | 0 mg/L | <1 mg/L | >3 mg/L |
| **Nitrate** | 20-100 mg/L | <200 mg/L | >300 mg/L |
| **Salinity** | 0 ppt (fresh) | 0-15 ppt | >25 ppt |

**Special Note on Salinity:**
- Barramundi tolerate saltwater (up to 35 ppt)
- Freshwater is fine for aquaponics
- Some growers use low salinity (2-5 ppt) for disease control
- Plants limit salinity options

### 5.4 Feeding & Nutrition

#### High-Performance Diet Required

**Barramundi = Carnivore:**
- Need 45-50% protein (higher than tilapia)
- Require high-quality fishmeal
- Excellent feed conversion (FCR 1.2-1.5)
- Aggressive feeders

```
FEEDING SCHEDULE BY SIZE

Life Stage    Size        Protein  Feed Size    Rate (%BW/day)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Fry           <1"         55%      Powder       10-15%
Fingerling    1-3"        50%      Crumble      5-8%
Juvenile      3-6"        48%      3mm pellet   3-5%
Grower        6-10"       45%      5mm pellet   2-3%
Finisher      10-14"      42%      7mm pellet   1.5-2%
```

**Feeding Behavior:**

```
BARRAMUNDI FEEDING PATTERN

Surface Feeders (aggressive!)

Water Surface  [Feed floats here]
═══════════════════════════════════
         ╱╲    ← Barramundi strike upward
        ╱  ╲     (Unlike catfish)
       ╱ 🐟 ╲
      ╱      ╲

Feeding Tips:
• Use floating pellets
• Feed at same time daily (they learn!)
• Spread feed to reduce competition
• Remove uneaten feed after 15-20 minutes
• Multiple small feedings better than one large
```

**Feed Recommendations:**

| Brand | Protein | FCR | Cost | Notes |
|-------|---------|-----|------|-------|
| **Skretting** | 45-48% | 1.2-1.3 | $$$$ | Premium, best results |
| **Zeigler** | 45% | 1.3-1.4 | $$$ | Good quality |
| **Purina AquaMax** | 45% | 1.4-1.5 | $$ | Economical |
| **High-quality trout feed** | 45% | 1.3-1.5 | $$$ | Works well |

**Feed Cost Reality:**
- $1.00-1.50/lb for quality barramundi feed
- To produce 1 lb fish: 1.3 lbs feed = $1.30-2.00 feed cost
- Compare to tilapia: $0.70-1.00 feed cost per lb fish
- **Higher input costs balanced by higher selling price**

### 5.5 Stocking Density

```
DENSITY GUIDELINES FOR BARRAMUNDI

System Type        Density           Notes
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Backyard/Hobby    0.25-0.5 lb/gal   Conservative
                  (30-60 g/L)

Commercial        0.5-1.0 lb/gal    Standard production
                  (60-120 g/L)

Intensive         1.0-1.5 lb/gal    High aeration, expert
                  (120-180 g/L)

Example: 500-gallon tank (commercial density)
──────────────────────────────────────────────
Target: 0.75 lb/gal = 375 lbs biomass
Market size: 1.5 lbs each
Capacity: 250 barramundi at harvest
```

**Density Factors:**
- Higher oxygen demands than tilapia
- Aggressive behavior at high density
- Size variation increases with density
- Cannibalism risk if size differences >30%

**Managing Aggression:**
- Sort by size regularly (every 2-3 weeks)
- Provide adequate space
- Maintain consistent feeding schedule
- Reduce competition through multiple feed points
- Remove aggressive individuals

### 5.7 Health Management

#### Common Issues

| Disease/Issue | Cause | Symptoms | Treatment | Prevention |
|--------------|-------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Streptococcus** | Bacteria | Erratic swimming, hemorrhages | Antibiotics (if legal) | Reduce stress |
| **Columnaris** | Bacteria | White patches, fin rot | Antibiotics | Water quality |
| **Monogenean Flukes** | Parasites | Flashing, excess mucus | Formalin, salt bath | Quarantine |
| **Swim Bladder Disorder** | Dietary/stress | Floating/sinking | Adjust feeding | Quality feed |
| **Aggression Wounds** | Behavior | Bite marks, fin damage | Remove aggressor | Sort by size |

```
HEALTH MONITORING CHECKLIST

DAILY OBSERVATIONS:
□ Feeding response (should be aggressive!)
□ Swimming behavior (active, schooling)
□ Color (silver-gray, not dark/pale)
□ Mortality check (remove any dead immediately)

WEEKLY CHECKS:
□ Size variation (grade if >30% difference)
□ Fin condition (intact, no erosion)
□ Body condition (robust, not skinny)
□ External parasites

MONTHLY:
□ Growth rate (weigh sample of 10-20 fish)
□ FCR calculation
□ Water quality full panel
□ System maintenance
```

### 5.9 Other Premium Warmwater Species

#### Hybrid Striped Bass

```
HYBRID STRIPED BASS (Morone saxatilis × M. chrysops)

Temperature:    70-80°F
Growth:         Fast (8-12 months to 1.5 lbs)
FCR:            1.5-2.0
Price:          $8-12/lb
Market:         Strong (especially US East Coast)

Advantages:
• Fast growth
• Good market
• Hardy (tolerates wide conditions)
• Excellent eating quality

Challenges:
• Cannot reproduce naturally (sterile hybrid)
• Must purchase fingerlings annually
• Need high protein feed (carnivore)
• Can be aggressive
```

### 5.10 System Design for Barramundi

**Key Differences from Tilapia Systems:**

```
BARRAMUNDI SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS

Component          Specification           Vs. Tilapia
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Temperature        80-86°F stable          Higher (78-82°F)
Control            ±2°F variation          More precise

Aeration           >6 mg/L DO              Higher (>4 mg/L)
                   Venturi + airstones     More aeration

Filtration         High capacity           Higher waste load
                   Mechanical + bio        Better filtration

Tank Design        Smooth surfaces         Reduce aggression
                   Rounded tanks           injuries

Water Depth        4-6 feet                Deeper than tilapia
                                           (2-3 feet OK)

Heating            Precise control         Higher temps
                   Backup system critical  More critical

Feeding            Automated feeders       More frequent
System             3-4x daily              (2x for tilapia)
```
