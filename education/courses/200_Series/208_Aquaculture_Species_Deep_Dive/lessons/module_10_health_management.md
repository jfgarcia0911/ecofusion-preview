### 10.2 Disease Prevention

#### The 5 Pillars of Fish Health

```
1. WATER QUALITY (MOST IMPORTANT)
   ✓ Maintain optimal parameters
   ✓ Test regularly
   ✓ Act on early warnings
   Rule: 90% of disease starts with poor water quality

2. NUTRITION
   ✓ High-quality feed
   ✓ Proper storage
   ✓ Adequate vitamins
   Rule: Well-fed fish = strong immune systems

3. STOCKING DENSITY
   ✓ Don't overcrowd
   ✓ Provide adequate space
   ✓ Regular grading
   Rule: Stress = disease

4. BIOSECURITY
   ✓ Quarantine new fish
   ✓ Clean equipment
   ✓ Limit visitors
   Rule: Prevention beats cure

5. OBSERVATION
   ✓ Daily health checks
   ✓ Know what's normal
   ✓ Act early
   Rule: Early detection = easier treatment
```

### 10.3 Common Fish Diseases

#### Parasitic Diseases

**Ichthyophthirius (Ich / White Spot Disease)**

```
ICH IDENTIFICATION

Visual:        White spots (salt-like) on body/fins
Behavior:      Flashing (rubbing on surfaces)
               Rapid breathing
Progression:   Spots increase, fish weakens

LIFE CYCLE:
Adult parasite on fish (feeding)
    ↓
Drops off, forms cyst on bottom
    ↓
Cyst divides (100-1000 babies)
    ↓
Free-swimming stage (48 hours)
    ↓
Finds new host fish
    ↓
CYCLE REPEATS (3-7 days total)

KEY: Can only kill free-swimming stage!
     Must treat for full cycle
```

**Treatment Protocol:**

| Method | Dose | Duration | Effectiveness |
|--------|------|----------|---------------|
| **Salt + Heat** | 3-5 ppt salt, 85°F | 10-14 days | Good (85%) |
| **Formalin** | 25 ppm | 1 hour daily × 3 days | Excellent (95%) |
| **Copper** | 0.15-0.20 ppm | 10-14 days | Good (80%) |

⚠️ **CAUTION:** Remove plants before treating with salt/copper!

#### Bacterial Diseases

**Columnaris (Flavobacterium columnare)**

| Symptom | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Appearance** | White/gray patches on mouth, body, fins |
| **Behavior** | Lethargy, gasping |
| **Progression** | Cotton-like growths, fin erosion |
| **Triggers** | Stress, injury, poor water quality |

**Treatment:**
- Salt bath (5-10 ppt for 5-10 minutes)
- Antibiotics (oxytetracycline in feed if legal)
- Improve water quality
- Reduce stocking density

---

**Streptococcus**

```
STREPTOCOCCUS (Serious bacterial infection)

Symptoms:
• Erratic swimming, spiraling
• Bulging eyes (exophthalmia)
• Hemorrhages on body
• Sudden death in severe cases

Risk Factors:
• High water temperature (>85°F)
• High density
• Stress
• Poor nutrition

Treatment:
⚠ DIFFICULT - Often fatal
• Antibiotics (erythromycin, oxytetracycline) IF legal
• Cull severely affected fish
• Improve conditions
• Reduce stress

Prevention:
✓ Maintain temperatures in optimal range
✓ Avoid overcrowding
✓ Excellent water quality
✓ Consider vaccination (commercial operations)
```

---

#### Fungal Diseases

**Saprolegnia (Water Mold)**

```
FUNGAL INFECTION

Appearance:  Cotton-like white/gray tufts
Location:    Usually on wounds, damaged areas
Cause:       Secondary infection (after injury/stress)

Treatment:
1. Salt bath (10 ppt for 10-30 minutes)
2. Methylene blue (2 ppm)
3. Improve water quality
4. Address underlying cause (injury, parasites)

Prevention:
• Handle fish gently (avoid injuries)
• Maintain water quality
• Treat primary infections early
```

### 10.4 Health Monitoring Protocol

**Daily Observation Checklist:**

```
DAILY HEALTH CHECK (5-10 minutes)

FEEDING TIME OBSERVATION:
□ All fish actively feeding?
□ Aggressive competition for food?
□ Any fish hanging back?
□ Any food refusal?

VISUAL INSPECTION:
□ Any fish with abnormal coloration?
□ Any visible spots, lesions, or growths?
□ Are fins erect or clamped?
□ Any fish separated from school?

BEHAVIORAL CHECK:
□ Normal swimming patterns?
□ Any flashing (scratching)?
□ Any gasping at surface?
□ Any spiraling or erratic movement?

WATER QUALITY (Quick Check):
□ Water clarity good?
□ No unusual odors?
□ Equipment functioning?

MORTALITY:
□ Any dead fish? (Remove immediately!)
□ If yes, examine for cause

RED FLAGS (Act immediately):
🚨 Sudden death of multiple fish
🚨 Entire school gasping at surface
🚨 Widespread disease symptoms
🚨 Ammonia/nitrite spike
```

### 10.5 Quarantine Protocols

**New Fish Quarantine (MANDATORY)**

```
30-DAY QUARANTINE PROTOCOL

SETUP:
• Completely separate system (no shared water!)
• Dedicated equipment (nets, buckets)
• Hospital tank setup (bare bottom, heater, air)

PROCEDURE:

Day 0:   Receive new fish
         Observe for shipping stress
         Light feeding only

Day 1-3: Observe closely
         Monitor water quality
         Gradual feeding increase

Day 7:   First health inspection
         Look for parasites, disease signs
         Prophylactic salt bath (5 ppt, 10 min)

Day 14:  Mid-quarantine inspection
         Should be eating normally
         Any issues = extend quarantine

Day 30:  Final inspection
         If healthy = can introduce to main system
         If any concerns = extend or cull

NEVER mix quarantine water with main system!
```

### 10.6 Treatment Decision Matrix

```
WHEN TO TREAT vs. CULL

TREAT if:                        CULL if:
────────────                    ────────────

✓ Early stage disease           ✗ Advanced disease (>50% affected)
✓ Treatable condition           ✗ Untreatable/unknown disease
✓ Valuable fish (breeders)      ✗ Low-value fish (fingerlings)
✓ Isolated cases (few fish)     ✗ Widespread outbreak
✓ Legal treatment available     ✗ No approved treatment
✓ Fish still eating             ✗ Fish refusing food
✓ Economic to treat             ✗ Cost > fish value

CALCULATION:
Treatment cost per fish: $______
Fish value: $______
Survival probability: ______%

If (Fish Value × Survival %) > Treatment Cost = TREAT
Otherwise = CULL

Note: Sometimes culling sick fish prevents spread
      and is more humane than prolonged suffering
```

### 10.7 Approved Treatments (USA)

**FDA-Approved for Food Fish:**

| Treatment | Use | Withdrawal Period |
|-----------|-----|-------------------|
| **Salt (NaCl)** | Parasites, stress | 0 days |
| **Formalin** | External parasites | 0 days |
| **Hydrogen Peroxide** | Fungus, external parasites | 0 days |
| **Oxytetracycline** | Bacterial infections | 21 days |
| **Sulfadimethoxine/Ormetoprim** | Bacterial infections | 42 days |

⚠️ **CRITICAL:** Using non-approved drugs = ILLEGAL for food fish
   Can result in:
   - Product seizure
   - Fines
   - Criminal charges
   - Facility shutdown

**For Ornamental Fish (Not for human consumption):**
- Much wider range of treatments available
- Malachite green, copper sulfate, antibiotics, etc.
- NO withdrawal period needed (not food)

### 10.8 Necropsy (Fish Autopsy)

**Learning from Mortality:**

```
BASIC NECROPSY PROCEDURE

BEFORE YOU START:
• Fresh dead fish (within hours)
• Clean cutting board
• Sharp knife or scalpel
• Gloves
• Camera (document findings)

EXTERNAL EXAMINATION:
1. Overall body condition (thin, normal, bloated)
2. Skin (lesions, parasites, discoloration)
3. Fins (erosion, hemorrhage)
4. Eyes (cloudy, protruding, sunken)
5. Gills (color - should be red, not pale/gray)

INTERNAL EXAMINATION:
1. Make ventral cut from anus to gills
2. Observe organs:
   • Liver (should be dark red, not pale/yellow)
   • Spleen (small, dark)
   • Intestines (full? inflamed?)
   • Swim bladder (intact? discolored?)
   • Gonads (if mature)

COMMON FINDINGS:
• Pale gills = anemia, gill disease
• Pale liver = nutritional deficiency
• No food in gut = not eating (why?)
• Bloody intestines = bacterial infection
• Worms in body cavity = internal parasites
```

### 10.9 Species-Specific Health Issues

| Species | Common Issues | Prevention |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **Tilapia** | Streptococcus, trichodina | Avoid >85°F, reduce density |
| **Catfish** | Columnaris, ich | Good water quality, handle gently |
| **Trout** | Whirling disease, furunculosis | Source certified stock, vaccines |
| **Barramundi** | Streptococcus, monogenean flukes | Temperature control, quarantine |
| **Goldfish** | Ich, dropsy, fungus | Avoid temperature swings |
| **Koi** | Koi herpesvirus, anchor worm | Strict quarantine, biosecurity |
| **Prawns** | Bacterial shell disease, stress | Calcium, water quality, hides |
