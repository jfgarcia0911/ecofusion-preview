### 3.2 Channel Catfish Biology

#### Physical Characteristics

```
CHANNEL CATFISH ANATOMY

                    Dorsal Fin
                        │
      Adipose Fin      ┌┴┐
           │      ╱───┤ ├───╲
          ┌┴┐    ╱    └─┘    ╲
          └─┘ ╱ ●              ╲══════╗
     ╱╲╱╲╱  ╱  Whiskers         ║      ║ Tail Fin
    Barbels ║    (Barbels)       ║      ║ (Forked)
           ║                    ║      ║
            ╲                  ╱ ══════╝
     Pectoral Fin              Anal Fin
      (Has spine!)

Key Features:
• 8 barbels (whiskers) - sensory organs
• Forked tail fin
• Scaleless skin (slimy mucus layer)
• Sharp spines in dorsal and pectoral fins (⚠ can sting!)
• Small eyes (rely on barbels to find food)
```

**Adult Size:**
- Market size: 1-2 lbs (9-18 months)
- Can grow to 50+ lbs in wild
- Typical aquaponics harvest: 1.5 lbs

| Temperature | Response | Management |
|------------|----------|------------|
| **Below 55°F** | Minimal feeding, growth stops | Acceptable for overwintering |
| **55-70°F** | Slow growth | OK but sub-optimal |
| **70-75°F** | Good growth | Minimum for production |
| **75-85°F** | OPTIMAL | Target range |
| **85-90°F** | Acceptable | Monitor DO carefully |
| **Above 90°F** | Stress, reduced appetite | Cooling needed |

**Key Advantage:** Can survive cold snaps that kill tilapia!

---

#### Complete Water Quality Guide

| Parameter | Optimal | Acceptable | Action Required |
|-----------|---------|------------|----------------|
| **Temperature** | 75-85°F | 60-90°F | <55°F or >95°F |
| **pH** | 7.0-8.0 | 6.5-8.5 | <6.0 or >9.0 |
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | >5 mg/L | >3 mg/L | <2 mg/L |
| **Ammonia** | 0 mg/L | <1 mg/L | >2 mg/L |
| **Nitrite** | 0 mg/L | <2 mg/L | >5 mg/L |
| **Nitrate** | 20-100 mg/L | <200 mg/L | >400 mg/L |
| **Alkalinity** | 100-200 mg/L | 50-300 mg/L | <50 mg/L |

**Special Note on Dissolved Oxygen:**
Catfish can survive DO levels as low as 1-2 mg/L (tilapia cannot), but:
- Growth suffers below 3 mg/L
- Disease susceptibility increases
- Feed conversion worsens
- Aim for >5 mg/L for optimal production

### 3.4 Feeding & Nutrition

#### Feed Requirements by Life Stage

| Life Stage | Size | Protein % | Feed Size | Feeding Rate (%BW/day) |
|------------|------|-----------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Fry** | <1 inch | 45-50% | Powder | 10-15% |
| **Fingerling** | 1-3 inches | 38-42% | Crumble | 5-8% |
| **Juvenile** | 3-6 inches | 32-36% | 3/16" pellet | 3-5% |
| **Grow-out** | >6 inches | 28-32% | 5/16" pellet | 2-3% |

```
CATFISH FEEDING BEHAVIOR

Unlike tilapia (surface feeders), catfish are BOTTOM FEEDERS:

   Water Surface
   ═══════════════════════════════════
                                    [Floating pellets = Wasted]
                    [Mid-water = OK]

   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  Bottom
         🐟 Catfish feed here

   Best Feed Types:
   • SINKING pellets (not floating!)
   • Slow-sink pellets (acceptable)
   • High palatability (attract via barbels)
```

**Feeding Best Practices:**
- Use sinking or slow-sink pellets
- Feed in evening (catfish are nocturnal)
- Spread feed around tank (reduce competition)
- Remove uneaten feed after 1 hour
- Reduce feeding when temperature <70°F

**Catfish Feed Brands:**
- Purina AquaMax (industry standard)
- Cargill (commercial operations)
- Generic catfish pellets (budget option)
- Can use high-quality trout feed (expensive but works)

### 3.5 Stocking Density

```
STOCKING DENSITY BY SYSTEM TYPE

System               Density              Notes
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Backyard/Hobby      0.25-0.5 lb/gal      Conservative, forgiving
                    (30-60 g/L)

Commercial          0.5-1.0 lb/gal       High aeration required
(High management)   (60-120 g/L)

Research/Intensive  Up to 1.5 lb/gal     Pure oxygen, expert management
                    (180 g/L)

Example: 1000-gallon tank
──────────────────────────────
Conservative:  250 lbs = ~165 fish @ 1.5 lb each
Moderate:      500 lbs = ~330 fish @ 1.5 lb each
Intensive:     1000 lbs = ~660 fish @ 1.5 lb each
```

**Factors Affecting Catfish Density:**
1. **Oxygen availability** (most critical)
2. Filtration capacity
3. Experience level
4. Target market size
5. Water exchange rate

**Signs of Overstocking:**
- Fish gulping at surface (despite catfish tolerance)
- Barbel damage from abrasion
- Elevated ammonia/nitrite
- Slow growth despite adequate feeding
- Fin erosion

### 3.6 Reproduction & Breeding

#### Natural Breeding Behavior

Unlike tilapia, catfish breeding is **NOT a problem** in aquaponics:

```
WHY CATFISH DON'T OVERPOPULATE SYSTEMS

1. REQUIRE NESTING CAVITIES
   • Need containers, pipes, or structures
   • Will not spawn without proper sites

2. PARENTAL CARE
   • Male guards nest aggressively
   • Not compatible with production systems

3. EGG MASS PROTECTION
   • Eggs in gelatinous mass
   • Easy to spot and remove if desired

4. CONTROLLED CONDITIONS
   • Unlikely to breed in commercial stocking densities
   • Temperature/photoperiod manipulation prevents spawning

   Result: Breeding is typically NOT an issue
           (Unlike tilapia's constant reproduction!)
```

**If Breeding Occurs:**
- Remove eggs/nests
- Remove any nesting cavities
- Increase stocking density slightly
- Adjust photoperiod (less "natural" conditions)

### 3.7 Health Management

#### Common Diseases

| Disease | Cause | Symptoms | Treatment | Prevention |
|---------|-------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Columnaris** | Bacteria | Gray-white patches, fin rot | Antibiotics if allowed | Good water quality |
| **Ich** | Parasite | White spots | Salt (5 ppt), heat | Quarantine new fish |
| **Enteric Septicemia (ESC)** | Bacteria | Reddened fins, lethargy | Antibiotics (feed-based) | Reduce stress |
| **External Parasites** | Various | Flashing, excess mucus | Formalin, salt dips | Good biosecurity |

```
CATFISH HEALTH INDICATORS

HEALTHY CATFISH                SICK CATFISH
───────────────               ───────────────

✓ Active, especially evening  ✗ Lethargic all times
✓ Barbels intact, no fraying  ✗ Damaged, short barbels
✓ Smooth, slimy skin          ✗ Lesions, loss of slime coat
✓ Even, dark coloration       ✗ Pale or blotchy
✓ Feeds aggressively          ✗ Refuses food
✓ Erect fins                  ✗ Clamped, damaged fins
✓ No visible parasites        ✗ White spots, worms
```

**Catfish Health Advantages:**
- Generally disease-resistant
- Hardy to handling
- Tolerate salt treatments well
- Good survival rates with proper care

**Preventive Health Management:**
- Quarantine all new fish (30 days)
- Avoid sharp objects in tank (protect barbels)
- Maintain DO >3 mg/L minimum
- Feed high-quality diet
- Minimize handling

### 3.8 Growth Performance

#### Expected Growth Rates

```
CATFISH GROWTH TIMELINE (75-80°F optimal temp)

Month   Size        Weight    Cumulative Feed   FCR
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
0       Fingerling  50g       -                 -
3       Juvenile    150g      150g              1.5
6       Market      300g      375g              1.5
9       Market+     500g      675g              1.6
12      Large       750g      1075g             1.7

At market (1 lb / 454g):
• Time: 8-9 months
• Feed consumed: ~650g per fish
• FCR: ~1.5
```

**Growth Variables:**
- Temperature (75-85°F optimal)
- Feed quality and frequency
- Stocking density
- Genetics (select improved strains)
- Dissolved oxygen levels

**Performance Tracking:**

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **FCR** | Feed ÷ Weight gain | 1.5-1.8 |
| **SGR** | [(ln W2 - ln W1) ÷ days] × 100 | 1-2%/day |
| **Survival** | (Final ÷ Initial) × 100 | >85% |
| **Uniformity** | CV of weights | <20% |

### 3.11 Production Tips from Commercial Growers

**Expert Insights:**

1. **"Catfish grow best in the dark"**
   - Nocturnal feeders
   - Reduce light stress
   - Evening feeding = better consumption

2. **"Protect the barbels"**
   - Smooth surfaces only
   - No rough concrete
   - Barbel damage = reduced feeding

3. **"They'll survive low DO, but don't let them"**
   - Just because they can doesn't mean they should
   - Keep DO >5 mg/L for optimal growth

4. **"Bigger markets, smaller fish"**
   - 1-1.5 lb = sweet spot for most markets
   - 2+ lb harder to sell (too big for plate)

5. **"Genetics matter"**
   - Source from reputable hatcheries
   - Improved strains grow 20-30% faster
