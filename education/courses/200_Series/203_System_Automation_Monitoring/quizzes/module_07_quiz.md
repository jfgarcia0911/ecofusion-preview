## Answer Key

1. **b) Greenhouse controllers focus on climate (temp, humidity, CO₂, light); aquaculture controllers focus on water quality (DO, pH, ammonia)** - These are specialized for different applications. Greenhouse controllers manage environmental conditions for plant growth (climate, irrigation, lighting). Aquaculture controllers prioritize fish health and water quality (dissolved oxygen, pH, ammonia, temperature). Some systems can do both for aquaponics operations.

2. **d) $10,000-50,000+** - Professional-grade systems like Priva Connext are enterprise solutions for serious commercial operations (20,000+ sq ft). They include advanced algorithms, multi-zone control, weather integration, and comprehensive support. Entry-level systems are $100-500, mid-range are $2,000-8,000. The high cost reflects decades of development, reliability, and ongoing support.

3. **b) They provide proven reliability, warranty, technical support, and professional-grade components** - While DIY systems ($100-500) work for learning and low-risk applications, mid-range commercial controllers offer professional reliability, vendor support when problems arise, warranties that protect your investment, and components designed for 24/7 operation. For operations with significant revenue at stake, this support is worth the premium.

4. **b) Hardware, installation, training, maintenance, calibration, support contracts, and replacement costs** - TCO reveals the true cost over 3-5 years. A $2,000 system with $500/year support costs $4,500 over 5 years. A $5,000 system with minimal ongoing costs might cost $6,000 over 5 years. Include installation labor, training time, sensor replacements, calibration supplies, and potential service contracts when comparing options.

5. **c) Mid-range like Link4 ($3,000-8,000)** - For 10,000 sq ft, you have significant production value to protect but don't need enterprise features. Mid-range systems offer excellent reliability, expandability, and support at reasonable cost. DIY is too risky for this scale. Entry-level ($1,500-3,000) might work but leaves less room to grow. High-end ($10,000+) is overkill unless the operation is more complex or valuable.

6. **b) It's a cloud-based software platform that aggregates data from various sensors/controllers without being hardware-specific** - Agrilyst is software-as-a-service (SaaS) that works with existing hardware from multiple vendors. It provides centralized dashboards, production tracking, inventory management, and business intelligence across multiple facilities. Cost is $100-500/month. Unlike traditional controllers, it doesn't directly control equipment but focuses on data aggregation and analytics.

7. **b) They provide predictive analytics, yield prediction, and disease detection using computer vision and machine learning** - Motorleaf uses cameras and sensors combined with AI to predict yields before harvest, detect diseases early through visual analysis, and optimize climate settings based on machine learning. Cost is $5,000-15,000 hardware plus $200-1,000/month software. Best for high-tech commercial operations with resources to leverage advanced analytics.

8. **c) Reliability, redundancy, and 24/7 support for life-support systems** - For high-value aquaculture where $30,000+ in fish could die from equipment failure, reliability is paramount. Look for: redundant sensors, backup alarms, proven track record, 24/7 emergency support, and failsafe design. Companies like Pentair and AquaOptima specialize in mission-critical aquaculture systems. Features and cost are secondary to keeping livestock alive.

9. **b) Define requirements, research options, request quotes, pilot test if possible, and plan implementation** - Professional vendor evaluation follows a structured process: (1) Document your needs and constraints, (2) Research 3-5 options that fit your requirements, (3) Request detailed quotes including all costs, (4) If possible, pilot test in one zone before full deployment, (5) Plan implementation timeline with training and transition. This reduces risk and ensures you choose the right system.

10. **c) Prevented loss + 5 years of yield improvement: $15,000 + (5 × $16,000) = $95,000** - Benefits compound over time. One prevented crop loss = $15,000. Annual 8% yield improvement on $200,000 = $16,000/year × 5 years = $80,000. Total benefit = $95,000. Subtract the $8,000 system cost = $87,000 net benefit over 5 years, making it an excellent investment. This doesn't include labor savings or other operational improvements.
