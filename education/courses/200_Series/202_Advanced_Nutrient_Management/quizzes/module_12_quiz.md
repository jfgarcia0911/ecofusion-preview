## Questions

**1. What should be the first step in troubleshooting nutrient problems?**
   - a) Immediately add more nutrients
   - b) Carefully observe and document symptoms
   - c) Change everything at once
   - d) Give up and start over

**2. Before adding nutrients to correct a suspected deficiency, you should:**
   - a) Add nutrients immediately without testing
   - b) Check pH and nutrient availability first
   - c) Wait 6 months to see if it resolves
   - d) Remove all affected plants

**3. If you add iron but deficiency symptoms persist, you should:**
   - a) Add even more iron
   - b) Check pH—the iron may be present but unavailable
   - c) Conclude it's definitely not an iron problem
   - d) Change to a different crop species

**4. When multiple nutrient deficiencies appear simultaneously, this often suggests:**
   - a) Multiple unrelated problems
   - b) A root problem or pH issue preventing overall uptake
   - c) Bad luck
   - d) Normal plant behavior

**5. Lettuce tipburn occurring despite adequate calcium in the solution suggests:**
   - a) The calcium test was wrong
   - b) A transport/environmental issue (low transpiration, high humidity)
   - c) Laboratory error
   - d) An impossible scenario

**6. Systematic troubleshooting includes which sequence?**
   - a) Random guessing
   - b) Observe, gather data, test, analyze, prioritize, act, evaluate
   - c) Panic and overcorrect
   - d) Single-step diagnosis only

**7. Root disease in plants often causes:**
   - a) No visible symptoms
   - b) Nutrient deficiency symptoms due to impaired uptake
   - c) Better overall growth
   - d) Increased nutrient availability

**8. When multiple problems exist simultaneously, you should:**
   - a) Try to fix everything at once
   - b) Prioritize (life-threatening issues first, availability before addition)
   - c) Fix nothing until all problems are identified
   - d) Address problems in random order

**9. Yellow leaves appearing on older leaves first suggests deficiency of:**
   - a) An immobile nutrient (Ca, Fe, B)
   - b) A mobile nutrient (N, P, K, Mg)
   - c) Only a root problem
   - d) A light-related issue only

**10. After correcting a mobile nutrient deficiency, what improvement timeline is expected?**
   - a) Symptoms resolve within minutes
   - b) Symptoms stop progressing in days; some recovery of affected tissue possible
   - c) Immediate and complete recovery
   - d) No improvement is ever possible

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) Carefully observe and document symptoms** - Accurate diagnosis requires systematic observation before taking corrective action.

2. **b) Check pH and nutrient availability first** - Many deficiencies result from nutrients being present but unavailable due to pH lockout.

3. **b) Check pH—the iron may be present but unavailable** - Iron availability drops dramatically above pH 6.5; adding more doesn't help if pH is the problem.

4. **b) A root problem or pH issue preventing overall uptake** - Multiple simultaneous deficiencies often indicate a systemic issue rather than multiple individual problems.

5. **b) A transport/environmental issue (low transpiration, high humidity)** - Calcium moves with transpiration; environmental factors can limit transport even when calcium is present.

6. **b) Observe, gather data, test, analyze, prioritize, act, evaluate** - Systematic troubleshooting follows a logical sequence for accurate diagnosis and effective solutions.

7. **b) Nutrient deficiency symptoms due to impaired uptake** - Damaged roots cannot absorb nutrients effectively, causing deficiency symptoms even in nutrient-rich solutions.

8. **b) Prioritize (life-threatening issues first, availability before addition)** - Address critical problems first; fix availability issues before adding more nutrients.

9. **b) A mobile nutrient (N, P, K, Mg)** - Mobile nutrients can be translocated from older to younger tissue, causing older leaves to show symptoms first.

10. **b) Symptoms stop progressing in days; some recovery of affected tissue possible** - New growth improves first; existing damage may partially recover over time.
