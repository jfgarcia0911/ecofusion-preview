## Questions

**1. Water hardness measures the concentration of:**
   - a) pH
   - b) Calcium and magnesium ions
   - c) All dissolved minerals
   - d) Temperature

**2. Alkalinity measures:**
   - a) The same thing as hardness
   - b) The water's pH buffering capacity
   - c) Electrical conductivity
   - d) Nitrogen levels

**3. Reverse osmosis (RO) systems remove approximately what percentage of dissolved solids?**
   - a) 10-25%
   - b) 50-75%
   - c) 95-99%
   - d) Only bacteria, not minerals

**4. What is a safe sodium level in source water for most crops?**
   - a) 0 ppm only (no sodium allowed)
   - b) Less than 50-100 ppm is acceptable
   - c) 500+ ppm is fine
   - d) Unlimited sodium is acceptable

**5. Strawberries are sensitive to chloride. What is the recommended limit?**
   - a) Less than 1 ppm
   - b) Less than 50 ppm
   - c) Less than 500 ppm
   - d) Unlimited chloride is acceptable

**6. Water softeners typically replace calcium and magnesium with:**
   - a) Nothing (they just filter)
   - b) Sodium (which is problematic for plants)
   - c) Potassium
   - d) Iron

**7. Before formulating a custom nutrient solution, you should:**
   - a) Guess the water quality based on location
   - b) Test source water to know existing mineral content
   - c) Use any standard formula regardless of water
   - d) Add maximum nutrients to be safe

**8. If your source water contains 60 ppm calcium and your target is 160 ppm, you should add:**
   - a) 160 ppm of calcium
   - b) 100 ppm of calcium (subtract existing from target)
   - c) 220 ppm of calcium
   - d) No additional calcium is needed

**9. What filtration is recommended as a minimum for all growing systems?**
   - a) No filtration is needed
   - b) Sediment and carbon filters at minimum
   - c) Carbon filtration only
   - d) Sediment filtration only

**10. Blending RO water with tap water is:**
   - a) Never recommended
   - b) A cost-effective compromise for moderately hard water
   - c) Impossible to do correctly
   - d) Makes water quality worse

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) Calcium and magnesium ions** - Hardness is specifically the measure of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ concentrations, typically expressed as ppm CaCO₃.

2. **b) The water's pH buffering capacity** - Alkalinity measures bicarbonates and carbonates that resist pH changes, not the same as hardness.

3. **c) 95-99%** - Quality RO systems remove nearly all dissolved solids, providing near-pure water for precise nutrient formulation.

4. **b) Less than 50-100 ppm is acceptable** - While lower is better, most crops tolerate moderate sodium levels without significant problems.

5. **b) Less than 50 ppm** - Strawberries and other chloride-sensitive crops can show leaf burn and reduced yields with higher chloride levels.

6. **b) Sodium (which is problematic for plants)** - Ion-exchange softeners replace Ca/Mg with sodium, creating water unsuitable for growing systems.

7. **b) Test source water to know existing mineral content** - Accurate water analysis allows proper adjustment of nutrient recipes to account for background minerals.

8. **b) 100 ppm of calcium (subtract existing from target)** - Always subtract existing minerals from targets to avoid over-application and toxicity.

9. **b) Sediment and carbon filters at minimum** - Sediment removal protects equipment; carbon removes chlorine/chloramines that harm beneficial microbes.

10. **b) A cost-effective compromise for moderately hard water** - Blending reduces RO costs while still lowering problematic mineral concentrations.
