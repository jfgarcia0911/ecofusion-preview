## Questions

**1. Electrical conductivity (EC) measures:**
   - a) The pH level of a solution
   - b) Total dissolved salts/ions in the solution
   - c) Only nitrogen concentration
   - d) Water temperature

**2. What is the typical EC range for lettuce production?**
   - a) 0.5-0.8 mS/cm
   - b) 1.0-1.6 mS/cm
   - c) 3.0-4.0 mS/cm
   - d) 5.0+ mS/cm

**3. What is the typical EC range for tomatoes during the fruiting stage?**
   - a) 0.5-1.0 mS/cm
   - b) 1.0-2.0 mS/cm
   - c) 3.0-4.0 mS/cm
   - d) 10+ mS/cm

**4. If the EC in your reservoir is rising over time, this indicates that plants are:**
   - a) Using more water than nutrients (water loss concentrates solution)
   - b) Using more nutrients than water
   - c) Dying from disease
   - d) Growing perfectly

**5. If the EC in your reservoir is falling over time, this indicates that plants are:**
   - a) Using more water than nutrients
   - b) Using more nutrients than water (nutrient depletion)
   - c) Suffering from disease
   - d) Not growing at all

**6. High EC levels cause:**
   - a) Increased water uptake by plants
   - b) Osmotic stress and reduced nutrient/water uptake
   - c) Better growth in all situations
   - d) No effect on plant physiology

**7. Running tomatoes at higher EC typically results in:**
   - a) Larger but less flavorful fruit
   - b) Smaller but more flavorful fruit with higher brix
   - c) No fruit production
   - d) Root rot

**8. EC can be expressed in which units?**
   - a) mS/cm only
   - b) ppm only
   - c) CF only
   - d) All of these (mS/cm, ppm, CF)

**9. Because temperature affects EC readings, you should:**
   - a) Ignore temperature effects
   - b) Use temperature-compensated EC meters
   - c) Only measure EC at night
   - d) Never measure EC

**10. Aquaponic systems typically have what EC range?**
   - a) Very high (5+ mS/cm)
   - b) Moderate (2-3 mS/cm)
   - c) Low (0.5-1.2 mS/cm)
   - d) Zero (pure water)

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) Total dissolved salts/ions in the solution** - EC measures the solution's ability to conduct electricity, which increases with dissolved ion concentration.

2. **b) 1.0-1.6 mS/cm** - Lettuce is a moderate-EC crop that performs best with lower nutrient concentrations than fruiting vegetables.

3. **c) 3.0-4.0 mS/cm** - Fruiting tomatoes benefit from higher EC to improve fruit quality, flavor, and brix levels.

4. **a) Using more water than nutrients (water loss concentrates solution)** - When plants transpire more water than nutrients absorbed, the remaining solution becomes more concentrated.

5. **b) Using more nutrients than water (nutrient depletion)** - Actively growing plants taking up nutrients faster than water will deplete the solution concentration.

6. **b) Osmotic stress and reduced nutrient/water uptake** - High EC creates osmotic pressure that makes it harder for plants to absorb water and nutrients.

7. **b) Smaller but more flavorful fruit with higher brix** - Mild osmotic stress from higher EC concentrates sugars and flavor compounds in fruit.

8. **d) All of these (mS/cm, ppm, CF)** - EC is expressed in millisiemens per centimeter (mS/cm), parts per million (ppm), or conductivity factor (CF).

9. **b) Use temperature-compensated EC meters** - Temperature affects ion mobility; compensated meters adjust readings to a standard temperature (usually 25°C).

10. **c) Low (0.5-1.2 mS/cm)** - Aquaponic systems rely on fish waste for nutrients, resulting in lower EC than hydroponic systems with added fertilizers.
