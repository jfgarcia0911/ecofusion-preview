## Questions

**1. Why are commercial nutrient solutions typically provided in two-part formulations?**
   - a) They are cheaper to manufacture
   - b) To prevent calcium from precipitating with phosphates and sulfates
   - c) They are easier to mix
   - d) They taste better to plants

**2. What percentage of nitrogen does calcium nitrate provide?**
   - a) 5%
   - b) 11.9%
   - c) 25%
   - d) 50%

**3. What does a "100x stock solution" concentration mean?**
   - a) The solution is 100 liters total
   - b) Use 1 ml of stock per 1 liter of final solution
   - c) The solution is at 100% concentration
   - d) Mix the solution 100 times

**4. Monopotassium phosphate (MKP) provides which nutrients?**
   - a) Only phosphorus
   - b) Only potassium
   - c) Both phosphorus and potassium
   - d) Nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium

**5. When mixing a two-part nutrient solution, you should:**
   - a) Add Part A and Part B directly together before adding to water
   - b) Add each part separately to water, never mixing concentrates directly
   - c) Always add Part B first to the reservoir
   - d) The order and method doesn't matter

**6. Which acid is best for lowering pH while also adding phosphorus?**
   - a) Nitric acid
   - b) Sulfuric acid
   - c) Phosphoric acid
   - d) Citric acid

**7. Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) provides which nutrients?**
   - a) Only magnesium
   - b) Magnesium and sulfur
   - c) Only sulfur
   - d) Calcium and magnesium

**8. Why should micronutrient stock solutions be stored in dark bottles?**
   - a) For aesthetic purposes
   - b) Because micronutrients are light-sensitive and degrade in light
   - c) Dark bottles are cheaper
   - d) No storage precautions are needed

**9. What is the typical EC range for a general-purpose hydroponic solution?**
   - a) 0.5-1.0 mS/cm
   - b) 1.8-2.2 mS/cm
   - c) 5.0-6.0 mS/cm
   - d) 10+ mS/cm

**10. How should you modify nutrient formulation when transitioning plants to the fruiting stage?**
   - a) Increase nitrogen, decrease potassium
   - b) Decrease nitrogen, increase potassium
   - c) No changes needed during fruiting
   - d) Remove all nutrients and use plain water

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) To prevent calcium from precipitating with phosphates and sulfates** - Calcium reacts with phosphates and sulfates in concentrated form, creating precipitates that clog systems and reduce nutrient availability.

2. **b) 11.9%** - Calcium nitrate (Ca(NO₃)₂) contains approximately 11.9% nitrogen and 16.9% calcium.

3. **b) Use 1 ml of stock per 1 liter of final solution** - A 100x concentration means the stock is 100 times stronger than the final solution, requiring 1:100 dilution.

4. **c) Both phosphorus and potassium** - MKP (KH₂PO₄) provides 52% P₂O₅ and 34% K₂O, making it an efficient source of both nutrients.

5. **b) Add each part separately to water, never mixing concentrates directly** - Mixing concentrated Parts A and B directly causes precipitation. Each part should be diluted in water separately.

6. **c) Phosphoric acid** - Phosphoric acid lowers pH while contributing phosphorus to the solution, serving dual purposes.

7. **b) Magnesium and sulfur** - Epsom salt (MgSO₄·7H₂O) provides approximately 10% magnesium and 13% sulfur.

8. **b) Because micronutrients are light-sensitive and degrade in light** - Iron chelates and other micronutrients can break down when exposed to light, reducing their effectiveness.

9. **b) 1.8-2.2 mS/cm** - This range is typical for most vegetable crops and provides adequate nutrient concentration without causing osmotic stress.

10. **b) Decrease nitrogen, increase potassium** - Fruiting plants need less vegetative growth (nitrogen) and more support for fruit development and quality (potassium).
