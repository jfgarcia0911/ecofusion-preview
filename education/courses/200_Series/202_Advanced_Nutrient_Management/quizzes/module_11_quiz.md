## Questions

**1. Plant tissue analysis shows:**
   - a) What nutrients are currently in the solution
   - b) What nutrients the plant has actually absorbed
   - c) Future growth potential
   - d) Only pH levels

**2. Solution analysis shows:**
   - a) What nutrients the plant has absorbed
   - b) What nutrients are available in the solution
   - c) Plant tissue nutrient levels
   - d) Nothing useful for management

**3. How frequently should pH and EC be tested in production systems?**
   - a) Yearly
   - b) Monthly
   - c) Daily
   - d) Never

**4. A SPAD meter measures:**
   - a) Electrical conductivity
   - b) Chlorophyll content (which indicates nitrogen status)
   - c) pH levels
   - d) Water temperature

**5. When sampling plant tissue for analysis, you should:**
   - a) Sample diseased or damaged plants
   - b) Sample healthy, representative plants mid-morning
   - c) Sample any plant at any time
   - d) Never sample tissue

**6. If reservoir EC is rising over time, you should:**
   - a) Add more nutrients
   - b) Add water to dilute (plants using more water than nutrients)
   - c) Change to a different crop
   - d) Ignore the change

**7. If reservoir EC is falling over time, you should:**
   - a) Add only water
   - b) Add nutrients (plants depleting nutrients faster than water)
   - c) Check for pH problems only
   - d) Harvest immediately

**8. What is the typical cost range for a basic nutrient solution test?**
   - a) Free
   - b) $25-100 depending on the analysis panel
   - c) $1,000+
   - d) Testing services don't exist

**9. Tissue testing is most valuable for:**
   - a) Nothing practical
   - b) Confirming deficiencies and enabling precision nutrient management
   - c) pH measurement only
   - d) Decorative purposes

**10. Tracking data trends over time helps:**
   - a) Waste valuable production time
   - b) Predict needs, optimize management, and identify problems early
   - c) Confuse operators with too much information
   - d) Nothing useful

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) What nutrients the plant has actually absorbed** - Tissue analysis reveals actual nutrient uptake and accumulation in plant material.

2. **b) What nutrients are available in the solution** - Solution testing shows what's present in the growing solution, not what plants have taken up.

3. **c) Daily** - Regular monitoring catches problems early before they affect crop quality or yield.

4. **b) Chlorophyll content (which indicates nitrogen status)** - SPAD meters use light transmission to estimate chlorophyll, which correlates with nitrogen nutrition.

5. **b) Sample healthy, representative plants mid-morning** - Representative sampling from healthy plants mid-morning provides accurate baseline data for management decisions.

6. **b) Add water to dilute (plants using more water than nutrients)** - Rising EC indicates water loss exceeds nutrient uptake; add water to restore target concentration.

7. **b) Add nutrients (plants depleting nutrients faster than water)** - Falling EC indicates high nutrient demand; supplement to maintain target levels.

8. **b) $25-100 depending on the analysis panel** - Basic panels cost less; comprehensive analyses with micronutrients cost more but provide better information.

9. **b) Confirming deficiencies and enabling precision nutrient management** - Tissue analysis confirms visual diagnoses and enables data-driven nutrient optimization.

10. **b) Predict needs, optimize management, and identify problems early** - Historical data reveals patterns, enabling proactive rather than reactive management.
