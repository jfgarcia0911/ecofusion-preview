# Quick Reference: Nutrient Deficiency Visual Guide

**Course 202: Advanced Nutrient Management**

---

## Mobile Nutrients (Deficiency appears on OLD leaves first)

### Nitrogen (N)
- **Symptom:** General yellowing of entire older leaves
- **Pattern:** Uniform chlorosis, progressing from bottom up
- **Other signs:** Stunted growth, pale green overall
- **Quick fix:** Add N source (calcium nitrate, potassium nitrate)
- **Target:** 150-200 ppm (hydro), 40-80 ppm (aqua)

### Phosphorus (P)
- **Symptom:** Dark purple/blue-green leaves
- **Pattern:** Purple tints on stems and leaf undersides
- **Other signs:** Stunted, compact growth
- **Quick fix:** Add MKP or phosphoric acid
- **Target:** 30-80 ppm

### Potassium (K)
- **Symptom:** Marginal chlorosis and necrosis (scorched edges)
- **Pattern:** Edges yellow then brown and crispy
- **Other signs:** Weak stems, poor fruit quality
- **Quick fix:** Add K₂SO₄ or KNO₃
- **Target:** 200-400 ppm (varies by crop)

### Magnesium (Mg)
- **Symptom:** Interveinal chlorosis with GREEN veins
- **Pattern:** "Christmas tree" pattern on older leaves
- **Other signs:** Leaf edges may curl upward
- **Quick fix:** Add Epsom salt (MgSO₄)
- **Target:** 40-75 ppm

### Molybdenum (Mo)
- **Symptom:** Similar to N (general yellowing)
- **Pattern:** Leaf cupping, "whiptail" in brassicas
- **Other signs:** Check N levels (if high but plants yellow, suspect Mo)
- **Quick fix:** Add sodium molybdate, raise pH
- **Target:** 0.05-0.1 ppm

## Immobile Nutrients (Deficiency appears on NEW leaves/growing points first)

### Calcium (Ca)
- **Symptom:** Death of growing points, tipburn
- **Pattern:** Brown tips on young lettuce leaves, blossom end rot on fruits
- **Other signs:** Deformed new growth, poor root development
- **Quick fix:** Add Ca(NO₃)₂ or CaCl₂, foliar spray
- **Target:** 150-250 ppm
- **Note:** Often a TRANSPORT issue, not deficiency

### Sulfur (S)
- **Symptom:** General chlorosis of YOUNG leaves
- **Pattern:** Similar to N but affects new growth
- **Other signs:** Thin stems, stunted growth
- **Quick fix:** Add MgSO₄ or K₂SO₄
- **Target:** 50-150 ppm

### Iron (Fe)
- **Symptom:** Interveinal chlorosis on NEW leaves
- **Pattern:** DISTINCT net-vein or fishbone pattern
- **Other signs:** Severe cases show white/bleached leaves
- **Quick fix:** Add Fe-EDTA (hydro) or Fe-DTPA/EDDHA (aqua)
- **Target:** 2-4 ppm
- **Check pH first!** (unavailable above 7.0)

### Manganese (Mn)
- **Symptom:** Interveinal chlorosis with NECROTIC SPOTS
- **Pattern:** Small brown spots between veins on mid-age leaves
- **Other signs:** Less distinct net-vein than Fe
- **Quick fix:** Add Mn-EDTA or MnSO₄
- **Target:** 0.5-1.0 ppm
- **Check pH!** (unavailable above 7.0)

### Zinc (Zn)
- **Symptom:** ROSETTING (very short internodes)
- **Pattern:** Small leaves, bunched at growing tip
- **Other signs:** Interveinal chlorosis, stunted growth
- **Quick fix:** Add Zn-EDTA or ZnSO₄
- **Target:** 0.3-0.5 ppm
- **Check P levels!** (high P causes Zn deficiency)

### Copper (Cu)
- **Symptom:** Wilting of young leaves despite adequate water
- **Pattern:** Pale bluish-green new growth
- **Other signs:** Death of shoot tips, multiple shoots
- **Quick fix:** Add Cu-EDTA (cautiously)
- **Target:** 0.05-0.15 ppm
- **Caution:** Toxic to fish at low levels

### Boron (B)
- **Symptom:** Death of terminal buds
- **Pattern:** Thick, brittle leaves; hollow stems
- **Other signs:** Poor flowering/fruiting, corky tissue
- **Quick fix:** Add boric acid (carefully - narrow range!)
- **Target:** 0.3-0.6 ppm
- **Warning:** Very narrow sufficiency range

## Quick Diagnostic Flowchart

```
START: Observe Plant Symptom

├─ OLD LEAVES affected?
│  ├─ General yellowing → N or Mo
│  ├─ Purple color → P
│  ├─ Interveinal chlorosis (green veins) → Mg
│  └─ Marginal scorch → K
│
└─ NEW LEAVES affected?
   ├─ Growing point death → Ca or B
   ├─ General yellowing → S
   ├─ Interveinal with net-vein → Fe (CHECK pH!)
   ├─ Interveinal with necrotic spots → Mn
   ├─ Rosetting, small leaves → Zn (check P levels)
   └─ Wilting → Cu

ALWAYS CHECK pH FIRST!
Most "deficiencies" are availability problems, not absence.
```

## Critical pH Ranges

```
Nutrient    Optimal pH    Unavailable Above
N           5.5-8.0       Rarely unavailable
P           5.5-7.0       pH 8.0+
K           5.5-8.5       Rarely unavailable
Ca          6.0-8.0       Rare
Mg          6.0-8.0       Rare
S           5.5-8.0       Rare
Fe          5.0-6.5       pH 7.0+ (CRITICAL)
Mn          5.0-6.5       pH 7.5+
Zn          5.0-7.0       pH 7.5+
Cu          5.0-6.5       pH 7.5+
B           5.0-7.5       Wide range
Mo          6.0-8.0       pH <5.5 (opposite!)
```

**Target pH:**
- Hydroponics: 5.8-6.2
- Aquaponics: 6.4-7.0

## Emergency Response

### Step 1: Verify pH
- Most "deficiencies" are pH-related
- Adjust pH BEFORE adding nutrients
- Wait 24 hours after pH adjustment

### Step 2: Confirm Deficiency
- Visual symptoms
- Match to charts above
- Consider mobile vs. immobile

### Step 3: Quick Correction
- **Foliar spray** for fast response (immobile nutrients)
- **Root application** for long-term correction
- Use appropriate form (chelated micronutrients)

### Step 4: Monitor Response
- Immobile nutrients: New growth improves in 3-7 days
- Mobile nutrients: Symptoms stop progressing in 3-7 days
- Old damage does NOT recover (immobile deficiencies)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Adding more of deficient nutrient without checking pH**
   → Always verify pH first!

2. **Confusing Fe with Mg deficiency**
   → Check which leaves: Fe = new, Mg = old

3. **Over-supplementing narrow-range nutrients (B, Cu)**
   → Start low, increase gradually

4. **Ignoring antagonisms**
   → High K causes Mg deficiency
   → High P causes Zn deficiency

5. **Expecting old damage to recover**
   → Immobile nutrients: Only new growth improves
   → Focus on preventing further damage
