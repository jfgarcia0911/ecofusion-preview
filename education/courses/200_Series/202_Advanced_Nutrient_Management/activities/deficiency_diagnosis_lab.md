## Case Study 2: Tomato Plants (8 points)

**System Information:**
- System: DWC hydroponics
- Crop: Tomatoes, week 10 (fruiting)
- EC: 2.8 mS/cm
- pH: 7.6
- Solution last changed: 3 weeks ago

**Symptoms:**
- Newest leaves showing bright yellow between veins
- Veins remain green (net-vein pattern)
- Older leaves normal green
- Growth slowing
- Roots appear healthy (white, extensive)

**Questions:**

1. **What nutrient deficiency is this?** (1 point)

2. **What is the PRIMARY cause?** (2 points)

3. **Why isn't this nutrient being absorbed despite being in the solution?** (2 points)

4. **What should you do FIRST before adding more of the deficient nutrient?** (1 point)

5. **Calculate the correction:** (2 points)
   - Current pH: 7.6
   - Target pH: 6.0
   - If you lower pH and symptoms persist, how much chelated iron would you add to a 500 L system to provide 2 ppm Fe? (Show calculation)

## Answer Key (For Instructor)

**Case 1:** Calcium deficiency/tipburn - transport issue due to low transpiration (high humidity, heat), high EC. Correct with airflow, reduce EC, foliar Ca.

**Case 2:** Iron deficiency - pH 7.6 makes Fe unavailable. Fix pH FIRST. Calculation: ~400g Fe-DTPA for 500L to add 2ppm.

**Case 3:** Nitrogen and Phosphorus deficiency. N calc: ~300g K NO₃ for 50ppm N. P calc: ~200g MKP for 30ppm P. Add over 2-3 days.

**Case 4:** Zinc deficiency caused by high P (P-Zn antagonism). Increase Zn to 0.5-0.6ppm. Consider reducing P back to 60ppm.

**Bonus:** Primary problem is root disease (low DO, high temp, brown slimy roots). Fix DO first, then address pH, then nutrients. Root disease prevents uptake causing apparent deficiencies.
