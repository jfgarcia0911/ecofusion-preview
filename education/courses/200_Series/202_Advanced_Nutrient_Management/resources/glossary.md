# Nutrient Management Glossary

**Course 202: Advanced Nutrient Management**

---

## A

**Active Transport** - Nutrient uptake mechanism requiring cellular energy (ATP) to move substances against concentration gradients. Essential for most mineral nutrient absorption.

**Alkalinity** - Water's capacity to neutralize acid, indicating buffering capacity. Measured in ppm as CaCO₃. Target: 0-40 ppm for hydroponics, 60-120 ppm for aquaponics.

**Anion** - Negatively charged ion (e.g., NO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻, SO₄²⁻). Plants absorb anionic forms of nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur.

**Antagonism** - Negative interaction where excess of one nutrient reduces uptake of another (e.g., high K reducing Mg uptake).

**Aquaponics** - Integrated system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics, using fish waste as nutrient source for plants.

---

## B

**Blossom End Rot (BER)** - Physiological disorder in fruiting crops (tomatoes, peppers) caused by calcium deficiency or transport failure, appearing as dark, sunken areas on fruit bottom.

**Buffering Capacity** - See Alkalinity. Resistance to pH change when acids or bases are added.

---

## C

**Cation** - Positively charged ion (e.g., Ca²⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, NH₄⁺). Plants absorb cationic forms of calcium, potassium, magnesium, and ammonium.

**Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)** - Capacity of a growing medium to hold and exchange positively charged ions. Important in media-based systems; minimal in solution culture.

**Chelate** - Organic molecule that binds to metal ions (especially iron, manganese, zinc, copper) keeping them soluble and plant-available. Examples: EDTA, DTPA, EDDHA.

**Chlorosis** - Yellowing of plant tissue due to chlorophyll loss, common symptom of nutrient deficiency.

---

## D

**Deep Water Culture (DWC)** - Hydroponic method where plant roots are suspended in aerated nutrient solution.

**Deficiency** - Insufficient supply of an essential nutrient, leading to impaired plant function and visual symptoms.

**Diffusion** - Passive movement of nutrients along concentration gradient from areas of high to low concentration.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)** - Amount of oxygen dissolved in water, measured in mg/L or ppm. Target: 5-8 mg/L for optimal root function and nutrient uptake.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)** - Measure of water's ability to conduct electricity, indicating total dissolved salts. Measured in mS/cm or μS/cm. Indicates overall nutrient concentration but doesn't identify specific elements.

**EDTA** - Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, common chelating agent for micronutrients. Stable at pH 4.0-6.5, suitable for standard hydroponics.

**EDDHA** - Ethylenediamine-di(o-hydroxyphenylacetic acid), iron chelate stable at high pH (4.0-9.0). Best for aquaponics and high pH systems.

---

## F

**Fertigation** - Application of fertilizer through irrigation system.

**Foliar Application** - Spraying nutrient solutions directly onto leaves for rapid absorption. Useful for immobile nutrients and emergency corrections.

---

## G

**General Hydroponics (GH)** - Common measurement of water hardness, representing calcium and magnesium content.

---

## H

**Hardness** - Concentration of calcium and magnesium in water, measured in ppm as CaCO₃. Soft: 0-60 ppm, Hard: 121-180 ppm, Very Hard: >180 ppm.

**Hydroponic** - Method of growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions in water.

**Hydrogen Ion (H⁺)** - Positive hydrogen ion that determines acidity. Higher H⁺ concentration = lower pH = more acidic.

---

## I

**Immobile Nutrient** - Nutrient that cannot be redistributed from old to new tissue after absorption. Deficiency appears in new growth first. Examples: Ca, S, Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B.

**Interveinal Chlorosis** - Yellowing between leaf veins while veins remain green. Classic symptom of iron, manganese, zinc, or magnesium deficiency.

**Ion** - Atom or molecule with electrical charge due to gain or loss of electrons. Nutrients are absorbed as ions.

**Ion-Selective Electrode (ISE)** - Sensor that measures concentration of specific ions in solution (e.g., NO₃, K, Ca).

---

## L

**Luxury Consumption** - Uptake of nutrients beyond plant needs when highly available, potentially causing imbalances or induced deficiencies.

---

## M

**Macronutrient** - Essential element required in large quantities. Primary macronutrients: N, P, K. Secondary: Ca, Mg, S.

**Mass Flow** - Nutrient delivery mechanism where dissolved nutrients move to roots via water uptake driven by transpiration. Accounts for 70-90% of nitrogen and calcium uptake.

**Micronutrient** - Essential element required in small quantities (trace elements): Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni.

**Mobile Nutrient** - Nutrient that can be translocated from old to new tissue. Deficiency appears in old growth first. Examples: N, P, K, Mg, Mo.

---

## N

**Necrosis** - Death of plant tissue, appearing as brown, tan, or black areas. Can indicate nutrient deficiency, toxicity, or disease.

**Net-Vein Pattern** - Symptom where leaf veins remain green while tissue between veins yellows, creating fishbone or net appearance. Classic iron deficiency symptom.

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)** - Hydroponic method where a thin film of nutrient solution continuously flows over roots in channels.

**Nitrification** - Bacterial conversion of ammonia (NH₃) to nitrite (NO₂⁻) then nitrate (NO₃⁻). Critical process in aquaponics; produces acid, lowering pH.

---

## O

**Osmotic Pressure** - Pressure required to prevent water movement across a membrane. High salt concentration (high EC) creates osmotic stress, reducing water uptake.

---

## P

**Passive Transport** - Nutrient movement without energy expenditure, following concentration or electrochemical gradients.

**pH** - Measure of hydrogen ion concentration indicating acidity or alkalinity. Scale: 0-14, with 7 neutral. Target: 5.8-6.2 (hydroponics), 6.4-7.0 (aquaponics).

**pH Down** - Acid used to lower pH (phosphoric, nitric, sulfuric, or citric acid).

**pH Up** - Base used to raise pH (potassium hydroxide, potassium carbonate, calcium hydroxide).

**ppm (Parts Per Million)** - Unit of concentration. 1 ppm = 1 mg per liter. Common unit for nutrient concentrations.

**Precipitation** - Formation of insoluble solid when incompatible ions combine in solution (e.g., calcium + phosphate → calcium phosphate precipitate).

---

## R

**Reverse Osmosis (RO)** - Water purification method removing 95-99% of dissolved solids by forcing water through semi-permeable membrane.

**Rhizosphere** - Narrow zone of solution or medium immediately surrounding roots, with distinct chemical and biological properties.

**Root Exudates** - Organic compounds secreted by roots (sugars, amino acids, organic acids) that mobilize nutrients and support beneficial microbes.

**Root Interception** - Nutrient uptake mechanism where roots physically contact nutrients as they grow through medium. Minor contributor (<5% typically).

**Rosetting** - Growth pattern with very short internodes causing leaves to bunch at growing tip. Classic zinc deficiency symptom.

---

## S

**SPAD Meter** - Chlorophyll meter measuring leaf greenness, indicating nitrogen status.

**Stock Solution** - Concentrated nutrient solution diluted to working strength. Common concentrations: 100x, 200x, 500x.

**Sufficiency Range** - Nutrient concentration range in plant tissue indicating adequate nutrition without deficiency or toxicity.

**Synergistic** - Positive interaction where presence of one nutrient enhances effectiveness of another (e.g., N + S for protein synthesis).

---

## T

**TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)** - Measure of all dissolved substances in water, calculated from EC. Units: ppm. TDS = EC × conversion factor (0.5, 0.64, or 0.7).

**Tipburn** - Necrosis of leaf tips and edges, especially on young lettuce leaves. Caused by calcium deficiency or transport failure despite adequate Ca in solution.

**Tissue Analysis** - Laboratory testing of plant tissue to determine nutrient concentrations actually absorbed by the plant.

**Toxicity** - Excessive nutrient level causing plant damage. More common with micronutrients (Cu, B, Mn) which have narrow sufficiency ranges.

**Transpiration** - Water loss from plant leaves through stomata. Drives water and nutrient uptake via mass flow. Affected by temperature, humidity, and air movement.

---

## Two-Part Solution

**Two-Part Solution** - Nutrient formulation separated into Part A (calcium-containing) and Part B (phosphates, sulfates, everything else) to prevent precipitation reactions.

---

## W

**Water Hardness** - See Hardness.

**Whiptail** - Molybdenum deficiency symptom specific to cauliflower and broccoli, where leaves become narrow and strap-like.

---

## Common Abbreviations

**ATP** - Adenosine Triphosphate (cellular energy molecule)
**CEC** - Cation Exchange Capacity
**CF** - Conductivity Factor (EC unit, 1 CF = 1000 μS/cm)
**DO** - Dissolved Oxygen
**DWC** - Deep Water Culture
**EC** - Electrical Conductivity
**EDTA** - Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (chelate)
**EDDHA** - Ethylenediamine-di(o-hydroxyphenylacetic acid) (iron chelate)
**DTPA** - Diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid (chelate)
**GH** - General Hardness
**ISE** - Ion-Selective Electrode
**KH** - Carbonate Hardness (Alkalinity)
**MKP** - Monopotassium Phosphate (KH₂PO₄)
**mS/cm** - milliSiemens per centimeter (EC unit)
**NFT** - Nutrient Film Technique
**ppm** - Parts Per Million
**RO** - Reverse Osmosis
**TDS** - Total Dissolved Solids
**μS/cm** - microSiemens per centimeter (EC unit, 1000 μS/cm = 1 mS/cm)

## Chemical Formulas Reference

### Macronutrient Fertilizers
- **Calcium Nitrate:** Ca(NO₃)₂·4H₂O
- **Potassium Nitrate:** KNO₃
- **Monopotassium Phosphate (MKP):** KH₂PO₄
- **Magnesium Sulfate (Epsom Salt):** MgSO₄·7H₂O
- **Potassium Sulfate:** K₂SO₄
- **Calcium Hydroxide (Hydrated Lime):** Ca(OH)₂
- **Calcium Carbonate:** CaCO₃
- **Potassium Hydroxide:** KOH
- **Potassium Carbonate:** K₂CO₃

### Acids and Bases
- **Phosphoric Acid:** H₃PO₄
- **Nitric Acid:** HNO₃
- **Sulfuric Acid:** H₂SO₄
- **Citric Acid:** C₆H₈O₇

### Micronutrients
- **Boric Acid:** H₃BO₃
- **Sodium Molybdate:** Na₂MoO₄
