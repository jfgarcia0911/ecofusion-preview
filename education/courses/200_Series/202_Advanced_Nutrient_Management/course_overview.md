## Course Description

Master the science and art of plant nutrition in aquaponic and hydroponic systems. This advanced course covers nutrient chemistry, deficiency diagnosis, supplementation strategies, and optimization techniques for maximum crop quality and yield. Learn to read your plants, interpret water chemistry data, and make precise adjustments for peak performance.

---

## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, learners will be able to:

1. **Explain** nutrient uptake mechanisms and plant physiology
2. **Diagnose** nutrient deficiencies and toxicities from visual symptoms
3. **Develop** supplementation programs for aquaponic systems
4. **Formulate** nutrient solutions for hydroponic applications
5. **Optimize** pH and EC for specific crops
6. **Interpret** water and tissue analysis results
7. **Troubleshoot** complex nutrient-related problems

## Key Topics by Module

### Module 1: Nutrient Uptake Science
- Ion exchange at root surfaces
- Active vs. passive transport
- Root zone physiology
- Factors affecting uptake efficiency
- Cation exchange capacity (CEC)

### Module 2: Macronutrients Deep Dive
- Nitrogen (N): Forms, cycling, and management
- Phosphorus (P): Availability and interactions
- Potassium (K): Functions and deficiency
- Calcium (Ca): Structure and signaling
- Magnesium (Mg): Photosynthesis and mobility
- Sulfur (S): Proteins and flavor compounds

### Module 3: Micronutrients Mastery
- Iron (Fe): Chelation and availability
- Manganese (Mn): Enzyme activation
- Zinc (Zn): Hormone regulation
- Copper (Cu): Oxidation and toxicity
- Boron (B): Cell wall integrity
- Molybdenum (Mo): Nitrogen metabolism
- Chlorine (Cl): Requirements and excess

### Module 4: Deficiency Diagnosis
- Visual symptom identification
- Mobile vs. immobile nutrient symptoms
- Deficiency progression patterns
- Toxicity recognition
- Photo guide to common problems

### Module 5: Aquaponics Supplementation
- Fish-provided nutrients vs. gaps
- Common deficiencies in aquaponics (Fe, K, Ca)
- Supplementation products and methods
- Maintaining system balance
- Organic-approved supplements

### Module 6: Hydroponic Formulation
- Stock solution preparation
- Two-part vs. three-part formulas
- Recipe development methodology
- Mixing procedures and safety
- Concentration calculations

### Module 7: pH Management
- pH effects on nutrient availability
- Buffering and stability
- Adjustment products and methods
- Target ranges by system type
- pH troubleshooting

### Module 8: EC Management
- Electrical conductivity basics
- Crop-specific EC targets
- EC and growth stage relationships
- Salt accumulation management
- Conductivity factor conversions

### Module 9: Water Quality Impact
- Source water analysis interpretation
- Alkalinity and hardness
- Contaminants of concern
- Treatment options (RO, filters)
- Blending strategies

### Module 10: Crop-Specific Programs
- Leafy greens nutrition
- Herb nutrition requirements
- Fruiting crop nutrition
- Microgreens considerations
- Specialty crops

### Module 11: Advanced Monitoring
- Tissue analysis procedures
- Solution analysis
- Real-time sensor technologies
- Data interpretation
- When to test and what to test

### Module 12: Troubleshooting Workshop
- Case study analysis
- Problem-solving methodology
- Complex multi-factor issues
- Developing corrective action plans

## EC Targets by Crop

| Crop Category | EC Range (mS/cm) | Notes |
|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Leafy Greens** | 1.0-1.6 | Lower EC prevents tipburn |
| Lettuce | 1.0-1.4 | Sensitive to high EC |
| Spinach | 1.4-1.8 | Tolerates higher EC |
| Kale/Chard | 1.4-2.0 | Moderate tolerance |
| **Herbs** | 1.0-2.0 | Varies by species |
| Basil | 1.0-1.6 | Lower for tender leaves |
| Cilantro | 1.2-1.8 | Moderate |
| Mint | 1.4-2.0 | Tolerates higher |
| **Fruiting Crops** | 2.0-4.0 | Higher for fruit development |
| Tomatoes | 2.0-3.5 | Increase during fruiting |
| Peppers | 2.0-3.0 | Moderate range |
| Cucumbers | 1.8-2.5 | Sensitive to high EC |
