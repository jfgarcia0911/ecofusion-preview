## Introduction to HACCP

**Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP)** is a systematic preventive approach to food safety that identifies physical, chemical, and biological hazards in production processes.

This template provides a framework for developing your operation's HACCP plan.

## Part 2: Hazard Analysis (Principle 1)

For each process step, identify potential hazards and determine if they are significant.

### 2.1 Hazard Categories

**Biological:** Bacteria, viruses, parasites (E. coli, Salmonella, Listeria, Norovirus, etc.)
**Chemical:** Pesticides, sanitizers, heavy metals, allergens
**Physical:** Glass, metal, plastic, stones, wood, jewelry

### 2.2 Hazard Analysis Worksheet

| Process Step | Potential Hazard | Biological/Chemical/Physical | Significant? (Y/N) | Justification | Control Measures |
|--------------|------------------|------------------------------|---------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Seeds | Salmonella (B) | Biological | Y | Contaminated seeds can introduce pathogens | Source from reputable supplier, certificates |
| Water source | E. coli (B) | Biological | Y | Water contacts edible portion | Regular testing, treatment system |
| Growing | Listeria (B) | Biological | N | Low risk in hydro systems | Good sanitation practices |
| Harvest | Pathogens (B) | Biological | Y | Worker contact with RTE product | Hand washing, hygiene training |
| Washing | Cross-contamination (B) | Biological | Y | Wash water can spread pathogens | Water testing, sanitizer monitoring |
| Cooling | Pathogen growth (B) | Biological | Y | Temperature abuse allows growth | Temperature monitoring |
| Packing | Foreign matter (P) | Physical | N | Controlled environment | Visual inspection |
| Storage | Temperature (B) | Biological | Y | Warm storage accelerates growth | Continuous temperature monitoring |

## Part 3: Critical Control Points (Principle 2)

A Critical Control Point (CCP) is a step where control can be applied and is essential to prevent or eliminate a food safety hazard.

### 3.1 CCP Decision Tree

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    CCP DECISION TREE                       │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                            │
│  Q1: Do preventive control measures exist for this hazard?│
│      ↓ NO → Modify step, process, or product              │
│      ↓ YES → Continue                                      │
│                                                            │
│  Q2: Is this step specifically designed to eliminate or   │
│      reduce the hazard to an acceptable level?            │
│      ↓ YES → This is a CCP                                │
│      ↓ NO → Continue                                       │
│                                                            │
│  Q3: Could contamination occur or increase to             │
│      unacceptable levels?                                 │
│      ↓ NO → NOT a CCP (stop)                              │
│      ↓ YES → Continue                                      │
│                                                            │
│  Q4: Will a later step eliminate or reduce the hazard     │
│      to an acceptable level?                              │
│      ↓ YES → NOT a CCP (but later step IS a CCP)          │
│      ↓ NO → This is a CCP                                 │
│                                                            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Part 4: Critical Limits (Principle 3)

Critical limits are the maximum or minimum values to which a biological, chemical, or physical parameter must be controlled at a CCP.

### 4.1 Critical Limits for Each CCP

**CCP-1: HARVEST (Worker Hygiene)**

| Parameter | Critical Limit | Basis |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Hand washing | Before harvest, after breaks, after restroom | FSMA PSR requirements |
| Worker health | No workers with illness (vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice) | FDA Food Code |
| Glove condition | Clean, intact, changed when contaminated | Industry best practice |
| Training | All harvest workers trained annually | FSMA PSR requirements |

---

**CCP-2: WASHING (Water Sanitation)**

| Parameter | Critical Limit | Basis |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Water microbial quality | No detectable E. coli per 100mL | FSMA PSR standards |
| Free chlorine | 50-200 ppm | Industry standard for produce wash |
| pH | 6.5-7.5 | Optimal for chlorine efficacy |
| Water temperature | ≥10°F warmer than produce | Prevent infiltration |
| Contact time | Minimum 30 seconds | Adequate sanitizer contact |

---

**CCP-3: INITIAL COOLING**

| Parameter | Critical Limit | Basis |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Cooling time | Reduce to ≤40°F within 2 hours of harvest | Prevent pathogen growth |
| Method validation | Validated cooling method (forced-air, hydrocool) | Process validation study |

---

**CCP-4: COLD STORAGE**

| Parameter | Critical Limit | Basis |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Storage temperature | 34-38°F (crop-specific) | Slow pathogen growth |
| Maximum time out of cooler | <30 minutes during packing | Temperature maintenance |
| Transport temperature | ≤40°F | FDA recommendations |

## Part 6: Corrective Actions (Principle 5)

Define actions to take when monitoring indicates a critical limit has not been met.

### 6.1 Corrective Action Procedures

**CCP-1: HARVEST (Worker Hygiene)**

| Deviation | Corrective Action | Responsible Person | Documentation |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Worker observed not washing hands | • Immediately stop worker<br>• Require hand washing<br>• Retrain worker<br>• Increase monitoring | Supervisor | Corrective action log |
| Ill worker present | • Remove from produce contact immediately<br>• Assess potential product contamination<br>• Clean/sanitize contacted surfaces | Crew leader/Manager | Illness report, corrective action log |
| Incomplete training | • Do not allow untrained worker to harvest<br>• Complete training before work<br>• Document training | Manager | Training records |

---

**CCP-2: WASHING (Water Sanitation)**

| Deviation | Corrective Action | Responsible Person | Documentation |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Chlorine <50 ppm or >200 ppm | • Stop washing immediately<br>• Adjust chlorine injection<br>• Re-test until within range<br>• Segregate product washed during deviation<br>• Evaluate product disposition | Wash operator/QC Manager | Corrective action log, water quality log |
| E. coli detected in wash water | • Stop using water source immediately<br>• Switch to backup water source<br>• Investigate contamination source<br>• Re-test before resuming<br>• Trace and evaluate exposed product | QC Manager/Production Manager | Lab results, corrective action log, traceability records |
| pH out of range (not 6.5-7.5) | • Adjust pH with citric acid or sodium hydroxide<br>• Re-test to confirm in range<br>• Continue monitoring | Wash operator | Water quality log |
| Water temperature <10°F above produce | • Adjust water heater<br>• Wait for temperature recovery<br>• Evaluate infiltration risk for washed product | Wash operator | Water quality log, corrective action log |

---

**CCP-3: INITIAL COOLING**

| Deviation | Corrective Action | Responsible Person | Documentation |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Product not cooled to ≤40°F within 2 hours | • Immediately move to faster cooling<br>• Extend cooling time and re-monitor<br>• If >4 hours to cool, evaluate product safety<br>• May need to discard product | Cooler operator/QC Manager | Cooling log, corrective action log |
| Cooling equipment failure | • Transfer to backup cooler immediately<br>• Call for repair<br>• Monitor product temperature closely | Cooler operator/Maintenance | Corrective action log, maintenance records |

---

**CCP-4: COLD STORAGE**

| Deviation | Corrective Action | Responsible Person | Documentation |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Temperature >40°F | • Check if thermometer accurate<br>• Adjust cooler thermostat<br>• If >40°F for >2 hours, evaluate product<br>• May discard product if >4 hours at >40°F | QC Manager/Maintenance | Temperature log, corrective action log |
| Cooler failure | • Transfer all product to backup cooler<br>• Call for emergency repair<br>• Evaluate product if warm for extended time | Production Manager/Maintenance | Corrective action log |

## Part 7: Verification Procedures (Principle 6)

Establish procedures to verify that the HACCP system is working effectively.

### 7.1 Verification Activities

| Activity | Method | Frequency | Responsible Person |
|----------|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **CCP monitoring verification** | Review monitoring logs for completeness and accuracy | Weekly | QC Manager |
| **Calibration of equipment** | Calibrate thermometers, pH meters, chlorine testers | Monthly or per manufacturer | Maintenance/QC |
| **Corrective action review** | Review all corrective actions taken | Weekly | HACCP Team Leader |
| **Environmental monitoring** | Test surfaces, equipment for pathogens | Monthly | QC Manager |
| **End-product testing** | Microbial testing of finished product | Weekly or per lot | QC Manager |
| **HACCP plan review** | Comprehensive review of entire plan | Annually or when changes occur | HACCP Team |
| **Regulatory compliance review** | Verify alignment with FSMA, FDA requirements | Annually | External auditor/consultant |
| **Internal audit** | Full system audit of HACCP implementation | Quarterly | QC Manager |
| **Mock recall exercise** | Test traceability and recall procedures | Annually | HACCP Team |

### 7.2 Verification Records

- Equipment calibration logs
- Environmental testing results
- Product testing results
- Internal audit reports
- HACCP plan review meeting minutes
- Mock recall reports

## Part 8: Record-Keeping (Principle 7)

Establish documentation procedures for the HACCP system.

### 8.1 Required HACCP Records

**Planning Documents:**
- HACCP Plan (this document)
- Hazard Analysis
- CCP determination documentation
- Flow diagrams (verified)

**Monitoring Records:**
- Water quality logs (CCP-2)
- Temperature logs (CCP-3, CCP-4)
- Hygiene monitoring logs (CCP-1)
- Training records (CCP-1)

**Verification Records:**
- Equipment calibration logs
- Product testing results
- Environmental monitoring results
- Internal audit reports
- HACCP plan review records

**Corrective Action Records:**
- Corrective action logs for all CCPs
- Product disposition records
- Investigation reports

**Supporting Documents:**
- Supplier certificates (seeds, packaging)
- Lab testing results
- Maintenance records
- Employee health records (confidential)
- Training materials

### 8.2 Record Retention

| Record Type | Retention Period | Storage Location |
|-------------|------------------|------------------|
| HACCP Plan and supporting documents | Life of plan + 2 years after superseded | Office - HACCP binder |
| Monitoring records | Minimum 2 years | Office - filing cabinet |
| Corrective action records | Minimum 2 years | Office - filing cabinet |
| Verification records | Minimum 2 years | Office - filing cabinet |
| Training records | 2 years after employee separation | Office - personnel files |
| Product testing | 2 years | Office - QC files |

### 8.3 Record Review Procedures

- Records reviewed daily by production supervisor
- Records reviewed weekly by QC Manager
- Records reviewed monthly by HACCP Team Leader
- Incomplete or missing records addressed immediately
- All records signed/initialed by person completing
