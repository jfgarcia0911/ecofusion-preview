# Food Safety Glossary
## Course 210: Food Safety & FSMA Compliance

---

## A

**Agricultural Water**
Water used in growing activities including irrigation, dust abatement, frost protection, and application of crop chemicals. Subject to microbial quality standards under FSMA.

**ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)**
A molecule present in all living cells, used as an indicator of organic residue and cleanliness on surfaces. Measured with luminometer for sanitation verification (not pathogen-specific).

**Aquaponics**
Food production system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation) in a recirculating system where fish waste provides nutrients for plants.

---

## B

**BSAO (Biological Soil Amendment of Animal Origin)**
Any soil amendment containing animal materials (manure, blood meal, fish emulsion, etc.). Subject to application interval requirements or composting validation under FSMA.

**Biofilm**
Layer of microorganisms encased in protective matrix that adheres to surfaces. Difficult to remove and can harbor pathogens. Requires vigorous mechanical scrubbing.

**BSE (Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy)**
Mad cow disease - not a produce concern but illustrates importance of food safety across supply chain.

---

## C

**CCP (Critical Control Point)**
A step in the production process where control is essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a food safety hazard to acceptable levels.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Food production in structures where environmental factors (temperature, humidity, light, CO2) are controlled. Includes greenhouses, vertical farms, hydroponics, aquaponics.

**CFU (Colony Forming Unit)**
Unit of measurement for viable bacteria or fungal cells. Used in water testing: "CFU/100mL" indicates number of colony-forming units per 100 milliliters.

**Chlorination**
Water treatment method using chlorine compounds to sanitize. Target 50-200 ppm for produce washing. Most effective at pH 6.5-7.5.

**Compost**
Decomposed organic matter used as soil amendment. Properly composted material (131-170°F, adequate time/turning) eliminates pathogens and has no FSMA application interval.

**Covered Produce**
Fruits and vegetables in RAC (raw agricultural commodity) form typically consumed raw. Subject to FSMA Produce Safety Rule. Excludes rarely consumed raw produce.

**Cross-Contamination**
Transfer of harmful microorganisms from one surface, food, or person to another. Major food safety concern prevented by proper sanitation and hygiene.

---

## D

**Danger Zone**
Temperature range 41-135°F where pathogenic bacteria multiply rapidly. Produce should be kept below 41°F to slow microbial growth.

---

## E

**E. coli (Escherichia coli)**
Bacteria found in intestines of warm-blooded animals. Generic E. coli used as indicator organism for fecal contamination. E. coli O157:H7 is pathogenic strain causing severe illness.

**Environmental Monitoring**
Testing of production environment (equipment, surfaces, water) for indicators of sanitation effectiveness. May include ATP testing, pathogen testing of non-product surfaces.

**Exemption (Qualified)**
Modified FSMA PSR requirements for farms with average annual produce sales $250-500K and >50% sales to qualified end-users. Requires disclosure statement.

---

## F

**FDA (Food and Drug Administration)**
Federal agency responsible for FSMA enforcement, produce safety regulations, and food safety oversight.

**Foodborne Illness**
Disease caused by consuming contaminated food. Approximately 48 million Americans affected annually, with produce responsible for 46% of cases.

**Food Contact Surface**
Any surface that touches food during production, processing, or packing. Must be smooth, cleanable, non-porous, and properly sanitized.

**FSMA (FDA Food Safety Modernization Act)**
Comprehensive reform of U.S. food safety laws signed in 2011. Shifts focus from responding to outbreaks to preventing them through mandatory preventive controls.

**FSVP (Foreign Supplier Verification Program)**
FSMA rule requiring importers to verify foreign suppliers meet U.S. food safety standards.

---

## G

**GAP (Good Agricultural Practices)**
Voluntary USDA audit program verifying on-farm food safety practices. Often required by buyers. More comprehensive than FSMA minimum requirements.

**Geometric Mean**
Type of average calculated by multiplying values and taking nth root. Used for water quality assessment. Must be ≤126 CFU E. coli/100mL for agricultural water.

**GLOBALG.A.P.**
International food safety certification standard commonly required for exports. Covers food safety, environmental sustainability, and worker welfare.

---

## H

**HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point)**
Systematic preventive approach to food safety. Identifies biological, chemical, and physical hazards, establishes critical control points, and implements monitoring procedures.

**Harvest Water**
Water that directly contacts produce during or after harvest, including hand washing and produce washing. Must have no detectable generic E. coli.

**HPC (Heterotrophic Plate Count)**
Total count of aerobic bacteria in water. Indicator of overall water quality and treatment effectiveness (not specific to pathogens).

**Hydroponics**
Method of growing plants in nutrient solution without soil. Water directly contacts edible portions, requiring same standards as harvest/packing water.

---

## I

**Indicator Organism**
Microorganism whose presence indicates potential contamination. E. coli used as indicator of fecal contamination and potential presence of pathogens.

**ISO 17025**
International standard for testing and calibration laboratories. Preferred accreditation for water testing labs.

---

## L

**LGMA (Leafy Greens Marketing Agreement)**
California and Arizona voluntary programs establishing enhanced food safety practices for leafy greens following 2006 spinach outbreak.

**Listeria monocytogenes**
Pathogenic bacteria causing listeriosis. Grows at refrigeration temperatures. Caused deadly 2011 cantaloupe outbreak (33 deaths).

**Lot Code**
Unique identifier assigned to specific harvest or production batch, enabling traceability. Should link to harvest date, location, and all relevant records.

---

## M

**Mock Recall**
Practice exercise simulating product recall to test traceability system effectiveness. Should be conducted annually minimum, completed within 4 hours.

---

## N

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)**
Hydroponic method where thin film of nutrient solution flows over plant roots. Common in commercial leafy greens production.

**Norovirus**
Highly contagious virus causing gastroenteritis. Often spread by infected food handlers. Emphasizes importance of illness reporting and hand washing.

---

## O

**Outbreak**
Occurrence of illness affecting two or more people from common contaminated food source. Triggers investigation and potential recalls.

**ORP (Oxidation-Reduction Potential)**
Measurement of water's ability to oxidize contaminants. Used to monitor ozone and other sanitizer effectiveness.

---

## P

**Pathogen**
Microorganism (bacteria, virus, parasite) capable of causing disease. Major produce pathogens: E. coli O157:H7, Salmonella, Listeria, Norovirus, Hepatitis A.

**Potable Water**
Water safe for human consumption meeting EPA drinking water standards. Required for hand washing, produce washing, and ice making.

**PPM (Parts Per Million)**
Unit of concentration for sanitizers. For example, 100 ppm chlorine = 100 parts chlorine per 1 million parts water.

**Preventive Controls**
Science-based procedures implemented to minimize or prevent food safety hazards. Core principle of FSMA.

**PSA (Produce Safety Alliance)**
Collaborative effort developing FSMA produce safety training curriculum. PSA Grower Training Course required for FSMA qualified supervisors.

**PSR (Produce Safety Rule)**
FSMA rule establishing science-based minimum standards for safe growing, harvesting, packing, and holding of produce. Final rule published November 2015.

---

## Q

**Qualified End-User**
Consumer of food (individual) OR restaurant/retail food establishment in same state or within 275 miles of farm. Relevant for qualified exemption determination.

**Qualified Exemption**
See Exemption (Qualified)

**Qualified Supervisor**
Person who has successfully completed PSA Grower Training or equivalent. At least one required per covered farm. Responsible for oversight of compliance.

**Quaternary Ammonium (Quat)**
Type of sanitizer alternative to chlorine. Stable across pH range. Typical concentration 200 ppm with 1-minute contact time.

---

## R

**RAC (Raw Agricultural Commodity)**
Product in its raw or natural state. FSMA PSR applies to produce consumed as RAC.

**Recall**
Removal of potentially unsafe food from market. Can be voluntary (company-initiated) or mandatory (FDA-directed). Effective traceability minimizes scope.

**RLU (Relative Light Units)**
Measurement unit for ATP testing. Higher values indicate more organic residue (less clean). Benchmarks: <10 excellent, 10-30 acceptable, >30 re-clean.

---

## S

**Salmonella**
Pathogenic bacteria causing salmonellosis. Common produce contaminant from animal sources. Symptoms include diarrhea, fever, cramps appearing 6-72 hours post-exposure.

**Sanitizer**
Chemical agent that reduces microorganisms to safe levels. Common sanitizers: chlorine, quaternary ammonium, hydrogen peroxide. Applied after cleaning.

**SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)**
Written instructions describing how to perform routine operation safely and effectively. Should include: purpose, scope, procedure, monitoring, records, corrective actions.

**SQF (Safe Quality Food)**
Food safety and quality certification program recognized by GFSI (Global Food Safety Initiative). Comprehensive standard for processors and producers.

**SSOP (Sanitation Standard Operating Procedure)**
Specific SOP for cleaning and sanitizing procedures. Details what, how often, who, and verification methods.

**STV (Statistical Threshold Value)**
90th percentile value of distribution. For water quality, calculated from set of E. coli samples. Must be ≤410 CFU/100mL for agricultural water.

---

## T

**Third-Party Audit**
Independent assessment of food safety practices by certified auditor. Common certifications: GLOBALG.A.P., Primus GFS, SQF. Often required by buyers.

**Traceability**
Ability to track food through supply chain (one step back to supplier, one step forward to customer). Essential for effective recalls.

**Traceability Lot Code**
See Lot Code

**Turbidity**
Cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles. Measured in NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Units). High turbidity reduces sanitizer effectiveness. Target <5 NTU for treatment systems.

---

## U

**USDA (United States Department of Agriculture)**
Federal agency overseeing GAP audits, organic certification, and agricultural programs. Shares food safety oversight with FDA.

**UV (Ultraviolet) Treatment**
Water treatment using UV-C light (254 nm wavelength) to damage microbial DNA. Requires clear water (<5 NTU). Target dose ≥40 mJ/cm². No residual protection.

---

## V

**Validation**
Obtaining evidence that control measures are capable of effectively controlling identified hazards. One-time activity (e.g., validating composting process kills pathogens).

**Verification**
Ongoing activities confirming that food safety system is implemented as planned and effective. Includes record review, testing, observations, audits.

**Vertical Farming**
Indoor agriculture using vertically stacked growing systems, often with hydroponics and LED lighting. Highly controlled environment minimizing many field-based risks.

---

## W

**Water Activity (aw)**
Measure of available water in food. Low aw inhibits microbial growth. Less relevant for fresh produce (high aw) than processed foods.

**Windrow Composting**
Composting method using long piles (windrows) turned regularly for aeration. FSMA requirement: 15 days at 131-170°F with minimum 5 turns.

## Common Units of Measurement

| Unit | What It Measures | Example Use |
|------|-----------------|-------------|
| CFU/100mL | Bacterial count in water | Water testing results |
| ppm (mg/L) | Concentration | Chlorine sanitizer levels |
| °F | Temperature | Cooler monitoring |
| RLU | Organic residue | ATP cleanliness testing |
| NTU | Water clarity | Turbidity measurement |
| mJ/cm² | UV dose | UV treatment intensity |
| pH | Acidity/alkalinity | Water treatment monitoring |

## Temperature References

| Temperature | Fahrenheit | Celsius | Significance |
|-------------|-----------|---------|---------------|
| **Danger Zone** | 41-135°F | 5-57°C | Rapid bacterial growth |
| **Optimal Pathogen Growth** | 70-100°F | 21-38°C | Peak multiplication |
| **Produce Storage** | 32-40°F | 0-4°C | Slows growth, maintains quality |
| **Composting** | 131-170°F | 55-77°C | Kills pathogens |
| **Pasteurization** | 161°F+ | 72°C+ | Rapid pathogen destruction |
