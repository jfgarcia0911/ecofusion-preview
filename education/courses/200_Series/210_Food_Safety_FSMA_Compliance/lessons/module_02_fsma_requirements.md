### 2.1 FSMA Produce Safety Rule Overview

#### What is the Produce Safety Rule?

The FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) Produce Safety Rule establishes science-based minimum standards for the safe growing, harvesting, packing, and holding of fruits and vegetables grown for human consumption.

**Rule Finalized:** November 27, 2015
**Compliance Began:** January 26, 2016 (phased by size)
**Legal Authority:** 21 CFR Part 112

#### Core Requirements

The rule addresses five key areas:

```
FSMA PRODUCE SAFETY RULE REQUIREMENTS

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                      │
│  1. AGRICULTURAL WATER                               │
│     Quality standards and testing                    │
│                                                      │
│  2. BIOLOGICAL SOIL AMENDMENTS                       │
│     Manure and compost application standards         │
│                                                      │
│  3. WORKER TRAINING & HEALTH                         │
│     Hygiene, illness protocols, qualifications       │
│                                                      │
│  4. EQUIPMENT, TOOLS & BUILDINGS                     │
│     Sanitation and maintenance standards             │
│                                                      │
│  5. DOMESTICATED & WILD ANIMALS                      │
│     Monitoring and intrusion prevention              │
│                                                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Covered Produce

**What IS Covered:**
- Raw fruits and vegetables typically consumed raw
- Leafy greens, tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers
- Melons, berries, fresh herbs
- Microgreens, sprouts

**What is NOT Covered:**
- Produce rarely consumed raw (asparagus, potatoes)
- Produce receiving commercial processing (kill step)
- Produce for personal/on-farm consumption

### 2.2 Coverage Determination Process

#### Step 1: Are You a "Farm"?

```
FARM DEFINITION FLOWCHART

Do you grow or harvest produce?
         │
         ├── NO → Not covered by Produce Safety Rule
         │
         └── YES
               ↓
Do you also pack or hold produce grown on your farm?
         │
         ├── YES → You are a "FARM"
         │
         └── NO → Evaluate if "secondary activities" exception applies
                    ↓
      Are packing/holding activities majority of business?
                    │
         ├── NO → Still considered "FARM"
         │
         └── YES → May be "facility" (different rules apply)
```

**Farm Definition (21 CFR 112.3):**

An operation that grows, harvests, packs, or holds produce, where the majority of produce is grown on the farm or another farm under same management.

**Key Points:**
- Includes greenhouses, hydroponics, aquaponics
- Can include multiple physical locations
- Packing/holding must be of own produce primarily
- Secondary activities (≤50% of value) allowed

#### Step 2: Calculate Your Sales

**Average Annual Produce Sales Formula:**

```
SALES CALCULATION (3-YEAR ROLLING AVERAGE)

Year 1 Produce Sales: $_________
Year 2 Produce Sales: $_________
Year 3 Produce Sales: $_________
                      ──────────
Total 3-Year Sales:   $_________

÷ 3 = Average Annual Sales: $_________

THRESHOLDS:
• >$500,000  → Full Compliance Required
• $250,000-$500,000 → May Qualify for Exemption
• <$250,000  → Exempt (but state rules may apply)
```

**Sales Thresholds (adjusted for inflation):**

| Threshold | 2015 | 2020 | 2025 (estimated) |
|-----------|------|------|------------------|
| Large Farm | $500,000 | $577,500 | $632,000 |
| Small Farm | $250,000 | $288,750 | $316,000 |

**What to Count:**
✓ All produce sales (raw fruits & vegetables)
✓ Retail and wholesale combined
✓ Value-added if produce not fully processed
✓ Sales from all locations under management

**What NOT to Count:**
✗ Non-produce sales (eggs, meat, grains)
✗ Produce receiving commercial kill step
✗ Produce not covered by rule

#### Step 3: Coverage Categories

Based on your sales calculation:

**Category 1: Large Farm (>$500,000)**
- Full compliance required
- All PSR requirements apply
- Must have qualified supervisor
- Annual training required

**Category 2: Small Farm ($250,000-$500,000)**
- May qualify for Qualified Exemption
- Modified requirements if exempt
- Disclosure requirements
- Can lose exemption if conditions not met

**Category 3: Very Small Farm (<$250,000)**
- Exempt from PSR
- State regulations may still apply
- Best practices still recommended
- Customer requirements may exceed PSR

### 2.3 Exemptions and Exclusions

#### Qualified Exemption

**Requirements for Qualified Exemption:**

1. **Sales Threshold**
   - Average annual produce sales <$500,000 (3-year rolling)

2. **Qualified End-User Sales**
   - During previous 3-year period
   - More than half of produce sold to "qualified end-users"

**Qualified End-User Definition:**

```
WHO IS A QUALIFIED END-USER?

┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                       │
│  CONSUMER OF FOOD                                     │
│  Direct sales to individuals (not for resale)        │
│  • Farmers markets                                    │
│  • Roadside stands                                    │
│  • CSA programs                                       │
│  • U-pick operations                                  │
│                                                       │
│         OR                                            │
│                                                       │
│  RESTAURANT OR RETAIL FOOD ESTABLISHMENT              │
│  Located in same state OR within 275 miles            │
│  • Local restaurants                                  │
│  • Grocery stores                                     │
│  • Food co-ops                                        │
│  • Institutional food service                         │
│                                                       │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Calculating Qualified End-User Sales

**Example Calculation:**

| Year | Total Produce Sales | QEU Sales | % to QEU |
|------|-------------------|-----------|----------|
| 2022 | $400,000 | $280,000 | 70% |
| 2023 | $450,000 | $315,000 | 70% |
| 2024 | $425,000 | $255,000 | 60% |
| **Avg** | **$425,000** | **$283,333** | **67%** |

**Result:** Qualified for exemption (>50% to QEU, <$500K avg sales)

#### Disclosure Requirements for Qualified Exemption

If you have a qualified exemption, you MUST disclose:

**At Point of Sale:**
```
REQUIRED DISCLOSURE STATEMENT

"Produced by [Farm Name] at [Farm Address].
This product is not subject to FDA's Produce Safety Rule
because [Farm Name] has annual sales less than $500,000
and sells more than half of its produce directly to
qualified end-users such as consumers, restaurants,
and retail establishments within [State] or within
275 miles of the farm."
```

**Disclosure Methods:**
- Labels on product
- Signs at farmers market booth
- Invoices/receipts
- Website statements
- Email communications

#### Withdrawal of Qualified Exemption

FDA can withdraw exemption if:

1. **Active investigation** of multistate outbreak
2. **Reasonable belief** farm caused outbreak
3. **Determination** farm had opportunity to observe or became aware of potential hazard but failed to take adequate corrective actions

**Process:**
- Written notice to farm
- Opportunity to respond
- Administratively final decision
- Right to appeal

### 2.4 Additional Exemptions and Exclusions

#### Produce Rarely Consumed Raw

**Automatically Excluded:**
- Asparagus, beans (except green beans), beets, broccoli
- Brussels sprouts, cabbage, carrots, cauliflower
- Corn, eggplant, okra, peas, potatoes (white), pumpkin
- Rutabaga, squash (winter), sweet potato, turnip, water chestnuts

**Reasoning:** Low risk due to cooking/processing

#### Produce with Commercial Processing

Excluded if:
- Adequate processing (kill step) will be applied
- Documentation exists that produce is for processing
- Disclosure requirements met

**Examples:**
- Tomatoes for canning
- Cucumbers for pickling
- Apples for sauce/juice
- Berries for jam

**Required Documentation:**
```
COMMERCIAL PROCESSING DISCLOSURE

Farm must obtain written assurance from customer that:
1. Produce will receive commercial processing
2. Processing will adequately reduce microorganisms
3. Customer will take steps to ensure no raw consumption
```

#### Other Exclusions

| Exclusion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Personal Use** | Produce for farmer's own consumption |
| **On-Farm Consumption** | Eaten on farm (agritourism tastings) |
| **Grain** | Covered by different regulations |
| **Research** | Produce not for sale |
| **Pre-2011 Harvest** | Before January 1, 2011 (grandfathered) |

### 2.5 Compliance Dates and Timelines

#### Phased Implementation

FSMA compliance was phased based on farm size:

```
COMPLIANCE DATE TIMELINE

LARGE FARMS (>$500,000)
├─ January 26, 2018: Full compliance required
│
SMALL FARMS ($250,000-$500,000)
├─ January 26, 2019: Full compliance required
│  (or qualified exemption requirements)
│
VERY SMALL FARMS (<$250,000)
└─ Exempt (but best practices recommended)

WATER RULE CHANGES
└─ January 26, 2022: New water testing timeline
   (updated to address practical implementation)
```

#### What Compliance Means

**By Your Compliance Date, You Must:**

✓ Implement all applicable PSR requirements
✓ Complete required training (supervisor + workers)
✓ Establish written procedures where required
✓ Begin monitoring and record-keeping
✓ Make records available for inspection
✓ Maintain required documentation (2 years)

#### Training Requirements

**Qualified Supervisor:**
- At least one per farm
- Successful completion of PSA Grower Training Course
- Or equivalent training curriculum

**Worker Training:**
- All workers handling covered produce
- Training on hygiene and health
- In language worker understands
- Before or at start of work

### 2.6 State and International Considerations

#### State Produce Safety Programs

Many states have cooperative agreements with FDA:

**State Program Functions:**
- Farm inspections
- Education and outreach
- Enforcement actions
- Training delivery

**State Programs May:**
- Have additional requirements
- Apply to farms exempt from federal rule
- Provide cost-share for compliance
- Offer free training and resources

#### International Operations

**Imported Produce:**
- Must meet PSR if covered
- Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) applies
- Importers responsible for compliance
- FDA can inspect foreign farms

**Exports:**
- Destination country requirements may differ
- Some require GLOBALG.A.P. or other certifications
- PSR compliance often insufficient for exports
- Check specific market requirements

### 2.7 Practical Coverage Examples

#### Example 1: Small Aquaponics Greenhouse

**Operation:**
- 5,000 sq ft greenhouse
- Lettuce, herbs, tomatoes
- Direct sales at farmers market
- Annual sales: $85,000

**Coverage Determination:**
- Average annual sales: $85,000
- Below $250,000 threshold
- **Result: EXEMPT from PSR**
- Best practices still recommended
- State rules may apply

#### Example 2: Medium Hydroponic Farm

**Operation:**
- 1 acre CEA facility
- Mixed greens, microgreens
- 60% to local restaurants
- 40% to regional distributor
- Annual sales: $450,000

**Coverage Determination:**
- Average annual sales: $450,000
- Between $250K-$500K
- 60% to restaurants <275 miles = QEU
- **Result: QUALIFIED EXEMPTION**
- Must post disclosure statement
- Modified requirements apply

#### Example 3: Large Vertical Farm

**Operation:**
- 50,000 sq ft facility
- Year-round production
- National distribution
- Annual sales: $2.5M

**Coverage Determination:**
- Average annual sales: $2.5M
- Exceeds $500,000
- Not majority to QEU
- **Result: FULL PSR COMPLIANCE**
- All requirements apply
- Must have PSA-trained supervisor
