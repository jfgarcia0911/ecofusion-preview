## Lesson Content

### 6.1 Worker Training Requirements

#### Qualified Supervisor

**Who Needs PSA Training:**
- At least one person per farm
- Supervises production activities
- Knowledgeable in produce safety

**Training Options:**
- Produce Safety Alliance Grower Training (most common)
- Equivalent training curriculum
- Combination of training and experience

**Content Covered:**
- FSMA Produce Safety Rule overview
- All PSR requirements in detail
- Implementation strategies
- Record-keeping

#### Worker Training

**Who Must Be Trained:**
- ALL workers who handle covered produce
- Harvest crew
- Packing shed staff
- Equipment operators

**When:**
- Before or at the start of work activities
- Annual refresher recommended
- When practices change

**Required Topics:**

```
WORKER TRAINING CONTENT

1. HEALTH & HYGIENE
   ├─ Importance of hand washing
   ├─ When to wash hands
   ├─ Proper technique
   ├─ Illness reporting
   └─ Personal cleanliness

2. FOOD SAFETY AWARENESS
   ├─ How produce becomes contaminated
   ├─ Worker role in prevention
   └─ Consequences of contamination

3. SITE-SPECIFIC PRACTICES
   ├─ Where facilities are located
   ├─ Your farm's specific procedures
   └─ Who to report concerns to
```

**Language Requirements:**
- Training in language worker understands
- Visual aids for low-literacy workers
- Verify comprehension (Q&A, demonstration)

### 6.2 Health Screening Protocols

#### Reportable Conditions

**Workers Must Notify Supervisor If:**

| Condition | Reason | Action |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Vomiting** | Potential viral illness | Exclude from work |
| **Diarrhea** | Bacterial/viral illness | Exclude from work |
| **Jaundice** | Hepatitis A indicator | Exclude from work |
| **Sore throat with fever** | Potential pathogen shedding | Restrict from produce contact |
| **Infected cuts/boils** | S. aureus risk | Cover or restrict |
| **Diagnosis of illness** | E. coli, Salmonella, Hepatitis A, etc. | Exclude per health dept |

#### Creating a Non-Punitive Reporting Culture

**Challenges:**
- Fear of lost wages
- Fear of termination
- Pressure to work when sick
- Cultural barriers

**Solutions:**

```
ENCOURAGING ILLNESS REPORTING

PAID SICK LEAVE
├─ Remove financial barrier
└─ Show commitment to safety

CLEAR POLICY
├─ Written, translated procedures
├─ Posted visibly
└─ Explained during training

NON-PUNITIVE
├─ No disciplinary action for reporting
├─ Thank workers for reporting
└─ Privacy protection

ALTERNATIVE WORK
├─ Offer non-contact work if possible
└─ Shows care for worker welfare
```

#### Screening Documentation

**What to Record:**
- Illness reports received
- Symptoms/diagnosis
- Date of report
- Action taken (excluded, restricted, allowed)
- Return-to-work date
- Medical clearance (if required)

**Privacy Considerations:**
- Comply with ADA, medical privacy laws
- Store health records separately
- Limit access to need-to-know basis

### 6.3 Hand Washing Facilities

#### PSR Requirements

**Restroom Facilities:**
```
TOILET REQUIREMENTS

LOCATION
├─ Readily accessible to all workers
├─ Located to prevent contamination
└─ Within reasonable distance of work area

DESIGN
├─ Sufficient number for workforce
├─ Toilet paper, waste receptacle
├─ Lighting
└─ Self-closing doors (if enclosed)

MAINTENANCE
├─ Serviced and cleaned regularly
├─ Supplied with toilet paper
├─ Waste removed frequently
└─ In good repair

PORTABLE TOILETS
├─ Securely positioned
├─ Professional servicing
├─ Hand wash station nearby
└─ Protected from contamination sources
```

**Hand Washing Stations:**

| Component | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Water** | Potable, running water (not standing basin) |
| **Soap** | Liquid or bar, individual dispensing |
| **Drying** | Single-use towels or air dryer |
| **Signage** | Directions in appropriate languages |
| **Waste** | Receptacle for towels |

#### Field Hand Wash Stations

**Acceptable Designs:**
- Mobile hand wash trailer
- Gravity-fed hand wash stand
- Water tank with foot pump
- Porta-john with built-in sink

**NOT Acceptable:**
- Shared bucket/basin
- Spray bottle only
- No soap/drying method
- Water not potable

**Location Guidelines:**
- Within 1/4 mile of working area
- At or near restrooms
- Easily accessible path
- Clearly marked/visible

### 6.4 Hygiene Practices

#### Hand Washing Procedure

```
PROPER HAND WASHING TECHNIQUE

1. WET hands with running water

2. APPLY soap

3. SCRUB for 20 seconds
   ├─ Palms, backs of hands
   ├─ Between fingers
   ├─ Under nails
   └─ Wrists

4. RINSE thoroughly under running water

5. DRY with single-use towel or air dryer

6. TURN OFF water with towel (if manual)
```

**When to Wash:**
- Before starting work
- After using restroom
- After breaks, eating, smoking
- After handling trash, animals, soil
- After coughing, sneezing, touching face
- After handling contaminated equipment
- Whenever necessary to prevent contamination

#### Glove Use

**PSR Requirements:**
- Clean and intact
- Changed when contaminated
- Maintained in sanitary condition
- NOT a substitute for hand washing

**When Gloves Are Beneficial:**
- Harvest of delicate produce
- Workers with cuts/abrasions
- Cold/wet working conditions
- Packing operations

**Common Mistakes:**
- Using gloves instead of hand washing
- Not changing contaminated gloves
- Re-using single-use gloves
- Touching face/phone with gloved hands

### 6.5 Personal Cleanliness

#### Clothing and Personal Items

**Requirements:**
- Clean clothing appropriate for work
- Hair restraints in packing areas (recommended)
- Remove jewelry that could harbor pathogens
- No eating, drinking, tobacco in production areas

#### Designated Areas

**Separate Zones:**

```
FACILITY ZONING

PRODUCTION AREA (Red Zone)
├─ No eating, drinking, smoking
├─ Minimize personal items
├─ Clean clothing/footwear
└─ Gloves/hair restraints if needed

BREAK AREA (Yellow Zone)
├─ Hand washing station at entrance
├─ Eating, drinking allowed
├─ Trash receptacles
└─ Separate from production

RESTROOMS (Yellow Zone)
├─ Hand washing required upon exit
├─ Not opened directly to production
└─ Regular cleaning schedule
```

### 6.6 Training Program Development

#### Effective Training Strategies

**Know Your Audience:**
- Education level
- Language(s) spoken
- Cultural background
- Learning styles
- Previous experience

**Training Methods:**

| Method | Advantages | Best For |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **In-person demonstration** | Hands-on, interactive | Hand washing technique |
| **Visual posters** | Language-neutral, permanent | Reminders, key points |
| **Videos** | Engaging, reusable | Overview, storytelling |
| **Hands-on practice** | Builds skills | Sanitation procedures |
| **Written materials** | Reference, documentation | Literate workers, review |

#### Sample Training Outline

**Pre-Season Worker Training (30 minutes):**

```
WORKER TRAINING AGENDA

0:00-0:05  Welcome & Importance
           Why food safety matters

0:05-0:15  Health & Hygiene
           Illness reporting
           Hand washing demonstration
           Facility locations

0:15-0:25  Farm-Specific Practices
           Our procedures
           Where things are located
           Who to ask questions

0:25-0:30  Questions & Verification
           Open Q&A
           Understanding check
           Sign training roster
```

#### Documentation Requirements

**Training Records Must Include:**
- Worker name
- Training date
- Topics covered
- Trainer name
- Worker signature or verification

### 6.7 Monitoring and Enforcement

#### Observation and Feedback

**Regular Monitoring:**
- Supervisor observations during work
- Random facility checks
- End-of-day inspections
- Worker interviews/feedback

**Positive Reinforcement:**
- Acknowledge good practices
- Reward consistent compliance
- Share success stories
- Build food safety culture

**Corrective Actions:**
- Immediate correction of unsafe practices
- Re-training if gaps identified
- Progressive discipline if willful
- Document corrective actions

#### Common Violations and Solutions

| Violation | Root Cause | Solution |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Not washing hands** | Inconvenient, don't understand | Add stations, re-train, supervise |
| **Dirty gloves** | Don't know when to change | Clarify policy, provide ample supply |
| **Eating in field** | No break area | Designate area, communicate rules |
| **Not reporting illness** | Fear consequences | Build trust, paid sick leave |

### 6.8 Multilingual and Cross-Cultural Training

#### Language Accessibility

**Translation Needs:**
- Written policies and procedures
- Training materials
- Signage (hand washing, facilities)
- Emergency procedures

**Strategies:**
- Hire bilingual supervisors
- Use professional translation services
- Pictorial/graphic-heavy materials
- Videos in multiple languages

#### Cultural Considerations

**Respect Differences:**
- Hand washing norms vary
- Restroom expectations differ
- Hierarchical vs. egalitarian cultures
- Direct vs. indirect communication

**Adapt Approach:**
- Use culturally appropriate examples
- Involve respected community members
- Consider gender dynamics
- Build relationships, not just rules
