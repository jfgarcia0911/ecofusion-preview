### 7.1 Sanitary Harvest Practices

#### Pre-Harvest Assessments

**Before Harvest Begins:**

```
PRE-HARVEST CHECKLIST

FIELD CONDITIONS
☐ No standing water in harvest area
☐ No evidence of animal intrusion
☐ No sick/damaged plants requiring removal
☐ Equipment inspected and clean
☐ Harvest containers sanitized

WORKER READINESS
☐ All harvesters trained
☐ Clean clothing and hands
☐ No ill workers present
☐ Hand wash stations functional
☐ Restroom facilities available

WEATHER CONSIDERATIONS
☐ No harvest during/after heavy rain
☐ Adequate drying time if irrigated
☐ Temperature appropriate (cool morning preferred)
```

#### Harvest Timing

**Food Safety Considerations:**

| Factor | Guidance |
|--------|----------|
| **Time of day** | Early morning when cool (quality + safety) |
| **After irrigation** | Wait for foliage to dry (24 hrs+) |
| **After rain** | Minimum 24 hours drying time |
| **After manure application** | Follow 90/120-day intervals |
| **Temperature** | Harvest when produce temp is lowest |

### 7.2 Harvest Equipment & Containers

#### Container Requirements

**Harvest Bins/Totes:**
- Smooth, non-porous surfaces (plastic, stainless steel)
- Free of cracks, chips, sharp edges
- Cleanable and drainable
- Dedicated to produce (never chemicals/trash)
- Properly stored off ground when not in use

**Field Equipment:**
- Harvest knives: Clean, sanitized between uses
- Cutting tools: Dedicated to produce
- Field trailers: Clean, no animal/chemical contamination
- Tarps/covers: Clean, dry, food-grade

#### Cleaning and Sanitizing Protocol

```
HARVEST CONTAINER SANITATION

DAILY (Minimum)
1. REMOVE debris (brush, rinse)

2. WASH with detergent
   ├─ Scrub all surfaces
   ├─ Pay attention to corners, handles
   └─ Rinse thoroughly

3. SANITIZE
   ├─ Chlorine solution (50-200 ppm)
   ├─ Contact time: 1-2 minutes
   └─ Alternative: quaternary ammonium, hydrogen peroxide

4. DRY
   ├─ Air dry completely
   ├─ Store inverted or on rack
   └─ Protect from re-contamination

5. INSPECT before use
```

**Sanitizer Concentrations:**

| Sanitizer | Concentration | Contact Time | pH Consideration |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Chlorine | 50-200 ppm | 1-2 minutes | Most effective 6.5-7.5 |
| Quaternary ammonium | 200 ppm | 1 minute | Stable across pH |
| Hydrogen peroxide | 80 ppm | 30 seconds | Works in organic |

### 7.3 Packing Facility Design

#### Facility Requirements

**Location and Construction:**

```
PACKING FACILITY STANDARDS

FLOORS
├─ Concrete or equivalent
├─ Smooth, cleanable surface
├─ Sloped to drains
├─ No standing water
└─ Crack-free, well-maintained

WALLS & CEILINGS
├─ Smooth, cleanable
├─ Light-colored (shows dirt)
├─ No flaking paint
├─ Moisture-resistant
└─ Sealed penetrations

LIGHTING
├─ Adequate for cleaning/inspection
├─ Shielded or shatterproof bulbs
├─ Minimum 50 foot-candles work areas
└─ 10 foot-candles storage areas

VENTILATION
├─ Adequate air circulation
├─ Prevents condensation
├─ Filtered if needed
└─ Positive pressure preferred
```

#### Traffic Flow

**Prevent Cross-Contamination:**

```
FLOW PATTERN (One-Way Preferred)

Receiving → Pre-Cooling → Washing → Drying → Packing → Cold Storage → Shipping
    ↓           ↓           ↓          ↓         ↓          ↓            ↓
Dirty       Transition    Clean      Clean    Clean    Cold Chain    Distribution
Zone         Zone         Zone       Zone     Zone     Maintained

PRINCIPLES:
├─ Dirty to clean flow (never backwards)
├─ Separate raw from processed
├─ Dedicated equipment by zone
└─ Hand washing between zones
```

### 7.4 Produce Washing Operations

#### When to Wash

**Washing Purposes:**
- Remove field heat (cooling)
- Remove soil and debris
- Reduce microbial load
- Improve appearance

**Important Considerations:**
- Washing can spread contamination if done improperly
- Water quality is critical
- Temperature differential matters
- Some produce should NOT be washed (berries, mushrooms)

#### Water Quality Standards

**Post-Harvest Water = Harvest/Packing Water:**

```
WASH WATER REQUIREMENTS

MICROBIAL STANDARD
└─ No detectable generic E. coli

PRACTICAL REQUIREMENTS
├─ Potable water OR
├─ Treated to potable standard OR
└─ Continuous sanitization system

MONITORING
├─ Test before use each day
├─ Monitor sanitizer levels continuously
├─ Re-test if system upset
└─ Record all monitoring
```

#### Dump Tank Management

**Critical Parameters:**

| Parameter | Standard | Monitoring |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| **Water temperature** | ≥10°F warmer than produce | Continuous thermometer |
| **Sanitizer level** | 50-200 ppm chlorine (or equivalent) | Hourly testing |
| **pH** | 6.5-7.5 for chlorine | Every 2 hours |
| **Turbidity** | Change water when cloudy | Visual + NTU meter |
| **Water turnover** | Replace frequently | Based on volume |

**Why Temperature Matters:**

```
TEMPERATURE DIFFERENTIAL

Produce Cooler Than Water
        ↓
Water enters through stem scar/wounds
        ↓
Pathogens internalized
        ↓
Cannot be washed off
        ↓
CONTAMINATION LOCKED IN

SOLUTION: Water ≥10°F warmer than produce
```

### 7.5 Temperature Control

#### The Importance of Cooling

**Pathogen Growth Temperatures:**
- Danger Zone: 41-135°F
- Optimal growth: 70-100°F
- Growth slows: <41°F
- Growth stops: <32°F (most produce won't tolerate)

**Cooling Objectives:**
- Remove field heat rapidly
- Slow respiration and ripening
- Inhibit microbial growth
- Extend shelf life

#### Cooling Methods

```
PRODUCE COOLING OPTIONS

HYDROCOOLING
├─ Cold water shower/immersion
├─ Very rapid (minutes)
├─ Best for: Leafy greens, herbs
└─ Requires excellent water quality

FORCED-AIR COOLING
├─ Cold air blown through pallets
├─ Rapid (1-2 hours)
├─ Best for: Wide variety of produce
└─ No water contamination risk

ROOM COOLING
├─ Product placed in cold room
├─ Slow (8-24 hours)
├─ Best for: Sturdy produce, small volumes
└─ Least expensive

ICE/TOP ICING
├─ Ice added to containers
├─ Continuous cooling during transport
├─ Best for: Broccoli, sweet corn
└─ Ice must be from potable water
```

#### Temperature Monitoring

**Required Monitoring:**

| Location | Frequency | Target | Action if Deviation |
|----------|-----------|--------|---------------------|
| **Coolers** | Continuous | 34-38°F | Investigate, repair |
| **Wash water** | Continuous | 50-70°F | Adjust heater |
| **Product (core)** | Each lot | Crop-specific | Re-cool if needed |
| **Transport** | Departure & arrival | Maintain cold chain | Reject if warm |

**Temperature Log Example:**

| Date | Time | Cooler | Product | Wash Water | Initials | Notes |
|------|------|--------|---------|------------|----------|-------|
| 6/1 | 6am | 36°F | 38°F | 55°F | JD | All good |
| 6/1 | 2pm | 39°F | 36°F | 58°F | JD | Cooler high - checked |

### 7.6 Packaging and Labeling

#### Food Contact Materials

**Requirements:**
- Food-grade materials only
- Clean and unused (or sanitized)
- Stored to prevent contamination
- Supplier certification of food-grade status

**Common Materials:**

| Material | Advantages | Considerations |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Plastic clamshells** | Protective, stackable | Single-use, cost |
| **Cardboard boxes** | Economical, recyclable | Moisture-sensitive |
| **Mesh bags** | Breathable, low-cost | Limited protection |
| **Reusable totes** | Sustainable | Requires washing |

#### Traceability Labeling

**Minimum Information:**
- Farm/business name
- Product name
- Lot code or harvest date
- Pack date (if different)
- Contact information

**Lot Coding System:**

```
LOT CODE EXAMPLE: 2024-06-15-A

2024    = Year
06      = Month
15      = Day
A       = Field/greenhouse designation

ENABLES:
├─ Trace product to specific field/date
├─ Targeted recalls if needed
├─ Internal quality tracking
└─ Customer confidence
```

### 7.7 Sanitation Standard Operating Procedures (SSOPs)

#### Developing SSOPs

**Components:**

1. **Scope:** What is being cleaned
2. **Frequency:** How often
3. **Responsibility:** Who does it
4. **Procedure:** Step-by-step
5. **Chemicals:** What sanitizers/detergents
6. **Monitoring:** How to verify
7. **Records:** What to document

#### Sample SSOP: Harvest Bin Cleaning

```
SSOP: HARVEST BIN SANITATION

FREQUENCY: Daily after use

RESPONSIBLE: Harvest supervisor

PROCEDURE:
1. Remove all plant debris
2. Rinse with hose
3. Apply detergent, scrub with brush
4. Rinse thoroughly
5. Prepare sanitizer solution (100 ppm chlorine)
6. Immerse or spray all surfaces
7. Allow 1 minute contact time
8. Air dry, store inverted

MONITORING:
- Visual inspection for cleanliness
- ATP swab test weekly (optional)
- Chlorine test strips for sanitizer

RECORDS:
- Daily cleaning log (date, initials)
- Corrective actions if needed
```

#### Master Sanitation Schedule

**Comprehensive Cleaning Plan:**

| Area/Equipment | Task | Frequency | Responsible |
|----------------|------|-----------|-------------|
| Harvest bins | Full sanitation | Daily | Crew lead |
| Packing tables | Clean & sanitize | Daily | Packer |
| Dump tank | Drain, clean, refill | Daily | Supervisor |
| Cooler | Sweep, mop, inspect | Weekly | Maintenance |
| Hand wash stations | Refill, clean | Daily | Anyone |
| Restrooms | Full cleaning | Daily | Janitorial |

### 7.8 Verification and Monitoring

#### Environmental Monitoring

**ATP Testing:**
- Rapid cleanliness test
- Measures organic residue
- Not pathogen-specific but indicates sanitation
- Use for equipment, surfaces, hands

**Procedure:**
1. Swab surface with ATP swab
2. Insert in luminometer
3. Read RLU (Relative Light Units)
4. Compare to benchmark

**Benchmarks:**
- <10 RLU: Excellent
- 10-30 RLU: Acceptable
- >30 RLU: Re-clean and re-test

#### Pathogen Testing

**When to Test:**
- Environmental monitoring (floors, drains)
- Equipment verification (after cleaning)
- Product testing (if risk indicated)
- Routine surveillance (recommended)

**Indicator Organisms:**
- Generic E. coli (fecal contamination)
- Total coliforms (general sanitation)
- Enterobacteriaceae (process hygiene)
