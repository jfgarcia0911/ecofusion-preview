# IPM Glossary & Terminology
## Course 207: Integrated Pest Management

---

## A

**Action Threshold** - The pest population level at which management action should be taken to prevent economic damage. Lower than the economic injury level.

**Active Ingredient (AI)** - The chemical component in a pesticide product that actually kills or controls the pest.

**Arachnid** - Class of arthropods with 8 legs; includes mites and spiders (not insects, which have 6 legs).

**Augmentative Biological Control** - Releasing beneficial organisms to supplement natural populations; includes inoculative and inundative releases.

**Azadirachtin** - Active ingredient from neem tree seeds; acts as insect growth regulator and feeding deterrent.

---

## B

**Banker Plant System** - Using non-crop plants to support alternative prey/hosts for beneficial insects, providing continuous breeding population.

**Beneficials** - Organisms that provide pest control services, including predators, parasitoids, and pathogens.

**Biological Control** - Use of living organisms (predators, parasites, pathogens) to reduce pest populations.

**Biopesticide** - Pesticide derived from natural materials (plants, bacteria, minerals).

**Botrytis** - Fungal pathogen causing gray mold; favored by high humidity and poor air circulation.

**Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)** - Beneficial bacterium producing proteins toxic to specific insect larvae (caterpillars, fungus gnats).

---

## C

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)** - Growing plants in structures where environmental factors are controlled (greenhouses, vertical farms).

**Chemical Control** - Using pesticides to manage pests; top of IPM pyramid (last resort).

**Classical Biological Control** - Importing and establishing natural enemies from a pest's native range.

**Conservation Biological Control** - Creating conditions (habitat, food sources) that support natural enemy populations.

**Cultural Control** - Pest management through crop production practices (sanitation, spacing, variety selection, environmental management).

---

## D

**Damping Off** - Seedling disease caused by various fungi (Pythium, Rhizoctonia); causes seedling collapse.

**Diatomaceous Earth (DE)** - Fossilized algae powder with sharp edges; damages soft-bodied insects' exoskeletons.

**Disease Resistance** - Plant's genetic ability to prevent or reduce disease; indicated by codes on seed packets (V, F, T, etc.).

**Downy Mildew** - Fungal disease causing yellow angular patches and purple/gray fuzz on leaf undersides; requires moisture.

---

## E

**Economic Injury Level (EIL)** - The pest density at which crop value loss equals the cost of control; higher than action threshold.

**Economic Threshold** - See Action Threshold.

**Encarsia formosa** - Tiny parasitoid wasp that controls greenhouse whitefly.

**Entomopathogenic** - Disease-causing to insects (e.g., entomopathogenic nematodes, fungi).

**EPA Registration Number** - U.S. Environmental Protection Agency identification number on pesticide labels; legally required.

**Exclusion** - Preventing pests from entering facility through physical barriers (screens, sealing, quarantine).

---

## F

**Formulation** - How a pesticide active ingredient is prepared for use (liquid concentrate, wettable powder, ready-to-use, etc.).

**Fungicide** - Pesticide that kills or inhibits fungi.

**Fungus Gnats** - Small dark flies whose larvae live in growing media and feed on roots and organic matter.

---

## G

**Generation Time** - Time from egg to egg-laying adult; how quickly pest populations can grow.

---

## H

**Hand Lens** - Magnifying tool (typically 10-20x) essential for pest identification.

**Honeydew** - Sugary excretion produced by sap-feeding insects (aphids, whiteflies, scales); leads to sooty mold.

**Horticultural Oil** - Refined petroleum or plant oil used as insecticide; works by suffocation.

**Host Plant Resistance** - Plant's genetic ability to resist pest attack or damage.

---

## I

**Infestation** - Presence of pest population on plants or in facility.

**Inoculative Release** - Releasing small numbers of beneficial insects intended to establish breeding populations.

**Instar** - Developmental stage between molts in insects and mites.

**Insecticidal Soap** - Potassium salts of fatty acids; disrupts insect cell membranes.

**Integrated Pest Management (IPM)** - Comprehensive approach using multiple tactics to manage pests economically while minimizing environmental and health risks.

**Inundative Release** - Releasing large numbers of beneficial insects for immediate pest reduction (not long-term establishment).

**IRAC** - Insecticide Resistance Action Committee; classifies insecticides by mode of action.

---

## L

**Larva (plural: larvae)** - Immature, wingless, worm-like stage of insects with complete metamorphosis.

**Leafminer** - Insect larvae that tunnel between leaf surfaces, creating visible trails or blotches.

---

## M

**Mass Trapping** - Using high density of traps to reduce pest populations, not just monitoring.

**Mealybug** - Soft-bodied insect covered in white waxy coating; produces honeydew.

**Microbial Pesticide** - Living microorganisms used as pesticides (Bt, Beauveria bassiana, beneficial nematodes).

**Mode of Action (MOA)** - How a pesticide kills or affects a pest; rotating MOAs prevents resistance.

**Monitoring** - Regular systematic observation to detect and track pest populations.

**Mummy** - Parasitized aphid that appears tan, bloated, and papery; contains developing parasitoid wasp.

---

## N

**Neem Oil** - Botanical pesticide from neem tree; contains azadirachtin (insect growth regulator).

**Nematodes (Beneficial)** - Microscopic roundworms that parasitize insect pests; applied as drench to media.

**Nymph** - Immature stage of insects with incomplete metamorphosis (looks like small adult).

---

## P

**Parasitoid** - Organism that lives on or in a host insect, eventually killing it (e.g., wasps that lay eggs in aphids).

**Parthenogenesis** - Reproduction without mating; allows aphids to reproduce rapidly.

**Pathogen** - Disease-causing organism (fungus, bacterium, virus).

**Pesticide** - Substance used to kill or control pests; includes insecticides, fungicides, herbicides, etc.

**PHI (Pre-Harvest Interval)** - Minimum days between pesticide application and harvest; ensures safe residue levels.

**Physical Control** - Pest management using physical methods (traps, barriers, temperature, removal).

**Phytoseiulus persimilis** - Predatory mite that feeds on two-spotted spider mites.

**Powdery Mildew** - Fungal disease appearing as white powder on plant surfaces; doesn't require free moisture.

**PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)** - Safety gear required when handling pesticides (gloves, goggles, respirator, etc.).

**Predator** - Organism that kills and eats multiple prey organisms during its lifetime.

**Pythium** - Water mold causing root rot; favored by waterlogged conditions.

---

## Q

**Quarantine** - Isolating new plants for observation period before introducing to production area.

---

## R

**REI (Re-Entry Interval)** - Time after pesticide application before workers can safely re-enter treated area without PPE.

**Resistance (Pesticide)** - Genetic ability of pest population to survive pesticide applications that previously controlled them.

**Resistant Variety** - Plant variety with genetic resistance to specific pests or diseases.

**Rouging** - Removing and destroying entire plants (usually due to disease or severe infestation).

---

## S

**Sanitation** - Removal of plant debris, weeds, and other pest breeding sites; cornerstone of IPM.

**Scale Insect** - Immobile insect covered by protective shell; produces honeydew.

**Scouting** - Systematic inspection of crops for pests, diseases, and crop health.

**Signal Word** - Word on pesticide label indicating toxicity (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION).

**Sooty Mold** - Black fungus growing on honeydew; doesn't directly harm plants but blocks light.

**Spider Mites** - Tiny arachnids (8 legs) that feed on plant sap; produce fine webbing.

**Spinosad** - Biopesticide from soil bacterium; effective against thrips, caterpillars, leafminers.

**Sticky Trap** - Monitoring tool with adhesive surface to catch flying insects; yellow and blue common colors.

**Systemic** - Pesticide absorbed by plant and moved through vascular system; or plant-wide virus infection.

---

## T

**Threshold** - See Action Threshold.

**Thrips** - Tiny, slender insects that rasp plant cells and suck contents; can transmit viruses.

**Translaminar** - Pesticide that moves through leaf from one surface to the other (but not systemically through whole plant).

**Trap Crop** - Plant more attractive to pests than main crop; pests concentrated on trap crop which is then destroyed.

---

## V

**Vector** - Organism that transmits disease (e.g., aphids transmit plant viruses).

**Virus** - Microscopic pathogen causing plant diseases; no cure, only prevention and removal.

---

## W

**Whitefly** - Tiny white sap-feeding insect; multiple life stages on leaf undersides.

**WPS (Worker Protection Standard)** - U.S. federal regulation protecting agricultural workers from pesticide exposure.

---

## Abbreviations

**AI** - Active Ingredient
**CAUTION** - Signal word for low toxicity pesticides (Category III-IV)
**CEA** - Controlled Environment Agriculture
**DANGER** - Signal word for highly toxic pesticides (Category I)
**DE** - Diatomaceous Earth
**DLI** - Daily Light Integral
**DM** - Downy Mildew
**DTM** - Days to Maturity
**EIL** - Economic Injury Level
**EPA** - Environmental Protection Agency
**FRAC** - Fungicide Resistance Action Committee
**HAF** - Horizontal Air Flow
**IGR** - Insect Growth Regulator
**IPM** - Integrated Pest Management
**IRAC** - Insecticide Resistance Action Committee
**LD50** - Lethal Dose, 50% (toxicity measure)
**MOA** - Mode of Action
**OMRI** - Organic Materials Review Institute
**PHI** - Pre-Harvest Interval
**PM** - Powdery Mildew
**PPE** - Personal Protective Equipment
**REI** - Re-Entry Interval
**RH** - Relative Humidity
**TMV** - Tobacco Mosaic Virus
**ToMV** - Tomato Mosaic Virus
**TSWV** - Tomato Spotted Wilt Virus
**WARNING** - Signal word for moderately toxic pesticides (Category II)
**WPS** - Worker Protection Standard
