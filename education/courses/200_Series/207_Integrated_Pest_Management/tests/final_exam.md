### Section D: Short Answer

**36.** Acceptable components:
- Prevention/cultural controls
- Regular monitoring and scouting
- Action thresholds
- Multiple control tactics (biological, physical, chemical)
- Record keeping and documentation
- Staff training
- Program evaluation and adjustment

**37.** Rotating modes of action prevents resistance development. When same chemical family used repeatedly, resistant individuals survive and reproduce, making pesticides ineffective. Rotation maintains effectiveness and provides sustainable long-term control.

**38.** Monitoring: 1 trap per 500-1000 sq ft - detects pest presence, tracks populations. Mass trapping: 1 trap per 25-50 sq ft - actually reduces pest populations through capture. Use monitoring for ongoing surveillance, mass trapping when populations building.

**39.** Acceptable cultural practices:
- Daily removal of plant debris/sanitation
- Proper plant spacing for airflow
- Drip irrigation (not overhead)
- Disease-resistant varieties
- Quarantine incoming plants
- Screening on vents
- Optimal environmental conditions
- Appropriate watering/fertilization

**40.** Wrong identification leads to wrong treatment and wasted money/time. Different pests require different control methods. Accurate ID enables selection of most effective, efficient, economical tactics. Prevents unnecessary pesticide use and environmental harm.
