## Trapping Systems

### Sticky Traps (Mass Trapping)

```
    When used in HIGH DENSITY, sticky traps control populations

    MONITORING vs. MASS TRAPPING:

    Monitoring Density:           Mass Trapping Density:
    1 trap per 500-1000 sq ft    1 trap per 25-50 sq ft

         [Y]                      [Y][Y][Y][Y][Y]
                                  [Y][Y][Y][Y][Y]
                                  [Y][Y][Y][Y][Y]
         [Y]                      [Y][Y][Y][Y][Y]

    EFFECTIVE FOR:
    • Whiteflies (yellow traps)
    • Fungus gnats (yellow horizontal traps on media)
    • Thrips (blue traps)
    • Shore flies

    LIMITATIONS:
    • Catches beneficial insects too
    • Must replace frequently when full
    • Labor intensive
    • Ongoing cost

    BEST PRACTICES:
    • Place just above canopy height
    • Both sides sticky for double surface
    • Replace when 50% covered or weekly
    • Combine with other controls
```

## Mechanical Removal

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                                           ║
║                    HAND REMOVAL TECHNIQUES                                ║
║                                                                           ║
║   PICKING/CRUSHING:                                                       ║
║   ══════════════                                                          ║
║   Best for: Large insects (caterpillars, beetles, slugs)                ║
║   Method: Hand pick and destroy                                          ║
║   Timing: Early morning (pests less active)                              ║
║   Frequency: Daily during outbreaks                                      ║
║   Tools: Gloves, collection container with soapy water                   ║
║                                                                           ║
║   PRUNING INFESTED PARTS:                                                 ║
║   ════════════════════════                                                ║
║   Best for: Localized infestations, diseased tissue                      ║
║   Method:                                                                 ║
║   1. Identify heavily infested leaves/branches                           ║
║   2. Cut below infestation                                               ║
║   3. Place immediately in sealed bag                                     ║
║   4. Remove from facility and destroy                                    ║
║   5. Sanitize tools between cuts                                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   CAUTION: Don't shake plants (spreads pests to clean areas!)            ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ROUGING (Removing Entire Plants):                                       ║
║   ═══════════════════════════════════                                     ║
║   When: Severe infestation or virus                                      ║
║   Method:                                                                 ║
║   1. Bag plant while still in place                                      ║
║   2. Remove carefully (don't shake)                                      ║
║   3. Seal bag                                                             ║
║   4. Remove from facility immediately                                    ║
║   5. Sanitize area and tools                                             ║
║                                                                           ║
║   Decision criteria:                                                      ║
║   • Cost to treat > value of plant = Remove                              ║
║   • Virus present = Remove immediately                                   ║
║   • Source of ongoing infestation = Remove                               ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   WATER SPRAY:                                                            ║
║   ═══════════                                                             ║
║   Best for: Aphids, spider mites                                         ║
║   Method: High-pressure water spray (hose or sprayer)                    ║
║   Target: Undersides of leaves                                           ║
║   Timing: Early in day (plants dry before night)                         ║
║   Frequency: Every 2-3 days until controlled                             ║
║                                                                           ║
║   PROS:                           CONS:                                   ║
║   • No chemicals                  • Creates humidity (disease risk)      ║
║   • Effective on soft-bodied     • Doesn't kill, just dislodges         ║
║   • Reduces populations          • Pests may return to plants            ║
║                                   • Wet foliage                           ║
║                                                                           ║
║   BEST FOR: Small-scale, low pest populations                            ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   VACUUMING:                                                              ║
║   ══════════                                                              ║
║   Best for: Whiteflies, adult fungus gnats                               ║
║   Equipment: Handheld vacuum or specialized insect vacuum                ║
║   Method:                                                                 ║
║   • Walk through crop with vacuum                                        ║
║   • Disturb plants to make adults fly                                    ║
║   • Vacuum flying insects                                                ║
║   • Empty into soapy water or freeze bag                                 ║
║                                                                           ║
║   Effectiveness: Reduces adult populations but not eggs/nymphs           ║
║   Best use: Supplement to other controls                                 ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Temperature Manipulation

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                                           ║
║                    HEAT TREATMENT                                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   CONCEPT: Most pests cannot survive prolonged high temperatures         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   PEST MORTALITY TEMPERATURES:                                            ║
║   ════════════════════════════                                            ║
║   Pest            Lethal Temp      Duration                               ║
║   ─────────────────────────────────────────                              ║
║   Spider mites    110°F (43°C)     10-30 minutes                         ║
║   Aphids          120°F (49°C)     10-20 minutes                         ║
║   Whiteflies      110°F (43°C)     20-30 minutes                         ║
║   Thrips          120°F (49°C)     20-30 minutes                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   Most plants tolerate 110-120°F for short periods                       ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────              ║
║                                                                           ║
║   APPLICATION METHODS:                                                    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   1. GREENHOUSE HEAT TREATMENT                                            ║
║      • Close all vents                                                   ║
║      • Heat greenhouse to 110-120°F                                      ║
║      • Maintain for 30-60 minutes                                        ║
║      • Monitor plant stress carefully                                    ║
║      • Best done on empty facility or between crops                      ║
║                                                                           ║
║   2. STEAM STERILIZATION                                                  ║
║      • For growing media, pots, equipment                                ║
║      • 180°F for 30 minutes                                              ║
║      • Kills pests, pathogens, weed seeds                                ║
║                                                                           ║
║   3. SOLARIZATION (Outdoor/High tunnels)                                  ║
║      • Cover moist soil with clear plastic                               ║
║      • Solar heat raises soil temp to 110-140°F                          ║
║      • 4-6 weeks in summer                                               ║
║      • Kills soilborne pests and diseases                                ║
║                                                                           ║
║   LIMITATIONS:                                                            ║
║   • Risk of plant damage                                                 ║
║   • Requires facility downtime                                           ║
║   • Energy intensive                                                     ║
║   • Not practical with plants present (usually)                          ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════    ║
║                                                                           ║
║                    COLD TREATMENT                                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   Most greenhouse pests are tropical/subtropical origin                  ║
║   → Vulnerable to cold                                                    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   APPLICATIONS:                                                           ║
║                                                                           ║
║   1. Winter cold break (temperate greenhouses):                          ║
║      • Turn off heat for 24-48 hours in winter                           ║
║      • Allow temperature to drop to 35-45°F                              ║
║      • Kills many pests, suppresses reproduction                         ║
║      • Use on cold-tolerant crops only!                                  ║
║                                                                           ║
║   2. Quarantine cold storage:                                             ║
║      • Hold incoming plants at 35-40°F for 24-48 hours                   ║
║      • Suppresses pest activity                                          ║
║      • Easier to inspect dormant plants                                  ║
║                                                                           ║
║   LIMITATIONS:                                                            ║
║   • Crop damage risk                                                     ║
║   • Not feasible in tropical/warm climates                               ║
║   • Production interruption                                              ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Barriers & Exclusion

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                                           ║
║   PHYSICAL BARRIERS:                                                      ║
║   ══════════════════                                                      ║
║                                                                           ║
║   INSECT SCREENING (Covered in Module 5):                                ║
║   • 50-80 mesh on all vents and openings                                 ║
║   • Keeps pests out of facility                                          ║
║   • Most cost-effective physical control!                                ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ROW COVERS (For pest exclusion):                                        ║
║   • Lightweight fabric draped over crops                                 ║
║   • Excludes flying insects                                              ║
║   • Allows light, air, water penetration                                 ║
║   • Remove for pollination if needed                                     ║
║   • Best for: Leafy greens, early protection                             ║
║                                                                           ║
║   TRUNK/STEM BARRIERS:                                                    ║
║   • Sticky bands around stems                                            ║
║   • Prevent crawling insects from ascending                              ║
║   • Useful for ants (aphid farmers)                                      ║
║   • Replace when dusty or full                                           ║
║                                                                           ║
║   COPPER BARRIERS (for slugs/snails):                                     ║
║   • Copper tape or mesh                                                  ║
║   • Creates mild electric shock to mollusks                              ║
║   • Place around bench legs or perimeter                                 ║
║                                                                           ║
║   DIATOMACEOUS EARTH (DE):                                                ║
║   • Fossilized algae with sharp edges                                    ║
║   • Damages soft-bodied insects' exoskeleton                             ║
║   • Apply as dust barrier                                                ║
║   • Only works when DRY                                                  ║
║   • Good for crawling insects, slugs                                     ║
║   • Use food-grade DE only                                               ║
║   • Wear dust mask when applying                                         ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Substrate & Media Management

```
    PREVENTING ROOT-ZONE PESTS:

    [1] MEDIA STERILIZATION
        • Steam treatment (180°F for 30 min)
        • Baking (200°F for 30 min)
        • Microwave (small quantities)
        • Commercial pasteurization

    [2] USE STERILE/SOILLESS MEDIA
        • Commercial growing mixes
        • Avoid field soil in greenhouses
        • Fresh media each crop cycle

    [3] PROPER DRAINAGE
        • Prevents waterlogging
        • Reduces fungus gnats
        • Stops root rot pathogens

    [4] SURFACE COVERS
        • Layer of sand or perlite on top
        • Reduces fungus gnat breeding
        • Prevents algae growth
        • Reflects light to lower leaves

    [5] MINIMIZE ORGANIC MATTER
        • Don't use uncomposted materials
        • Remove plant debris promptly
        • Keep growing area clean
```

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Physical controls are non-chemical** pest management tactics using barriers, removal, traps, and environmental manipulation
2. **Trapping has two roles**: Monitoring (low density) and mass trapping (high density for control)
3. **Mechanical removal** (hand-picking, pruning, vacuuming) is labor-intensive but immediately effective
4. **Temperature extremes** can kill pests but risk crop damage - use carefully
5. **Barriers and exclusion** (screens, row covers) prevent pests from reaching crops
6. **Physical controls work best** integrated with cultural, biological, and chemical tactics

### Physical Control Decision Matrix

| Pest | Best Physical Control | When to Use |
|------|-----------------------|-------------|
| Whiteflies | Yellow sticky traps (mass trapping) | Prevention & early infestations |
| Aphids | Water spray, pruning infested parts | Small populations, localized |
| Spider Mites | Water spray, increase humidity | Early detection, sensitive crops |
| Fungus Gnats | Yellow traps on media, reduce watering | Always (monitoring + trapping) |
| Caterpillars | Hand-picking, row covers | Small numbers, high-value crops |
| Thrips | Blue sticky traps, remove flowers | Prevention, monitoring |
| Diseases | Remove infected tissue, improve airflow | First symptoms, prevent spread |
