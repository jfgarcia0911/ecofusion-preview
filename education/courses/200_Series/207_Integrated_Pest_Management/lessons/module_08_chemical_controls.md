## Pesticide Categories

### By Origin

```
    BIOPESTICIDES (Natural origin):
    ═══════════════════════════════

    • Botanical: Plant-derived (neem, pyrethrins, essential oils)
    • Microbial: Beneficial organisms (Bt, Beauveria bassiana)
    • Biochemical: Naturally-occurring substances (insect hormones, pheromones)

    Advantages: Lower toxicity, rapid breakdown, minimal residues
    Limitations: Often slower acting, may need repeat applications

    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    SYNTHETIC PESTICIDES (Lab-created):
    ══════════════════════════════════

    • Organophosphates
    • Carbamates
    • Pyrethroids
    • Neonicotinoids
    • Many others

    Advantages: Fast-acting, longer residual, often cheaper
    Limitations: Higher toxicity, resistance issues, environmental persistence
```

│   ════════                                                                │

## Resistance Management

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                                           ║
║                    PESTICIDE RESISTANCE                                   ║
║                                                                           ║
║   What is it? Pest population becomes less sensitive to pesticide        ║
║                                                                           ║
║   How it develops:                                                        ║
║   1. Pesticide kills susceptible individuals                             ║
║   2. Resistant individuals survive and reproduce                         ║
║   3. Resistance genes increase in population                             ║
║   4. Pesticide becomes ineffective                                       ║
║                                                                           ║
║   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   PREVENTING RESISTANCE:                                                  ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [1] ROTATE MODES OF ACTION (MOA)                                       ║
║       • Don't use same chemical family repeatedly                        ║
║       • Check IRAC/FRAC codes on labels                                  ║
║       • Alternate between different MOA groups                           ║
║                                                                           ║
║       Example rotation for aphids:                                       ║
║       Week 1: Insecticidal soap (no MOA group)                           ║
║       Week 4: Neem oil (IGR - Group UN)                                  ║
║       Week 7: Pyrethrin (Group 3) - if necessary                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [2] USE FULL LABELED RATES                                             ║
║       • Under-dosing selects for resistance                              ║
║       • Follow label exactly                                             ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [3] ENSURE COMPLETE COVERAGE                                            ║
║       • Pests that survive due to poor coverage can be resistant        ║
║       • Spray to runoff when label allows                                ║
║       • Hit undersides of leaves                                         ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [4] INTEGRATE WITH NON-CHEMICAL TACTICS                                ║
║       • Cultural, biological, physical controls                          ║
║       • Reduces selection pressure                                       ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [5] USE PESTICIDES ONLY WHEN NEEDED                                    ║
║       • Follow thresholds                                                ║
║       • Fewer applications = less resistance pressure                    ║
║                                                                           ║
║   [6] TANK MIXTURES (when labeled)                                       ║
║       • Combine products with different MOA                              ║
║       • Check label for compatibility                                    ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### IRAC Mode of Action Groups

```
    Common groups for greenhouse pests:

    Group   Mode of Action              Examples
    ═════   ══════════════              ════════
    1A      Acetylcholinesterase        Organophosphates
            inhibitors                   (Acephate, Malathion)

    1B      Acetylcholinesterase        Carbamates
            inhibitors                   (Carbaryl)

    3A      Sodium channel              Pyrethrins, Pyrethroids
            modulators                   (Bifenthrin, Permethrin)

    4A      Nicotinic acetylcholine     Neonicotinoids
            receptor agonists            (Imidacloprid, Thiamethoxam)

    5       Nicotinic acetylcholine     Spinosad
            receptor allosteric

    23      Inhibitors of lipid         Insecticidal soaps, oils
            synthesis

    UN      Unknown or non-specific     Neem oil (azadirachtin)

    --      Microbials                  Bt, Beauveria bassiana

    ROTATE BETWEEN DIFFERENT NUMBERED GROUPS!
```

## Application Techniques

```
    SPRAY COVERAGE TIPS:

    • Start at far end, work backward (don't walk through spray)
    • Spray undersides of leaves (most pests there)
    • Spray to runoff (when label allows)
    • Overlap spray pattern slightly
    • Use appropriate nozzle (fine mist for insects, coarse for diseases)
    • Maintain consistent walking speed
    • Apply when plants dry (unless label says otherwise)
    • Avoid windy conditions (>5 mph)
    • Ideal temperature: 65-80°F (check label)
    • Avoid spraying in bright sun (phytotoxicity risk)
```

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Chemical controls are the top of IPM pyramid** - use only when other tactics insufficient
2. **Always choose least-toxic effective option** - insecticidal soaps and oils before synthetics
3. **The label is the law** - follow all instructions exactly
4. **Rotate modes of action** - prevent resistance development
5. **Safety first** - PPE, proper mixing, application techniques, REI compliance
6. **Document everything** - legal requirement and good practice

### Recommended Product Ladder (Least to Most Toxic)

1. Insecticidal soap (soft-bodied insects)
2. Horticultural oil (broad, suffocating action)
3. Neem oil/azadirachtin (IGR, multiple pests)
4. Spinosad (thrips, caterpillars, leafminers)
5. Pyrethrins (fast knockdown, last resort)
6. Synthetic pesticides (only when absolutely necessary)

**Always integrate with cultural, biological, and physical controls!**
