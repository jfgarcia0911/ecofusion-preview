## Course 207: IPM - Cheatsheet

## Predators

### LADYBUGS (Lady Beetles)
**Target:** Aphids, scale crawlers
**Release Rate:** 2-10 per plant
**Best For:** Inundative releases (don't establish well indoors)
**ID:** Red/orange with black spots, larvae orange with black
**Notes:** Adults and larvae both feed; larvae eat MORE

---

### GREEN LACEWINGS
**Target:** Aphids, spider mites, thrips, whitefly eggs
**Release Rate:** 1-5 eggs per plant weekly
**Best For:** Broad predation
**ID:** Adults green with lacy wings; larvae brown "aphid lions"
**Notes:** Only larvae are predatory; adults feed on pollen/nectar

---

### PREDATORY MITES
**Phytoseiulus persimilis**
- Target: Two-spotted spider mites (specialist)
- Rate: 2-10 per plant
- Needs: Humidity >60%, temps 65-80°F
- ID: Orange/red, very fast-moving

**Neoseiulus californicus**
- Target: Various spider mites (generalist)
- Rate: 5-25 per plant
- Needs: Tolerates lower humidity
- ID: Tan/light colored

**Amblyseius swirskii**
- Target: Whiteflies, thrips
- Rate: 50-100 per plant
- Needs: Warm temps, preventive releases
- ID: Cream colored

**Stratiolaelaps scimitus (Hypoaspis miles)**
- Target: Fungus gnat larvae, thrips pupae
- Rate: 50-100 per sq ft (media drench)
- Needs: Moist media
- ID: Brown, lives in soil/media

## Parasitoids

### APHID PARASITOIDS
**Aphidius colemani**
- Target: Green peach aphid, melon aphid
- Rate: 1-3 per aphid
- Cycle: 2-3 weeks egg to adult
- ID: Tiny black wasps; tan aphid "mummies" with exit holes
- Notes: Banker plants support continuous production

**Aphidius ervi**
- Target: Larger aphid species
- Similar to A. colemani but for different aphids

---

### WHITEFLY PARASITOIDS
**Encarsia formosa**
- Target: Greenhouse whitefly
- Rate: 3-5 per plant weekly
- Needs: 12+ hrs light, temps 65-85°F
- ID: Tiny yellow wasp; parasitized scales turn BLACK
- Notes: Establishes well in greenhouses

**Eretmocerus eremicus**
- Target: Silverleaf whitefly (Bemisia)
- Rate: 3-10 per plant
- Needs: Warmer temps than Encarsia
- ID: Yellow wasp; scales turn yellow/brown

## Microbial Biocontrols

### BT (Bacillus thuringiensis)
**Btk (kurstaki):** Caterpillars
**Bti (israelensis):** Fungus gnat larvae, mosquitoes
**Application:** Spray (Btk) or drench (Bti)
**Mode:** Must be eaten; toxin disrupts gut
**Safety:** Non-toxic to humans, beneficials, pollinators

---

### BEAUVERIA BASSIANA
**Target:** Whiteflies, aphids, thrips, fungus gnats
**Products:** BotaniGard, Mycotrol
**Application:** Spray plants and pests
**Needs:** Humidity >80% for 12+ hours
**Mode:** Fungal spores penetrate insect, grow inside

---

### BENEFICIAL NEMATODES
**Steinernema feltiae**
- Target: Fungus gnat larvae, thrips pupae
- Application: Media drench
- Conditions: Moist media, 55-85°F

**Heterorhabditis bacteriophora**
- Target: Root weevils, grubs
- Application: Media drench
- Conditions: Similar to Steinernema

**Storage:** Refrigerate, use quickly (short shelf life)

## Release Strategies

### INOCULATIVE
**Goal:** Establish reproducing population
**Rate:** Low numbers
**Frequency:** Once or few times
**Best for:** Long crops, enclosed environments
**Examples:** Predatory mites, parasitoids

### INUNDATIVE
**Goal:** Immediate knockdown
**Rate:** High numbers
**Frequency:** Repeat as needed
**Best for:** Acute infestations
**Examples:** Ladybugs, large lacewing releases

### PREVENTIVE
**Goal:** Maintain constant protection
**Rate:** Regular small releases
**Frequency:** Weekly or biweekly
**Best for:** High-value crops
**Examples:** Most beneficials on schedule

## Banker Plants for Aphids

**Crop:** Barley, wheat, or oats
**Pest:** Bird cherry-oat aphid (harmless to crops)
**Beneficial:** Aphidius colemani (parasitoid)
**Setup:** 1 pot per 100-200 sq ft
**Replace:** Every 4-8 weeks
**Benefit:** Continuous parasitoid production

## Compatibility Quick Check

**SAFE (Compatible):**
- Insecticidal soaps
- Horticultural oils
- Bt products
- Most fungicides

**CAUTION:**
- Neem oil
- Pyrethrins

**AVOID:**
- Synthetic pyrethroids
- Neonicotinoids
- Organophosphates
- Broad-spectrum insecticides

**RULE:** Always check product label for beneficial insect safety!

## Troubleshooting Biocontrol

**Problem:** Beneficials not establishing
**Solutions:**
- Check environmental conditions (temp, humidity)
- Ensure pest food source present
- Avoid incompatible pesticides
- Verify correct species for pest
- Increase release rates

**Problem:** Beneficials present but pests increasing
**Solutions:**
- Pest population may be too high (use knockdown first)
- Wrong beneficial for pest
- Environmental conditions favor pests
- Supplement with other tactics
