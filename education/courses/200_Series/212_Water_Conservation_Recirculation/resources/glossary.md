# Water Management Glossary
## Course 212: Water Conservation & Recirculation

---

## A

**Activated Carbon** - Highly porous carbon material used to remove chlorine, chloramine, organic compounds, and odors from water through adsorption.

**Aeration** - The process of introducing air into water to increase dissolved oxygen levels.

**Algae** - Photosynthetic organisms that can grow in nutrient-rich water when exposed to light; problematic in recirculating systems.

**Alkalinity** - The buffering capacity of water; ability to resist pH changes. Measured in ppm as CaCO₃. Target: 50-100 ppm for most systems.

**Aquifer** - Underground layer of water-bearing rock or sediment; source of groundwater.

---

## B

**Backwash** - The process of reversing water flow through a filter to remove accumulated solids and clean the filter media.

**Biofilm** - Layer of microorganisms attached to surfaces in contact with water; can harbor pathogens or be beneficial (biofilters).

**Biofilter** - A biological filtration system using beneficial bacteria to convert ammonia to nitrate (nitrification); essential in aquaponics.

**BOD (Biological Oxygen Demand)** - Amount of oxygen consumed by microorganisms breaking down organic matter; high BOD reduces dissolved oxygen.

---

## C

**Catchment Area** - Surface area (typically roof) used to collect rainwater.

**Catalytic Carbon** - Activated carbon modified to effectively remove chloramine; standard activated carbon is less effective.

**Chelate** - A compound that binds to metal ions (like iron) to keep them soluble and available to plants at various pH levels.

**Chloramine** - Disinfectant formed by combining chlorine and ammonia; more stable than chlorine but harmful to plants and beneficial bacteria.

**Chlorine** - Common municipal water disinfectant; must be removed before use in recirculating agricultural systems.

**Collection Efficiency** - Percentage of rainfall actually captured by a rainwater harvesting system (accounts for losses). Typically 85-95%.

**Conductivity** - See Electrical Conductivity (EC).

**Contact Time** - Duration water is exposed to a treatment process (e.g., UV exposure time, carbon filter residence time).

---

## D

**Discharge** - Water released from the system to waste/sewer; includes backwash, dumps, and leaks.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)** - Amount of oxygen dissolved in water, measured in mg/L or ppm. Critical for root health and beneficial bacteria.

**Dissolved Solids** - Minerals and salts dissolved in water; see TDS.

**DO Meter** - Instrument for measuring dissolved oxygen in water.

**Drum Filter** - Rotating cylindrical screen filter that continuously removes solids from water; common in aquaponics.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)** - Measure of water's ability to conduct electricity, indicating concentration of dissolved salts/nutrients. Measured in mS/cm or µS/cm.

**EDDHA** - Iron chelate effective at high pH (>6.5); more stable than EDTA in alkaline conditions.

**EDTA** - Common chelating agent for micronutrients; effective at pH <6.5.

**Evapotranspiration (ET)** - Combined water loss from evaporation and plant transpiration; largest water "loss" in recirculating systems (90-95%).

---

## F

**Feed Water** - Water entering a treatment system or process.

**Filtration Rate** - Flow rate through a filter per unit of filter area, measured in GPM per sq ft.

**First Flush Diverter** - Device that diverts initial contaminated runoff from a roof before rainwater collection.

**Flow Rate** - Volume of water moving through a system per unit time, typically measured in gallons per minute (GPM).

**Friction Loss** - Pressure drop caused by water flowing through pipes, fittings, and equipment.

---

## G

**GAC (Granular Activated Carbon)** - Form of activated carbon used in filtration; effective for chlorine removal.

**GPD (Gallons Per Day)** - Unit of measurement for water flow or production capacity.

**GPM (Gallons Per Minute)** - Common unit for measuring water flow rate in irrigation and recirculation systems.

**Greensand** - Manganese-coated filter media used for oxidizing and removing iron and manganese from water.

---

## H

**Hardness** - Concentration of calcium and magnesium in water, measured in ppm as CaCO₃. While problematic in household uses, moderate hardness is beneficial in agriculture (Ca and Mg are nutrients).

**Head** - Pressure in a pumping system expressed as height of water column (feet). See Total Dynamic Head.

**Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT)** - Average time water remains in a particular system component.

**Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂)** - Oxidizing agent sometimes used for water treatment or sanitation; breaks down to water and oxygen.

---

## I

**Ion Exchange** - Water treatment process that exchanges undesirable ions for acceptable ones; used in water softening (exchanges Ca/Mg for Na).

**Iron (Fe)** - Essential micronutrient for plants; can also be a contaminant in well water causing staining and precipitation.

---

## L

**Leachate** - Water that has percolated through a material (like growing media) and may contain dissolved or suspended materials.

---

## M

**Makeup Water** - Fresh water added to replace losses (evapotranspiration, discharge) in a recirculating system.

**Makeup Water Ratio (MWR)** - Percentage of system volume that must be replaced daily with fresh water.

**Membrane** - Semi-permeable barrier used in reverse osmosis or other separation processes.

**Micron (µm)** - Unit of measurement for particle size; 1 micron = 0.001 millimeter. Used to rate filters.

**mJ/cm²** - Millijoules per square centimeter; unit measuring UV dose/intensity.

**mS/cm** - Millisiemens per centimeter; common unit for measuring electrical conductivity (EC).

---

## N

**Nitrification** - Biological process converting ammonia (NH₃) to nitrite (NO₂) then nitrate (NO₃); performed by beneficial bacteria in biofilters.

**Nitrate (NO₃)** - Form of nitrogen readily used by plants; end product of nitrification. Target: 100-200 ppm in most systems.

**NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Units)** - Unit measuring water turbidity/cloudiness. Target: <5 NTU for effective UV sterilization.

---

## O

**ORP (Oxidation-Reduction Potential)** - Measure of water's ability to oxidize or reduce substances; used to monitor ozone effectiveness. Measured in millivolts (mV).

**Osmosis** - Movement of water across a semi-permeable membrane from low to high solute concentration.

**Oxidation** - Chemical reaction involving loss of electrons; used in water treatment to convert dissolved metals (Fe, Mn) to insoluble forms.

**Ozone (O₃)** - Powerful oxidizing agent used for water sterilization and organic matter breakdown.

---

## P

**Pathogen** - Disease-causing microorganism (bacteria, virus, fungus).

**Permeate** - Purified water product from a reverse osmosis system.

**pH** - Measure of acidity/alkalinity on scale of 0-14. Target for most hydroponic crops: 5.5-6.5.

**Potable Water** - Water safe for human consumption.

**ppm (Parts Per Million)** - Unit of concentration; 1 ppm = 1 mg/L for water solutions.

**Precipitation** - Formation of solid particles from dissolved substances; can occur when pH rises or solutions become concentrated.

---

## Q

**Quartz Sleeve** - Clear tube protecting UV lamp while allowing UV transmission; must be kept clean for effective sterilization.

---

## R

**Recirculation** - The process of reusing water by treating and returning it to the growing system rather than discharging to waste.

**Recirculation Rate** - Percentage of water that is reused rather than discharged. Target: >90-95% for efficient systems.

**Recovery Rate** - In RO systems, percentage of feed water that becomes product water (permeate). Typical: 25-50%.

**Reject Water** - Concentrated waste stream from reverse osmosis containing removed contaminants.

**Retention Time** - Time water remains in a particular treatment process or system component.

**Reverse Osmosis (RO)** - Water purification process using pressure to force water through semi-permeable membrane, removing 95-99% of dissolved solids.

---

## S

**Salinity** - Concentration of dissolved salts in water.

**Sand Filter** - Filtration system using sand media to remove particles (typically 20-50 microns). Common in recirculating systems.

**Screen Filter** - Coarse filter using mesh or perforated screen to remove large particles (100-500+ microns).

**Settling** - Allowing suspended solids to settle out of water by gravity; first stage in many filtration systems.

**Softening** - Process of removing calcium and magnesium (hardness) from water, typically by ion exchange.

**Sterilization** - Process of killing or inactivating microorganisms; accomplished by UV, ozone, or chemical treatment.

**Sump** - Collection tank or reservoir where water gathers before being pumped; typically lowest point in system.

**Suspended Solids** - Particles suspended in water that have not dissolved or settled; measured as turbidity.

---

## T

**TDH (Total Dynamic Head)** - Total equivalent height a pump must work against; includes vertical lift, friction losses, and pressure requirements.

**TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)** - Total concentration of dissolved substances in water, measured in ppm. Approximate conversion: EC (mS/cm) × 500 = TDS (ppm).

**Transpiration** - Process of water movement through plants and evaporation from leaves; major component of water loss in CEA.

**Turbidity** - Cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles; measured in NTU. High turbidity blocks UV light and reduces sterilization effectiveness.

**Turnover Time** - Time required for entire system volume to cycle through treatment and back to growing area.

---

## U

**UV (Ultraviolet)** - Electromagnetic radiation used for water sterilization. UV-C (254 nm wavelength) damages pathogen DNA/RNA.

**UV Dose** - Amount of UV energy delivered to water, measured in mJ/cm². Standard agricultural dose: 30-40 mJ/cm².

**UV Transmittance (UVT)** - Percentage of UV light that passes through water; reduced by turbidity and dissolved organics. Target: >85% for effective sterilization.

**µS/cm** - Microsiemens per centimeter; unit for measuring electrical conductivity. 1,000 µS/cm = 1 mS/cm.

---

## V

**VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)** - Electronic device that controls motor speed; used on pumps for energy efficiency and flow control.

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)** - Difference between actual water vapor in air and saturated capacity; drives transpiration rate. Optimal: 0.8-1.2 kPa.

---

## W

**Water Balance** - Accounting of all water inputs and outputs in a system; should balance within ±5%.

**Water Footprint** - Total volume of freshwater used to produce goods; measured as gallons per pound of crop.

**Water Productivity** - Economic value generated per unit of water used; measured as $ per m³.

**Water Quality** - Physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of water.

**Water Use Efficiency (WUE)** - Crop yield per unit of water consumed; measured as kg or lb per gallon or m³. Lower numbers indicate more efficient water use.

**Well Water** - Groundwater extracted from aquifer via well; quality varies by location.

---

## Z

**Zone** - Separate section of growing system with independent water management; used to isolate crops or risks in modular systems.
