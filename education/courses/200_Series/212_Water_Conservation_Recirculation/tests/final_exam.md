### Section C: Short Answer

**26.** Any four of: pH, EC/TDS, Temperature, Dissolved Oxygen, (also acceptable: Nitrate, Turbidity)

**27.** First flush diverter removes initial contaminated runoff from roof (dust, bird droppings, atmospheric pollution). It diverts the first 0.01-0.02 inches of rainfall to waste before allowing cleaner water to flow to storage. Chamber fills first, then overflow goes to tank.

**28.** Any three of: Vertical lift, Friction losses in pipe, Fitting losses (elbows, valves), Elevation changes, Equipment head requirements

**29.** Centralized: Single treatment system for entire facility; advantage is lower cost and simpler operation. Zoned: Separate loops for different areas; advantage is disease isolation and crop-specific management.

**30.** Water softening adds sodium to replace calcium/magnesium. Sodium accumulates in recirculating systems and is harmful to plants. Better alternatives are RO or accepting hardness (Ca/Mg are beneficial nutrients).

### Section E: Case Analysis

**34a.**
Current: 50,000 gal/month
At 70% recirculation: ~15,000 gal makeup (evap + discharge)
Evapotranspiration estimate: ~10,000 gal/month
At 95% recirculation: 10,000 + 5% discharge = ~10,500 gal/month
Reduction: ~79% (39,500 gal/month saved)

**34b.**
Rainwater: 10,000 × 38 × 0.623 × 0.9 = 213,018 gal/year = ~17,750 gal/month
Projected need: 10,500 gal/month
Result: Rainwater exceeds needs; provides 100%+ (with adequate storage)

**34c.**
1. Filtration system (sand filter + pre-filter): $5,000-7,000
2. UV sterilization (40-60 GPM): $2,000-3,500
3. Rainwater system (tank, collection, treatment): $8,000-12,000
(Also accept: monitoring equipment, pumps, reservoir, automation)

**34d.**
Budget appears adequate but tight:
- Recirculation upgrade: ~$15,000-20,000
- Rainwater system: ~$10,000-15,000
- Total: ~$25,000-35,000
With careful selection and possibly phased implementation, $35,000 is realistic for basic systems, but may need to prioritize or reduce scope.
