# Glossary of Plant Science Terms
## Course 104: Plant Science Essentials

---

## A

**Abscisic Acid (ABA):** A plant hormone involved in stress responses, dormancy, and stomatal closure.

**Abscission:** The natural shedding of leaves, flowers, or fruit from a plant, often triggered by ethylene.

**Active Transport:** Movement of substances across cell membranes against a concentration gradient, requiring energy (ATP).

**Aerobic Respiration:** Cellular respiration that requires oxygen, producing 36-38 ATP per glucose molecule.

**Anaerobic Respiration:** Respiration without oxygen, producing only 2 ATP per glucose and potentially toxic byproducts.

**Anthocyanins:** Red, purple, or blue pigments found in plant tissues, often produced in response to stress.

**Apical Dominance:** The phenomenon where the main central stem grows more strongly than lateral branches, controlled by auxin.

**Apical Meristem:** The growth region at the tips of roots and shoots, responsible for primary (lengthwise) growth.

**Apoplastic Pathway:** Movement of water and solutes through cell walls and intercellular spaces, outside the cell membranes.

**ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate):** The primary energy currency of cells, produced during respiration.

**Auxin:** A class of plant hormones that promote cell elongation, root initiation, and apical dominance.

---

## B

**Bolting:** Premature flowering in vegetable crops, often triggered by long days or heat stress.

**Boron (B):** A micronutrient essential for cell wall formation and sugar transport.

**Bundle Sheath Cells:** Specialized cells surrounding vascular bundles in C4 plants where the Calvin cycle occurs.

---

## C

**C3 Plants:** Plants that fix CO₂ directly via RuBisCO, producing a 3-carbon compound as the first product.

**C4 Plants:** Plants that concentrate CO₂ before the Calvin cycle, reducing photorespiration in hot conditions.

**Calcium (Ca):** A secondary macronutrient essential for cell wall structure and membrane stability.

**Calvin Cycle:** The light-independent reactions of photosynthesis where CO₂ is fixed into sugar.

**CAM Plants (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism):** Plants that open stomata at night to conserve water, storing CO₂ for daytime photosynthesis.

**Cambium:** A layer of meristematic tissue between xylem and phloem, responsible for secondary (thickness) growth.

**Carbon Fixation:** The process of incorporating atmospheric CO₂ into organic molecules.

**Cardinal Temperatures:** The minimum, optimum, and maximum temperatures for a biological process.

**Carotenoids:** Yellow, orange, and red pigments that assist in photosynthesis and protect against light damage.

**Casparian Strip:** A waxy band in the endodermis that forces water and minerals through cell membranes.

**Chelated:** A form of metal micronutrients bound to organic molecules for improved availability and uptake.

**Chilling Injury:** Damage to tropical plants from cold (but above-freezing) temperatures.

**Chlorophyll:** Green pigments in chloroplasts that absorb light for photosynthesis.

**Chloroplast:** The organelle where photosynthesis occurs, containing thylakoid membranes and stroma.

**Chlorosis:** Yellowing of plant tissue due to insufficient chlorophyll, often caused by nutrient deficiency.

**Climacteric:** A pattern of fruit ripening characterized by a sharp increase in respiration and ethylene production.

**Compensation Point:** The light intensity at which photosynthesis equals respiration (net CO₂ exchange = 0).

**Cortex:** Ground tissue between the epidermis and vascular tissue in roots and stems.

**Cotyledon:** Seed leaves; the first leaves to emerge from a germinating seed.

**Cuticle:** A waxy, waterproof layer on the outer surface of leaves and stems.

**Cytokinin:** A class of plant hormones that promote cell division and shoot growth.

---

## D

**Daily Light Integral (DLI):** The total amount of photosynthetically active light received by a plant each day, measured in mol/m²/day.

**Devernalization:** The reversal of vernalization by exposure to high temperatures.

**Dicot:** A plant with two cotyledons (seed leaves), typically with net-veined leaves and vascular bundles in rings.

**DIF:** The difference between day and night temperature; affects stem elongation.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity):** A measure of the total dissolved salts in a solution, indicating nutrient concentration.

**Electron Transport Chain (ETC):** A series of protein complexes that transfer electrons and generate ATP during respiration.

**Endodermis:** A single layer of cells surrounding the vascular tissue in roots, containing the Casparian strip.

**Epidermis:** The outermost layer of cells on leaves, stems, and roots.

**Ethylene:** A gaseous plant hormone that promotes fruit ripening, senescence, and abscission.

**Evapotranspiration (ET):** The combined water loss from evaporation and plant transpiration.

---

## F

**Far-Red Light:** Light in the 700-800nm range that influences flowering, shade responses, and stem elongation.

**Fermentation:** Anaerobic breakdown of glucose, producing alcohol or lactic acid with minimal ATP.

**Field Capacity:** The amount of water held in soil after drainage, available for plant uptake.

**Florigen:** A hormone-like signal that triggers flowering, transported from leaves to shoot meristem.

---

## G

**Gibberellins (GA):** Plant hormones that promote stem elongation, seed germination, and flowering.

**Glycolysis:** The first stage of cellular respiration, occurring in the cytoplasm, breaking glucose into pyruvate.

**Grana:** Stacks of thylakoid membranes within chloroplasts where light reactions occur.

**Guard Cells:** Specialized cells that surround stomata and control their opening and closing.

---

## H

**Hardening Off:** Gradually acclimating plants to outdoor conditions before transplanting.

---

## I

**Immobile Nutrients:** Nutrients that cannot be translocated within the plant; deficiency symptoms appear in new growth (Ca, S, Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B).

**Internode:** The stem section between two nodes.

**Interveinal Chlorosis:** Yellowing of leaf tissue between veins while veins remain green, often indicating Mg or Fe deficiency.

---

## K

**Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle):** The second stage of aerobic respiration, occurring in mitochondria.

---

## L

**Lateral Meristem:** Growth tissue that produces secondary growth (stem thickness), including cambium.

**Leaching:** The washing away of nutrients from soil by water.

**Light Reactions:** The first stage of photosynthesis where light energy is captured and converted to ATP and NADPH.

**Light Saturation Point:** The light intensity above which photosynthesis no longer increases.

**Long-Day Plants:** Plants that flower when the night period is shorter than a critical length.

---

## M

**Macronutrients:** Nutrients required in large quantities (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S).

**Matric Potential:** The component of water potential due to water adhesion to surfaces.

**Meristem:** Regions of actively dividing cells that produce new growth.

**Mesophyll:** The photosynthetic tissue inside a leaf, including palisade and spongy mesophyll.

**Micronutrients:** Nutrients required in trace amounts (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl, Ni).

**Mitochondria:** Organelles where cellular respiration occurs, producing ATP.

**Mobile Nutrients:** Nutrients that can be translocated from old to new tissue; deficiency appears in old growth (N, P, K, Mg).

**Monocot:** A plant with one cotyledon, typically with parallel leaf veins and scattered vascular bundles.

**Mycorrhizae:** Beneficial fungi that form symbiotic relationships with plant roots, enhancing nutrient uptake.

---

## N

**NADPH:** An electron carrier molecule produced during the light reactions and used in the Calvin cycle.

**Necrosis:** Death of plant tissue, often appearing as brown or black areas.

**Nitrogen (N):** A primary macronutrient essential for chlorophyll, proteins, and nucleic acids.

**Nitrogen Fixation:** The conversion of atmospheric N₂ into plant-available forms by bacteria.

**Node:** The point on a stem where leaves attach.

---

## O

**Osmosis:** The movement of water across a membrane from low to high solute concentration.

**Osmotic Potential (Solute Potential):** The component of water potential due to dissolved solutes.

---

## P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation):** Light in the 400-700nm wavelength range used for photosynthesis.

**Parthenocarpy:** Fruit development without fertilization, resulting in seedless fruit.

**Passive Transport:** Movement of substances across membranes without energy input, following concentration gradients.

**Pericycle:** A layer of cells inside the endodermis that gives rise to lateral roots.

**Permanent Wilting Point:** The soil moisture level below which plants cannot recover from wilting.

**Phloem:** Vascular tissue that transports sugars and other organic compounds throughout the plant.

**Phosphorus (P):** A primary macronutrient essential for energy transfer (ATP), DNA, and root development.

**Photoperiod:** The duration of light exposure in a 24-hour period.

**Photoperiodism:** Plant responses to day/night length, often controlling flowering.

**Photoreceptors:** Proteins that detect light signals and trigger plant responses (phytochrome, cryptochrome).

**Photorespiration:** A wasteful process where RuBisCO fixes O₂ instead of CO₂, reducing photosynthetic efficiency.

**Photosynthesis:** The process by which plants convert light energy, CO₂, and water into glucose and oxygen.

**Phototropism:** Plant growth response toward or away from light.

**Phytochrome:** A photoreceptor that detects red and far-red light, influencing germination, flowering, and shade responses.

**Pith:** The central, ground tissue in stems.

**Plasmodesmata:** Channels connecting adjacent plant cells, allowing material exchange.

**Potassium (K):** A primary macronutrient essential for enzyme activation, water regulation, and disease resistance.

**PPF (Photosynthetic Photon Flux):** The total light output from a fixture, measured in μmol/s.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density):** Light intensity at a point, measured in μmol/m²/s.

**Pressure Potential (Turgor Pressure):** The component of water potential due to physical pressure against cell walls.

---

## Q

**Q10:** The factor by which a biological rate increases for every 10°C rise in temperature (typically ~2).

---

## R

**Respiration:** The process of breaking down sugars to release energy as ATP.

**Rhizosphere:** The zone of soil immediately surrounding plant roots, characterized by high microbial activity.

**Root Cap:** A protective layer of cells covering the root tip.

**Root Exudates:** Organic compounds released by roots into the surrounding soil.

**Root Hair:** A fine extension of a root epidermal cell that increases surface area for absorption.

**RuBisCO:** Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase; the enzyme that fixes CO₂ in photosynthesis.

---

## S

**Saturation Point:** The maximum rate of a process (photosynthesis, respiration) that cannot be increased by adding more of a limiting factor.

**Senescence:** The process of aging and deterioration in plants.

**Short-Day Plants:** Plants that flower when the night period exceeds a critical length.

**SPAC:** Soil-Plant-Atmosphere Continuum; the pathway of water movement through a plant.

**Stoma (pl. Stomata):** Pores in the leaf epidermis that allow gas exchange.

**Stratification:** Cold treatment of seeds to break dormancy.

**Stroma:** The fluid-filled space inside chloroplasts where the Calvin cycle occurs.

**Sulfur (S):** A secondary macronutrient essential for amino acids and proteins.

**Symplastic Pathway:** Movement of water and solutes through the cytoplasm of connected cells via plasmodesmata.

---

## T

**Thylakoid:** Membrane-bound compartments inside chloroplasts where light reactions occur.

**Tip Burn:** Browning at leaf tips or margins, often caused by calcium deficiency or low transpiration.

**Transpiration:** The loss of water vapor from plant surfaces, primarily through stomata.

**Trichome:** Hair-like structures on plant surfaces that may deter pests or reduce water loss.

**Tropism:** A growth response toward or away from a stimulus (light, gravity, touch).

**Turgor:** The pressure exerted by water inside a cell against the cell wall, keeping the plant firm.

---

## V

**Vacuole:** A large, fluid-filled organelle that maintains turgor pressure and stores substances.

**Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD):** The difference between the amount of moisture in the air and how much moisture the air can hold when saturated.

**Vascular Bundle:** A strand of xylem and phloem tissue.

**Vernalization:** Exposure to cold that enables a plant to flower.

---

## W

**Water Potential (Ψ):** The tendency of water to move from one area to another, measured in pressure units (MPa).

**Wilting Point:** The point at which a plant can no longer extract water from the soil and loses turgor.

---

## X

**Xylem:** Vascular tissue that transports water and minerals from roots to shoots.

---

## Z

**Zinc (Zn):** A micronutrient essential for enzyme function and hormone production.
