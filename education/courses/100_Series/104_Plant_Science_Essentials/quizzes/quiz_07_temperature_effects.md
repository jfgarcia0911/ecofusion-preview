## Questions

### Question 1
What is the optimal temperature range for most vegetable crops?

a) 32-45°F (0-7°C)
b) 60-75°F (15-24°C) ✓
c) 90-105°F (32-40°C)
d) 110°F+ (43°C+)

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Most vegetables grow best at 60-75°F. This range optimizes photosynthesis, respiration balance, and enzyme activity.*

---

### Question 2
What is DIF in plant growth?

a) Difficulty level
b) Difference between day and night temperatures ✓
c) Disease infection factor
d) Daily irrigation frequency

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: DIF (Day-Night Temperature Difference) affects plant morphology. Positive DIF (warmer days) promotes stem elongation; negative DIF promotes compactness.*

---

### Question 3
What happens to plants at temperatures above their optimal range?

a) They always grow better
b) Respiration exceeds photosynthesis, reducing growth ✓
c) They become dormant immediately
d) Nothing changes

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: High temperatures increase respiration faster than photosynthesis, creating negative carbon balance. Plants use more energy than they produce.*

---

### Question 4
What is heat stress in plants?

a) Plants getting too much water
b) Cellular damage from excessive temperatures ✓
c) Too much fertilizer
d) Insect damage

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Heat stress (typically >90°F for cool-season crops) causes protein denaturation, membrane damage, reduced photosynthesis, and potential cell death.*

---

### Question 5
What is vernalization?

a) Planting in Vermont
b) Cold treatment required for some plants to flower ✓
c) Spring planting
d) Using fertilizer in summer

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Vernalization is exposure to cold temperatures (winter) that some plants (like tulips, wheat) need to trigger flowering in spring.*

---

### Question 6
How does temperature affect enzyme activity?

a) No relationship
b) Activity increases with temperature until enzymes denature ✓
c) Colder is always better
d) Enzymes don't exist in plants

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Enzymes speed up with warmth (roughly doubling every 10°C) until they overheat and denature (usually >105°F), losing function.*

---

### Question 7
What is bolting in relation to temperature?

a) Plants falling over
b) Premature flowering triggered by heat stress ✓
c) Rapid root growth
d) Leaf expansion

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Bolting is stress-induced flowering, often triggered by heat. Lettuce bolts at 80°F+, becoming bitter and unmarketable.*

---

### Question 8
Why is night temperature important?

a) Plants don't need nighttime management
b) Cool nights reduce respiration, preserving energy for growth ✓
c) Night temperature has no effect
d) Plants photosynthesize at night

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Cooler nights (5-10°F drop) slow respiration, reducing energy consumption and allowing plants to convert daytime photosynthesis into growth.*

---

### Question 9
What is the base temperature for plant growth?

a) Freezing point (32°F/0°C)
b) Minimum temperature at which growth begins (varies by species) ✓
c) Always 50°F
d) There is no base temperature

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Base temperature is species-specific (e.g., lettuce ~40°F, tomato ~50°F). Below this, growth essentially stops.*

---

### Question 10
How does root zone temperature affect nutrient uptake?

a) No effect whatsoever
b) Affects membrane fluidity, enzyme activity, and uptake rates ✓
c) Only air temperature matters
d) Colder roots always uptake more

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Root temperature affects membrane permeability and metabolic rate. Too cold (<55°F) slows uptake; too hot (>80°F) can damage roots.*
