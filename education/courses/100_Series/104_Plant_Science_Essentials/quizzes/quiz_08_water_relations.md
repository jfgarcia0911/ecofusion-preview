## Questions

### Question 1
What is transpiration?

a) Water movement through soil
b) Water loss from leaves through stomata ✓
c) Root water absorption
d) Photosynthesis

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Transpiration is evaporation of water from leaves, primarily through stomata. It drives water and nutrient uptake from roots.*

---

### Question 2
What are stomata?

a) Plant diseases
b) Pores on leaves that control gas exchange and water loss ✓
c) Root structures
d) Flower parts

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Stomata are adjustable pores (mainly on leaf undersides) that regulate CO₂ intake, oxygen release, and water vapor loss.*

---

### Question 3
What is turgor pressure?

a) Pressure from wind
b) Water pressure inside plant cells that maintains rigidity ✓
c) Soil compaction
d) Atmospheric pressure

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Turgor pressure is internal cell water pressure pushing against cell walls. It maintains plant structure; loss causes wilting.*

---

### Question 4
What drives water movement from roots to leaves?

a) Gravity only
b) Transpiration pull and cohesion-tension ✓
c) Active pumping
d) Wind pressure

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Transpiration creates negative pressure (suction) in leaves. Water cohesion and xylem adhesion pull water up from roots in an unbroken column.*

---

### Question 5
What is VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)?

a) Very Poor Design
b) Difference in moisture between leaf and air, driving transpiration ✓
c) Plant variety code
d) Ventilation power demand

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: VPD is the driving force for transpiration—the difference between water vapor in the leaf and in surrounding air. Higher VPD = faster transpiration.*

---

### Question 6
What happens when VPD is too high?

a) Plants grow perfectly
b) Excessive transpiration, potential wilting and stress ✓
c) No transpiration occurs
d) Leaves get wet

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: High VPD (hot, dry air) causes excessive water loss. If transpiration exceeds root uptake, plants wilt and stress.*

---

### Question 7
What happens when VPD is too low?

a) Perfect growth always
b) Slow transpiration, reduced nutrient uptake, disease risk ✓
c) Instant wilting
d) Faster photosynthesis

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Low VPD (cool, humid air) slows transpiration, reducing nutrient flow and creating conditions for fungal diseases like powdery mildew.*

---

### Question 8
What is guttation?

a) Root rot
b) Water droplets exuded from leaf edges when transpiration is low ✓
c) Leaf wilting
d) Stem elongation

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Guttation occurs when root pressure forces water out through hydathodes (leaf edge pores) during high humidity nights when transpiration is minimal.*

---

### Question 9
What is wilting?

a) Disease caused by bacteria
b) Loss of turgor pressure due to water deficit ✓
c) Normal afternoon plant behavior
d) Excessive growth

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Wilting occurs when water loss exceeds uptake, reducing cell turgor pressure. Leaves droop and stems lose rigidity.*

---

### Question 10
How does humidity affect transpiration?

a) No relationship
b) Higher humidity slows transpiration; lower humidity increases it ✓
c) Higher humidity increases transpiration
d) Humidity only matters at night

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: High humidity reduces the vapor pressure gradient between leaf and air, slowing transpiration. Low humidity increases the gradient, speeding transpiration.*
