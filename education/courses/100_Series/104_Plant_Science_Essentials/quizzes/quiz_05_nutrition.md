## Matching

**9. Match each symptom with the most likely nutrient deficiency:**

| Symptom | Answer |
|---------|--------|
| A. Brown/scorched leaf margins (old) | 1. Nitrogen |
| B. Interveinal chlorosis (old leaves) | 2. Phosphorus |
| C. Purple stems and leaves | 3. Potassium |
| D. General yellowing (old leaves first) | 4. Magnesium |

---

**10. When you observe nutrient deficiency symptoms, what should you check FIRST?**

A) Nutrient concentration levels
B) Light intensity
C) pH of the solution
D) Air temperature

---

## Answer Key

1. **B) Calcium** - Calcium is a secondary macronutrient. N-P-K are the primary macronutrients.

2. **C) Oldest leaves** - Nitrogen is mobile, so the plant moves it from old leaves to new growth.

3. **B) Interveinal chlorosis on new leaves** - Iron is immobile, so deficiency appears in new growth first.

4. **B) Transpiration is insufficient due to high humidity** - Ca moves primarily through transpiration; low VPD reduces Ca transport.

5. **B) 5.5 - 6.5** - This range provides good availability for most nutrients.

6. **B) False** - This describes IMMOBILE nutrients. Mobile nutrients move TO new growth, so deficiency shows in OLD growth.

7. **A) True** - K, Ca, and Mg compete for uptake. Excess K can cause Ca and Mg deficiency.

8. **B) False** - P becomes less available at both high and low pH; it's most available around 6.0-7.0.

9. **Matching:**
   - A-3 (Potassium) - Marginal necrosis is classic K deficiency
   - B-4 (Magnesium) - Interveinal chlorosis in old leaves is classic Mg deficiency
   - C-2 (Phosphorus) - Purple coloration indicates P deficiency
   - D-1 (Nitrogen) - General yellowing of old leaves first is N deficiency

10. **C) pH of the solution** - pH affects nutrient availability; many "deficiencies" are actually pH lockout problems.
