## Questions

### Question 1
What wavelengths of light do plants use for photosynthesis?

a) X-rays and gamma rays
b) Blue (400-500nm) and red (600-700nm) ✓
c) Only ultraviolet
d) Only infrared

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Plants primarily use blue light (400-500nm) and red light (600-700nm) for photosynthesis. This range is called PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation).*

---

### Question 2
What does PPFD measure?

a) Plant growth rate
b) Light intensity at a specific point (μmol/m²/s) ✓
c) Total electricity used
d) Plant height

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density) measures the amount of PAR light reaching a specific area per second (μmol/m²/s).*

---

### Question 3
What is DLI (Daily Light Integral)?

a) Total light received over 24 hours ✓
b) Light intensity at noon
c) Lamp wattage
d) Distance from light source

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: DLI is the total amount of PAR photons received per square meter per day (mol/m²/day). It accounts for both intensity and photoperiod.*

---

### Question 4
Which light spectrum promotes compact, bushy growth?

a) Red
b) Blue ✓
c) Green
d) Far-red

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Blue light promotes compact, bushy vegetative growth with thick stems and dark green leaves. It also regulates stomata opening.*

---

### Question 5
Which light spectrum promotes flowering and stem elongation?

a) Blue
b) Green
c) Red ✓
d) Ultraviolet

**Correct Answer: c**

*Explanation: Red light promotes stem elongation, flowering, and fruiting. Far-red light (>700nm) induces shade avoidance responses (stretching).*

---

### Question 6
What is photoperiodism?

a) Plant response to day length (light duration) ✓
b) Fear of cameras
c) Periodic photography
d) Time of planting

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: Photoperiodism is how plants respond to day/night length ratios to trigger flowering, dormancy, and other developmental changes.*

---

### Question 7
What are "short-day" plants?

a) Plants that flower when days are long (>12 hours)
b) Plants that flower when days are short (<12 hours) ✓
c) Plants that grow short
d) Plants that live short times

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Short-day plants (like poinsettias, chrysanthemums) flower when night length exceeds a critical duration (technically, they're "long-night" plants).*

---

### Question 8
What DLI do tomatoes typically need?

a) 5-10 mol/m²/day
b) 12-17 mol/m²/day
c) 25-35 mol/m²/day ✓
d) 60+ mol/m²/day

**Correct Answer: c**

*Explanation: Fruiting crops like tomatoes need high DLI (25-35+) for optimal production. Leafy greens need less (12-17).*

---

### Question 9
What is phototropism?

a) Plant growth toward light sources ✓
b) Plant fear of light
c) Plant color change
d) Light measurement

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: Phototropism is directional growth toward light. Stems show positive phototropism (grow toward light); roots show negative phototropism.*

---

### Question 10
What happens if plants receive too much light?

a) They always grow better
b) Photoinhibition and leaf bleaching can occur ✓
c) Nothing ever goes wrong
d) They grow in darkness instead

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Excessive light intensity can overwhelm photosynthetic capacity, causing photoinhibition, bleaching, and leaf damage. Each crop has optimal PPFD ranges.*
