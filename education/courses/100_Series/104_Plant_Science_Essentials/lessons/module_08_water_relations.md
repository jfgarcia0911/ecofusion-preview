# Module 8: Water Relations
## Course 104: Plant Science Essentials

## Introduction

Water is the lifeblood of plants. It makes up 80-95% of plant tissue, drives nutrient transport, enables photosynthesis, cools leaves, and maintains cell structure. Understanding water relations helps you optimize irrigation, prevent stress, and maximize plant productivity.

---

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Explain how water moves through plants (SPAC)
- Describe transpiration and its controlling factors
- Understand water potential and its components
- Recognize symptoms of water stress
- Apply proper irrigation strategies for different systems

## 2. The SPAC Continuum

### Soil-Plant-Atmosphere Continuum

Water moves along a continuous pathway from soil to atmosphere.

```
    THE SPAC SYSTEM

                    ATMOSPHERE
                    (driest)
                        │
                        │ TRANSPIRATION
                        │ (water vapor out)
                        ▼
    ╔═══════════════════════════════════╗
    ║              LEAF                 ║
    ║                                   ║
    ║  Stomata → Water evaporates       ║
    ║  Creates "pull" on water column   ║
    ╚═══════════════════════════════════╝
                        │
                        │ XYLEM TRANSPORT
                        │ (liquid water up)
                        ▼
    ╔═══════════════════════════════════╗
    ║              STEM                 ║
    ║                                   ║
    ║  Xylem vessels act as pipes       ║
    ║  Water under tension (negative    ║
    ║  pressure)                        ║
    ╚═══════════════════════════════════╝
                        │
                        │ ROOT UPTAKE
                        │ (from soil)
                        ▼
    ╔═══════════════════════════════════╗
    ║              ROOT                 ║
    ║                                   ║
    ║  Root hairs absorb water          ║
    ║  Water follows gradient           ║
    ╚═══════════════════════════════════╝
                        │
                        │ SOIL WATER
                        │
                        ▼
                      SOIL
                    (wettest)
```

### The Driving Force: Water Potential Gradient

Water moves from high water potential (soil) to low water potential (atmosphere).

```
    WATER POTENTIAL GRADIENT

    Location          Water Potential (MPa)
    ──────────────────────────────────────────
    Atmosphere        -100 to -200 (very dry air)
                           ▲
                           │
    Leaf interior     -0.5 to -3.0
                           ▲
                           │
    Root              -0.2 to -0.8
                           ▲
                           │
    Soil (moist)      -0.01 to -0.1
    ──────────────────────────────────────────

    WATER FLOWS: High potential → Low potential
    (Soil → Roots → Stem → Leaves → Atmosphere)
```

## 3. Transpiration

### The Engine of Water Movement

Transpiration is the evaporation of water from plant surfaces (mainly leaves).

```
    TRANSPIRATION AT THE LEAF

              CO₂ in
                ↓
    ════════════╪════════════ Leaf surface
                │
         ┌──────┴──────┐
         │   STOMATA   │ ◄── Adjustable pore
         │  (open/close) │
         └──────┬──────┘
                │
                │     H₂O out ←──── TRANSPIRATION
                │     ↗
         ┌──────┴──────┐
         │   GUARD     │
         │   CELLS     │
         └─────────────┘
    ════════════════════════
```

### Stomatal Control

Guard cells control stomata opening, balancing CO₂ uptake against water loss.

```
    STOMATAL OPERATION

    OPEN STOMATA                    CLOSED STOMATA
    ══════════════                  ═══════════════

       ┌─────────┐                    ┌───────┐
      ╱    ○     ╲                   │       │
     │     ○     │                   │  ─○─  │
      ╲    ○     ╱                   │       │
       └─────────┘                    └───────┘

    Guard cells turgid              Guard cells flaccid
    (full of water)                 (lost water)

    CONDITIONS:                     CONDITIONS:
    • Light present                 • Darkness
    • Adequate water                • Water stress
    • Low CO₂ inside                • High CO₂ inside
    • Cool temperatures             • Extreme heat
```

### Factors Affecting Transpiration Rate

| Factor | Effect on Transpiration |
|--------|------------------------|
| Light | ↑ Light = ↑ Stomata open = ↑ Transpiration |
| Temperature | ↑ Temp = ↑ Evaporation = ↑ Transpiration |
| Humidity | ↓ Humidity = ↑ VPD = ↑ Transpiration |
| Wind | ↑ Wind = removes boundary layer = ↑ Transpiration |
| Soil moisture | ↓ Soil water = stomata close = ↓ Transpiration |
| Plant factors | Leaf area, stomatal density, cuticle thickness |

## 4. Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)

### The Driving Force for Transpiration

VPD measures the "drying power" of the air—the difference between actual water vapor and saturation.

```
    VAPOR PRESSURE DEFICIT EXPLAINED

    SATURATED AIR (100% RH)     ACTUAL AIR (e.g., 60% RH)
    ═══════════════════════     ═════════════════════════

    ○○○○○○○○○○○○○○○○○          ○○○○○○○○○○░░░░░░░░░░
    Maximum water vapor         Current water vapor
    possible at this temp       in the air

    VPD = (Saturation vapor pressure) - (Actual vapor pressure)

    Higher VPD = Greater transpiration drive
    Lower VPD = Less transpiration drive
```

### VPD Target Ranges

```
    VPD AND PLANT RESPONSE

    VPD (kPa)    Plant Response
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    < 0.4        TOO LOW - High disease risk
                 Stomata open wide, but little water movement
                 Calcium deficiency risk (no transpiration pull)
                 Fungal diseases thrive

    0.4 - 0.8    VEGETATIVE OPTIMAL
                 Good stomatal opening
                 Active transpiration
                 Strong nutrient transport

    0.8 - 1.2    FLOWERING OPTIMAL
                 Good water movement
                 Moderate stress acceptable

    1.2 - 1.6    STRESS ZONE
                 Stomata begin closing
                 Reduced photosynthesis
                 Plant working to conserve water

    > 1.6        TOO HIGH - Severe stress
                 Stomata closed
                 Wilting
                 Growth stops
```

### VPD Calculation

```
    CALCULATING VPD

    VPD = SVP × (1 - RH/100)

    Where:
    SVP = Saturation Vapor Pressure (temperature dependent)
    RH = Relative Humidity (%)

    SVP Reference (approximate):
    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │ Temp (°C)    SVP (kPa)     │
    │ 15           1.70          │
    │ 20           2.34          │
    │ 25           3.17          │
    │ 30           4.24          │
    └─────────────────────────────┘

    Example:
    Temperature: 25°C, Humidity: 60%
    VPD = 3.17 × (1 - 0.60) = 1.27 kPa
```

## 5. Water Potential Components

### Understanding Water Potential (Ψ)

Water potential is the tendency of water to move. It has several components:

```
    WATER POTENTIAL EQUATION

    Ψ = Ψs + Ψp + Ψg + Ψm

    Where:
    Ψ  = Total water potential
    Ψs = Solute (osmotic) potential (always negative)
    Ψp = Pressure (turgor) potential (usually positive in cells)
    Ψg = Gravity potential (usually ignored for short plants)
    Ψm = Matric potential (water held by surfaces)
```

### In Practice

```
    WATER POTENTIAL IN A PLANT CELL

    TURGID CELL                     PLASMOLYZED CELL
    (Full of water)                 (Water stressed)

    ┌─────────────────┐             ┌─────────────────┐
    │█████████████████│             │       ░░░       │
    │█ Cell contents █│             │   ░░░░░░░░░    │
    │█████████████████│             │  ░  contents ░  │
    │█████████████████│             │   ░░░░░░░░░    │
    │█████████████████│             │       ░░░       │
    └─────────────────┘             └─────────────────┘
     Cell membrane                   Cell membrane
     pressed against wall            pulled away from wall

    Ψp = HIGH (positive)            Ψp = LOW or ZERO
    Cell is firm                    Cell is limp
```

### Wilting and Water Potential

| Condition | Ψp (Pressure) | Plant Appearance |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|
| Fully turgid | High positive | Firm, upright |
| Incipient wilting | Approaching zero | Starting to droop |
| Permanent wilting | Zero or negative | Wilted, may not recover |

## 6. Soil Water

### Soil Water Categories

```
    SOIL WATER CLASSIFICATION

    WATER CONTENT
    HIGH ────────────────────────────────────────► LOW

    ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░│                │
    GRAVITATIONAL  AVAILABLE       UNAVAILABLE
    WATER          WATER           WATER

    │            │                │               │
    Saturation   Field           Permanent       Oven
                 Capacity        Wilting         Dry
                                 Point

    ▓ = Drains away (too much = waterlogging)
    ░ = Plant available (ideal range)
      = Held too tightly (plants can't extract)
```

### Key Soil Water Terms

| Term | Definition | Typical Value |
|------|------------|---------------|
| **Saturation** | All pores filled | 100% pore space |
| **Field Capacity** | Water after drainage | -0.01 to -0.03 MPa |
| **Available Water** | FC minus PWP | Varies by soil type |
| **Permanent Wilting Point** | Plants can't extract | -1.5 MPa |
| **Unavailable Water** | Held too tightly | < -1.5 MPa |

### Soil Type and Water Holding

```
    WATER HOLDING BY SOIL TYPE

    PWP     │   │        │░░░  │      │░░░░░  │
```

## 7. Water Stress

### Types of Water Stress

```
    WATER STRESS TYPES

    DROUGHT STRESS (Too Little Water)
    ═════════════════════════════════

    Causes:
    • Insufficient irrigation
    • High transpiration demand
    • Root damage
    • High salinity (osmotic drought)

    Sequence of Events:
    1. Soil dries → 2. Root water potential drops →
    3. Stomata close → 4. Photosynthesis decreases →
    5. Growth slows → 6. Wilting → 7. Damage/death

    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

    WATERLOGGING (Too Much Water)
    ═════════════════════════════

    Causes:
    • Over-irrigation
    • Poor drainage
    • High water table

    Sequence of Events:
    1. Soil saturates → 2. O₂ depletes →
    3. Roots can't respire → 4. Roots die →
    5. Nutrient uptake stops → 6. Wilting (paradoxically)
```

### Visual Symptoms of Water Stress

```
    DROUGHT STRESS SYMPTOMS

    EARLY SIGNS:
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  • Darker green/blue-green color        │
    │  • Slight drooping in afternoon         │
    │  • Leaf rolling (grasses)               │
    │  • Slower growth                        │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────┘

    MODERATE STRESS:
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  • Visible wilting                      │
    │  • Leaf curling                         │
    │  • Older leaves yellowing               │
    │  • Flower/fruit drop                    │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────┘

    SEVERE STRESS:
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  • Permanent wilting                    │
    │  • Leaf scorching/browning              │
    │  • Leaf drop                            │
    │  • Stem dieback                         │
    │  • Plant death                          │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 8. Irrigation Strategies

### Irrigation Methods Comparison

| Method | Efficiency | Best For | Challenges |
|--------|------------|----------|------------|
| Surface/Flood | 40-60% | Field crops | Water waste, uneven |
| Sprinkler | 60-80% | Lawns, field crops | Disease, evaporation |
| Drip | 85-95% | Row crops, gardens | Clogging, cost |
| Subirrigation | 90%+ | Controlled environments | Installation complexity |
| Hydroponics | 95%+ | CEA production | Technical requirements |

### Irrigation Scheduling Approaches

```
    IRRIGATION SCHEDULING METHODS

    1. CALENDAR-BASED (Simplest)
    ════════════════════════════
    Water on fixed schedule (e.g., every 2 days)
    Problem: Ignores actual plant needs

    2. SENSOR-BASED (Better)
    ═══════════════════════
    Measure soil moisture, water when threshold reached
    More responsive to conditions

    3. EVAPOTRANSPIRATION (ET) BASED (Best)
    ══════════════════════════════════════
    Calculate water loss from weather data
    Replace what plants use
    Most scientific approach

    4. PLANT-BASED (Most Direct)
    ══════════════════════════
    Measure plant water status directly
    Stem psychrometers, dendrometers
    Research-level precision
```

## 9. Water in Hydroponic Systems

### Hydroponic Water Management

```
    WATER CONSIDERATIONS IN HYDROPONICS

    WATER QUALITY
    ═════════════
    • EC of source water (starting point)
    • pH of source water
    • Alkalinity (buffering capacity)
    • Contaminants (pathogens, minerals)

    DISSOLVED OXYGEN
    ════════════════
    • Target: >6 mg/L (ppm)
    • Lower at higher temperatures
    • Air stones, venturis, waterfalls
    • Critical for root respiration

    NUTRIENT SOLUTION TEMPERATURE
    ═════════════════════════════
    • Optimal: 18-22°C
    • Cold: slow uptake
    • Warm: low O₂, disease risk
```

### System-Specific Water Management

| System | Water Management Key Points |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **DWC** | Aeration critical; maintain water level |
| **NFT** | Continuous flow; backup power essential |
| **Drip** | Regular dripper checks; filter maintenance |
| **Ebb & Flow** | Proper cycle timing; drainage |
| **Aeroponics** | Misting intervals; nozzle clogging |

```
    DAILY WATER REQUIREMENTS (approximate)

    ──────────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Water Stress Prevention Checklist

```
    ✓ Monitor soil/media moisture regularly
    ✓ Check plants during hottest part of day
    ✓ Know your crop's water requirements
    ✓ Maintain proper VPD in controlled environments
    ✓ Ensure adequate drainage (prevent waterlogging)
    ✓ Monitor EC in hydroponics (high EC = osmotic stress)
    ✓ Check irrigation system function regularly
    ✓ Have backup irrigation plan
```

## Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Water moves** from high potential (soil) to low potential (atmosphere)
2. **Transpiration** drives water movement and cools leaves
3. **VPD** determines transpiration rate; 0.8-1.2 kPa is optimal
4. **Stomata** control the trade-off between CO₂ uptake and water loss
5. **Soil type** determines water holding capacity
6. **Both drought and waterlogging** cause stress—balance is key

### The Water Management Priority

```
    FOR HEALTHY PLANTS:

    1. Maintain adequate soil/root zone moisture
    2. Ensure proper drainage/aeration
    3. Monitor and adjust for environmental conditions
    4. Watch for early stress signs
    5. Match irrigation to plant needs, not calendar
```
