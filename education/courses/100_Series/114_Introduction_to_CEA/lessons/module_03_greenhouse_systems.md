```
└────────────────────────┘

AIR CONDITIONING
┌────────────────────────┐
│  [AC Unit]             │
│     ↓ Cold air         │
│      ☀️               │
│  🌱🌱🌱🌱🌱           │
│  Sealed greenhouse     │
└────────────────────────┘

Cost: $$$$ (very high)
Effectiveness: Excellent
Climate: All (high energy cost)
```

#### Cooling Performance

```
TEMPERATURE REDUCTION BY METHOD

Outside temp: 95°F

Natural ventilation:    85-90°F  (5-10°F reduction)
Forced ventilation:     82-87°F  (8-13°F reduction)
Evaporative cooling:    70-78°F  (17-25°F reduction)
Air conditioning:       65-75°F  (20-30°F reduction)
Shade + evap cooling:   68-75°F  (20-27°F reduction)

Evaporative cooling works best in:
• Low humidity (<50% RH)
• Hot, dry climates
• Large greenhouses

Less effective in humid climates (Gulf, Southeast)
```

### 3.5 Supplemental Lighting in Greenhouses

#### When to Use Supplemental Lighting

```
SUPPLEMENTAL LIGHTING DECISION

CONSIDER LIGHTING IF:
├─ Winter months (low DLI)
├─ High latitude (>40°)
├─ Cloudy climate (Pacific NW)
├─ High-value crops
├─ Year-round production needed
└─ Faster crop cycles desired

NATURAL LIGHT DLI BY SEASON/LOCATION

Location        Winter  Spring  Summer  Fall
──────────────────────────────────────────────
Florida         15-25   25-40   30-45   20-30
California      10-20   25-40   35-50   15-25
Netherlands     2-8     15-30   25-40   5-15
Arizona         15-25   30-45   40-55   20-30

Target DLI for crops: 12-17 (lettuce), 20-30 (tomatoes)

Supplement when natural DLI < target
```

#### Lighting Strategies

```
GREENHOUSE SUPPLEMENTAL LIGHTING

PHOTOPERIOD EXTENSION
├─ Lights on during dark hours
├─ Extend day length to 16-18 hours
├─ Low intensity needed (50-100 μmol/m²/s)
└─ Cost-effective

INTENSITY BOOST
├─ Lights on during day
├─ Supplement low winter sun
├─ Higher intensity (200-400 μmol/m²/s)
└─ More expensive

HYBRID APPROACH
├─ Day: Boost on cloudy days
├─ Evening: Extend photoperiod
├─ Adaptive to conditions
└─ Optimized cost/benefit
```

### 3.7 Energy Efficiency

#### Energy Conservation Strategies

```
GREENHOUSE ENERGY SAVING

STRUCTURE
├─ Double-layer poly (+40% heat retention)
├─ Thermal screens/curtains (40-60% savings)
├─ North wall insulation (15-25% savings)
└─ Reduce air leakage (10-20% savings)

EQUIPMENT
├─ High-efficiency boilers (85-95%)
├─ Variable-speed fans (30% savings)
├─ LED supplemental lights (50-70% vs HPS)
└─ Heat recovery ventilators

MANAGEMENT
├─ Optimal temperature setpoints
├─ Day/night temperature differential (DIF)
├─ Zone control (heat only where needed)
├─ Humidity management (reduce condensation)
└─ Light curtains (reduce heat loss at night)

ALTERNATIVE ENERGY
├─ Solar thermal (water heating)
├─ Photovoltaic panels (electricity)
├─ Geothermal (heating/cooling)
├─ Biomass boilers (wood pellets)
└─ Combined heat & power (CHP)
```

#### Energy Costs

```
TYPICAL GREENHOUSE ENERGY USE

Energy breakdown (cold climate):
├─ Heating: 60-80% of total
├─ Supplemental lighting: 10-30%
├─ Ventilation/cooling: 5-15%
├─ Irrigation pumps: 2-5%
└─ Controls/misc: 2-5%

Annual energy cost examples:
├─ Heated greenhouse (North): $3-8/sq ft/year
├─ Unheated hoop house: $0.10-0.50/sq ft/year
├─ Climate-controlled: $5-12/sq ft/year
└─ Vertical indoor farm: $15-40/sq ft/year

Energy is often 20-40% of operating costs
```

### 3.8 Greenhouse vs. Indoor Vertical Farming

#### Comparison Matrix

```
GREENHOUSE vs INDOOR VERTICAL

Factor              Greenhouse    Indoor Vertical
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Light source        90% natural   100% artificial
Energy use          Lower         Higher
Construction cost   $15-40/sq ft  $100-300/sq ft
Operating cost      Lower         Higher
Yield (per floor)   Moderate      Very high
Space efficiency    1× floor      4-12× floors
Location            Rural/peri-   Urban possible
                    urban
Weather impact      Some          None
Seasonal variation  Some          None
Crop variety        Wide          Limited
Setup time          3-6 months    6-18 months
Scalability         Excellent     Moderate
Sustainability*     Variable      Variable

*Depends on energy source, design, location
```

#### When to Choose Greenhouse

```
CHOOSE GREENHOUSE IF:

✓ Land is available and affordable
✓ Climate suitable for greenhouse growing
✓ Energy costs are concern
✓ Growing diverse crop varieties
✓ Lower initial capital available
✓ Leveraging natural light
✓ Fruiting crops (tomatoes, peppers, cukes)
✓ Larger plant sizes

Examples:
• Tomato production
• Cut flowers
• Nursery plants
• Leafy greens (where climate suitable)
```

#### When to Choose Indoor Vertical

```
CHOOSE INDOOR VERTICAL IF:

✓ Limited land area (urban core)
✓ Climate unsuitable for greenhouse
✓ Year-round consistency critical
✓ High-value, fast-cycle crops
✓ Food safety paramount (pharmaceuticals)
✓ Capital available, investors secured
✓ Focus on leafy greens, herbs, microgreens
✓ Extreme climates (desert, arctic)

Examples:
• Urban lettuce production
• Microgreens
• Basil and herbs
• Research facilities
• Arctic/desert communities
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Hoop House** | Simple arched greenhouse with polyethylene covering |
| **Venlo** | Multi-span glass greenhouse design from Netherlands |
| **Gutter-Connected** | Multiple greenhouse bays connected by shared gutters |
| **Light Transmission** | Percentage of outside light passing through covering |
| **R-Value** | Thermal resistance - higher values = better insulation |
| **Evaporative Cooling** | Cooling by evaporating water (pad and fan system) |
| **Thermal Screen** | Retractable curtain to reduce heat loss at night |
| **DIF** | Day-night temperature differential affecting stem elongation |
| **Photoperiod** | Day length (hours of light per 24-hour period) |
| **Headhouse** | Work area attached to greenhouse for potting, packing |
