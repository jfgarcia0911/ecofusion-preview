## Questions

### Question 1
**What are the five critical water parameters to monitor in aquaponics?**

A) Color, smell, taste, clarity, temperature

B) Ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, temperature ✓

C) Salt, sugar, alcohol, caffeine, protein

D) Only pH matters

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: The five essential parameters are ammonia (NH3), nitrite (NO2), nitrate (NO3), pH, and temperature. These directly affect fish health and system function.*

---

### Question 2
**How often should you test water in a new system?**

A) Once per month

B) Daily during cycling, then weekly when established ✓

C) Never—just observe the fish

D) Only when problems occur

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Test daily during the 4-6 week cycling period to track bacteria establishment. Once stable, weekly testing is sufficient for established systems.*

---

### Question 3
**What does high ammonia indicate?**

A) Too many plants

B) Bacterial colony insufficient for waste load or overfeeding ✓

C) Perfect system balance

D) Water is too clean

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Ammonia spikes indicate bacteria can't keep up with waste production—either from too many/large fish, overfeeding, or insufficient biofilter colonization.*

---

### Question 4
**If pH is too low (below 6.5), what should you do?**

A) Add vinegar to lower it further

B) Add calcium carbonate or potassium carbonate to raise pH ✓

C) Do nothing—pH doesn't matter

D) Remove all fish immediately

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Low pH can be raised using calcium carbonate (limestone), potassium carbonate, or potassium hydroxide. These also supplement calcium and potassium for plants.*

---

### Question 5
**What causes pH to drop in aquaponics systems?**

A) Too much sunlight

B) Nitrification process produces acids, lowering pH over time ✓

C) Fish swimming releases bases

D) Plants increase pH

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Bacterial nitrification consumes alkalinity and produces nitric acid, causing pH to gradually decline. Regular monitoring and buffering are necessary.*

---

### Question 6
**What is the ideal water temperature for a tilapia system?**

A) 40-50°F (4-10°C)

B) 60-70°F (15-21°C)

C) 75-85°F (24-29°C) ✓

D) 90-100°F (32-38°C)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Tilapia thrive at 75-85°F. Lower temperatures slow growth and feeding; below 50°F is dangerous; above 90°F stresses fish and reduces dissolved oxygen.*

---

### Question 7
**What is total dissolved solids (TDS)?**

A) Solid fish waste in water

B) Concentration of all dissolved minerals and salts in water ✓

C) Amount of bacteria present

D) Number of plants in system

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: TDS measures all dissolved substances in water (minerals, salts, nutrients). High TDS can indicate buildup of salts and minerals over time.*

---

### Question 8
**Why is dissolved oxygen (DO) important?**

A) Only fish need oxygen

B) Fish, plant roots, and bacteria all require oxygen to survive ✓

C) Oxygen is harmful to aquaponics

D) Plants produce all the oxygen needed

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Fish breathe dissolved oxygen, plant roots need oxygen for respiration, and aerobic bacteria require oxygen for nitrification. Low DO crashes the system.*

---

### Question 9
**What should you do if nitrite levels spike above 1.0 ppm?**

A) Add more fish

B) Stop feeding, increase aeration, add aquarium salt if needed ✓

C) Drain and refill entire system

D) Ignore it

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: During nitrite spikes, stop feeding fish, increase aeration, and optionally add aquarium salt (1-3 ppt) to reduce nitrite toxicity while Nitrobacter bacteria establish.*

---

### Question 10
**How do you remove chlorine from tap water?**

A) Boil it for 1 hour

B) Let it sit for 24 hours or use dechlorinator ✓

C) Add more chlorine to neutralize it

D) Chlorine is beneficial, don't remove

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Chlorine off-gases in 24 hours if water sits exposed to air. Dechlorinator (sodium thiosulfate) instantly neutralizes both chlorine and chloramine.*
