## Questions

### Question 1
**What is the correct order of the nitrogen cycle in aquaponics?**

A) Nitrate → Nitrite → Ammonia → Plants

B) Ammonia → Nitrite → Nitrate → Plants ✓

C) Nitrite → Ammonia → Nitrate → Plants

D) Ammonia → Nitrate → Nitrite → Plants

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: The nitrogen cycle follows this order: Fish produce ammonia, Nitrosomonas bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite, Nitrobacter bacteria convert nitrite to nitrate, and plants absorb nitrate.*

---

### Question 2
**Which bacteria converts ammonia to nitrite?**

A) Nitrobacter

B) E. coli

C) Nitrosomonas ✓

D) Lactobacillus

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Nitrosomonas bacteria are responsible for the first step of nitrification, converting toxic ammonia (NH3) to nitrite (NO2).*

---

### Question 3
**At what ammonia level should you be concerned about fish health?**

A) 0.1 ppm

B) 0.5 ppm ✓

C) 2.0 ppm

D) 5.0 ppm

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Ammonia levels above 0.5 ppm are dangerous for fish. The ideal level is 0 ppm in an established system.*

---

### Question 4
**What is the optimal pH range for aquaponics that balances fish, plant, and bacterial needs?**

A) 5.5-6.0

B) 6.8-7.2 ✓

C) 8.0-8.5

D) 9.0-9.5

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: pH 6.8-7.2 is the compromise zone where fish, plants, and bacteria can all function adequately, though none is at their individual optimum.*

---

### Question 5
**What do beneficial bacteria require to survive and function?**

A) Darkness and still water

B) Surface area, oxygen, and ammonia source ✓

C) Soil and organic matter

D) Sunlight and CO2

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Beneficial bacteria need surface area to colonize (biofilter media), dissolved oxygen, and a consistent ammonia source from fish waste.*

---

### Question 6
**How long does it typically take to cycle a new aquaponics system?**

A) 1-2 days

B) 1-2 weeks

C) 4-6 weeks ✓

D) 4-6 months

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Fishless cycling typically takes 4-6 weeks for bacterial colonies to establish and process ammonia/nitrite efficiently.*

---

### Question 7
**Which nitrogen compound is the primary plant nutrient?**

A) Ammonia (NH3)

B) Nitrite (NO2)

C) Nitrate (NO3) ✓

D) Nitrogen gas (N2)

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Nitrate (NO3) is the form of nitrogen that plants readily absorb and use for growth. It's also the safest form for fish.*

---

### Question 8
**What is the maximum safe nitrite level for fish?**

A) 0.25 ppm

B) 1.0 ppm ✓

C) 5.0 ppm

D) 10.0 ppm

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Nitrite levels above 1.0 ppm are dangerous for fish, causing "brown blood disease" by interfering with oxygen transport.*

---

### Question 9
**A system is "cycled" when:**

A) Water flows through all components

B) Ammonia and nitrite drop to zero within 24 hours of adding ammonia ✓

C) Fish are added to the tank

D) Plants begin to grow

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: A system is fully cycled when beneficial bacteria can process 2-4 ppm of ammonia to 0 ppm ammonia and 0 ppm nitrite within 24 hours.*

---

### Question 10
**What should you do if ammonia spikes to dangerous levels?**

A) Add more fish to dilute it

B) Increase feeding to help plants grow

C) Stop feeding, perform water change, increase aeration ✓

D) Add chlorine to kill ammonia-producing bacteria

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: In an ammonia emergency, stop feeding immediately, do a 25% water change with dechlorinated water, and increase aeration. Never add chlorine!*

---

### Question 11
**Why is ammonia MORE toxic at higher pH levels?**

A) Higher pH kills the detoxifying bacteria

B) A greater percentage exists as toxic un-ionized ammonia (NH3) ✓

C) Fish produce more ammonia at high pH

D) Plants can't absorb ammonia at high pH

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: At higher pH, more of the Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN) exists as toxic un-ionized ammonia (NH3) rather than less-toxic ionized ammonium (NH4+).*

---

### Question 12
**What can kill beneficial bacteria in an aquaponics system?**

A) Fish waste

B) Chlorine and extreme pH ✓

C) Plant roots

D) Moderate temperature changes

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Chlorine (from tap water) and extreme pH (below 6 or above 8) can kill beneficial bacteria. Always dechlorinate water before adding to your system.*

---

### Question 13
**During cycling, you observe high nitrite but declining ammonia. This indicates:**

A) System failure

B) Nitrosomonas bacteria are established, Nitrobacter still developing ✓

C) Too many fish

D) Plants are dying

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: This pattern shows that Nitrosomonas (ammonia→nitrite) are working, but Nitrobacter (nitrite→nitrate) haven't fully established yet. This is normal mid-cycle.*

---

### Question 14
**What is the recommended nitrate level for healthy plant growth?**

A) 0 ppm

B) 20-60 ppm ✓

C) 100-150 ppm

D) 200+ ppm

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Nitrate levels of 20-60 ppm indicate good nutrient availability for plants while remaining safe for fish. Too low means hungry plants; too high suggests imbalance.*

---

### Question 15
**Which of the following will NOT help establish beneficial bacteria faster?**

A) Adding filter media from an established system

B) Using commercial bacterial supplements

C) Adding antibiotics to keep fish healthy ✓

D) Maintaining consistent temperature and pH

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Antibiotics kill bacteria—including the beneficial ones you need! Never add antibiotics during cycling. Use seeded media or bacteria supplements instead.*
