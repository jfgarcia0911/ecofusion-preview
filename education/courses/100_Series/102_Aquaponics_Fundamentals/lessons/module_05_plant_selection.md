### 5.1 Understanding Aquaponic Plant Growth

#### Why Some Plants Thrive in Aquaponics

**Key Success Factors:**

```
AQUAPONIC PLANT ADVANTAGES

Traditional Soil                    Aquaponics
────────────────                    ──────────
Nutrients in soil     ──────►       Nutrients in solution
    ↓                                   ↓
Roots search          ──────►       Direct nutrient access
    ↓                                   ↓
Variable availability ──────►       Constant availability
    ↓                                   ↓
Slower growth         ──────►       30-50% FASTER GROWTH

Key Difference: Plants spend energy on growth,
                not searching for nutrients
```

#### Plant Categories for Aquaponics

**Three Main Groups:**

| Category | Examples | Nutrient Demand | System Match |
|----------|----------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Leafy Greens** | Lettuce, spinach, kale, chard | Low-Medium | All systems, beginner-friendly |
| **Fruiting Plants** | Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers | High | Mature systems, supplementation |
| **Herbs** | Basil, cilantro, parsley, mint | Low-Medium | All systems, high value |

### 5.4 Fruiting Plants - Advanced Production

#### Nutrient-Dense Crops

**Fruiting plants require:**
- Mature, established system (3+ months old)
- Higher nitrate levels (40-80 ppm)
- Sometimes nutrient supplementation
- More space and support structures
- Longer growing cycles

#### Tomatoes

```
TOMATO PRODUCTION GUIDE

Determinate vs. Indeterminate
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
│  DETERMINATE (Bush)                          │
│  Height: 3-4 feet                            │
│  Harvest: All at once                        │
│  Duration: 60-80 days                        │
│  Best for: Beginner, limited space           │
│  Examples: Roma, Celebrity                   │
│                                              │
│  INDETERMINATE (Vining)                      │
│  Height: 6-10+ feet                          │
│  Harvest: Continuous over months             │
│  Duration: 70-90 days to first fruit         │
│  Best for: Maximum production                │
│  Examples: Beefsteak, Cherry                 │
│                                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

System Requirements:
• Media bed or Dutch bucket system
• Staking or trellising
• Strong light (14-18 hours LED or full sun)
• Warm temps (65-85°F)
• pH 6.0-6.8

Spacing: 18-24" (determinate), 24-36" (indeterminate)

Potential Yield: 10-25 lbs per plant over season
```

#### Peppers

| Type | Days to Harvest | Spacing | Notes |
|------|-----------------|---------|-------|
| **Sweet Bell** | 70-90 | 18-24" | High value, heavy feeders |

**Pepper Tips:**
- Prefer warmer water (70-80°F)
- Require good calcium (prevent blossom end rot)

- Harvest regularly for continued production

#### Strawberries

**Unique Advantages:**
- Perennial (produce for years)
- Vertical growing (tower systems)
- Continuous harvest
- High market value

**Requirements:**
- Cool period for flowering (can be simulated)
- 12-18" spacing
- June-bearing or Ever-bearing varieties
- pH 6.0-6.5
- Harvest: Year 2+ (1st year establishment)

### 5.5 Plant Spacing and Density

#### Calculating Production Capacity

```
PRODUCTION PLANNING EXAMPLE

System: 100 sq ft growing space (NFT)
Crop: Lettuce
Spacing: 8" × 8" (0.44 sq ft per plant)

Plants per 100 sq ft:
100 sq ft ÷ 0.44 sq ft = 227 plants

Harvest cycle: 35 days
Annual cycles: 365 ÷ 35 = 10.4 cycles

Annual production:
227 plants × 10.4 cycles = 2,361 heads/year

Weekly production (steady state):
2,361 ÷ 52 weeks = 45 heads per week
```

### 5.6 Propagation Techniques

#### Seed Starting

```
SEED STARTING PROCESS

WEEK 0: GERMINATION
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. Pre-soak rockwool cubes    │
│     pH 5.5-6.0                 │
│                                │
│  2. Insert 1-2 seeds per cube  │
│     Depth: 2× seed diameter    │
│                                │
│  3. Place in humidity dome     │
│     Temperature: 70-80°F       │
│                                │
│  4. Keep moist, not waterlogged│
└────────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
WEEK 1: GERMINATION
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│  Seeds sprout                  │
│  🌱 Remove dome gradually      │
│  🌱 Provide light (12-16 hrs)  │
│  🌱 Keep moist                 │
└────────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
WEEK 2-3: SEEDLING DEVELOPMENT
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│  True leaves appear            │
│  🌿🌿 Increase light            │
│  🌿🌿 Begin light feeding       │
│      (1/4 strength)            │
└────────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
WEEK 3-4: READY TO TRANSPLANT
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│  3-4 true leaves               │
│  🌿🌿🌿 Strong root development │
│  🌿🌿🌿 Transplant to system    │
└────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Growing Media for Seeds

| Media Type | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|------------|------|------|----------|
| **Rockwool** | Sterile, consistent, widely available | Not biodegradable, pH adjustment needed | Commercial, all crops |
| **Coco Coir** | Renewable, pH neutral, biodegradable | Can hold too much water | Organic operations |
| **Peat Pellets** | Easy to use, self-contained | Not sustainable source | Home growers |
| **Oasis Cubes** | pH neutral, good air:water ratio | More expensive | Professional propagation |

### 5.7 Nutrient Requirements

#### NPK and Micronutrients

**Fish feed provides (in fish waste):**

```
NUTRIENT AVAILABILITY IN AQUAPONICS

PRIMARY NUTRIENTS (Macronutrients)
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Nitrogen (N)                      │
│  ✓ Abundant from fish waste        │
│  ✓ Rarely deficient                │
│  • Nitrate form (NO₃⁻) from cycling│
│                                    │
│  Phosphorus (P)                    │
│  ✓ Adequate from fish feed         │
│  ⚠ May be deficient in soft water  │
│  • Supplement if needed            │
│                                    │
│  Potassium (K)                     │
│  ⚠ Often deficient                 │
│  ⚠ Fish feed doesn't provide enough│
│  • Common supplement needed        │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

SECONDARY NUTRIENTS
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Calcium (Ca)                      │
│  ⚠ May be deficient                │
│  • Add via water source or shells  │
│                                    │
│  Magnesium (Mg)                    │
│  ⚠ May be deficient                │
│  • Epsom salt supplement           │
│                                    │
│  Sulfur (S)                        │
│  ✓ Usually adequate                │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

MICRONUTRIENTS (Trace Elements)
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Iron (Fe)                         │
│  ⚠ Most common deficiency          │
│  • Chelated iron supplement        │
│                                    │
│  Others: Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl    │
│  ✓ Usually adequate from fish feed │
│  ⚠ May need occasional supplement  │
└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Recognizing Deficiencies

**Visual Deficiency Guide:**

| Nutrient | Symptoms | Location | Solution |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | Yellowing, stunted growth | Older leaves first | Increase fish feed, check cycling |
| **Phosphorus (P)** | Purple/red tints, dark green | Older leaves | Rock phosphate, bone meal |
| **Potassium (K)** | Yellow edges, brown spots | Older leaves | Potassium hydroxide, kelp |
| **Calcium (Ca)** | Tip burn, blossom end rot | New growth, fruits | Calcium hydroxide, crushed shells |
| **Magnesium (Mg)** | Yellowing between veins | Older leaves | Epsom salt (MgSO₄) |
| **Iron (Fe)** | Yellowing, green veins | New growth | Chelated iron (Fe-DTPA) |

```
DEFICIENCY PROGRESSION

Nitrogen Deficiency:
Leaf color: Green ──► Pale Green ──► Yellow ──► Brown

Iron Deficiency:
Young leaves: Dark Green ──► Light Green ──► Yellow with green veins

Potassium Deficiency:
Leaf edges: Green ──► Yellow ──► Brown/crispy
```

#### Safe Supplementation

**Common Supplements:**

| Supplement | Target Nutrient | Dosage | Frequency |
|------------|----------------|--------|-----------|
| **Chelated Iron** | Fe | 2 ppm | Every 3 weeks |
| **Potassium Hydroxide** | K (+ pH up) | To maintain pH | As needed |
| **Calcium Hydroxide** | Ca (+ pH up) | To maintain pH | As needed |
| **Epsom Salt** | Mg, S | 1 tsp per 100 gal | Monthly |
| **Seaweed Extract** | Micronutrients | Label rate | Bi-weekly |

**Important Notes:**
- Test before supplementing
- Add gradually, never all at once
- Monitor fish after any addition
- Keep records of supplementation

### 5.8 Plant Health Management

#### Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

```
IPM PYRAMID FOR AQUAPONICS

                    ▲
                   ╱ ╲
              Last Resort
             ╱  Organic  ╲
            ╱  Pesticides ╲
           ╱ (Fish-safe)   ╲
          ╱─────────────────╲
         ╱                   ╲
        ╱   Biological        ╲
       ╱  Control (Beneficial ╲
      ╱   Insects/Predators)   ╲
     ╱───────────────────────────╲
    ╱                             ╲
   ╱        Cultural Controls      ╲
  ╱    (Spacing, Sanitation,       ╲
 ╱     Environment Management)      ╲
╱─────────────────────────────────────╲
          PREVENTION
   Healthy Plants, Monitoring

Start at bottom, work up only if needed
```

**Fish-Safe Pest Controls:**

| Pest | Prevention | Organic Control |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| **Aphids** | Inspect transplants, air circulation | Neem oil, insecticidal soap, ladybugs |
| **Whiteflies** | Screen vents, sticky traps | Yellow sticky cards, parasitic wasps |
| **Spider Mites** | Humidity control, inspection | Predatory mites, neem oil |
| **Fungus Gnats** | Don't overwater, cover media | Beneficial nematodes, BTI |
| **Caterpillars** | Inspect plants, netting | Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis), handpick |

**NEVER Use:**
- Systemic pesticides
- Copper-based fungicides (toxic to fish)
- Synthetic pyrethroids
- Malathion or organophosphates
- Any product not labeled safe for food fish

### 5.9 Harvest and Post-Harvest

#### Harvesting Techniques

**Leafy Greens - Whole Plant:**

```
HARVEST PROCEDURE

1. CHECK MATURITY
   • Lettuce: Firm head, appropriate size
   • Spinach: 4-6" leaves
   • Kale: 8-10" leaves

2. CUT OR PULL
   • Cut just above root crown
   • Or lift entire net pot
   • Keep cool immediately

3. POST-HARVEST
   • Rinse gently in cold water
   • Shake dry or spin
   • Refrigerate immediately (34-36°F)

4. ROOT DISPOSAL
   • Compost roots and media
   • Rinse and reuse net pots
   • Clean growing space
```

**Cut-and-Come-Again:**

| Crop | Cutting Method | Regrowth Time |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| **Basil** | Pinch above leaf node | 7-10 days |
| **Lettuce** | Cut 1" above crown | 10-14 days |
| **Kale** | Outer leaves only | 7-10 days |
| **Chard** | Outer leaves only | 7-10 days |

**Fruiting Crops:**

| Crop | Harvest Indicator | Frequency |
|------|------------------|-----------|
| **Tomatoes** | Full color, slight give | Daily when producing |
| **Peppers** | Full size, color change | 2-3 times weekly |
| **Cucumbers** | Appropriate size, firm | Daily |
| **Strawberries** | Red, sweet aroma | Daily |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cut-and-Come-Again** | Harvesting technique leaving plant to regrow |
| **True Leaves** | Leaves with characteristic shape (after cotyledons) |
| **Bolting** | Premature flowering, often due to heat stress |
| **Determinate** | Plant with defined growth limit (bush tomatoes) |
| **Indeterminate** | Plant with continuous growth (vining tomatoes) |
| **Chelated** | Chemically bound form of nutrient for plant uptake |
| **Succession Planting** | Staggered planting for continuous harvest |
| **Transplant Shock** | Stress from moving seedlings to new environment |
