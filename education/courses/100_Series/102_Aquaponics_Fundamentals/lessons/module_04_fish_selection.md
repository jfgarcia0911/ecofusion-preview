## Lesson Content

### 4.1 Fish Selection Criteria

#### Why Fish Choice Matters

The fish you select will determine:
- System temperature requirements
- Feed costs and conversion rates
- Harvest timeline
- Market value and salability
- Legal requirements and permits
- Overall system management complexity

```
FISH SELECTION DECISION TREE

What's your primary goal?
    │
    ├─► Food Production
    │   │
    │   ├─► Warm water available (75°F+) ──► Tilapia
    │   │
    │   ├─► Cool water (60-75°F) ──► Trout, Perch
    │   │
    │   └─► Cold water (<60°F) ──► Trout
    │
    ├─► Ornamental/Educational
    │   │
    │   └─► Goldfish, Koi
    │
    └─► Experimental/Research
        │
        └─► Catfish, Bass, Bluegill
```

#### Critical Selection Factors

| Factor | Consideration | Impact on System |
|--------|--------------|------------------|
| **Temperature Range** | Species optimal range | Heating/cooling costs |
| **Growth Rate** | Time to harvest size | System turnover |
| **Feed Conversion** | Feed efficiency | Operating costs |
| **Stocking Density** | Fish per gallon tolerance | System capacity |
| **Market Value** | Price per pound | Revenue potential |
| **Legal Status** | Permits required | Regulatory compliance |
| **Hardiness** | Disease resistance | Management ease |

### 4.2 Popular Aquaponic Fish Species

#### Tilapia - The Aquaponic Workhorse

```
TILAPIA PROFILE CARD

Species: Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
        Blue Tilapia (Oreochromis aureus)

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  TEMPERATURE                             │
│  Optimal: 75-86°F (24-30°C)             │
│  Minimum: 60°F (will not feed below)    │
│  Lethal: Below 50°F                     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  GROWTH                                  │
│  Fingerling to plate: 6-9 months        │
│  Market size: 1-2 lbs                   │
│  Feed conversion: 1.5-1.8:1             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  STOCKING                                │
│  Density: 1 lb per 3-5 gallons          │
│  Max: 1 lb per 1-2 gal (intensive)      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Tilapia Advantages:**
- Fast growth rate (harvest in 6-9 months)
- Omnivorous (eat plant-based feed)
- Tolerant of varying water quality
- High stocking density tolerance
- Mild, marketable flavor
- Good for beginners

**Tilapia Challenges:**
- Require warm water (heating costs in cold climates)
- Regulated or illegal in some states (invasive species concerns)
- Prolific breeding (can overpopulate)
- Temperature-sensitive (die quickly if water cools)

#### Trout - The Cool Water Champion

```
TROUT PROFILE CARD

Species: Rainbow Trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss)
        Brook Trout (Salvelinus fontinalis)

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  TEMPERATURE                             │
│  Optimal: 55-65°F (13-18°C)             │
│  Maximum: 70°F (stress above this)      │
│  Minimum: 35°F (will slow feeding)      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  GROWTH                                  │
│  Fingerling to market: 12-16 months     │
│  Market size: 1-1.5 lbs                 │
│  Feed conversion: 1.0-1.3:1             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  STOCKING                                │
│  Density: 1 lb per 5-7 gallons          │
│  Requires high oxygen (6+ ppm)          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Trout Advantages:**
- Premium market value
- Excellent feed conversion
- Cool water species (good for northern climates)
- Don't breed in captivity (population control)
- High-quality protein

**Trout Challenges:**
- Require cold water (chilling costs in warm climates)
- High oxygen demand (intensive aeration)
- More sensitive to water quality
- Slower growth than tilapia
- Higher feed costs (carnivorous)

### 4.4 Stocking Density Guidelines

#### Understanding Stocking Density

**Definition:** The weight of fish per unit of water volume

```
STOCKING DENSITY CALCULATION

Example System:
Fish tank volume: 100 gallons

Conservative stocking (1 lb per 5 gallons):
100 gallons ÷ 5 = 20 lbs total fish at maturity

Moderate stocking (1 lb per 3 gallons):
100 gallons ÷ 3 = 33 lbs total fish at maturity

Intensive stocking (1 lb per 2 gallons):
100 gallons ÷ 2 = 50 lbs total fish at maturity
(Requires excellent management, high aeration)
```

#### Stocking Recommendations by System Type

| System Type | Recommended Density | Rationale |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Beginner Media Bed** | 1 lb per 5-7 gallons | Safety margin, easier management |
| **Experienced Media Bed** | 1 lb per 3-5 gallons | Good balance of production/stability |
| **NFT with Biofilter** | 1 lb per 3-4 gallons | Requires robust biofiltration |
| **DWC Commercial** | 1 lb per 2-3 gallons | Intensive management, monitoring |

#### Stocking Timeline

```
GRADUAL STOCKING APPROACH

New System (Weeks 0-8):
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Week 0-4: CYCLING                  │
│ • No fish OR 2-3 hardy fish only   │
│ • Testing daily                    │
│                                    │
│ Week 4-6: INITIAL STOCKING         │
│ • Add 25% of final fish load       │
│ • Monitor ammonia/nitrite closely  │
│                                    │
│ Week 6-8: GRADUAL INCREASE         │
│ • Add another 25% (now at 50%)     │
│ • Continue monitoring              │
│                                    │
│ Week 8-12: FULL STOCKING           │
│ • Add remaining 50% gradually      │
│ • System stabilizes                │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

Never add all fish at once!
Gradual stocking allows bacteria to keep pace
```

### 4.5 Fish Feeding Strategies

#### Feed Types and Quality

**Commercial Fish Feed Components:**

| Component | Purpose | Typical % |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| **Protein** | Growth, tissue repair | 30-40% |
| **Fat** | Energy, vitamin carrier | 5-15% |
| **Carbohydrates** | Energy source | 20-30% |
| **Vitamins/Minerals** | Health, immune function | 5-10% |
| **Fiber** | Digestion | 5-10% |

**Feed Types:**

```
FISH FEED OPTIONS

Floating Pellets
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ Pros:                           │
│ • Easy to observe feeding       │
│ • Know when fish are done       │
│ • Reduces waste                 │
│                                 │
│ Cons:                           │
│ • More expensive                │
│ • Can lead to surface feeding   │
└─────────────────────────────────┘

Sinking Pellets
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ Pros:                           │
│ • Cheaper                       │
│ • Natural feeding behavior      │
│                                 │
│ Cons:                           │
│ • Harder to monitor intake      │
│ • Uneaten food creates waste    │
│ • Can cloud water               │
└─────────────────────────────────┘

Pellet Size Guide:
• Fry: <1mm (crumbles)
• Fingerlings: 1-3mm
• Juveniles: 3-5mm
• Adults: 5-8mm
```

#### Feeding Rates and Schedules

**Temperature-Based Feeding:**

| Water Temp (°F) | % Body Weight per Day | Feeding Frequency |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **50-60°F** | 0.5-1% | Once daily or skip |
| **60-70°F** | 1-2% | 1-2 times daily |
| **70-80°F** | 2-3% | 2-3 times daily |
| **80-90°F** | 1.5-2.5% | 2-3 times daily |
| **>90°F** | Reduce feeding | Fish stressed |

**Feeding Calculation Example:**

```
DAILY FEED AMOUNT CALCULATION

Current fish biomass: 20 lbs
Water temperature: 75°F
Feed rate: 2% body weight per day

Daily feed amount:
20 lbs × 0.02 = 0.4 lbs (6.4 oz) per day

Split into 2 feedings:
Morning: 3.2 oz
Evening: 3.2 oz

Growth estimate:
Feed conversion 1.5:1
6.4 oz feed × 30 days = 192 oz = 12 lbs feed/month
12 lbs feed ÷ 1.5 = 8 lbs fish growth per month
```

#### Feeding Best Practices

```
FEEDING PROTOCOL

BEFORE FEEDING
□ Observe fish behavior
□ Check water temperature
□ Confirm fish are active and hungry

DURING FEEDING
□ Feed small amounts at a time
□ Watch for feeding response
□ Stop when fish slow or stop eating
□ Typical feeding: 5-10 minutes max

AFTER FEEDING
□ Observe for any uneaten food
□ Remove excess feed if visible
□ Record amount fed in log
□ Note fish appetite and behavior

DAILY MONITORING
□ Morning: Check fish activity
□ Feeding time: Assess appetite
□ Evening: Check for any issues
□ Weekly: Weigh sample fish, adjust feed
```

### 4.6 Fish Health and Welfare

#### Signs of Healthy Fish

**Normal Behavior:**

| Indicator | Healthy Fish | Unhealthy Fish |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Swimming** | Active, smooth, coordinated | Lethargic, erratic, listing |
| **Feeding** | Eager, competitive | Disinterested, slow |
| **Appearance** | Clear eyes, vibrant color | Cloudy eyes, pale/dark |
| **Breathing** | Steady gill movement | Rapid gasping at surface |
| **Social** | Schooling normally | Isolated, hiding |
| **Physical** | Intact fins, smooth scales | Damaged fins, lesions |

#### Common Health Problems

**Stress:**

```
STRESS INDICATORS AND CAUSES

Physical Signs:
• Rapid gill movement
• Gasping at surface
• Clamped fins
• Loss of appetite
• Hiding behavior
• Dark coloration

Common Causes:
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ Poor Water Quality             │
│ • High ammonia/nitrite         │
│ • pH swings                    │
│ • Low oxygen                   │
├────────────────────────────────┤
│ Environmental                  │
│ • Temperature fluctuations     │
│ • Overcrowding                 │
│ • Excessive noise/vibration    │
├────────────────────────────────┤
│ Management                     │
│ • Overfeeding                  │
│ • Aggressive tankmates         │
│ • Poor handling                │
└────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Diseases:**

| Disease | Symptoms | Cause | Treatment |
|---------|----------|-------|-----------|
| **Ich (White Spot)** | White spots, flashing | Parasite | Salt bath, temperature increase |
| **Fin Rot** | Ragged, discolored fins | Bacterial | Water quality, salt treatment |
| **Fungus** | Cotton-like growth | Fungal | Salt bath, antifungal |
| **Columnaris** | White/gray patches, lethargy | Bacterial | Antibiotics (consult vet) |
| **Dropsy** | Swollen body, raised scales | Bacterial/organ failure | Often fatal, quarantine |

#### Disease Prevention

```
PREVENTION STRATEGIES

WATER QUALITY (Primary Defense)
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Test regularly               │
│ ✓ Maintain stable parameters   │
│ ✓ Adequate filtration          │
│ ✓ Sufficient aeration          │
└────────────────────────────────┘

STRESS REDUCTION
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Proper stocking density      │
│ ✓ Stable temperature           │
│ ✓ Consistent feeding           │
│ ✓ Minimize handling            │
└────────────────────────────────┘

BIOSECURITY
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Quarantine new fish (2 weeks)│
│ ✓ Don't share equipment        │
│ ✓ Clean hands before/after     │
│ ✓ Monitor daily for issues     │
└────────────────────────────────┘

NUTRITION
┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ High-quality feed            │
│ ✓ Proper storage (dry, cool)   │
│ ✓ Don't overfeed               │
│ ✓ Remove uneaten food          │
└────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.7 Legal and Ethical Considerations

#### Regulatory Requirements

**Species Regulations:**

| Jurisdiction Level | Typical Requirements |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Federal** | No permits for native species (usually) |
| **State** | Aquaculture license, species restrictions |
| **Local** | Zoning compliance, water discharge permits |

**Common Restrictions:**

```
SPECIES LEGAL STATUS (EXAMPLES - Check Local Laws!)

Generally Allowed Everywhere:
✓ Goldfish
✓ Koi
✓ Channel Catfish (in native range)

Often Regulated:
⚠ Tilapia (prohibited in some states as invasive)
⚠ Trout (may require special permits)
⚠ Bass (game fish regulations may apply)

Region-Specific:
• Check your state fish and wildlife agency
• Some species illegal in certain states
• Transport across state lines may be restricted
• Breeding permits may be required
```

#### Animal Welfare Standards

**Five Freedoms for Fish:**

```
FISH WELFARE PRINCIPLES

1. FREEDOM FROM HUNGER AND THIRST
   □ Adequate, appropriate nutrition
   □ Clean water access

2. FREEDOM FROM DISCOMFORT
   □ Appropriate environment
   □ Temperature control
   □ Adequate space

3. FREEDOM FROM PAIN, INJURY, DISEASE
   □ Prevention through good management
   □ Rapid diagnosis and treatment
   □ Humane euthanasia when necessary

4. FREEDOM TO EXPRESS NORMAL BEHAVIOR
   □ Sufficient space
   □ Proper social groups
   □ Environmental enrichment

5. FREEDOM FROM FEAR AND DISTRESS
   □ Conditions avoiding mental suffering
   □ Gentle, minimal handling
   □ Stress reduction
```

#### Humane Harvesting

**Approved Methods:**

| Method | Description | Suitability |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Ice Slurry** | Gradual temperature reduction | Small fish, rapid |
| **Percussive Stunning** | Sharp blow to head | Large fish, immediate |
| **Pithing** | Destroy brain/spinal cord | After stunning, certain |
| **Electrostunning** | Electric current | Commercial, equipment needed |

**NOT Acceptable:**
- Asphyxiation in air (causes suffering)
- Freezing alive (painful)
- Boiling alive
- Carbon dioxide overdose (distressing)
