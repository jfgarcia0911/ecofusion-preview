## Project Overview

This capstone project demonstrates your mastery of aquaponic system design by creating a complete, buildable system design package for a home aquaponic system. This is the type of comprehensive documentation you would create before building a real system or presenting to a client.

### Project Goal:

Design a complete home aquaponic system that is:
- **Functional:** Properly sized and configured to work
- **Practical:** Actually buildable with available materials
- **Economical:** Within reasonable budget for home hobbyist
- **Documented:** Complete enough that someone could build from your plans

### Section 6: Troubleshooting Guide

**Problem: Ammonia or Nitrite Spike**
- Possible causes: Overfeeding, dead fish, insufficient bacteria
- Immediate actions: Stop or reduce feeding, increase aeration, partial water change (10-20%)
- Prevention: Feed appropriate amounts, remove dead matter promptly

**Problem: pH Drift (Rising or Falling)**
- Typical direction: pH tends to drop in mature systems
- Causes: Nitrification produces acid, water source
- Correction: If below 6.0, add calcium carbonate or potassium bicarbonate; if above 8.0, investigate water source
- Prevention: Buffer system with crushed coral or limestone chips in media

**Problem: Fish Gasping at Surface**
- Causes: Low dissolved oxygen
- Immediate action: Increase aeration immediately
- Check: Water temperature (warm water holds less oxygen), check aerator function
- Prevention: Adequate aeration sizing, avoid overheating

**Problem: Poor Plant Growth**
- Causes: Insufficient nutrients, wrong pH, inadequate light, pests/disease
- Diagnostics: Check nitrate levels (should be 20-40 ppm), verify pH 6.5-7.5, assess light levels
- Actions: Address specific deficiency found

**Problem: Cloudy Water**
- Causes: Bacterial bloom (new systems), overfeeding, insufficient filtration
- Normal: Some cloudiness during cycling is normal
- Action: Reduce feeding, add filtration, wait for system maturity

### Section 7: Safety Considerations

**Electrical Safety:**
- All outlets must be GFCI protected
- Keep electrical connections above water level
- Use outdoor-rated components for outdoor/greenhouse
- Unplug equipment before maintenance
- Regular GFCI testing (monthly)

**Water Safety:**
- Have overflow protection to prevent flooding
- Know location of water shut-off
- Floor must support water weight: _____ lbs total
- Slip hazards - keep floor dry

**Chemical Safety:**
- Store fish feed properly to avoid contamination
- Use food-grade materials for all water contact
- If using pH adjusters, wear gloves and eye protection

**Biological Safety:**
- Wash hands after handling fish or water
- Don't eat fish that have been medicated
- Use separate equipment for aquaponics (don't cross-contaminate with other uses)

## Tips for Success

### Getting Started:
1. **Week 1:** Decide on system type, fish species, rough sizing
2. **Week 2:** Complete all calculations, verify they work
3. **Week 3:** Create drawings and finalize component selections
4. **Week 4:** Complete bill of materials and operation manual, review and polish

### Design Tips:
- Start with proven system types - don't over-innovate
- Simple designs are often better than complex ones
- Make sure components are actually available to purchase
- Include redundancy for critical equipment (backup air pump)
- Design for ease of maintenance

### Documentation Tips:
- Be specific with specifications (not just "pump" but "350 GPH submersible pump")
- Include part numbers or model numbers when possible
- Organize information logically
- Use tables and bullet points for clarity
- Proofread everything

### Common Pitfalls:
- Undersizing grow beds (remember 1:1 minimum ratio)
- Forgetting to account for head loss in pump sizing
- Missing small but essential items (air line tubing, check valves)
- Unrealistic budgets (too low, not researched)
- Drawings without dimensions or scale
