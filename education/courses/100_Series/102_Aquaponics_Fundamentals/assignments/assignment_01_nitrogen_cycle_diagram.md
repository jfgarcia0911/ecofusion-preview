## Background

The nitrogen cycle is the biological engine of aquaponics. Fish waste (ammonia) is converted by beneficial bacteria into nitrites and then nitrates, which plants use as fertilizer. Understanding this cycle is essential for maintaining a healthy, productive aquaponic system.

### Key Components to Include:

1. **Fish** - Producers of ammonia through respiration and waste
2. **Ammonia (NH₃/NH₄⁺)** - Toxic to fish, first form of nitrogen waste
3. **Nitrosomonas bacteria** - Convert ammonia to nitrite
4. **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)** - Toxic to fish, intermediate compound
5. **Nitrobacter bacteria** - Convert nitrite to nitrate
6. **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)** - Plant fertilizer, less toxic to fish
7. **Plants** - Absorb nitrates for growth
8. **Clean Water** - Returns to fish tank

## Tips for Success

2. **Use arrows** to clearly show the direction of flow
3. **Make it visual** - use shapes, colors, and icons to enhance understanding

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Missing one of the eight key components
- ❌ Incorrect chemical formulas (NH₃ vs. NH₄, NO₂ vs. NO₃)
- ❌ Arrows going the wrong direction
- ❌ Confusing Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter bacteria
- ❌ Not showing that water returns to the fish tank
- ❌ Illegible handwriting or unclear organization
