## Background

Water quality management is one of the most critical skills in aquaponics. Regular testing and proper interpretation of results allows you to:
- Ensure fish health and survival
- Optimize plant nutrient availability
- Detect problems early before they become crises
- Track system cycling and maturity
- Make informed management decisions

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Acceptable Range | Ideal Range | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| **pH** | 6.0-8.0 | 6.8-7.2 | Balance between fish and plant needs |
| **Ammonia (NH₃)** | < 1.0 ppm | 0 ppm | Toxic above 2 ppm |
| **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)** | < 1.0 ppm | 0 ppm | Toxic above 5 ppm |
| **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)** | 5-150 ppm | 20-40 ppm | Plant fertilizer |

## Lab Procedure

### Part 1: Perform Water Tests (10 minutes)

For **each of the three water samples**, perform the following tests:

#### Test 1: pH
1. Fill test tube to 5ml line with water sample
2. Add 3 drops of pH test solution
3. Cap and shake for 5 seconds
4. Compare color to pH color chart
5. Record result to nearest 0.2 pH unit

#### Test 2: Ammonia (NH₃)
1. Fill test tube to 5ml line with water sample
2. Add 8 drops of Ammonia Test Solution #1
3. Add 8 drops of Ammonia Test Solution #2
4. Cap and shake for 5 seconds
5. Wait 5 minutes for color development
6. Compare to ammonia color chart
7. Record result in ppm

#### Test 3: Nitrite (NO₂⁻)
1. Fill test tube to 5ml line with water sample
2. Add 5 drops of Nitrite Test Solution
3. Cap and shake for 5 seconds
4. Wait 5 minutes for color development
5. Compare to nitrite color chart
6. Record result in ppm

#### Test 4: Nitrate (NO₃⁻)
1. Fill test tube to 5ml line with water sample
2. Add 10 drops of Nitrate Test Solution #1
3. Cap and shake vigorously for 30 seconds (important!)
4. Add 10 drops of Nitrate Test Solution #2
5. Cap and shake again for 1 minute
6. Wait 5 minutes for color development
7. Compare to nitrate color chart
8. Record result in ppm

**Repeat for all three samples (A, B, C)**

### Part 3: Data Interpretation (10 minutes)

For each water sample, answer the following questions on your worksheet:

#### Sample A Analysis (New System)
1. What do the ammonia and nitrite levels tell you about this system?
2. Is this system safe for fish? Why or why not?

#### Sample B Analysis (Cycling System)
1. How do the parameters differ from Sample A?
2. What evidence shows that beneficial bacteria are establishing?
3. Estimate how much longer until this system is fully cycled.

#### Sample C Analysis (Mature System)
1. Which parameters indicate this is a mature, healthy system?
2. Are any parameters outside ideal ranges? If so, what actions would you recommend?
3. Is this system ready for maximum fish and plant stocking?

#### Comparative Analysis

## Tips for Success

### Testing Tips:
1. **Read the color chart in good lighting** - fluorescent or natural light works best
2. **Start timing** as soon as you shake the tube - set a timer!
3. **Hold the tube against the white part** of the color chart
4. **Don't rush the waiting periods** - color development takes time
5. **Rinse tubes between tests** if reusing for the same parameter

### Interpretation Tips:
1. **Think about the nitrogen cycle** - what comes first, second, third?
2. **Consider time** - newer systems should have different profiles
3. **Safe ranges** - remember what's toxic to fish
4. **Trends matter** - is ammonia rising or falling?

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Not shaking the nitrate test vigorously enough (30 seconds + 1 minute required!)
- ❌ Skipping the wait time before reading colors
- ❌ Reading colors in poor lighting conditions
- ❌ Forgetting to include units (ppm)
- ❌ Not distinguishing between acceptable and ideal ranges
- ❌ Misidentifying which bacteria convert which compounds

## Real-World Application

In a real aquaponic system, you would:
- **Week 1-2:** Test daily (ammonia and pH)
- **Week 3-6:** Test every 2-3 days (all parameters)
- **Mature system:** Test weekly (all parameters)
- **When problems arise:** Test daily until resolved

This assignment simulates the testing you'll do throughout your aquaponics journey.
