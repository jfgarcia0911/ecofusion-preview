## The Scenario

You are the production manager at Green Valley Urban Farm, a 6,000 sq ft greenhouse facility producing:
- Butterhead and romaine lettuce (60% of production)
- Fresh herbs (basil, cilantro, mint) (25% of production)
- Cherry tomatoes (15% of production)

It's mid-March, and during your Monday morning scouting, you observe the following:

### Observations

**Lettuce Section (3,600 sq ft):**
- Yellow sticky traps: Average 12 small dark flies per trap (up from 3 last week)
- Several young lettuce plants are stunted and wilting despite adequate water
- When you remove a struggling plant, you see small translucent larvae with black heads in the growing media
- Approximately 5% of lettuce transplants from the last two weeks are affected

**Herb Section (1,500 sq ft):**
- Basil plants: Curled leaves on new growth, sticky residue on some leaves
- Inspecting leaf undersides reveals clusters of small green insects
- Approximately 15 plants out of 200 show visible infestation
- A few leaves show black sooty mold

**Tomato Section (900 sq ft):**
- Plants appear healthy at first glance
- Closer inspection with hand lens reveals tiny mites and fine webbing on undersides of lower leaves
- Affected leaves show stippling (tiny yellow dots)
- 3 of 24 plants showing symptoms

## Answer Key (Instructor Reference)

### Pest Identification
- **Lettuce:** Fungus gnats (larvae feeding on roots, adults on sticky traps)
- **Herbs:** Aphids (green clusters, sticky honeydew, curled leaves, sooty mold)
- **Tomatoes:** Two-spotted spider mites (webbing, stippling, found on leaf undersides)

### Recommended Controls
- **Fungus gnats:** Hypoaspis miles + Bt (israelensis), reduce watering, improve drainage
- **Aphids:** Aphidius colemani wasps or ladybugs, remove heavily infested plants, reduce N
- **Spider mites:** Phytoseiulus persimilis, increase humidity, remove lower leaves
