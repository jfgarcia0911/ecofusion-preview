# Glossary: Crop Production & Management Terms
## Course 106

---

## A

**Action Threshold**
The pest population level at which control measures should be implemented to prevent economic damage. Part of IPM decision-making.

**Auxin**
A plant hormone that promotes root development. Synthetic auxins (IBA, NAA) are used in rooting hormones for cuttings.

---

## B

**Biological Control**
The use of living organisms (beneficial insects, mites, nematodes, or microorganisms) to control pest populations.

**Bolting**
Premature flowering in leafy crops, triggered by stress, long days, or high temperatures. Causes bitter flavor and reduced marketability.

**Bottom Heat**
Heating applied beneath propagation trays to maintain optimal root zone temperature (typically 70-75°F), accelerating germination and rooting.

---

## C

**Cohort**
A group of plants seeded at the same time and moving through production together.

**Crop Cycle**
The total time from seeding to harvest for a given crop.

**Cultivar**
A cultivated variety of a plant selected for specific traits. Different from a species.

**Cultural Controls**
Pest and disease management practices that modify the growing environment or practices to make conditions less favorable for pests (e.g., sanitation, spacing, watering practices).

---

## D

**Days to Maturity (DTM)**
The expected number of days from transplanting (or direct seeding) until harvest.

**Determinate**
A growth habit where plants reach a set size and produce fruit in a concentrated period. Common in bush tomatoes.

**DIF (Differential)**
The difference between day and night temperatures. Used to control plant height and flowering.

**Direct Seeding**
Planting seeds directly in the final growing location rather than transplanting.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)**
A measure of dissolved salts in nutrient solution, indicating nutrient concentration. Measured in mS/cm or dS/m.

---

## F

**F1 Hybrid**
First-generation cross between two distinct parent lines. Exhibits hybrid vigor but does not breed true from saved seed.

**Field Heat**
The heat retained in produce at harvest that must be removed quickly to extend shelf life.

**Forcing**
Manipulating environmental conditions to cause plants to grow or bloom out of their natural season.

---

## G

**Germination Rate**
The percentage of seeds expected to sprout under optimal conditions.

**Growing Media**
The material in which plants grow in soilless systems (rockwool, coco coir, perlite, etc.).

---

## H

**Hardening Off**
The process of gradually acclimating plants to more stressful conditions before transplanting.

**Headhouse**
The area used for propagation, transplanting, and other non-production activities.

**Honeydew**
Sticky, sugary excretion produced by aphids, whiteflies, and other sucking insects. Promotes sooty mold growth.

---

## I

**Indeterminate**
A growth habit where plants continue growing and producing fruit throughout the season. Common in vining tomatoes.

**IPM (Integrated Pest Management)**
A science-based approach to pest management that combines prevention, monitoring, and intervention using multiple control methods while minimizing environmental impact.

---

## L

**Lot Number**
A unique identifier assigned to a batch of plants or produce for traceability purposes.

---

## M

**Microgreens**
Young vegetable greens harvested 7-21 days after germination at the cotyledon or first true leaf stage.

---

## O

**Oomycete**
Water molds that cause diseases like Pythium root rot and downy mildew. Not true fungi but managed similarly.

**Open-Pollinated (OP)**
Varieties that breed true from seed when isolated from cross-pollination.

---

## P

**Pathogen**
A disease-causing organism (fungus, bacteria, virus, or oomycete).

**Photoperiod**
The duration of light in a 24-hour period. Influences flowering in photoperiod-sensitive crops.

**Plug**
A young transplant grown in a small cell, ready for transplanting.

**Post-Harvest**
Activities occurring after harvest, including cooling, washing, grading, and packaging.

**Pre-Cooling**
Rapidly removing field heat from harvested produce to extend shelf life.

**Priming (Seed)**
A technique where seeds are partially germinated then dried for storage, improving germination speed and uniformity.

**Propagation**
The process of creating new plants from seeds, cuttings, or other plant parts.

---

## R

**Respiration Rate**
The speed at which harvested produce consumes sugars and produces CO2 and heat. Higher rates mean faster quality decline.

**Rooting Hormone**
A product containing auxins that promotes root development on cuttings.

---

## S

**Scouting**
Regular, systematic inspection of crops to detect pest and disease problems early.

**Seed Vigor**
A seed's ability to germinate and establish strong seedlings, even under suboptimal conditions.

**Sooty Mold**
Black fungal growth that develops on honeydew secreted by sucking insects. Cosmetic damage; indicates pest presence.

**Stippling**
Tiny light-colored dots on leaves caused by piercing-sucking insects (spider mites, thrips) feeding on cell contents.

**Stratification**
Cold treatment required by some seeds to break dormancy and enable germination.

**Succession Planting**
Planting the same crop at regular intervals to achieve continuous harvest rather than a single large harvest.

---

## T

**Traceability**
The ability to track a product through all stages of production, processing, and distribution.

**Transplant**
Moving a young plant from a propagation area to its final growing location.

**True Leaves**
The leaves that develop after the cotyledons (seed leaves), showing the plant's characteristic leaf shape.

**Turns (per year)**
The number of complete crop cycles that can be achieved in a growing space annually.

---

## V

**Variety**
A distinct type within a crop species with consistent characteristics.

**Vegetative Growth**
Plant growth focused on leaves, stems, and roots rather than flowers and fruit.

**Vigor**
The health, robustness, and growth rate of a plant or seed lot.

---

## Y

**Yield**
The quantity of harvestable product produced, typically expressed as weight per unit area or per plant.
