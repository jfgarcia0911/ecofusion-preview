_______________________________________

---

## Answer Key

1. **C** - 2+ years
2. **A** - Track a product from harvest back to seed lot and growing location
3. **A** - Records that identify what's working and what's not
4. **B** - A unique identifier linking product to production records
5. **B** - Easier searching, analysis, and backup
6. **False** - Records are valuable for both compliance AND continuous improvement
7. **True** - Regular/continuous logging enables trend analysis and problem diagnosis
8. **True** - Tax-related records should be kept 7 years per IRS guidelines

**9. Acceptable production records (any three):**
- Planting/seeding logs
- Environmental data (temp, humidity, CO2)
- Nutrient/fertilizer records
- IPM/scouting logs
- Irrigation records
- Harvest records
- Yield data
- Quality assessments
- Labor hours
- Equipment maintenance logs

**10. Answer:** Records enable rapid identification of all affected products, their distribution, and customers who received them. This allows targeted recall notification, limits scope of recall, demonstrates due diligence to regulators, and protects consumers from potential harm. Without records, a recall may need to cover all products from an extended period.
