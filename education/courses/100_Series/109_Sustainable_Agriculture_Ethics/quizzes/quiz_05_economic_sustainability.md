## Questions

### Question 1
What does economic sustainability mean for farmers?

a) Making maximum profit immediately
b) Building financially viable operations that can continue long-term
c) Economic concerns don't matter for sustainable farming
d) Sustainability means accepting poverty

### Question 2
Why is diversification important for farm economic resilience?

a) Diversification complicates operations unnecessarily
b) Multiple income streams reduce risk from market or crop failures
c) Specialization is always better
d) Diversification reduces quality

a) Pricing doesn't affect sustainability
b) Adequate prices enable farmers to cover costs and reinvest in sustainable practices
c) Farmers should always charge the lowest prices
d) Fair pricing is only for consumers

### Question 4

a) Adding unnecessary features
b) Processing or enhancing products to increase their market value

d) Only for large corporations

c) Sustainable farming should always be cheaper

a) ROI doesn't apply to sustainability
b) Track resource savings, premium pricing, and long-term cost reductions
c) Sustainability never provides ROI
d) Only through government subsidies

### Question 7
What role does local market development play in economic sustainability?

a) Local markets are too small to matter
b) Reduces dependence on distant markets and builds community support
c) Only export markets are valuable
d) Market location is irrelevant

### Question 8
Why is financial planning essential for sustainable operations?

a) Planning is unnecessary bureaucracy
b) Ensures resources for continued operation and sustainable practices
c) Sustainable farms don't need planning
d) Only conventional farms need financial planning

### Question 9
How can farmers balance ethical practices with profitability?

a) Ethics and profit are mutually exclusive
b) Sustainable practices can reduce costs and attract premium markets
c) Always choose profit over ethics
d) Ethical farming means financial failure

### Question 10
What is the concept of true cost accounting?

a) Just counting money
b) Including environmental and social costs in pricing decisions
c) True cost accounting is too complicated
d) Only direct costs matter
