## Lesson Content

### 3.1 Foundations of Economic Justice

#### Core Concepts

**Economic Justice:** The moral principle that all people should have fair access to wealth, opportunities, and economic security.

**Key Principles:**

| Principle | Description | Agricultural Application |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------------|
| **Distributive Justice** | Fair allocation of resources | Who gets profits from food system? |
| **Procedural Justice** | Fair processes and rules | Transparent markets, equal access |
| **Corrective Justice** | Remedying unfair outcomes | Addressing historical inequities |
| **Retributive Justice** | Accountability for wrongdoing | Consequences for exploitation |

```
THE ECONOMIC JUSTICE FRAMEWORK

           ┌──────────────────────────┐
           │   ECONOMIC JUSTICE       │
           │   IN AGRICULTURE         │
           └────────┬─────────────────┘
                    │
        ┌───────────┼───────────┐
        │           │           │
        ▼           ▼           ▼
    ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐
    │ FAIR   │ │ FAIR   │ │ FAIR   │
    │ ACCESS │ │ PRICES │ │ PROFIT │
    └────────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘
        │           │           │
        ▼           ▼           ▼
    Resources   Living      Equitable
    & Markets   Wages      Distribution
```

#### Economic Rights in Agriculture

**Farmers' Rights:**
- Right to fair price for products
- Right to land ownership/access
- Right to save and exchange seeds
- Right to organize collectively
- Right to information and markets

**Workers' Rights:**
- Right to living wage
- Right to safe working conditions
- Right to organize and bargain
- Right to dignity and respect
- Right to legal protections

**Consumers' Rights:**
- Right to safe, nutritious food
- Right to information (labeling)
- Right to affordable prices
- Right to choose
- Right to participate in food policy

### 3.2 The Economics of Food Production

#### True Cost Accounting

**Problem:** Market prices often don't reflect true costs and benefits.

```
WHAT'S MISSING FROM THE PRICE TAG?

Market Price: $3.00/lb tomatoes
        │
        ├─ Visible Costs (Included)
        │  ├─ Seeds & inputs: $0.50
        │  ├─ Labor: $1.00
        │  ├─ Distribution: $0.75
        │  └─ Profit margin: $0.75
        │
        └─ Hidden Costs (Externalized)
           ├─ Environmental damage: $0.80
           ├─ Water depletion: $0.40
           ├─ Health impacts (pesticides): $0.30
           ├─ Climate change: $0.50
           └─ Soil degradation: $0.20

True Cost = $6.20/lb (Market shows only $3.00)
```

**Ethical Questions:**
- Who should pay for externalized costs?
- Should prices reflect environmental impact?
- How do we internalize true costs without making food unaffordable?

#### Market Structure and Power

**Concentration in Agriculture:**

| Sector | Top 4 Companies Control |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Seed Industry** | 60% of global market |
| **Agrochemicals** | 75% of global market |
| **Grain Trading** | 90% of global market |
| **Meat Processing** | 85% of US beef market |
| **Food Retail** | 65% of US grocery market |

**Power Dynamics:**

```
AGRICULTURAL POWER STRUCTURE

         ┌─────────────────────┐
         │   CORPORATIONS      │
         │   (High Power)      │
         └──────┬──────────────┘
                │
                │ Set Prices
                │ Control Markets
                │
         ┌──────▼──────────────┐
         │   FARMERS           │
         │   (Low Power)       │
         └──────┬──────────────┘
                │
                │ Price Takers
                │ Limited Options
                │
         ┌──────▼──────────────┐
         │   WORKERS           │
         │   (Lowest Power)    │
         └─────────────────────┘
```

**Ethical Issues:**
- Monopoly pricing power
- Farmer dependency
- Suppressed competition
- Regulatory capture
- Wealth concentration

### 3.3 Fair Pricing Ethics

#### What is a Fair Price?

**Different Perspectives:**

| Approach | Definition of Fair Price | Ethical Basis |
|----------|--------------------------|---------------|
| **Market-Based** | Price determined by supply/demand | Efficiency, consumer sovereignty |
| **Cost-Plus** | Cost of production + fair profit | Sustainability, producer rights |
| **Living Wage** | Ensures decent living for all | Dignity, basic needs |
| **Value-Based** | Reflects quality, ethics, externalities | True cost, transparency |

#### The Farmer's Dilemma

**Case Study: Commodity Pricing**

```
CORN FARMER ECONOMICS (Example)

Production Cost per Bushel: $4.50
├─ Seed: $0.80
├─ Fertilizer: $1.20
├─ Pesticides: $0.40
├─ Fuel: $0.60
├─ Equipment: $0.70
├─ Labor: $0.50
└─ Land: $0.30

Market Price: $4.00/bushel

Result: -$0.50 loss per bushel

Why Continue?
├─ Hope prices will rise
├─ Government subsidies ($0.60/bushel)
├─ No other options (debt, specialization)
└─ Love of farming
```

**Ethical Questions:**
- Is it just for farmers to lose money on production?
- Are subsidies fair (to taxpayers, competing farmers, other countries)?
- Who benefits from low commodity prices?
- What is the social contract with farmers?

#### Fair Trade Principles

**Core Commitments:**

| Principle | Implementation | Impact |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| **Fair Price** | Minimum price floor + premium | Price stability, investment |
| **Fair Labor** | No child/forced labor, safe conditions | Worker dignity, rights |
| **Direct Trade** | Eliminate exploitative middlemen | More income to producers |
| **Democratic** | Cooperative organization | Farmer empowerment |
| **Environmental** | Sustainable practices required | Ecological protection |
| **Transparency** | Open supply chains | Accountability, trust |

### 3.4 Labor Justice in Agriculture

#### The Agricultural Labor Crisis

**Statistics:**

| Fact | Number | Ethical Concern |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| **Farmworkers in US** | ~2.4 million | Vulnerable population |
| **Median Income** | $25,000/year | Below poverty line |
| **Immigrant Workers** | 73% | Immigration status exploitation |
| **Experience Food Insecurity** | 31% | Feed nation, can't feed families |
| **Lack Health Insurance** | 51% | Health/safety risks |

```
AGRICULTURAL LABOR HIERARCHY

Farm Owners/Operators
       │
       │ Often struggle financially
       │
       ▼
Labor Contractors (sometimes)
       │
       │ Take cut of wages
       │
       ▼
Farmworkers
       │
       ├─ H-2A Guest Workers (legal, temporary)
       ├─ Documented Immigrants
       └─ Undocumented Workers

All face:
├─ Low wages
├─ Seasonal instability
├─ Hazardous conditions
├─ Limited legal protections
└─ Pesticide exposure
```

#### Living Wage vs. Minimum Wage

**Living Wage:** Income needed to meet basic needs (housing, food, healthcare, childcare, transportation) in a specific location.

**Comparison:**

| Location | Minimum Wage | Living Wage (1 adult, 2 children) | Gap |
|----------|--------------|----------------------------------|-----|
| **Federal (US)** | $7.25/hr | $31.00/hr | $23.75/hr |
| **California** | $15.50/hr | $36.00/hr | $20.50/hr |
| **Florida** | $11.00/hr | $29.00/hr | $18.00/hr |

**Ethical Implications:**
- Minimum wage is not a living wage
- Workers subsidize cheap food with poverty
- Society subsidizes with social services
- Intergenerational poverty perpetuated

### 3.5 Scale, Consolidation, and Corporate Ethics

#### The Get Big or Get Out Paradigm

**Historical Context:**
- 1950s: 5.6 million farms in US (avg. 213 acres)
- 2020s: 2.0 million farms in US (avg. 445 acres)
- Result: Fewer, larger farms; loss of small/mid-size operations

**Economics of Scale:**

| Farm Size | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| **Small (<100 acres)** | Diversity, quality, local markets, agility | Higher costs/unit, less efficiency, market access |
| **Medium (100-1000 acres)** | Balance, community integration | Squeezed by both sides, limited support |
| **Large (>1000 acres)** | Efficiency, market power, access to capital | Environmental impact, monoculture, community disruption |

**Ethical Questions:**
- Is bigger always better?
- What is lost in consolidation?
- Do small farms have intrinsic value?
- What is the optimal scale for sustainability?

#### Corporate Responsibility in Agriculture

**Ethical Obligations of Agricultural Corporations:**

```
CORPORATE AGRICULTURAL ETHICS

Shareholders
    │
    │ Profit Maximization?
    │
    ▼
Corporation
    │
    ├──► Farmers: Fair contracts, fair prices
    │
    ├──► Workers: Living wages, safe conditions
    │
    ├──► Consumers: Safe products, honest marketing
    │
    ├──► Communities: Local investment, tax responsibility
    │
    ├──► Environment: Minimize harm, restore ecosystems

```

**Case Study: Seed Industry Consolidation**

**Ethical Issues:**
- Patent control over life

### 3.6 Alternative Economic Models

#### Cooperative Economics

**Principles:**
- Member ownership
- Democratic control (one member, one vote)
- Member economic participation
- Autonomy and independence
- Education and training
- Cooperation among cooperatives
- Concern for community

**Examples:**
- Farmer cooperatives (Land O'Lakes, Ocean Spray)
- Worker cooperatives (farm and food processing)
- Consumer cooperatives (food co-ops)
- Multi-stakeholder cooperatives

**Benefits:**
- Equitable profit distribution
- Local economic development
- Democratic decision-making
- Values-driven business

#### Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)

**Model:** Consumers purchase "shares" of harvest in advance, sharing risk and reward with farmer.

**Economic Ethics:**
- Fair price paid upfront
- Risk-sharing between farmer and consumer
- Direct relationship
- Seasonal eating
- Community building

#### Solidarity Economy

**Vision:** Economic system based on cooperation, equity, and sustainability rather than competition and profit maximization.

**Components:**
- Worker ownership
- Fair trade
- Cooperative banking
- Community land trusts
- Time banking
- Local currencies

### 3.7 Economic Trade-offs and Dilemmas

#### Affordable Food vs. Living Wages

**The Dilemma:**
- Low food prices benefit poor consumers
- Low food prices often mean low farmer/worker incomes
- Who should subsidize whom?

**Options:**
| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| **Keep prices low** | Accessible food | Exploits producers |
| **Raise prices** | Fair to producers | Excludes poor consumers |
| **Government subsidies** | Supports both | Taxpayer burden, market distortion |
| **Income support** | Targets need | Political challenges |

#### Efficiency vs. Resilience

**Tension:**
- Economic efficiency favors specialization, scale, global supply chains
- Resilience requires diversity, redundancy, local systems

**COVID-19 Example:**
- Efficient system couldn't adapt to disruption
- Food destroyed while people went hungry
- Regional food systems proved more resilient
