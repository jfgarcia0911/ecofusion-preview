## Lesson Content

### 3.1 The Circulatory System

#### Overview

Fish have a **single-loop circulatory system** - blood flows through the heart once per complete circuit.

```
FISH CIRCULATORY SYSTEM (Single Loop)

    ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                                     │
    │         BODY TISSUES                │
    │     (Oxygenated blood to)           │
    │                                     │
    └──────────┬──────────────────────────┘
               │
               ↓ (Deoxygenated)
         ┌─────────┐
         │ SINUS   │
         │ VENOSUS │
         └────┬────┘
              ↓
         ┌─────────┐
         │ ATRIUM  │ ← Single atrium
         └────┬────┘
              ↓
         ┌─────────┐
         │VENTRICLE│ ← Single ventricle
         └────┬────┘
              ↓
         ┌─────────┐
         │ BULBUS  │
         │ARTERIOSUS│
         └────┬────┘
              ↓ (Deoxygenated)
         ┌─────────┐
         │  GILLS  │ ← Oxygen pickup
         └────┬────┘
              ↓ (Oxygenated)
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │                      │
    │    BODY TISSUES      │ ← Oxygen delivery
    │                      │
    └──────────────────────┘
```

#### Heart Structure

**Four Sequential Chambers:**

| Chamber | Function | Characteristics |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| **Sinus Venosus** | Collection chamber | Receives deoxygenated blood from body |
| **Atrium** | Receiving chamber | Thin-walled, fills ventricle |
| **Ventricle** | Pumping chamber | Thick muscular wall, generates pressure |
| **Bulbus Arteriosus** | Pressure stabilizer | Elastic, smooths blood flow |

**Blood Flow Sequence:**
1. Deoxygenated blood from body → Sinus Venosus
2. Sinus Venosus → Atrium
3. Atrium → Ventricle
4. Ventricle → Bulbus Arteriosus
5. Bulbus → Gills (oxygenation)
6. Gills → Body tissues (delivery)
7. Body tissues → Back to Sinus Venosus

#### Comparison: Fish vs. Mammals

```
CIRCULATORY COMPARISON

FISH (Single Loop)          MAMMAL (Double Loop)

Heart: 2 chambers          Heart: 4 chambers
(1 atrium, 1 ventricle)    (2 atria, 2 ventricles)

  ┌→Gills→Body→┐              ┌→Lungs→┐
  │            │              │       ↓
Heart────────→ │            Heart←──Body
                             ↓       ↑
                             └→Body→┘

Lower pressure             Higher pressure
Slower circulation         Faster circulation
```

| Feature | Fish | Mammals |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Heart chambers** | 2 main (atrium, ventricle) | 4 (2 atria, 2 ventricles) |
| **Circuits** | Single loop | Double loop |
| **Blood pressure** | Lower | Higher |
| **Oxygen capacity** | Lower | Higher |
| **Metabolic rate** | Lower (cold-blooded) | Higher (warm-blooded) |

### 3.2 The Respiratory System

#### Gill Structure

```
GILL ANATOMY

FISH HEAD (Side View)          GILL ARCH (Close-up)
    ┌────────┐
    │  👁    │                 Gill Rakers
    │        │                      ↓
    │    ┌───┤                   │││││││
    │    │███│ ← Gills           │││││││
    │    │███│   (4-5 pairs)     ═══════ ← Gill Arch
    │    └───┤                   │││││││
    └────────┘                   │││││││
         ↑                          ↓
     Operculum                Gill Filaments
     (Gill Cover)                  ↓
                              ┌───────────┐
                              │ Lamellae  │ ← Blood vessels
                              │ │││││││  │    (gas exchange)
                              └───────────┘
```

#### Gill Components

| Structure | Function | Details |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Gill Arches** | Support | Bony or cartilaginous framework |
| **Gill Rakers** | Filter feeding | Prevent food particles from entering gills |
| **Gill Filaments** | Gas exchange surface | Thin, highly vascularized |
| **Lamellae** | Oxygen uptake | Thin plates on filaments, maximize surface area |
| **Blood Vessels** | Transport | Carry blood through lamellae |

#### Gas Exchange Process

```
COUNTERCURRENT EXCHANGE

Water Flow →→→→→→→→
    100% O₂ ──→ 70% O₂ ──→ 40% O₂ ──→ 20% O₂
         ↑          ↑          ↑          ↑
         │          │          │          │
    80% O₂ ←── 60% O₂ ←── 30% O₂ ←── 10% O₂
←←←←←←←← Blood Flow

Result: Blood reaches ~80% oxygen saturation
(Much more efficient than parallel flow)
```

**Countercurrent Mechanism:**
- **Water flows** one direction over gills
- **Blood flows** opposite direction
- **Maintains gradient** - Blood always encounters water with higher O₂
- **85-90% efficiency** - Extracts most oxygen from water

**Factors Affecting Respiration:**

| Factor | Effect on Oxygen Uptake | Aquaculture Implications |
|--------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Water temperature** | Warmer = less dissolved O₂ | Monitor closely in summer |
| **Salinity** | Saltwater holds less O₂ | Freshwater better for O₂ |
| **Water movement** | Flow increases O₂ exchange | Aeration important |
| **Gill surface area** | More area = more uptake | Larger fish need more O₂ |
| **Gill health** | Damage reduces efficiency | Prevent gill parasites/disease |

### 3.3 The Digestive System

#### Digestive Tract Anatomy

```
FISH DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

    Mouth
      ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │ Pharynx   │ ← Teeth (some species)
  └─────┬─────┘
        ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │ Esophagus │ ← Short tube
  └─────┬─────┘
        ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │  Stomach  │ ← Acid digestion
  └─────┬─────┘   (absent in some species)
        ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │ Pyloric   │ ← Caeca (finger-like
  │  Caeca    │   projections)
  └─────┬─────┘
        ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │ Intestine │ ← Nutrient absorption
  │           │   (length varies)
  │  ~~~~~~~~ │
  └─────┬─────┘
        ↓
  ┌───────────┐
  │  Rectum   │
  └─────┬─────┘
        ↓
      Anus

  ACCESSORY ORGANS:
  • Liver (bile production)
  • Pancreas (enzymes)
  • Gall bladder (bile storage)
```

#### Digestive System Variations

**By Diet Type:**

| Diet | Stomach | Intestine Length | Teeth | Examples |
|------|---------|------------------|-------|----------|
| **Carnivore** | Large, muscular | Short (0.6-2x body) | Sharp, pointed | Bass, pike, trout |
| **Omnivore** | Moderate | Medium (2-5x body) | Various | Tilapia, carp, catfish |
| **Herbivore** | Small/absent | Very long (5-25x body) | Grinding | Grass carp, parrotfish |

```
INTESTINE LENGTH COMPARISON

CARNIVORE (Bass)
╔════════════════╗
║     →→→→       ║  Short intestine
╚════════════════╝

OMNIVORE (Tilapia)
╔════════════════╗
║   →→→→→→→      ║  Medium intestine
╚════════════════╝

HERBIVORE (Grass Carp)
╔════════════════╗
║ →→→→→→→→→→→→→→ ║  Very long, coiled
╚════════════════╝
```

#### Digestive Process

**Steps:**
1. **Ingestion** - Food taken in through mouth
2. **Mechanical breakdown** - Pharyngeal teeth, stomach grinding
3. **Chemical digestion** - Enzymes break down proteins, fats, carbs
4. **Absorption** - Nutrients absorbed through intestinal wall
5. **Elimination** - Waste excreted through anus

**Key Digestive Organs:**

| Organ | Function | Secretions |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **Stomach** | Protein digestion, storage | Hydrochloric acid, pepsin |
| **Pyloric Caeca** | Increase surface area | Digestive enzymes |
| **Liver** | Metabolism, detoxification | Bile (fat digestion) |
| **Pancreas** | Enzyme production | Amylase, lipase, protease |
| **Intestine** | Nutrient absorption | Various enzymes |

### 3.4 The Swim Bladder

#### Structure and Function

```
SWIM BLADDER ANATOMY

    Vertebral Column
          ↓
    ══════════════════
    ╔════════════════╗
    ║                ║
    ║  Swim Bladder  ║ ← Gas-filled sac
    ║  (Pneumatic)   ║
    ║                ║
    ╚════════════════╝
          │
          ↓
    Pneumatic Duct
    (in some species)
          ↓
    Esophagus

FUNCTION: Buoyancy Control
```

**Types of Swim Bladders:**

| Type | Connection | Gas Exchange | Examples |
|------|------------|--------------|----------|
| **Physostomous** | Duct to esophagus | Gulps air at surface | Carp, trout, catfish |
| **Physoclistous** | No duct connection | Gas gland secretion | Bass, perch, tuna |

**Functions:**
1. **Buoyancy** - Maintains neutral buoyancy at depth
2. **Sound production** - Vibration for communication
3. **Sound reception** - Amplifies vibrations (some species)
4. **Respiration** - Supplemental in some species

**Buoyancy Control:**
```
DEPTH ADJUSTMENT

ASCENDING                DESCENDING
  (Gas expands)            (Gas compresses)
       ↑                        ↓
   ┌────────┐              ┌────────┐
   │  Swim  │              │  Swim  │
   │ Bladder│              │ Bladder│
   │  ○○○○  │              │   ○○   │
   └────────┘              └────────┘
       ↑                        ↓
  Release gas            Add gas/swim down
```

**Aquaculture Implications:**
- **Rapid pressure changes** can cause swim bladder trauma
- **Gas bubble disease** from supersaturated water
- **Barotrauma** from pumping fish too quickly

### 3.5 The Nervous System

#### Brain Structure

```
FISH BRAIN (Simplified)

Top View:
    Olfactory Bulbs
         ↓↓
      ┌─────┐
      │ ○ ○ │ ← Cerebrum (small)
      └──┬──┘
    ┌────┴────┐
    │ Optic   │ ← Optic Lobes (vision)
    │ Lobes   │
    └────┬────┘
      ┌──┴──┐
      │Cerebel│ ← Cerebellum (coordination)
      └──┬──┘
     ┌───┴───┐
     │ Medulla│ ← Medulla (vital functions)
     └───┬───┘
         │
    Spinal Cord
```

**Brain Regions and Functions:**

| Region | Primary Functions | Well-Developed In |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Olfactory Bulbs** | Smell, chemical detection | Bottom feeders, salmon |
| **Cerebrum** | Basic learning, behavior | All fish (relatively small) |
| **Optic Lobes** | Vision processing | Visual predators |
| **Cerebellum** | Movement coordination | Active swimmers |
| **Medulla** | Respiration, heart rate | All fish |

#### Spinal Cord and Nerves

**Organization:**
- **Central Nervous System** - Brain and spinal cord
- **Peripheral Nervous System** - Cranial and spinal nerves
- **Autonomic Nervous System** - Controls involuntary functions

**Reflex Arcs:**
```
REFLEX RESPONSE

Stimulus → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord → Motor Neuron → Muscle Response
(Touch)                    (Integration)              (Movement)

Example: Tail flip escape response (milliseconds)
```

### 3.6 Reproductive System

#### Types of Reproduction

**Sexual Reproduction Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Oviparous** | Lay eggs externally | Most fish (90%+) |
| **Ovoviviparous** | Eggs hatch internally, no placenta | Some sharks, mollies |
| **Viviparous** | Live birth with placental connection | Some sharks, guppies |

#### Reproductive Anatomy

```
MALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

    Testes (Paired)
         ↓↓
    ┌────────────┐
    │ ████████   │ ← Sperm production
    │ ████████   │
    └─────┬──────┘
          ↓
    Sperm Ducts
          ↓
    Urogenital Opening
          ↓
    Release (Milt)


FEMALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

    Ovaries (Paired)
         ↓↓
    ┌────────────┐
    │ ○○○○○○○○   │ ← Egg development
    │ ○○○○○○○○   │
    └─────┬──────┘
          ↓
    Oviducts
          ↓
    Urogenital Opening
          ↓
    Release (Eggs/Roe)
```

**Gonad Development:**

| Stage | Description | % Body Weight |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Immature** | Small, undeveloped | <1% |
| **Developing** | Growing, maturing | 1-5% |
| **Mature** | Ready to spawn | 10-30% |
| **Spawning** | Active release | Variable |
| **Spent** | Post-spawn, recovery | <2% |

#### Spawning Behaviors

**Common Patterns:**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Broadcast spawning** | Eggs/sperm released into water | Most marine fish |
| **Substrate spawning** | Eggs laid on surfaces | Bass, catfish |
| **Nest building** | Male prepares/guards nest | Sunfish, sticklebacks |
| **Mouthbrooding** | Eggs/fry held in mouth | Tilapia, some catfish |
| **Live bearing** | Internal fertilization, live young | Guppies, mollies |

**Environmental Triggers:**
- **Temperature** - Warming or cooling
- **Photoperiod** - Day length changes
- **Water level** - Rising water (flooding)
- **Substrate** - Availability of spawning sites
- **Pheromones** - Chemical signals from mates

### 3.7 Excretory and Osmoregulatory Systems

#### The Kidney

```
KIDNEY AND OSMOREGULATION

    Kidneys (Paired, along backbone)
         ↓↓
    ┌──────────────┐
    │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  │ ← Filters blood
    │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  │   Regulates salts
    └──────┬───────┘
           ↓
      Urinary Duct
           ↓
    Urogenital Opening
           ↓
      Urine Excretion
```

**Kidney Functions:**
1. **Waste removal** - Ammonia, urea excretion
2. **Osmoregulation** - Salt and water balance
3. **Blood pressure** - Fluid volume regulation

#### Osmoregulation Challenges

**Freshwater Fish:**
```
FRESHWATER OSMOREGULATION

Environment: Low salt, high water
Fish body: High salt, low water

    Water IN →→→→ ╔════╗
    (by osmosis)  ║FISH║ → Dilute urine OUT
                  ║    ║   (excess water)
    Salts OUT ←←← ╚════╝
    (through gills) ↑
                    │
              Active salt
              uptake (gills)
```

**Marine Fish:**
```
MARINE OSMOREGULATION

Environment: High salt, low water
Fish body: Low salt, high water

    Water OUT ←←← ╔════╗
    (by osmosis)  ║FISH║ ← Drinks seawater
                  ║    ║
    Salts OUT →→→ ╚════╝ → Concentrated urine
    (active excretion) ↑    (conserve water)
                       │
                 Specialized cells
                 excrete salts (gills)
```

| Environment | Challenge | Solution |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Freshwater** | Too much water in | Dilute urine, active salt uptake |
| **Marine** | Too much water out | Drink seawater, excrete salts |
| **Brackish** | Variable salinity | Flexible osmoregulation |

### 3.8 Integration and Homeostasis

#### Body Systems Working Together

```
INTEGRATED SYSTEMS

    NERVOUS SYSTEM
          │
          ├──► Coordinates all systems
          │
    ┌─────┴─────┬──────────┬─────────┐
    ▼           ▼          ▼         ▼
CIRCULATORY  RESPIRATORY  DIGESTIVE  EXCRETORY
    │           │          │         │
    └───────────┴──────────┴─────────┘
                  │
            HOMEOSTASIS
         (Stable internal
          environment)
```

**Example Integration: Feeding Response**

1. **Sensory Detection** - Smell, vision detect food
2. **Nervous System** - Signals hunger, initiates feeding
3. **Digestive System** - Breaks down food
4. **Circulatory System** - Distributes nutrients
5. **Excretory System** - Removes waste products
6. **Respiratory System** - Increases O₂ for metabolism
