```
    │     • Water quality requirements   │
    │     • Space needs                  │

                  ↓
    ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  2. PRODUCTION CHARACTERISTICS     │

    │     • Disease resistance           │
    └─────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                  ↓
    ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  3. ECONOMIC FACTORS               │
    │     • Market demand                │
    │     • Price per pound              │
    │     • Input costs                  │
    └─────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                  ↓
    ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  4. OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS       │
    │     • Technical skill needed       │
    │     • Infrastructure                │
    │     • Regulatory compliance        │
    └─────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                  ↓
            SPECIES CHOICE
```

#### Key Questions to Ask

**Environmental:**
- What is my water source (well, spring, municipal)?
- What temperature range can I maintain?
- What is my climate zone?
- How much space do I have?

**Technical:**
- What is my level of aquaculture experience?
- What systems can I build/maintain?
- Do I have access to quality fingerlings?
- Can I source appropriate feed?

**Economic:**
- What is my startup budget?
- What are ongoing costs?
- What markets can I access?
- What is my timeline to profitability?

**Regulatory:**
- What species are permitted in my location?
- What licenses/permits are required?
- Are there environmental restrictions?
- What are food safety requirements?

### 4.3 Major Aquaculture Species Profiles

#### Tilapia (Oreochromis spp.)

```
TILAPIA PROFILE

    ╔═══════════════════════╗
    ║     🐟 TILAPIA       ║
    ╚═══════════════════════╝

ADVANTAGES              CHALLENGES
✓ Fast growth          ✗ Cold sensitive
✓ Omnivorous           ✗ Reproduce rapidly
✓ Disease resistant    ✗ Limited market (some areas)
✓ Low O₂ tolerance     ✗ State restrictions (USA)
✓ High feed efficiency ✗ Sex reversal needed
```

**Key Characteristics:**

| Parameter | Value/Description |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Temperature** | 75-86°F (24-30°C) optimal |
| **Growth rate** | 1-2 lbs in 6-9 months |
| **Feed conversion** | 1.5-1.8:1 |
| **Market size** | 1-2 lbs (whole fish) |
| **Reproduction** | Breed year-round (managed) |
| **pH tolerance** | 6.5-9.0 |
| **DO requirement** | >4 mg/L (tolerates lower) |
| **Salinity** | Freshwater (some species tolerate brackish) |

**Best For:**
- Warm climate regions
- Indoor heated systems
- Aquaponics (excellent for plants)
- Beginner-intermediate producers
- Markets with tilapia demand

#### Channel Catfish (Ictalurus punctatus)

```
CATFISH PROFILE

    ╔═══════════════════════╗
    ║   🐟 CATFISH         ║
    ╚═══════════════════════╝

ADVANTAGES              CHALLENGES
✓ Hardy                ✗ Slow growth in cold
✓ Established markets  ✗ Disease issues (ESC, etc.)
✓ Good FCR             ✗ Needs good aeration
✓ Pond culture easy    ✗ Off-flavor potential
✓ Strong US demand     ✗ Sensitive to low O₂
```

**Key Characteristics:**

| Parameter | Value/Description |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Temperature** | 75-85°F (24-29°C) optimal |
| **Growth rate** | 1.5 lbs in 18-24 months |
| **Feed conversion** | 1.5-2.0:1 |
| **Market size** | 1-3 lbs (fillets) |
| **Reproduction** | Cavity nesters, parental care |
| **pH tolerance** | 6.5-8.5 |
| **DO requirement** | >5 mg/L critical |
| **Salinity** | Freshwater only |

**Best For:**
- Southern USA climates
- Pond-based operations
- Established markets
- Intermediate-advanced producers
- Fillet market access

```
TROUT PROFILE

    ╔═══════════════════════╗
    ║    🐟 TROUT          ║
    ╚═══════════════════════╝

ADVANTAGES              CHALLENGES
✓ Fast growth          ✗ Cold water needed
```

**Key Characteristics:**

| Parameter | Value/Description |

| **Market size** | 0.75-8+ lbs (variable) |
| **Reproduction** | Spawn in winter/spring |
| **pH tolerance** | 6.5-8.0 |
| **DO requirement** | >7 mg/L critical |
| **Salinity** | Freshwater (anadromous strains) |

**Best For:**
- Cold climate regions
- Spring-fed systems
- Premium/specialty markets
- Advanced producers
- Fee-fishing operations

### 4.4 Growth and Production Characteristics

#### Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) Comparison

```
FEED EFFICIENCY

EXCELLENT     GOOD        MODERATE     POOR
   │           │             │           │
   ▼           ▼             ▼           ▼
  1.0         1.5           2.0         3.0+
   │           │             │           │
   ├───────────┼─────────────┼───────────┤
   │ Trout     │             │           │
   │           │ Tilapia     │           │
   │           │ Catfish     │ Bass      │
   │           │             │           │
   └───────────┴─────────────┴───────────┘

Lower FCR = More efficient = Lower feed costs
```

**FCR by Species:**

| Species | Typical FCR | Feed Cost % of Total | Profit Impact |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Trout** | 1.1-1.3:1 | 40-50% | High efficiency, but expensive feed |
| **Tilapia** | 1.5-1.8:1 | 50-60% | Good efficiency, moderate feed cost |
| **Catfish** | 1.5-2.0:1 | 50-60% | Good efficiency |
| **Bass** | 2.0-4.0:1 | 60-70% | Poor efficiency, carnivorous diet |
| **Carp** | 1.5-2.5:1 | 40-50% | Good efficiency, can eat natural food |

**Price Factors:**

| Factor | Impact on Price |
|--------|----------------|
| **Form** | Whole < Gutted < Fillet < Value-added |

| **Size** | Plate-size premium for some species |
| **Certification** | Organic, sustainable = 20-50% premium |
| **Season** | Prices fluctuate with supply/demand |
| **Location** | Local premium vs. commodity price |

#### Startup Costs (Small-Scale Example)

**Tilapia Tank System (500 fish):**
- Tanks/plumbing: $2,000-5,000
- Aeration/pumps: $500-1,500
- Fingerlings: $200-400
- Feed (crop): $500-800
- Utilities: $300-600
- **Total first year:** $3,500-8,300

**Catfish Pond (1 acre):**
- Pond construction: $5,000-15,000
- Aeration: $2,000-5,000
- Fingerlings: $500-1,000
- Feed (crop): $3,000-5,000
- Harvest equipment: $1,000-3,000
- **Total first year:** $11,500-29,000

**Trout Raceway (1,000 fish):**
- Raceway construction: $5,000-15,000
- Water system: $3,000-8,000
- Fingerlings: $500-1,000
- Feed (crop): $2,000-3,000
- Oxygen system: $2,000-5,000
- **Total first year:** $12,500-32,000


#### Climate-Species Match

**Hot Climate (Southern USA, Tropical):**
- **Primary:** Tilapia, catfish, carp
- **Secondary:** Bass (seasonal)
- **Avoid:** Trout (too hot)

**Temperate Climate (Central USA):**
- **Primary:** Catfish, bass, carp
- **Secondary:** Tilapia (heated systems only)
- **Option:** Trout (if cold water available)

**Cold Climate (Northern USA, Canada):**
- **Primary:** Trout
- **Secondary:** Cool-water species (perch, walleye)
- **Avoid:** Tilapia, catfish (too cold)

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)** | Pounds of feed needed to produce one pound of fish |
| **Stocking Density** | Amount of fish per unit area or volume |
| **Grow-out** | Phase from fingerling to market size |
| **Market Size** | Target weight/size for sale or harvest |
| **Fingerling** | Juvenile fish, typically 1-4 inches |
| **Polyculture** | Raising multiple species together |
| **Crop Cycle** | Time from stocking to harvest |
| **Farm-gate Price** | Price received by farmer at farm |
