### 8.1 Integrated Best Management Practices (BMPs)

#### The BMP Framework

```
BEST MANAGEMENT PRACTICES PYRAMID

                    ▲
                   ╱ ╲
                  ╱   ╲
                 ╱  ⚙  ╲
                ╱CONTIN-╲
               ╱  UOUS   ╲
              ╱IMPROVEMENT╲
             ╱─────────────╲
            ╱  MONITORING  ╲
           ╱   & RECORDS    ╲
          ╱─────────────────╲
         ╱ OPERATIONAL MGMT ╲
        ╱  Feed, Health, QC  ╲
       ╱─────────────────────╲
      ╱  INFRASTRUCTURE BASE ╲
     ╱ Water, Systems, Design ╲
    ╱───────────────────────────╲

Foundation: Sound infrastructure
Layer 2: Daily operations excellence
Layer 3: Data-driven decisions
Peak: Always improving
```

#### BMP Categories

**1. Site Selection and Design**
- **Water source** - Adequate quantity and quality
- **Land suitability** - Soil, topography, drainage
- **Access** - Roads, utilities, markets
- **Buffer zones** - Distance from neighbors, waterways
- **Climate** - Match to species requirements
- **Permits** - Zoning, environmental compliance

**2. Water Quality Management**
```
WATER QUALITY BMP HIERARCHY

PREVENTION              MONITORING           RESPONSE
(Best approach)         (Early detection)    (Corrective)
     ↓                       ↓                    ↓
┌──────────────┐       ┌──────────────┐     ┌──────────────┐
│• Proper      │       │• Daily tests │     │• Aeration    │
│  stocking    │  ──►  │• Record      │ ──► │• Water       │
│• Quality feed│       │  keeping     │     │  exchange    │
│• Good design │       │• Trend       │     │• Adjust feed │
└──────────────┘       │  analysis    │     │• Emergency   │
                       └──────────────┘     │  protocols   │
                                            └──────────────┘
```

**3. Feed Management**
- **Quality feeds** - Fresh, properly stored
- **Appropriate rates** - Match to appetite, conditions
- **Feed timing** - Consistent schedule
- **Waste minimization** - Avoid overfeeding
- **FCR tracking** - Monitor efficiency

**4. Health Management**
- **Biosecurity protocols** - Prevent disease introduction
- **Quarantine procedures** - New stock isolation
- **Regular monitoring** - Daily observation
- **Vaccination** - Where available
- **Record keeping** - Track health events

**5. Genetics and Breeding**
- **Quality stock** - Certified disease-free sources
- **Genetic diversity** - Avoid inbreeding
- **Selection** - Breed for desirable traits
- **Traceability** - Know stock origin

#### Triple Bottom Line: People, Planet, Profit

```
SUSTAINABLE AQUACULTURE

        SOCIAL                 ENVIRONMENTAL             ECONOMIC
```

#### Environmental Stewardship

**Resource Efficiency:**

| Resource | Conservation Strategies | Metrics to Track |
|----------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Water** | Recirculation, reuse, minimize discharge | Gallons/lb fish produced |
| **Energy** | Efficient pumps, solar, gravity flow | kWh/lb fish produced |
| **Feed** | Optimize FCR, alternative proteins | FCR, feed waste % |
| **Land** | Intensive systems, vertical integration | lbs/acre or lbs/ft² |
| **Waste** | Nutrient recovery, aquaponics, composting | Waste discharged/lb produced |

**Waste Management:**

```
WASTE HIERARCHY (Best to Worst)

1. PREVENTION
   ↓ Reduce waste generation
   (Optimal feeding, stocking)

2. BENEFICIAL REUSE
   ↓ Convert waste to resource
   (Aquaponics, composting, fertilizer)

3. TREATMENT
   ↓ Process before discharge
   (Settling, biofilters, wetlands)

4. DISPOSAL
   ↓ Last resort
   (Permitted discharge, land application)
```

#### Carbon Footprint Reduction

**Aquaculture Carbon Sources:**
- **Feed production** - 40-70% of total
- **Energy use** - Pumps, aeration, heating
- **Transportation** - Inputs and outputs
- **Infrastructure** - Embodied energy in construction

**Reduction Strategies:**
- Use renewable energy (solar, wind)
- Maximize feed efficiency
- Source local inputs
- Optimize system design (reduce pumping)
- Market locally (reduce food miles)

#### Key Permits and Licenses

**Common Requirements (USA):**

| Permit/License | Issuing Agency | Purpose | Timeline |
|----------------|----------------|---------|----------|
| **Aquaculture License** | State Ag/Fish & Wildlife | Permission to farm fish | 1-3 months |
| **Water Rights** | State Water Board | Water withdrawal/use | 3-12 months |
| **NPDES Permit** | EPA or State | Wastewater discharge | 6-18 months |
| **Building Permit** | County/City | Construction approval | 1-3 months |
| **Business License** | County/City | Business operation | 1-2 weeks |
| **Food Safety** | State Health Dept | Processing/sales | 1-3 months |

**Compliance Best Practices:**
1. **Research requirements early** - Before investing
2. **Maintain good records** - Prove compliance
3. **Build relationships** - With regulators
4. **Plan ahead** - Permit timelines can be long
5. **Stay current** - Regulations change
6. **Join associations** - Collective advocacy

**SOP Template Example:**

```
STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

Title: Daily Water Quality Testing
Department: Production
Effective Date: XX/XX/XXXX
Revision: 1.0

PURPOSE:
Ensure optimal water conditions for fish health
and growth through consistent monitoring.

FREQUENCY:
Daily, 7:00-8:00 AM (before feeding)

EQUIPMENT NEEDED:
• Dissolved oxygen meter
• pH meter
• Thermometer
• Ammonia test kit
• Record sheet

PROCEDURE:
1. Calibrate meters (weekly)
2. Collect water sample from each tank
3. Measure and record:
   - Temperature
   - Dissolved oxygen
   - pH
4. Test ammonia (weekly minimum)
5. Record results immediately
6. Compare to acceptable ranges
7. Alert manager if out of range
8. Clean/store equipment

ACCEPTABLE RANGES:
Temperature: 75-85°F
DO: >5.0 mg/L
pH: 6.5-8.5
Ammonia: <0.5 mg/L

CORRECTIVE ACTIONS:
[Link to water quality response SOP]

RECORDS:
Water Quality Log (Form #WQ-01)
Retention: 3 years
```

### 8.5 Food Safety and Quality

#### HACCP Principles

```
HACCP: Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points

1. CONDUCT HAZARD ANALYSIS
   ↓ Identify potential hazards

2. DETERMINE CRITICAL CONTROL POINTS (CCPs)
   ↓ Where hazards can be prevented/controlled

3. ESTABLISH CRITICAL LIMITS
   ↓ Values that separate safe from unsafe

4. ESTABLISH MONITORING PROCEDURES
   ↓ How to verify CCPs are under control

5. ESTABLISH CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
   ↓ What to do if limits exceeded

6. ESTABLISH VERIFICATION PROCEDURES
   ↓ Confirm HACCP system works

7. ESTABLISH RECORD KEEPING
   ↓ Document the system
```

**Common Hazards in Aquaculture:**

| Hazard Type | Examples | Control Measures |
|-------------|----------|------------------|
| **Biological** | Bacteria, parasites, viruses | Cooking, chilling, hygiene |
| **Chemical** | Drug residues, pesticides | Withdrawal times, approved chemicals |
| **Physical** | Bones, scale fragments | Processing controls, inspection |

**Good Aquaculture Practices (GAqP):**
- Source quality inputs
- Maintain fish health
- Observe withdrawal times
- Proper handling and processing
- Temperature control
- Sanitation protocols
- Traceability

### 8.6 Business Planning and Economics

#### Production Cost Analysis

```
BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS

FIXED COSTS (Annual)           VARIABLE COSTS (Per lb)
• Land/lease: $2,000          • Fingerlings: $0.15
• Equipment depreciation: $3,000  • Feed: $0.75
• Insurance: $1,000           • Labor: $0.30
• Utilities (base): $1,200    • Utilities (var): $0.10
TOTAL FIXED: $7,200          TOTAL VARIABLE: $1.30/lb

Break-even = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price - Variable Cost)

If selling at $2.50/lb:
Break-even = $7,200 ÷ ($2.50 - $1.30)
          = $7,200 ÷ $1.20
          = 6,000 lbs/year

Must produce >6,000 lbs to profit
```

#### Risk Management

**Major Risks and Mitigation:**

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation Strategies |
|------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Disease outbreak** | 50-100% loss | Biosecurity, insurance, diversification |
| **Market price drop** | Reduced revenue | Contracts, value-added, multiple markets |
| **Equipment failure** | Production loss | Backup systems, maintenance, spares |
| **Water shortage** | Cannot operate | Water rights, backup source, storage |
| **Extreme weather** | Infrastructure damage | Insurance, robust design, emergency plan |
| **Regulatory changes** | Compliance costs | Industry involvement, adaptability |

**Risk Management Tools:**
- **Insurance** - Crop/aquatic animal insurance
- **Contracts** - Forward contracts with buyers
- **Diversification** - Multiple species, markets, revenue streams
- **Emergency fund** - 3-6 months operating capital
- **Contingency plans** - Written emergency procedures

#### The PDCA Cycle

```
PLAN-DO-CHECK-ACT (PDCA)

       PLAN
    Identify issue
    Set objectives
    Design solution
          ↓
    ┌────────────┐
    │            ↓
    │          DO
ACT │    Implement
    │     on small
Standardize    scale
or adjust      ↓
    │      CHECK
    │      Measure
    │      results
    │      Analyze
    └────────────┘

Example:
PLAN: Reduce FCR from 1.9 to 1.6
DO: Test new feeding schedule (1 tank)
CHECK: Measure FCR after 60 days
ACT: If improved, apply to all tanks
```
