### 2.2 Fins: Types and Functions

#### Fin Classification

**Paired Fins** (Locomotion & Stability)
- **Pectoral fins** - Steering, braking, hovering
- **Pelvic fins** - Stability, fine movement

**Unpaired Fins** (Propulsion & Balance)
- **Dorsal fin(s)** - Prevent rolling
- **Anal fin** - Stability
- **Caudal fin** - Primary propulsion

```
FIN FUNCTIONS

                         ╔════════╗
                         ║        ║

     ╗                   ╚════════╝
     ║                        │
     ╝                        ●
Stability                     │
                          ANAL FIN
                          Stability

        CAUDAL FIN (TAIL)
             ><
         Propulsion
```

#### Detailed Fin Anatomy

| Fin Type | Position | Primary Function | Secondary Functions |
|----------|----------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Pectoral** | Behind gills | Maneuvering | Lift, braking, backing up |
| **Pelvic** | Ventral, mid-body | Balance | Slow movement, turning |
| **Dorsal** | Back (top) | Anti-roll | Display, defense (spines) |
| **Anal** | Ventral, rear | Stability | Reproduction (modified in males) |
| **Caudal** | Tail end | Thrust | Turning, speed burst |
| **Adipose** | Small, fatty (some species) | Unknown | Possibly sensory |

### 2.3 Body Shapes and Adaptations

#### Common Body Forms

| Body Shape | Description | Habitat/Behavior | Examples |
|------------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| **Fusiform** | Torpedo-shaped | Fast swimmers, open water | Trout, tuna, bass |
| **Compressed** | Flattened side-to-side | Reefs, vegetation | Sunfish, angelfish, tilapia |
| **Depressed** | Flattened top-to-bottom | Bottom dwellers | Catfish, flounder, rays |
| **Elongate** | Long and thin | Crevices, vegetation | Eels, pipefish |
| **Globiform** | Round/spherical | Slow, protected habitats | Pufferfish, lumpfish |

```
FISH BODY SHAPES

FUSIFORM (Streamlined)
    ╔════════════════╗
    ║    →→→→→→→     ║  Fast swimmer
    ╚════════════════╝  (Bass, Salmon)

COMPRESSED (Laterally flattened)
       ╔═══╗
       ║   ║           Maneuvers in
       ║   ║           tight spaces
       ╚═══╝           (Sunfish, Tilapia)

DEPRESSED (Dorsoventrally flattened)
    ╔═════════╗
    ║_________║        Bottom dweller
                       (Catfish, Flounder)

ELONGATE (Eel-like)
    ════════════════→  Hides in crevices
                       (Eel, Pipefish)

GLOBIFORM (Round)
        ╔══╗
       ║    ║          Slow, protected
        ╚══╝           (Pufferfish)
```

#### Form and Function Relationships

**Speed Adaptations:**
- **Fusiform body** - Minimal drag
- **Forked caudal fin** - Maximum thrust
- **Streamlined head** - Reduces turbulence
- **Smooth scales** - Less friction

**Maneuverability Adaptations:**
- **Compressed body** - Quick turns
- **Large pectoral fins** - Fine control
- **Rounded caudal fin** - Acceleration
- **Flexible body** - Tight spaces

**Bottom-Dwelling Adaptations:**
- **Depressed body** - Low profile
- **Ventral mouth** - Feed from bottom
- **Barbels** - Sensory organs for searching
- **Reduced swim bladder** - Stay on bottom

### 2.4 Scales and Skin

#### Scale Types

```
FISH SCALE TYPES

PLACOID (Sharks)         GANOID (Gar, Sturgeon)
   ╱╲╱╲╱╲╱╲                ┌──┬──┬──┬──┐
  ╱  ╱  ╱  ╱              │  │  │  │  │
 ◄   ◄   ◄   ◄             └──┴──┴──┴──┘
Tooth-like               Diamond-shaped
denticles                Hard, bony

CYCLOID (Trout, Salmon)  CTENOID (Bass, Perch)
    ○○○○○○                  ◐◐◐◐◐◐
   ○○○○○○○                 ◐◐◐◐◐◐◐
  ○○○○○○○○                ◐◐◐◐◐◐◐◐
Smooth, round            Rough, comb-like
overlapping              edge
```

| Scale Type | Features | Fish Examples | Functions |
|------------|----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Placoid** | Tooth-like, pointed | Sharks, rays | Reduce drag, protection |
| **Ganoid** | Diamond-shaped, hard | Gar, sturgeon | Armor-like protection |
| **Cycloid** | Round, smooth edges | Salmon, trout, carp | Flexibility, smooth gliding |
| **Ctenoid** | Comb-like rear edge | Bass, perch, sunfish | Grip water, maneuverability |

#### Scale Functions

**Primary Functions:**
1. **Protection** - Physical barrier against injury
2. **Streamlining** - Reduce friction in water
3. **Osmoregulation** - Help regulate water/salt balance
4. **Camouflage** - Reflective properties blend with environment

**Scale Growth:**
- **Growth rings** - Like tree rings, show age and growth rate
- **Circuli** - Concentric rings deposited seasonally
- **Age determination** - Count annuli (yearly marks)

```
SCALE GROWTH RINGS

    ╔═══════════════════╗
    ║   ┌───────────┐   ║
    ║   │ ┌───────┐ │   ║  ← Year 3 (Wide spacing = good growth)
    ║   │ │ ┌───┐ │ │   ║  ← Year 2 (Narrow = slow growth)
    ║   │ │ │ + │ │ │   ║  ← Year 1
    ║   │ │ └───┘ │ │   ║
    ║   │ └───────┘ │   ║
    ║   └───────────┘   ║
    ╚═══════════════════╝

    Center = Nucleus (hatch year)
```

#### Skin Structure

**Layers (Epidermis to Dermis):**
1. **Epidermis** - Outer layer, mucus-producing cells
2. **Mucus layer** - Protective slime coating
3. **Dermis** - Contains scales, pigment cells
4. **Hypodermis** - Fat storage, insulation

**Mucus Functions:**
- **Protection** - Against pathogens, parasites
- **Lubrication** - Reduces friction
- **Osmoregulation** - Barrier to water/salt
- **Chemical communication** - Pheromones
- **Wound healing** - Antimicrobial properties

### 2.5 The Head Region

#### Mouth Types and Feeding

```
MOUTH POSITIONS

TERMINAL (Mid-level feeders)
    ════╗
        ║   →  Mouth at front
    ════╝      (Bass, Trout)

SUPERIOR (Surface feeders)
    ════╗
    ────╝   ↑  Mouth angled up
               (Hatchetfish)

INFERIOR (Bottom feeders)
    ════╗
    ────╝   ↓  Mouth on underside
               (Catfish, Carp)

PROTRUSIBLE (Suction feeders)
    ════╗      Extends forward
    ════╝  →→  (Tilapia, Carp)
```

| Mouth Type | Position | Feeding Strategy | Examples |
|------------|----------|------------------|----------|
| **Terminal** | Front of head | Mid-water, general | Bass, trout |
| **Superior** | Angled upward | Surface insects | Arowana, hatchetfish |
| **Inferior** | Underside | Bottom feeding | Catfish, sturgeon, carp |
| **Subterminal** | Slightly below | Near-bottom | Many cyprinids |

#### Eyes and Vision

**Eye Anatomy:**
```
FISH EYE STRUCTURE

        ┌──────────────┐
        │  ┌────────┐  │  ← Cornea (flat)
        │  │  ┌──┐  │  │  ← Lens (spherical)
        │  │  │●●│  │  │  ← Retina
        │  │  └──┘  │  │
        │  └────────┘  │
        └──────────────┘

No eyelids - Always open!
```

**Vision Characteristics:**
- **Monocular vision** - Each eye sees independently
- **Wide field of view** - Nearly 360° coverage
- **Excellent in water** - Spherical lens focuses clearly
- **Color vision** - Most species see colors
- **Light sensitivity** - Adapted to water conditions

**Eye Position and Behavior:**

| Eye Position | Vision Type | Habitat | Examples |
|--------------|-------------|---------|----------|
| **Lateral** | Wide field | Open water | Most fish |
| **Dorsal** | Upward view | Bottom dwellers | Flounder, rays |
| **Forward** | Binocular | Predators | Pike, barracuda |
| **Small/Reduced** | Limited | Cave/deep sea | Cavefish, deep sea fish |

#### Nostrils (Nares)

**Structure:**
- **Two openings per side** - Incurrent and excurrent
- **No connection to mouth** - Unlike mammals (not for breathing)
- **Olfactory organ** - Inside nasal cavity

**Function:**
```
FISH OLFACTION

    Water Flow Through Nares

    ┌→→→→→●→→→→→┐
    │  Incurrent │
    │   Naris    │
    │           │
    │  ┌─────┐  │
    │  │olfac│  │  ← Olfactory
    │  │tory │  │    organ
    │  │organ│  │
    │  └─────┘  │
    │           │
    │ Excurrent │
    └←←←←←●←←←←←┘
      Naris
```

**Olfactory Capabilities:**
- **Extremely sensitive** - Detect parts per billion
- **Chemical detection** - Food, predators, mates
- **Homing ability** - Salmon return to natal streams
- **Social communication** - Alarm pheromones

#### Operculum (Gill Cover)

**Structure and Function:**
- **Bony plate** - Protects delicate gills
- **Movable flap** - Opens/closes for respiration
- **Pumping action** - Draws water over gills
- **Species identification** - Unique patterns and colors

```
OPERCULUM MOVEMENT

INHALATION               EXHALATION
Mouth OPEN              Mouth CLOSED
Operculum CLOSED        Operculum OPEN

    ╔══╗                   ╔══╗
    ║  ║←──Water in        ║  ║
    ║  ║│                  ║  ║──→Water out
    ╚══╝│                  ╚══╝
```

### 2.6 The Lateral Line System

#### Structure and Function

```
LATERAL LINE SYSTEM

          Lateral Line
        (visible line)
             ↓↓↓↓↓↓↓
    ╔════════●●●●●●●══════════╗
    ║                          ║
    ║  Each dot = Neuromast    ║
    ║  (sensory receptor)      ║
    ║                          ║
    ╚══════════════════════════╝

NEUROMAST DETAIL (Microscopic)

    ┌──────────────┐
    │   Cupula     │  ← Gelatinous cap
    │   ╱│││╲      │
    │  ╱ │││ ╲     │
    │ │  │││  │    │
    │ └──┴┴┴──┘    │  ← Hair cells
    └──────────────┘
         ↕
    Water movement
    bends cupula
```

**Lateral Line Functions:**

| Function | How It Works | Benefit |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Detect water movement** | Senses pressure changes | Avoid obstacles in dark |
| **Locate prey** | Feels vibrations from struggling | Hunt without sight |
| **Avoid predators** | Detects approaching predators | Escape responses |
| **Schooling** | Maintains spacing in group | Coordinated movement |
| **Orientation** | Senses water currents | Navigation |

**Distribution:**
- **Main lateral line** - Along body midline
- **Head canals** - Complex network on head
- **Superficial neuromasts** - Surface of body

### 2.7 Other External Features

#### Barbels

**Structure:**
- **Whisker-like projections** - Near mouth
- **Contain taste buds** - Chemoreceptors
- **Highly sensitive** - Touch and taste combined

```
BARBELS ON CATFISH

        ┌──────┐
    ─── │ Maxillary ─── (2)
        │barbels
    ╔═══╪═══╗
    ║ 👁│👁 ║
    ║   └─  ║
    ║  ╰──╮ ║
    ╚═════╪═╝
    ──────┘
     Mental  (4-6)
     barbels
```

**Species with Barbels:**
- **Catfish** - 2-8 barbels for bottom feeding
- **Carp** - 4 barbels for substrate probing
- **Sturgeon** - 4 barbels ahead of mouth
- **Goatfish** - 2 chin barbels for sensing

#### Caudal Peduncle

**Definition:** Narrowed region where body meets tail fin

**Importance:**
- **Muscle attachment** - Powers tail movement
- **Flexibility** - Allows tail to generate thrust
- **Species identification** - Shape and depth vary

**Shapes:**
- **Deep and narrow** - Fast burst swimmers
- **Elongated and thin** - Sustained swimmers
- **Thick and muscular** - Powerful swimmers

### 2.8 Practical Applications for Aquaculture

#### Health Assessment Checklist

```
EXTERNAL HEALTH INSPECTION

EYES
□ Clear, not cloudy
□ Not bulging (exophthalmia)
□ No hemorrhaging

SKIN/SCALES
□ Intact, no lesions
□ Normal color
□ Adequate mucus
□ No parasites visible

FINS
□ Fully extended
□ No fraying or damage
□ No fungus
□ Proper swimming behavior

GILLS/OPERCULUM
□ Normal breathing rate
□ Operculum closes fully
□ No gasping at surface
□ Red/pink gill color (if visible)

BODY CONDITION
□ Normal body shape
□ Good flesh coverage
□ Straight spine
□ Active swimming
```

#### Proper Fish Handling

**Based on Anatomy:**

| Body Part | Handling Consideration | Why |
|-----------|----------------------|-----|
| **Skin/Mucus** | Use wet hands, minimize contact | Protect mucus layer |
| **Scales** | Handle gently, avoid scraping | Prevent scale loss |
| **Fins** | Support body, don't squeeze | Avoid fin damage |
| **Eyes** | Don't touch, avoid pressure | No eyelids to protect |
| **Gills** | Never touch, support head | Delicate respiratory organ |
| **Lateral line** | Minimize stress | Sensory system sensitivity |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Fusiform** | Torpedo-shaped body form ideal for fast swimming |
