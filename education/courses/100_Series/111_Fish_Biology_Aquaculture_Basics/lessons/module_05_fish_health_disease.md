## Lesson Content

### 5.1 Signs of Fish Health

#### Healthy Fish Indicators

```
HEALTHY FISH CHECKLIST

BEHAVIOR                    PHYSICAL
✓ Active swimming          ✓ Clear eyes
✓ Normal schooling         ✓ Intact fins
✓ Strong feeding response  ✓ Smooth scales
✓ Proper buoyancy          ✓ Good body condition
✓ Alert, responsive        ✓ Normal coloration
✓ No erratic movement      ✓ Uniform size (cohort)

     ╔════════════════╗
     ║  Healthy Fish  ║
     ║      🐟        ║
     ║                ║
     ║  • Streamlined │
     ║  • Active      ║
     ║  • Feeding     ║
     ╚════════════════╝
```

**Visual Health Assessment:**

| Body Part | Healthy Appearance | Problem Signs |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Eyes** | Clear, bright, not bulging | Cloudy, sunken, pop-eye |
| **Gills** | Bright red/pink, regular breathing | Pale, brown, rapid breathing |
| **Fins** | Extended, intact, no damage | Frayed, clamped, lesions |
| **Body** | Smooth, good flesh, straight spine | Ulcers, lumps, curved spine |
| **Scales** | Intact, even pattern | Missing, raised, discolored |
| **Mucus** | Clear, light coating | Excessive, cloudy, patchy |
| **Anus** | Normal, not inflamed | Red, swollen, trailing feces |

#### Stress Indicators

```
STRESS RESPONSE CASCADE

ENVIRONMENTAL          PHYSIOLOGICAL         BEHAVIORAL
   STRESSOR     →     STRESS RESPONSE    →   CHANGES
                             ↓
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  • Poor water quality                        │
    │  • Overcrowding                              │
    │  • Temperature extremes  →  Cortisol ↑      │
    │  • Handling                  Immune ↓       │
    │  • Inadequate feed          Energy ↓        │
    └──────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                       ↓
              DISEASE SUSCEPTIBILITY
```

**Common Stress Signs:**
- **Clamped fins** - Held close to body
- **Gasping at surface** - Low oxygen seeking
- **Rapid gill movement** - Respiratory distress
- **Lethargy** - Reduced activity
- **Loss of appetite** - Not feeding
- **Erratic swimming** - Flashing, jumping
- **Abnormal coloration** - Darkening or fading

### 5.2 The Disease Triangle

#### Understanding Disease Causation

```
DISEASE TRIANGLE

                HOST (Fish)
                    ●
                   ╱ ╲
                  ╱   ╲
           Immune╱     ╲Species
           Status       Susceptibility
                ╱       ╲
               ╱         ╲
              ╱           ╲
             ●─────────────●
         PATHOGEN      ENVIRONMENT
         (Disease       (Water quality,
          Agent)         Stressors)

ALL THREE FACTORS MUST ALIGN FOR DISEASE OUTBREAK
```

**Prevention Strategy:**
- **Strengthen Host** - Good nutrition, genetics, low stress
- **Reduce Pathogens** - Biosecurity, quarantine, sanitation
- **Optimize Environment** - Water quality, temperature, space

### 5.3 Major Disease Categories

#### Bacterial Diseases

**Common Bacterial Infections:**

| Disease | Pathogen | Species Affected | Signs |
|---------|----------|------------------|-------|
| **Columnaris** | *Flavobacterium columnare* | Most freshwater | White/gray patches, fin erosion |
| **Enteric Septicemia (ESC)** | *Edwardsiella ictaluri* | Catfish | Lesions, hemorrhaging, mortality |
| **Furunculosis** | *Aeromonas salmonicida* | Salmonids | Boils, ulcers, internal bleeding |
| **Streptococcosis** | *Streptococcus* spp. | Tilapia, bass | Eye bulging, erratic swimming |
| **Motile Aeromonas** | *Aeromonas* spp. | Many species | Ulcers, fin rot, hemorrhaging |

```
BACTERIAL INFECTION PROGRESSION

Day 1-2            Day 3-5            Day 7+
  Normal      →   Early Signs    →   Advanced
    🐟              🐟❓             🐟❗💀
                   ↓                  ↓
             • Lethargy          • Lesions
             • Reduced feed      • Hemorrhaging
             • Clamped fins      • High mortality

INTERVENTION WINDOW: Days 1-5 most critical
```

**Treatment Options:**
- **Antibiotics** - Medicated feed (requires veterinary prescription)
- **Improved water quality** - Reduce bacterial load
- **Reduce stocking density** - Less stress transmission
- **Salt bath** - Osmoregulatory relief, some antibacterial
- **Probiotics** - Beneficial bacteria competition

#### Viral Diseases

**Major Viral Threats:**

| Disease | Virus | Species | Impact |
|---------|-------|---------|--------|
| **Spring Viremia of Carp (SVC)** | Rhabdovirus | Carp, koi | Hemorrhaging, high mortality |
| **Infectious Hematopoietic Necrosis (IHN)** | Rhabdovirus | Salmon, trout | Internal bleeding, death |
| **Viral Hemorrhagic Septicemia (VHS)** | Novirhabdovirus | Many species | Bleeding, anemia, death |
| **Koi Herpesvirus (KHV)** | Cyprinid herpesvirus 3 | Koi, carp | Gill necrosis, mortality |
| **Tilapia Lake Virus (TiLV)** | Orthomyxovirus | Tilapia | Lethargy, mortality |

**Viral Disease Characteristics:**
- **No effective treatment** - Prevention is critical
- **Highly contagious** - Spreads rapidly
- **Temperature dependent** - Many activate at specific temps
- **Regulatory importance** - Reportable in many jurisdictions
- **Biosecurity essential** - Quarantine, disinfection

```
VIRAL DISEASE MANAGEMENT

        PREVENTION                NO CURE
             ↓                        ↓
    ┌─────────────────┐      ┌──────────────┐
    │ • Biosecurity   │      │ • Cull sick  │
    │ • Quarantine    │      │ • Isolate    │
    │ • Certification │  →→  │ • Disinfect  │
    │ • Vaccination*  │      │ • Report     │
    └─────────────────┘      └──────────────┘
         *Limited availability
```

#### Parasitic Diseases

**External Parasites:**

| Parasite | Type | Attachment | Signs |
|----------|------|------------|-------|
| **Ichthyophthirius (Ich)** | Protozoan | Skin, gills | White spots, flashing |
| **Trichodina** | Ciliate | Skin, gills | Excess mucus, lethargy |
| **Costia** | Flagellate | Skin, gills | Blue-gray film, flashing |
| **Anchor worm** | Crustacean | Embedded | Visible worms, inflammation |
| **Fish lice (Argulus)** | Crustacean | Attached | Round parasites, irritation |
| **Monogenean flukes** | Flatworm | Gills, skin | Respiratory distress |

```
ICH LIFE CYCLE (Temperature Dependent)

    Adult on Fish
         ↓
    (2-3 days feeding)
         ↓
    Falls off fish
         ↓
    Cyst on bottom
         ↓
    (3-7 days division)
         ↓
    Releases 100-1000
    Free-swimming
         ↓
    Must find host
    in 24-48 hours
    or DIE
         ↓
    Infects new fish ←─┐
         │              │
         └──────────────┘
       (Cycle repeats)

TREATMENT TARGETS FREE-SWIMMING STAGE
```

**Internal Parasites:**
- **Tapeworms** - Intestinal, nutrient theft
- **Nematodes** - Various organs
- **Myxozoan parasites** - Whirling disease (trout)

**Treatment Options:**
- **Salt baths** - 1-3% for external parasites
- **Formalin** - Effective against many protozoa
- **Copper sulfate** - Ich treatment (toxic at high doses)
- **Praziquantel** - Flukes, some worms
- **Temperature manipulation** - Speed up/break parasite cycles

#### Fungal Diseases

**Common Fungal Infections:**

| Fungus | Appearance | Cause | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Saprolegnia** | Cotton-like white growth | Secondary infection of wounds | Salt, malachite green |
| **Branchiomyces** | Gill rot | Poor water quality | Improve water, copper sulfate |

```
FUNGAL INFECTION (Saprolegnia)

    NORMAL FISH         INFECTED FISH
       🐟          →        🐟
                          ☁️☁️ ← Cotton-like
                                  growths

Usually secondary to:
• Injury
• Bacterial infection
• Stress
• Poor water quality
```

### 5.4 Environmental Diseases

#### Water Quality-Related Problems

**Ammonia Toxicity:**

| NH₃ Level | Effect | Signs |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **<0.02 mg/L** | Safe | Normal behavior |
| **0.05-0.1 mg/L** | Gill damage | Increased breathing, lethargy |
| **0.2-0.5 mg/L** | Severe stress | Gasping, erratic swimming |
| **>0.5 mg/L** | Lethal | Rapid mortality |

```
AMMONIA TOXICITY SIGNS

    ┌────────────────────────────┐
    │   AFFECTED AREAS           │
    ├────────────────────────────┤
    │                            │
    │  ╔════════╗                │
    │  ║  👁👁  ║ ← Eyes irritated│
    │  ║▓▓▓▓▓▓▓║ ← GILLS DAMAGED │
    │  ║        ║   (primary)     │
    │  ╚════════╝                │
    │      ↑                     │
    │   Gasping at surface       │
    └────────────────────────────┘
```

**Nitrite Toxicity ("Brown Blood Disease"):**
- **Mechanism** - Nitrite converts hemoglobin to methemoglobin
- **Signs** - Brown gills, gasping, lethargy
- **Treatment** - Salt (chloride blocks nitrite uptake)
- **Prevention** - Proper biofilter establishment

**Low Dissolved Oxygen:**

```
OXYGEN STRESS RESPONSE

8+ mg/L ████████████████ Optimal
        │
6 mg/L  ████████████     Adequate
        │
4 mg/L  ████████         Stressed ← Reduced growth
        │                          ← Increased disease
2 mg/L  ████             Critical ← Gasping
        │                          ← Mortality risk
0 mg/L  ░                Lethal   ← Death
```

**Signs:** Surface swimming, gulping air, reduced feeding, slow growth

**Gas Bubble Disease:**
- **Cause** - Supersaturated water (>110% saturation)
- **Signs** - Bubbles in fins, eyes, skin; pop-eye
- **Common in** - Pumped well water, heated systems
- **Prevention** - Aerate/degas incoming water

#### Nutritional Diseases

| Deficiency | Signs | Solution |
|------------|-------|----------|
| **Vitamin C** | Scoliosis, poor growth, hemorrhaging | Stabilized ascorbic acid in feed |
| **Vitamin E** | Muscle degeneration, anemia | Quality feed, proper storage |
| **Essential fatty acids** | Fin erosion, poor growth | Omega-3/6 in feed |
| **Protein (quantity)** | Stunted growth, poor condition | Increase protein % |
| **Protein (quality)** | Poor FCR, slow growth | Better amino acid profile |

### 5.5 Disease Prevention Strategies

#### Biosecurity Protocols

```
BIOSECURITY LAYERS

LEVEL 1: ISOLATION
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Separate water sources        │
│ • Quarantine new stock          │
│ • Visitor restrictions          │
└────────────┬────────────────────┘
             ↓
LEVEL 2: TRAFFIC CONTROL
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Footbaths at entry            │
│ • Dedicated equipment           │
│ • One-way flow (clean→dirty)    │
└────────────┬────────────────────┘
             ↓
LEVEL 3: SANITATION
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Disinfect equipment           │
│ • Remove dead fish daily        │
│ • Clean tanks/ponds regularly   │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Quarantine Procedures:**
1. **Separate system** - Physically isolated from production
2. **Minimum 30 days** - Observe for disease signs
3. **Health screening** - Examine samples, test if needed
4. **Treatment if needed** - Before introducing to main system
5. **Record keeping** - Document source, observations, treatments

#### Stocking Density Management

```
STOCKING DENSITY vs HEALTH

LOW DENSITY           OPTIMAL            OVERCROWDED
┌─────────┐          ┌─────────┐         ┌─────────┐
│ 🐟      │          │ 🐟 🐟   │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟│
│    🐟   │          │  🐟  🐟 │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟│
│      🐟 │          │ 🐟   🐟 │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟│
└─────────┘          └─────────┘         └─────────┘
   ✓ Healthy        ✓ Good growth       ✗ Stress
   ✗ Underutilized  ✓ Manageable        ✗ Disease
                    ✓ Profitable        ✗ Poor water
```

**Density Guidelines (Species-Specific):**
- **Follow recommendations** for your species
- **Adjust for system** - RAS can support higher density
- **Monitor indicators** - Growth, behavior, water quality
- **Start conservative** - Can increase with experience

#### Water Quality Monitoring

**Daily Monitoring:**
- **Temperature** - Morning and afternoon
- **Dissolved oxygen** - Early morning (lowest point)
- **Visual inspection** - Water clarity, foam, odor

**Weekly Monitoring:**
- **pH** - Affects ammonia toxicity, fish health
- **Ammonia** - Total and un-ionized
- **Nitrite** - Especially in new systems

**Monthly Monitoring:**
- **Nitrate** - Accumulation indicator
- **Alkalinity** - pH buffering capacity
- **Hardness** - Calcium, magnesium

```
WATER QUALITY RECORD KEEPING

DATE    TEMP  DO   pH   NH₃   NO₂   NOTES
────────────────────────────────────────────
1/15    76°F  6.2  7.1  0.25  0.1   Reduced feed
1/16    75°F  6.5  7.0  0.15  0.1   Normal
1/17    75°F  6.8  7.2  0.05  0.0   Good
1/18    77°F  5.8  7.1  0.10  0.0   Added aeration

TREND ANALYSIS REVEALS PROBLEMS EARLY
```

#### Nutrition and Feeding

**Feed Management for Health:**
- **Quality feed** - Fresh, properly stored, species-appropriate
- **Appropriate amount** - Avoid overfeeding (water quality issues)
- **Consistent schedule** - Reduces stress
- **Particle size** - Match to fish size
- **Immune support** - Vitamins, probiotics when available

### 5.6 Disease Diagnosis and Treatment

#### Diagnostic Approach

```
DISEASE INVESTIGATION STEPS

1. OBSERVE BEHAVIOR
   ↓
   What are fish doing?

2. WATER QUALITY TEST
   ↓
   Rule out environmental causes

3. EXTERNAL EXAM
   ↓
   Visual signs on body

4. GILL EXAMINATION
   ↓
   Color, parasites, damage

5. INTERNAL EXAM (if dead fish)
   ↓
   Organs, lesions, parasites

6. LABORATORY TESTING (if needed)
   ↓
   Bacterial culture, PCR, histology
```

**When to Call a Professional:**
- **Unexplained mortality** - >5% daily or 10% cumulative
- **Suspected viral disease** - No response to treatment
- **Regulatory concerns** - Reportable diseases
- **Valuable stock** - High economic impact
- **Uncertain diagnosis** - Multiple potential causes

#### Treatment Decision Tree

```
TREATMENT APPROACH

        Is it ENVIRONMENTAL?
              ↙       ↘
           YES        NO
            ↓          ↓
        Fix water   Is it PARASITIC?
        quality        ↙       ↘
                    YES        NO
                     ↓          ↓
                 External   Is it BACTERIAL?
                 treatment     ↙       ↘
                            YES        NO
                             ↓          ↓
                         Antibiotic  VIRAL/UNKNOWN
                         (if legal)      ↓
                                     Supportive
                                     care only
```

**Treatment Considerations:**
- **Legal requirements** - VFD for antibiotics (USA)
- **Withdrawal times** - Before harvest/sale
- **Cost-benefit** - Treatment cost vs. fish value
- **Success likelihood** - Some diseases untreatable
- **Record keeping** - Document all treatments

### 5.7 Emergency Response Plan

#### Disease Outbreak Protocol

```
OUTBREAK RESPONSE CHECKLIST

IMMEDIATE (Day 1)
□ Isolate affected population
□ Stop feeding (temporarily)
□ Increase aeration/water exchange
□ Test water quality
□ Count and remove dead fish
□ Document observations (photos)

SHORT-TERM (Days 2-7)
□ Identify disease (if possible)
□ Consult with aquaculture extension/vet
□ Begin appropriate treatment
□ Increase monitoring frequency
□ Restrict farm access (biosecurity)
□ Notify partners/regulators (if required)

LONG-TERM (Post-outbreak)
□ Review what went wrong
□ Update biosecurity protocols
□ Modify stocking/management
□ Disinfect equipment/facilities
□ Plan recovery strategy
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Biosecurity** | Practices to prevent disease introduction and spread |
| **Quarantine** | Isolation of new or sick fish to prevent disease transmission |
| **Pathogen** | Disease-causing organism (bacteria, virus, parasite, fungus) |
| **Zoonotic** | Disease that can transmit from animals to humans |
| **Ich** | Common parasitic disease (Ichthyophthirius multifiliis) |
| **VFD** | Veterinary Feed Directive - required for antibiotic use (USA) |
| **Withdrawal Time** | Period before harvest when medicated fish cannot be sold |
| **Pop-eye** | Exophthalmia - bulging eyes from gas, infection, or injury |
