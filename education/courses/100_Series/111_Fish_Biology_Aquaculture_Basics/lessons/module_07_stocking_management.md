### 7.1 Stocking Density Fundamentals

#### Understanding Stocking Density

```
STOCKING DENSITY UNITS

PONDS                    TANKS/RACEWAYS
fish/acre               fish/gallon
lbs/acre                lbs/cubic foot
kg/hectare              kg/cubic meter

┌─────────────┐         ┌─────────────┐
│   🐟 🐟     │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟 │
│  🐟   🐟    │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟 │
│    🐟   🐟  │         │🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟🐟 │
└─────────────┘         └─────────────┘
Low density             High density
(extensive)             (intensive)
```

**Density Categories:**

| System Type | Density Range | Management Level | Examples |
|-------------|---------------|------------------|----------|
| **Extensive** | <500 lbs/acre | Minimal | Fertilized ponds |
| **Semi-Intensive** | 500-5,000 lbs/acre | Moderate | Fed ponds |
| **Intensive** | 5,000-50,000 lbs/acre | High | Tanks, raceways |
| **Super-Intensive** | >50,000 lbs/acre | Very high | RAS systems |

#### Factors Affecting Stocking Density

```
DENSITY LIMITING FACTORS

    WATER QUALITY
         ↓
    Oxygen supply ────┐
    Waste removal     │
         ↓            │
    SYSTEM DESIGN     ├──→ MAXIMUM
         ↓            │    DENSITY
    Flow rate         │
    Filtration        │
    Aeration      ────┘
         ↓
    SPECIES TOLERANCE
         ↓
    Behavior
    Stress response
```

**Key Considerations:**

| Factor | Low Density Impact | High Density Impact |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | Sufficient | May be limiting |
| **Waste accumulation** | Minimal | Requires management |
| **Disease transmission** | Lower risk | Higher risk |
| **Growth uniformity** | More uniform | Variable (dominance) |
| **Feed competition** | Less competition | More aggression |
| **Profit per unit** | Lower | Higher (if managed) |

### 7.3 Stocking Calculations

#### Basic Stocking Formula

```
NUMBER TO STOCK = (Desired Harvest lbs)
                  ───────────────────────
                  (Harvest Weight) × (Survival %)

EXAMPLE: Tilapia in 1-acre pond
• Goal: 10,000 lbs at harvest
• Harvest size: 1.5 lbs each
• Expected survival: 75%

Stock = 10,000 ÷ (1.5 × 0.75)
      = 10,000 ÷ 1.125
      = 8,889 fingerlings
```

#### Density by Volume Calculation

```
TANK STOCKING EXAMPLE

Tank: 10 ft diameter × 4 ft deep

Step 1: Calculate volume
Volume = π × r² × h
       = 3.14 × 5² × 4
       = 314 cubic feet
       = 2,349 gallons

Step 2: Determine target density
Tilapia: 0.75 lbs/gallon at harvest

Step 3: Calculate capacity
Capacity = 2,349 gal × 0.75 lbs/gal
         = 1,762 lbs

Step 4: Calculate initial stocking
If growing from 0.1 lb to 1.5 lb:
Fish needed = 1,762 ÷ 1.5 = 1,175
Initial weight = 1,175 × 0.1 = 117.5 lbs
Initial density = 117.5 ÷ 2,349 = 0.05 lbs/gal ✓
```

#### Grading and Partial Harvest

```
GRADING STRATEGY

INITIAL STOCK          FIRST GRADE        SECOND GRADE
  (1,000 fish)         (Day 60)            (Day 120)
      │                    │                    │
      ↓                    ↓                    ↓
┌──────────┐          ┌─────────┐          ┌─────────┐
│ Size mix │   Sort   │ Large   │   Sort   │ Market  │
│ 🐟🐟🐟🐟  │   ───►   │ Medium  │   ───►   │ size    │
│ 🐟🐟🐟🐟  │          │ Small   │          │ uniform │
└──────────┘          └─────────┘          └─────────┘
                           │                    │
                      Separate by           Harvest
                      size groups            largest
```

**Benefits of Grading:**
- **Reduce cannibalism** - Size uniformity
- **Optimize feeding** - Size-appropriate pellets
- **Improve growth** - Less competition
- **Selective harvest** - Market when ready
- **Better FCR** - Even feeding access

### 7.4 Stocking Schedules and Crop Rotation

#### Continuous vs. Batch Production

```
BATCH PRODUCTION (All-in, All-out)

Month: 1    2    3    4    5    6    7    8    9
Pond A: [STOCK──────GROW──────HARVEST]
Pond B:              [STOCK──────GROW──────HARVEST]
Pond C:                          [STOCK──────GROW──

Advantages:
✓ Uniform size
✓ Complete cleaning between crops
✓ Easier inventory management

CONTINUOUS PRODUCTION (Multiple cohorts)

Month: 1    2    3    4    5    6    7    8    9
Pond:  [────────Mixed Ages────────────────────►
       Add fingerlings monthly
       Harvest market-size weekly

Advantages:
✓ Steady cash flow
✓ Continuous production
✓ Efficient space use
```

**Production System Comparison:**

| Factor | Batch Production | Continuous Production |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Management** | Simpler | Complex |
| **Biosecurity** | Better (complete cleaning) | Moderate (mixed ages) |
| **Cash flow** | Periodic (harvest events) | Steady (ongoing) |
| **Labor** | Peaks at stocking/harvest | Steady throughout |
| **Growth uniformity** | Excellent | Variable |
| **Disease risk** | Lower (single age) | Higher (age mixing) |

### 7.5 Grading Techniques

#### Size Sorting Methods

**Grading Bar System:**

```
GRADING BARS (Top view)

Large fish cannot pass
     ↓
┌────╫╫╫╫╫────┐
│ 🐟 ╫╫╫╫╫    │
│    ╫╫╫╫╫ 🐟 │  Bars spaced for
│ 🐟 ╫╫╫╫╫    │  target size
│    ╫╫╫╫╫ 🐟 │
└────╫╫╫╫╫────┘
     ↓
Small fish pass through
```

**Grading Table:**

| Fingerling Age | Screen/Bar Spacing | Separates Size |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **1-2 weeks** | 1/4 inch | <30mm from >30mm |
| **4-6 weeks** | 1/2 inch | <50mm from >50mm |
| **8-10 weeks** | 3/4 inch | <75mm from >75mm |
| **3-4 months** | 1 inch | <100mm from >100mm |

#### Grading Frequency

**Recommended Grading Schedule:**

| System | Frequency | Purpose |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Hatchery** | Weekly | Size uniformity, reduce cannibalism |
| **Fingerling production** | Bi-weekly to monthly | Optimize growth, separate sizes |
| **Grow-out tanks** | Monthly | Prepare for harvest, manage density |
| **Ponds** | Mid-season + pre-harvest | Partial harvest, market sorting |

### 7.6 Harvesting Techniques

#### Harvest Methods by System

**Pond Harvesting:**

```
SEINE NET HARVEST

    Shore                            Shore
     │                                │
     ↓                                ↓
    ═══════════════════════════════════
         ╱                       ╲
        ╱   Net dragged through   ╲
       ╱    pond, concentrates     ╲
      ╱          fish               ╲
     ╱                               ╲
    ╱─────────────────────────────────╲
              Harvest point

Steps:
1. Stop feeding 24-48 hours prior
2. Deploy net at one end
3. Slowly pull across pond
4. Concentrate fish in corner
5. Dip net or pump to harvest tank
```

**Tank Harvesting:**

| Method | Description | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Drain down** | Lower water, net fish | Small tanks |
| **Crowding** | Use panel to concentrate | Medium tanks |
| **Lift net** | Net installed at stocking | RAS systems |
| **Pumping** | Fish pump to harvest tank | Large volume |

**Raceway Harvesting:**

```
RACEWAY HARVEST

Flow →→→→ ╔═══════════╗ →→→→ Out
          ║ Fish      ║
          ║  🐟🐟🐟   ║
          ║  🐟🐟🐟   ║
          ╚═══╬═══════╝
              ↓
          [Harvest Net]
              ↓
         Concentrate &
         Remove fish
```

#### Pre-Harvest Preparation

**Checklist:**

```
48-24 HOURS BEFORE:
□ Stop feeding
□ Check water quality
□ Prepare harvest equipment
□ Clean harvest bins/tanks
□ Schedule labor/buyers
□ Ice/refrigeration ready

DAY OF HARVEST:
□ Optimal water temperature (cool morning)
□ Oxygen source available
□ Minimize stress
□ Process quickly
□ Maintain cold chain
```

**Purging (if needed):**
- **Purpose** - Remove off-flavors, empty digestive tract
- **Duration** - 3-7 days in clean water
- **Water** - High flow, no feed
- **Temperature** - Cool (slows metabolism)

### 7.7 Fish Handling Best Practices

#### Minimizing Stress and Injury

```
STRESS CASCADE DURING HANDLING

Physical           Physiological        Consequences
Disturbance        Response
    ↓                  ↓                     ↓
Capture/          Cortisol ↑           Immune ↓
Crowding              ↓                     ↓
Netting           Glucose ↑            Disease ↑
Transport         Heart rate ↑             ↓
    ↓                  ↓                 Mortality ↑
MINIMIZE ←─────── Recovery ←───────── or Recovery
STRESS            (if handled             time
                   properly)
```

**Handling Guidelines:**

| Practice | Why | How |
|----------|-----|-----|
| **Use wet hands** | Protect mucus layer | Dip hands before touching |
| **Minimize air exposure** | Prevent gill collapse | Keep in water or move quickly |
| **Support body** | Prevent internal injury | Support horizontally |
| **Avoid scale loss** | Protect from disease | Gentle handling, soft nets |
| **Cool water** | Reduce metabolism/stress | Harvest in morning, ice slurry |
| **Quick process** | Minimize total stress time | Efficient workflow |

#### Anesthesia Use

**When to Use Anesthesia:**
- Extended handling (>2-3 minutes)
- Grading operations
- Tagging or measurements
- Transport preparation
- Veterinary procedures

**Common Anesthetics:**

| Product | Concentration | Induction Time | Recovery Time |
|---------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **MS-222 (Tricaine)** | 50-100 ppm | 3-5 minutes | 5-10 minutes |
| **Clove oil** | 40-100 ppm | 5-10 minutes | 10-20 minutes |
| **CO₂** | Saturation | 1-3 minutes | 2-5 minutes |

**Safety Notes:**
- Follow withdrawal times before sale
- Maintain oxygen during anesthesia
- Have recovery tank ready
- Monitor fish closely

### 7.8 Population Management

#### Monitoring Growth Performance

```
GROWTH TRACKING

Sample monthly (minimum 30 fish/population)

DATE    AVG WT  SAMPLE  TOTAL   BIOMASS  FEED/DAY
        (lbs)   SIZE    EST#    (lbs)    (lbs)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
Jan 1   0.10    30      1,000   100      1.5
Feb 1   0.25    30      980     245      4.9
Mar 1   0.50    30      960     480      9.6
Apr 1   0.80    30      950     760      15.2
May 1   1.20    30      920     1,104    22.1
Jun 1   1.50    30      900     1,350    27.0
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Survival: 90%
Average daily gain: 0.008 lbs/day
Days to market: 180
```

**Key Performance Indicators:**

| Metric | Formula | Target Range |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| **Survival %** | (Final count ÷ Initial stock) × 100 | 75-95% |
| **FCR** | Feed given ÷ Weight gain | 1.1-2.0 (species dependent) |
| **Daily gain** | Weight gain ÷ Days | Species/temp dependent |
| **Condition factor** | (Weight × 100) ÷ Length³ | 1.0-2.0 |
| **Uniformity** | CV% of weights | <20% is good |

#### Inventory Management

```
INVENTORY TRACKING SYSTEM

PHYSICAL COUNT (Monthly or at grading):
• Count sample
• Weigh sample
• Calculate average weight
• Estimate total population
• Calculate biomass

MATHEMATICAL ESTIMATE (Weekly):
• Previous count
• - Mortalities (counted)
• - Harvests (counted)
• + New stockings
• = Current estimate

RECONCILE REGULARLY
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Stocking Density** | Number or weight of fish per unit area or volume |
| **Biomass** | Total weight of all fish in a system |
| **Grading** | Sorting fish by size into uniform groups |
| **Cohort** | Group of fish stocked at the same time |
| **Purging** | Holding fish in clean water before harvest to improve quality |
| **Seine Net** | Large net dragged through water to catch fish |
| **Condition Factor** | Measure of fish health based on weight-to-length ratio |
| **Batch Production** | All-in, all-out stocking and harvesting |
