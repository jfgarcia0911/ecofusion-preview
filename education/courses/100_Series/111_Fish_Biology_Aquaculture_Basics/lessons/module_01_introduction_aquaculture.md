### 1.1 What is Aquaculture?

#### Definition

**Aquaculture** (also called aquafarming) is the farming of aquatic organisms including fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and aquatic plants. It involves cultivating freshwater and saltwater populations under controlled conditions.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Controlled environment** - Managed water quality, feeding, and health
- **Intentional stocking** - Deliberate introduction of organisms
- **Ownership** - Individual or corporate property
- **Intervention** - Active management to enhance production
- **Harvest management** - Controlled timing and methods

#### Aquaculture vs. Fishing

| Aspect | Wild Fisheries | Aquaculture |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| **Source** | Ocean, lakes, rivers | Farms, tanks, ponds |
| **Control** | Limited (catch quotas) | High (all stages) |
| **Predictability** | Variable catches | Consistent production |
| **Sustainability** | Declining stocks | Growing production |
| **Genetics** | Wild populations | Selective breeding |
| **Disease** | Natural occurrence | Managed prevention |
| **Feed** | Natural food web | Formulated feeds |
| **Location** | Open waters | Designated sites |

### 1.3 Types of Aquaculture Systems

#### By Environment

**Freshwater Aquaculture**
- **Species:** Catfish, tilapia, trout, carp, bass
- **Systems:** Ponds, raceways, tanks, cages
- **Advantages:** Lower salinity stress, easier water management
- **Challenges:** Limited species diversity, temperature control

**Marine Aquaculture (Mariculture)**
- **Species:** Salmon, sea bass, shrimp, oysters, seaweed
- **Systems:** Net pens, longlines, bottom culture
- **Advantages:** Natural seawater, large-scale potential
- **Challenges:** Environmental permits, disease spread, storms

**Brackish Water Aquaculture**
- **Species:** Shrimp, mullet, milkfish
- **Systems:** Coastal ponds, enclosed bays
- **Advantages:** Utilizes marginal land, natural productivity
- **Challenges:** Tidal management, salinity fluctuations

### 1.5 Benefits of Aquaculture

#### Food Security

**Nutritional Benefits:**
- **High-quality protein** - Essential amino acids
- **Omega-3 fatty acids** - Heart and brain health
- **Vitamins & minerals** - Vitamin D, B12, selenium, iodine
- **Low saturated fat** - Healthier than many meats

**Production Efficiency:**

| Protein Source | Feed Conversion Ratio | Protein Yield (kg/ha/year) |
|----------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Fish (intensive) | 1.1-1.5 | 5,000-50,000 |
| Chicken | 1.7-2.0 | 2,000-8,000 |
| Pork | 2.7-3.5 | 1,000-4,000 |
| Beef | 6.0-10.0 | 500-2,000 |

#### Economic Benefits

**Employment:**
- **Direct jobs** - 20+ million globally
- **Indirect jobs** - 60+ million in supply chain
- **Rural development** - Income in coastal/rural areas
- **Export revenue** - Major income for developing nations

**Economic Impact (2020):**
- **Global value** - $281 billion
- **Trade value** - $164 billion in exports
- **Growth rate** - 5.3% annually

#### Environmental Benefits (When Done Right)

**Resource Efficiency:**
1. **Water use** - Less than terrestrial livestock
2. **Land use** - More productive per hectare
3. **Carbon footprint** - Lower than beef, pork
4. **Feed efficiency** - Better than land animals

**Ecosystem Services:**
- **Oyster/mussel farming** - Filters water, improves quality
- **Seaweed culture** - Absorbs CO2, reduces acidification
- **Integrated systems** - Recycles nutrients
- **Restocking programs** - Supports wild populations

### 1.6 Challenges in Aquaculture

#### Environmental Concerns

| Challenge | Impact | Mitigation Strategies |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| **Water pollution** | Nutrient discharge, eutrophication | Better feeds, waste treatment, siting |
| **Disease** | Spreads to wild fish | Biosecurity, vaccines, low density |
| **Escapes** | Genetic pollution | Secure nets, sterile fish, inland systems |
| **Habitat loss** | Mangrove destruction, wetland conversion | Sustainable siting, regulation |
| **Feed resources** | Overfishing for fishmeal | Alternative feeds, plant proteins |
| **Chemical use** | Antibiotics, pesticides | IPM, probiotics, better management |

#### Technical Challenges

**Production Issues:**
- **Disease outbreaks** - Can devastate operations
- **Water quality** - Requires constant monitoring
- **Seed supply** - Fingerling/larvae availability
- **Feed costs** - 50-70% of operating expenses
- **Technology** - High capital requirements

#### Social & Regulatory

**Concerns:**
- **Food safety** - Antibiotic residues, contaminants
- **Access rights** - Water use conflicts
- **Labor conditions** - Worker welfare
- **Community impacts** - Visual, noise, traffic
- **Regulatory complexity** - Permits, compliance costs

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Aquaculture** | Farming of aquatic organisms including fish, shellfish, and plants |
| **Mariculture** | Aquaculture conducted in marine (saltwater) environments |
| **Fingerling** | Juvenile fish, typically 1-4 inches long |
| **Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)** | Amount of feed needed to produce one unit of fish growth |
| **Stocking Density** | Number or weight of fish per unit volume/area |
| **Extensive Culture** | Low-density production relying on natural food |
| **Intensive Culture** | High-density production with complete artificial feeding |
| **RAS** | Recirculating Aquaculture System - closed-loop water reuse |
