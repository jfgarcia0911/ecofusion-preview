### Question 46 (2.5 points)
**Explain the three pillars of sustainability and give one example of how each applies to food production.**

**Model Answer:**
The three pillars of sustainability are:

1. **Environmental** - Protecting natural resources and ecosystems
   - Example: Using cover crops to prevent soil erosion and maintain soil health

2. **Economic** - Ensuring financial viability and fair distribution of benefits
   - Example: Fair trade practices that ensure farmers receive living wages

3. **Social** - Promoting equity, community health, and cultural values
   - Example: Creating food access programs in underserved communities

### Question 47 (2.5 points)
**Describe three environmental challenges of conventional agriculture and explain why they are concerns.**

**Model Answer:**
Three environmental challenges include:

1. **Soil Degradation** - 33% of global soil is degraded through erosion, compaction, and nutrient depletion. This is concerning because topsoil takes 500-1,000 years to form and is essential for food production.

2. **Water Pollution** - Agricultural runoff causes eutrophication and dead zones in waterways. Over 140 dead zones exist globally, destroying marine ecosystems and fisheries.

3. **Biodiversity Loss** - 75% of crop diversity has been lost, and pollinator populations are declining. This reduces ecosystem resilience and threatens future food security.

*Grading: 0.5 points for naming each challenge, ~0.33 points for explaining why each is concerning*

### Question 48 (2.5 points)
**Compare and contrast hydroponics and aquaponics. Include at least two similarities and two differences.**

**Model Answer:**

**Similarities:**
1. Both are soilless growing methods
2. Both can be used in controlled environments
3. Both allow for year-round production
4. Both use significantly less water than traditional agriculture

**Differences:**
1. Nutrient source: Hydroponics uses synthetic nutrient solutions, while aquaponics uses fish waste as the nutrient source
2. Products: Hydroponics produces only plants, while aquaponics produces both plants and fish
3. Complexity: Aquaponics is more complex, requiring management of fish, bacteria, and plants
4. Sustainability: Aquaponics is generally considered more sustainable due to its closed-loop system

*Grading: 0.5 points for each similarity (max 1.0), 0.5 points for each difference (max 1.5)*

### Question 49 (2.5 points)
**What is the nitrogen cycle in aquaponics? Describe the role of bacteria in this process.**

**Model Answer:**
The nitrogen cycle in aquaponics is the biological process that converts toxic fish waste into plant nutrients:

1. **Fish produce ammonia (NH3)** through their waste and gill excretions
2. **Nitrosomonas bacteria** colonize the biofilter and convert ammonia to nitrite (NO2)
3. **Nitrobacter bacteria** then convert nitrite to nitrate (NO3)
4. **Plants absorb the nitrate** as their primary nitrogen source
5. **Clean water returns to the fish tank**, completing the cycle

The bacteria are essential because both ammonia and nitrite are toxic to fish at low concentrations, while nitrate is relatively harmless and is the form of nitrogen plants can readily use. Without the bacteria, the system cannot function.

*Grading: 0.5 points for each step correctly described, 0.5 points for explaining bacterial importance*

### Question 50 (2.5 points)
**Describe three benefits of urban agriculture for food security and community development.**

**Model Answer:**
Three benefits of urban agriculture include:

1. **Improved Food Access** - Urban farms can provide fresh produce in food deserts where grocery stores are scarce, reducing the distance between production and consumption and ensuring communities have access to nutritious food.

2. **Economic Development** - Urban agriculture creates local jobs, keeps food dollars in the community, supports small businesses, and can revitalize vacant or underutilized urban spaces.

3. **Community Building** - Community gardens and urban farms bring people together, provide educational opportunities, preserve cultural food traditions, and create green spaces that improve mental health and neighborhood cohesion.

*Grading: ~0.83 points for each well-explained benefit*
