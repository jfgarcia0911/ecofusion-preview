## Lesson Content

### 1.1 What is a Food System?

#### Definition

A **food system** encompasses all the processes, infrastructure, and people involved in feeding a population. It includes:

- **Production** - Growing crops and raising animals
- **Processing** - Transforming raw materials into food products
- **Distribution** - Moving food from producers to consumers
- **Consumption** - How food is purchased, prepared, and eaten
- **Waste Management** - Handling food waste and byproducts

#### Key Statistics

| Fact | Number | Significance |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Global food production | ~10 billion tons/year | Enough to feed everyone |

### 1.2 Components of the Food System

#### A. Production

**Where does our food come from?**

| Production Type | Description | Examples |
|-----------------|-------------|----------|
| **Crop Agriculture** | Growing plants for food | Grains, vegetables, fruits |
| **Animal Agriculture** | Raising animals for food | Cattle, poultry, pigs |
| **Aquaculture** | Farming aquatic organisms | Fish, shrimp, shellfish |
| **Wild Harvest** | Gathering from nature | Fishing, foraging |

**Global Production Distribution:**

| Crop Category | Annual Production | Top Producers |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Cereals | 2.8 billion tons | China, USA, India |
| Vegetables | 1.1 billion tons | China, India, USA |
| Fruits | 880 million tons | China, India, Brazil |
| Meat | 340 million tons | China, USA, Brazil |
| Fish/Seafood | 180 million tons | China, Indonesia, India |

#### B. Processing

**What happens between farm and table?**

| Processing Level | Description | Examples |
|------------------|-------------|----------|
| **Minimal** | Cleaning, sorting, packaging | Bagged salads, fresh fruit |
| **Moderate** | Cutting, freezing, canning | Frozen vegetables, canned beans |
| **Extensive** | Chemical/physical transformation | Bread, cheese, cooking oils |
| **Ultra-processed** | Industrial formulations | Chips, sodas, ready meals |

### 1.3 The Journey of Food: From Farm to Fork

#### Food Miles

**Definition:** The distance food travels from where it is grown to where it is consumed.

**Average Food Miles by Product:**

| Product | Average Distance | Transportation |
|---------|------------------|----------------|
| Fresh produce (conventional) | 1,500 miles | Truck, rail |
| Imported produce | 5,000+ miles | Ship, air |
| Processed foods | 1,300 miles | Truck |
| Local produce | <100 miles | Truck |
| Home garden | 0 miles | Walking! |

#### The Typical Supermarket Tomato Journey

```
JOURNEY OF A TOMATO

1. SEED PRODUCTION (Netherlands)
   │ Seeds shipped to farm
   ▼
2. GROWING (Mexico)
   │ 6-8 weeks growing
   ▼
3. HARVEST (Mexico)
   │ Picked green for shipping
   ▼
4. PACKING HOUSE (Mexico)
   │ Sorted, boxed, cooled
   ▼
5. BORDER CROSSING (Texas)
   │ Inspection, documentation
   ▼
6. DISTRIBUTION CENTER (Regional)
   │ Stored, ripened with ethylene
   ▼
7. GROCERY STORE (Local)
   │ Displayed for sale
   ▼
8. YOUR KITCHEN
   │ Consumed or wasted
   ▼
9. LANDFILL (if wasted)

Total Distance: ~1,500 miles
Total Time: 2-3 weeks
Carbon Footprint: ~0.5 kg CO2 per tomato
```

#### Carbon Footprint of Food

**Definition:** The total greenhouse gas emissions caused by producing, processing, transporting, and disposing of food.

**Carbon Footprint by Food Type:**

| Food | kg CO2 per kg of food | Comparison |
|------|----------------------|------------|
| Beef | 27.0 | Highest impact |
| Cheese | 13.5 | High impact |
| Pork | 12.1 | Moderate-high |
| Chicken | 6.9 | Moderate |
| Eggs | 4.8 | Moderate |
| Rice | 2.7 | Lower |
| Vegetables | 2.0 | Lower |
| Fruits | 1.1 | Lowest |

### 1.4 Global vs. Local Food Systems

#### Comparison

| Factor | Global Food System | Local Food System |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Distance** | 1,000+ miles | <100 miles |
| **Freshness** | Days to weeks old | Hours to days old |
| **Variety** | Year-round availability | Seasonal |
| **Price** | Often lower (scale) | Variable |
| **Traceability** | Complex | Direct |
| **Economic Impact** | Global corporations | Local businesses |
| **Environmental** | Higher transport emissions | Lower transport, variable production |
| **Relationship** | Anonymous | Personal |

#### The Local Food Movement

**Key Components:**
- Farmers markets
- Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)
- Farm-to-table restaurants
- School gardens
- Urban agriculture
- Food hubs and cooperatives

**Benefits of Local Food:**
1. Fresher, more nutritious
2. Supports local economy
3. Builds community connections
4. Reduces transportation emissions
5. Preserves farmland
6. Increases food security
7. Promotes biodiversity

**Challenges of Local Food:**
1. Seasonal limitations
2. Higher prices (sometimes)
3. Limited variety
4. Requires behavior change
5. Infrastructure gaps
6. Scale limitations

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Food System** | All processes and infrastructure involved in feeding a population |
| **Food Miles** | Distance food travels from production to consumption |
| **Carbon Footprint** | Total greenhouse gas emissions from producing and transporting food |
| **Supply Chain** | Network of organizations involved in producing and delivering products |
| **Food Security** | Reliable access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food |
| **Food Sovereignty** | Right of peoples to define their own food systems |
| **Traceability** | Ability to track food through the supply chain |
| **CSA** | Community Supported Agriculture - direct farm-to-consumer model |
