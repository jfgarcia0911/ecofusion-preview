### 5.1 Defining Urban Agriculture

#### What is Urban Agriculture?

**Urban Agriculture (UA):** The practice of cultivating, processing, and distributing food in or around urban areas.

```
URBAN AGRICULTURE SPECTRUM

MICRO                                                    MACRO
  │                                                        │
  ▼                                                        ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                          │
│ Window    Container   Community   Rooftop    Vertical   │
│  Boxes  →  Gardens  →  Gardens  →  Farms  →   Farms    │
│                                                          │
│ Balcony   Backyard    School      Warehouse   Commercial│
│ Herbs  →   Veggie  →  Gardens →   Grows   →   Indoor    │
│                                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     Individual              Community            Commercial
```

#### Types of Urban Agriculture

**1. Community Gardens**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Size | Varies, typically 0.25-5 acres |
| Ownership | Shared plots, communal areas |
| Purpose | Food production, education, community building |
| Users | Neighborhood residents |
| Examples | P-Patches (Seattle), GreenThumb (NYC) |

**2. Rooftop Farms**

```
ROOFTOP FARM CROSS-SECTION

     [Plants growing in beds/containers]

    │    Insulation layer              │
    ├──────────────────────────────────┤

    └──────────────────────────────────┘

Benefits:
• Utilizes unused space
• Insulates building
• Manages stormwater
• Cools urban heat island
```

**3. Vertical Farms**

| Characteristic | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| Location | Indoor, controlled environment |
| Technology | Stacked growing, LED lights, hydroponics/aeroponics |
| Year-round | Climate independent |
| Space efficiency | 10-100x production per sq ft |
| Examples | AeroFarms (Newark), Plenty (SF) |

**4. Edible Landscaping**

- Street trees bearing fruit/nuts
- Public parks with edible plants
- "Food forests" in urban areas
- Ornamental gardens with edibles

### 5.2 Food Security and Urban Food Systems

#### Defining Food Security

**Food Security exists when** all people, at all times, have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.

```
THE FOUR PILLARS OF FOOD SECURITY

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
    │        FOOD SECURITY                 │
    └────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                 │
    ┌────────────┴────────────┐
    │                         │
┌───▼──────────┐      ┌──────▼────────┐
│ AVAILABILITY │      │ ACCESSIBILITY │
│              │      │               │
│ Sufficient   │      │ Physical &    │
│ quantities   │      │ economic      │
│ of food      │      │ access        │
└───┬──────────┘      └──────┬────────┘
    │                        │
    └───────────┬────────────┘
                │
    ┌───────────┴────────────┐
    │                        │
┌───▼──────────┐     ┌──────▼────────┐
│ UTILIZATION  │     │  STABILITY    │
│              │     │               │
│ Nutritious   │     │ Consistent    │
│ food, safe   │     │ access over   │
│ preparation  │     │ time          │
└──────────────┘     └───────────────┘
```

#### Urban Food Insecurity

**Statistics:**

| Metric | Global | US Urban Areas |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Food insecure population | 690 million | 35 million |
| Urban poor at risk | 1 billion+ | 14% of households |
| Children affected | 250 million | 11 million |

#### Food Deserts

**Food Desert:** Area with limited access to affordable, nutritious food (typically no full-service grocery within 1 mile urban, 10 miles rural)

```
FOOD DESERT CHARACTERISTICS

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           TYPICAL FOOD DESERT                      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                    │
│  ✗ No full-service grocery store                  │
│  ✗ Only convenience stores, fast food             │
│  ✗ Higher prices for fresh produce                │
│  ✗ Lower quality, limited selection                │
│  ✗ Transportation barriers                         │
│  ✗ Often low-income communities                   │
│  ✗ Disproportionately affects communities of color│
│                                                    │
│  HEALTH IMPACTS:                                   │
│  • Higher obesity rates                           │
│  • More diet-related diseases                     │
│  • Shorter life expectancy                        │
│                                                    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### How Urban Agriculture Addresses Food Insecurity

| Challenge | UA Solution |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Limited Access** | Grow food where people live |
| **High Prices** | Reduce food costs, sell at community prices |
| **Food Quality** | Ultra-fresh, pesticide-free |
| **Lack of Control** | Community ownership and decision-making |
| **Nutrition Education** | Learn by growing and cooking |
| **Economic Opportunity** | Jobs, entrepreneurship |

### 5.4 Challenges of Urban Agriculture

#### Space Constraints

| Challenge | Description | Solutions |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Limited Land** | High land costs, competition | Vertical growing, rooftops, vacant lots |
| **Small Parcels** | Fragmented space | Intensive methods, container growing |
| **Temporary Access** | Insecure land tenure | Mobile gardens, community land trusts |
| **Zoning** | Restricted uses | Policy advocacy, zoning changes |

#### Contamination Risks

```
URBAN SOIL CONTAMINATION

Sources:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Lead paint from old buildings         │
│ • Industrial pollution                   │
│ • Vehicle emissions (roadways)          │
│ • Treated lumber                         │
│ • Pesticides/herbicides                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘

Solutions:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Test soil before planting             │
│ ✓ Raised beds with clean soil           │
│ ✓ Phytoremediation (cleaning plants)    │
│ ✓ Container gardening                   │
│ ✓ Mulching to prevent dust              │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Resource Limitations

| Resource | Challenge | Solution |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Water** | Limited, expensive | Drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting |
| **Sunlight** | Building shade | Site selection, shade-tolerant crops |
| **Soil** | Must import | Composting, soil building |
| **Funding** | Startup and operating costs | Grants, crowdfunding, sales |
| **Knowledge** | Urban residents lack farming experience | Training programs, mentorship |

#### Social and Political Challenges

| Challenge | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Gentrification** | Gardens may increase property values, displace residents |
| **Equity** | Who benefits? Who decides? |
| **Governance** | Managing shared spaces |
| **Vandalism/Theft** | Security concerns |
| **Regulations** | Health codes, permits, liability |
| **NIMBYism** | Neighbor opposition |

### 5.6 Designing Successful Urban Agriculture

#### Site Selection Criteria

```
SITE ASSESSMENT CHECKLIST

☐ SUNLIGHT
  └─ Minimum 6 hours for vegetables, 8+ ideal

☐ WATER ACCESS
  └─ Nearby source, permission to use

☐ SOIL SAFETY
  └─ Test for contaminants, or use raised beds

☐ ACCESSIBILITY
  └─ Easy to reach, ADA considerations

☐ TENURE SECURITY
  └─ Long-term access (3+ years minimum)

☐ COMMUNITY SUPPORT
  └─ Neighbors supportive

☐ SIZE
  └─ Appropriate for goals and labor available

☐ DRAINAGE
  └─ Not prone to flooding

☐ WIND PROTECTION
  └─ Windbreaks if needed

☐ SAFETY
  └─ Well-lit, visible, secure
```

#### Design Principles

**1. Start Small, Expand Gradually**
- Begin with manageable size
- Learn and adjust
- Scale up with success

**2. Design for Multiple Functions**

| Function | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Food production | Vegetable beds |
| Education | Demonstration areas, signs |
| Community gathering | Seating areas |
| Habitat | Pollinator plants, birdhouses |
| Aesthetics | Beautiful plantings |
| Water management | Rain gardens |

**3. Universal Access**

```
ACCESSIBLE DESIGN FEATURES

┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  • Raised beds (wheelchair height)      │
│  • Wide pathways (48" minimum)          │
│  • Firm, level surfaces                 │
│  • Vertical growing (reduces bending)   │
│  • Adaptive tools available             │
│  • Seating areas                        │
│  • Clear signage                        │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**4. Build Community**
- Regular work parties
- Harvest celebrations
- Workshops and classes
- Intergenerational activities
- Cultural events

#### Crop Selection for Urban Gardens

**Best Crops for Urban Settings:**

| Crop Type | Examples | Why Good for Urban |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Leafy Greens** | Lettuce, kale, chard | Fast growing, high value, shade tolerant |
| **Herbs** | Basil, cilantro, parsley | High value, small space, continuous harvest |
| **Tomatoes** | Cherry, paste varieties | Popular, productive, vertical growing |
| **Beans** | Pole beans, snap peas | Vertical, nitrogen-fixing |
| **Root Crops** | Radishes, carrots, beets | Efficient space use, kid-friendly |
| **Flowers** | Marigolds, nasturtiums | Edible, attract pollinators, beautiful |

### 5.7 Economic Models for Urban Agriculture

#### Community-Based Models

**1. Community Garden**
- Plot fees cover costs
- Communal work and harvest
- Non-profit structure
- Grant funding

**2. Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)**
- Members pay upfront
- Receive weekly shares
- Shared risk and reward
- Community connection

**3. Donation Gardens**
- Grow for food banks
- Volunteer labor
- Grant funded
- Address food insecurity directly

#### Commercial Models

**4. Market Garden**
- Intensive production
- Direct sales (farmers market, restaurants)
- 1-2 full-time farmers
- Profitable on small acreage

**5. Vertical Farm**
- High-tech, controlled environment
- Year-round production
- Significant capital investment
- Wholesale to grocers, restaurants

**6. Urban Farm Incubator**
- Train new farmers
- Provide land and equipment
- Graduated rent structure
- Create new businesses

#### Hybrid Models

**7. Farm-to-Institution**
- Partner with schools, hospitals
- Guaranteed market
- Impact + income
- Often non-profit or social enterprise
