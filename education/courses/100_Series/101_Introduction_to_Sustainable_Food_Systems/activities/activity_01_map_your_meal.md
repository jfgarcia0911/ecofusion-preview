## Learning Objectives

By completing this activity, you will:
1. Trace the origins of food from a personal meal
2. Estimate the total food miles of your meal
3. Calculate the approximate carbon footprint
4. Reflect on the complexity of the global food system
5. Consider alternatives for reducing food miles

## Sample Completed Activity

**Example Meal: Chicken Stir-Fry with Rice**

| Ingredient | Origin | Miles |
|------------|--------|-------|
| Chicken breast | Arkansas | 800 |
| Broccoli | California | 2,000 |
| Bell peppers | Mexico | 1,500 |
| Soy sauce | Japan | 5,500 |
| Rice | Thailand | 8,500 |
| Garlic | China | 6,500 |
| Ginger | India | 8,000 |
| Vegetable oil | Midwest US | 500 |

**Total Food Miles:** 33,300 miles
**Average:** 4,163 miles per ingredient

**Carbon Footprint:**
- Chicken (0.3 kg × 6.9) = 2.07 kg CO2
- Vegetables (0.4 kg × 2.0) = 0.8 kg CO2
- Rice (0.2 kg × 2.7) = 0.54 kg CO2
- **Total:** 3.41 kg CO2

**Reflection:** "I was shocked that my 'simple' stir-fry involved ingredients from 6 different countries. The soy sauce, rice, garlic, and ginger all came from Asia. I could reduce food miles by buying local chicken from a farmers market, using American-grown rice from California, and substituting local onions for imported garlic."
