## Script

[ON SCREEN: "Module 7 - Aquaponics, Hydroponics & Aquaculture"]

This module is where everything in the course comes together - and it's also the heart of what EcoFusion itself is built around. We're going to meet three systems with confusingly similar names. By the end, you'll never mix them up again.

[ON SCREEN: Three boxes - Hydroponics (plants), Aquaculture (fish), Aquaponics (plants + fish)]

Here's the simple version. **Hydroponics** grows **plants**. **Aquaculture** raises **fish**. **Aquaponics** does **both, together, in one system**.

[ON SCREEN: Lettuce roots hanging in flowing water]

Let's take them one at a time. **Hydroponics** is growing plants **in water-based nutrient solutions, without soil**. The grower dissolves exactly the minerals the plants need into water and delivers it straight to the roots. There are **no fish** involved - just plants and a carefully mixed nutrient solution. It grows **leafy greens and herbs** beautifully, and fruiting plants like **tomatoes and peppers** too.

[ON SCREEN: Tanks of fish; a shellfish farm on a coast]

**Aquaculture** is **farming aquatic organisms** - **fish, shellfish, even aquatic plants** - in controlled conditions. It's the fastest-growing food production sector in the world, and it now supplies around half the fish people eat.

[ON SCREEN: A loop - fish tank → grow bed → clean water back to the fish]

And **aquaponics** is what happens when you connect the two. Here's the clever part. Fish produce waste - mostly **ammonia** - and in a fish-only system, you'd have to keep removing it, because ammonia is toxic to fish. Plants, meanwhile, need nitrogen to grow, which hydroponic growers have to buy as fertilizer.

Aquaponics solves both problems at once. The fish waste feeds the plants, and the plants clean the water for the fish.

[ON SCREEN: The nitrogen cycle, step by step - Fish → Ammonia → Bacteria → Nitrite → Nitrate → Plants]

But there's a hidden third partner making it work: **bacteria**. Plants can't use ammonia directly. So beneficial bacteria, living on every surface of the system, **convert fish waste into nutrients plants can use**. One group turns ammonia into **nitrite**; a second group turns nitrite into **nitrate** - and nitrate is plant food.

That chain is the **nitrogen cycle**: **fish, to ammonia, to bacteria, to nitrite, to nitrate, to plants.** It's worth memorising - it's the engine of every aquaponic system.

[ON SCREEN: Tilapia swimming]

Which fish? The favourite is **tilapia**. They're **hardy** - they tolerate a wide range of water conditions - they **grow fast**, and they're easy to raise. Perfect for beginners and for commercial systems alike.

[ON SCREEN: A basket of greens beside a fillet of fish - "Two products, one system"]

So why choose aquaponics over plain hydroponics? The big advantage is that it **produces two things: plants and protein**. Vegetables and fish from the same water, with little or no synthetic fertilizer.

[ON SCREEN: A water test kit - pH strip, thermometer]

The trade-off is complexity. You're now keeping three living things happy at once - fish, plants, and bacteria - and they don't always want the same conditions. **Balancing the needs of fish, plants, and bacteria** - the pH, the temperature, the nutrient levels - is the central skill of an aquaponics grower.
