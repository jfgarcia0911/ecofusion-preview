## Script

[ON SCREEN: "Module 8 - Getting Started: Your Sustainable Journey"]

We've covered a lot of ground. The global food system and what it costs us. The principles of sustainability. Organic, regenerative, and permaculture farms. Rooftops and vertical farms. Fish and plants feeding each other. This last module is different. It's not about the world's food system. It's about *yours* - and what you'll actually do next.

[ON SCREEN: A checklist - Goals, Resources, Space, Time]

The **first step** isn't buying equipment. It's taking honest stock. **Assess your goals, your resources, your space, and your time.** Why do you want to grow food - to feed your family, to learn, to start a business, to help your community? How much money can you put in? How much space do you really have - a windowsill, a backyard, a rooftop? And how many hours a week can you give it, honestly?

The answers point you towards very different first projects, and that's the point.

[ON SCREEN: A few pots on a balcony; a small countertop hydroponic unit]

If you're a beginner with limited space, **start with a container garden or a small hydroponic system**. A few pots of herbs, or a countertop hydroponic kit, will teach you more in a month than a year of reading.

[ON SCREEN: "Start small, think big"]

This is the principle behind everything in this module: **start small, think big**. **Begin with a manageable project, while planning for where you want to go.** The big vision keeps you motivated; the small start keeps you from getting overwhelmed.

[ON SCREEN: A single raised bed labelled "Pilot"]

That's what a **pilot project** is for. Before you invest in a large system, run a small one. It **lets you learn and test before you scale up** - you find out which crops thrive, what goes wrong, what people will actually buy - while the mistakes are still cheap.

[ON SCREEN: A crop-choice triangle - Climate, Skills, Market]

When you choose what to grow, don't just pick your favourite vegetable. Choose what **your climate, your skills, and your market** can support. What grows well where you are? What can you manage at your level? And if you plan to sell, what do people nearby actually want?

[ON SCREEN: A city block with a highlighted backyard - "Check the rules"]

If you're growing in a city, there's one more thing to check early: **zoning rules and space constraints**. Some areas limit what you can grow, whether you can keep bees or chickens, or whether you can sell produce from home. Five minutes with your local council's rules can save you a lot of trouble later.

[ON SCREEN: Hands in soil beside an open notebook]

How should you learn? **Start practising right away, with small experiments, while you keep learning.** Don't wait until you feel ready - nobody ever does. And when you look for information, go to **trusted sources**: university **extension services**, research universities, and well-moderated growing forums. Be wary of anyone promising easy profits.

[ON SCREEN: A wilting plant, then a person checking a guide, then a phone call]

Things *will* go wrong. Plants wilt, pests arrive, a fish tank goes cloudy. When it happens, **troubleshoot step by step, and ask experienced growers for advice.** Every problem you solve is a lesson you'll never forget.

[ON SCREEN: A group of growers at a community garden]

Which brings us to the most underrated resource of all: other people. **Connect with local growers and networks.** They share knowledge, swap seeds and tools, help when things go wrong, and cheer you on when they go right.

[ON SCREEN: Three columns - "Days 1-30: Foundation", "Days 31-60: Exploration", "Days 61-90: Commitment"]

In the lesson, you'll build a **30-60-90 day plan**. The first thirty days lay the **foundation** - your goals and your first small project. The next thirty are for **exploration** - learning, testing, connecting. The last thirty are for **commitment** - choosing your direction and taking your first real step.

[ON SCREEN: Instructor, full frame]

Read the Module 8 lesson, build your plan, then take Quiz 8. After that, there's just one video left.
