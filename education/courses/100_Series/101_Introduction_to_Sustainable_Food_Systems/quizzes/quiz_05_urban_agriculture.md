## Questions

### Question 1
**What is urban agriculture?**

A) Farming using urban planning techniques

B) Growing food within or around cities ✓

C) Agriculture focused on city aesthetics

D) Farming for urban export markets

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Urban agriculture is the practice of cultivating, processing, and distributing food in or around urban areas, including cities, towns, and metropolitan regions.*

---

### Question 2
**By 2050, what percentage of the world's population is expected to live in urban areas?**

A) 50%

B) 60%

C) 68% ✓

D) 75%

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: The United Nations projects that 68% of the world's population will live in urban areas by 2050, increasing demand for urban food production.*

---

### Question 3
**What is a "food desert"?**

A) Dry regions where food cannot be grown

B) Areas with limited access to affordable, nutritious food ✓

C) Desert regions used for agriculture

D) Areas where all food must be imported

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Food deserts are urban or rural areas where people have limited access to affordable and nutritious food, often due to lack of grocery stores or transportation.*

---

### Question 4
**Which is NOT a benefit of urban agriculture?**

A) Reduced food transportation costs

B) Improved urban air quality

C) Lower labor costs than rural farming ✓

D) Community building and education

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Urban agriculture typically has HIGHER labor costs due to urban wages and smaller-scale operations. The other options are genuine benefits.*

---

### Question 5
**What is a community garden?**

A) Garden maintained by professional landscapers

B) Shared growing space managed by community members ✓

C) Public park with ornamental plants

D) Commercial farm in a neighborhood

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Community gardens are shared spaces where community members collectively grow food, flowers, and herbs, fostering social connections and local food production.*

---

### Question 6
**Which urban farming method maximizes production in limited space?**

A) Extensive grazing

B) Vertical farming ✓

C) Wheat cultivation

D) Cattle ranching

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Vertical farming grows crops in stacked layers, maximizing production per square foot—ideal for space-limited urban environments.*

---

### Question 7
**What is rooftop farming?**

A) Farming grain crops on hillsides

B) Growing food on building rooftops ✓

C) Storing harvested crops on roofs

D) Sun-drying crops on elevated platforms

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Rooftop farming utilizes underused roof spaces for food production, providing insulation benefits while producing fresh food in dense urban areas.*

---

### Question 8
**How does urban agriculture contribute to food security?**

A) It completely replaces rural farming

B) It provides local, accessible fresh food and reduces supply chain vulnerability ✓

C) It only produces luxury foods

D) It eliminates the need for grocery stores

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Urban agriculture enhances food security by providing local food sources, reducing dependence on long supply chains, and improving access to fresh produce in underserved areas.*

---

### Question 9
**What is a key challenge of urban agriculture?**

A) Too much available space

B) Lack of sunlight in all locations

C) High land costs and potential soil contamination ✓

D) Excessive water availability

**Correct Answer: C**

*Explanation: Urban agriculture faces challenges including expensive land, potential soil contamination from previous industrial use, and zoning restrictions.*

---

### Question 10
**Which policy supports urban agriculture development?**

A) Banning all farming within city limits

B) Zoning ordinances that allow agricultural use in urban areas ✓

C) Requiring all food to come from rural areas

D) Prohibiting rooftop modifications

**Correct Answer: B**

*Explanation: Progressive zoning policies that permit agricultural activities, beekeeping, composting, and farmers markets help urban agriculture thrive legally.*
