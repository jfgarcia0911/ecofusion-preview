## Food Carbon Calculator

### Step 1: Find Your Food's Origin

| Food Item | Typical Origin | Miles (Approx.) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Bananas | Ecuador, Costa Rica | 2,500-3,000 |
| Coffee | Colombia, Brazil, Vietnam | 3,000-9,000 |
| Chocolate | Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana | 5,000+ |
| Rice (imported) | Thailand, India | 8,000-9,000 |
| Olive Oil | Spain, Italy, Greece | 5,000-6,000 |
| Avocados | Mexico, Peru, Chile | 1,800-5,500 |
| Shrimp | Thailand, Vietnam | 8,000-9,000 |
| Wine | France, Italy, Australia | 5,000-10,000 |
| Out-of-season produce | Southern hemisphere | 5,000-9,000 |

### Step 2: Calculate Transport Emissions

```
    SIMPLE CALCULATION:

    Food Miles × Weight × Emission Factor = Transport CO₂

    EMISSION FACTORS (kg CO₂ per kg-mile):
    • Air:   0.0022
    • Truck: 0.0007
    • Rail:  0.0002
    • Ship:  0.0001

    EXAMPLE:
    1 kg of air-freighted asparagus from Peru (3,500 miles):
    3,500 × 1 × 0.0022 = 7.7 kg CO₂

    vs. 1 kg of local asparagus (50 miles by truck):
    50 × 1 × 0.0007 = 0.035 kg CO₂

    Difference: 220x more carbon!
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Food miles matter** but are just one part of the carbon equation
2. **Transport method** matters more than distance (air vs. ship)
3. **Production method** often has the biggest impact (what > where)
4. **Seasonal eating** naturally reduces food miles
5. **Local food systems** offer benefits beyond just carbon reduction
