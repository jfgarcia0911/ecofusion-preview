# Course Glossary
## Course 101: Introduction to Sustainable Food Systems

---

## Complete Alphabetical Glossary

### A

**Aeroponics**
Growing plants with roots suspended in air and misted with nutrient solution. Uses very little water and allows for maximum oxygen exposure to roots.

**Agrochemicals**
Chemical products used in agriculture, including fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, and fungicides.

**Algae Bloom**
Rapid increase in algae population in water, often caused by excess nutrients, which can deplete oxygen and harm aquatic life.

**Ammonia (NH3)**
Toxic nitrogen compound excreted by fish; the first stage in the nitrogen cycle. Must be converted by bacteria for fish and plant safety.

**Aquaculture**
The farming of aquatic organisms including fish, crustaceans, mollusks, and aquatic plants in controlled conditions.

**Aquaponics**
An integrated food production system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant growing) in a symbiotic environment.

### B

**Beneficial Bacteria**
Microorganisms that perform helpful functions, such as Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter that convert ammonia to nitrate in aquaponic systems.

**Biodiversity**
The variety of life in a particular ecosystem or on Earth as a whole, including genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity.

**Biofilter**
A component in aquaponic/recirculating systems where beneficial bacteria colonize to convert ammonia to nitrate.

**Biofloc**
A technology using microbial communities to convert fish waste into protein-rich food within aquaculture systems.

### C

**Carbon Footprint**
The total amount of greenhouse gases (primarily CO2) produced directly and indirectly by an activity, product, or entity.

**Carbon Sequestration**
The process of capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide, often in soil or vegetation.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Any agricultural technology that allows growers to manipulate environmental conditions (light, temperature, humidity, etc.) for optimal plant growth.

**Climate Change**
Long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns, primarily caused by human activities since the Industrial Revolution.

**Closed-Loop System**
A system that recycles resources internally with minimal external inputs or waste outputs.

**Compost**
Decomposed organic matter used as a fertilizer and soil amendment.

**Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)**
A system where consumers purchase farm shares in advance and receive regular deliveries of produce throughout the growing season.

**Cover Crops**
Plants grown primarily to protect and enrich soil between main crop plantings.

### D

**Dead Zone**
An area of water with oxygen levels too low to support most marine life, typically caused by nutrient pollution.

**Deep Water Culture (DWC)**
A hydroponic method where plants float on rafts with roots suspended in aerated nutrient solution.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**
The amount of oxygen dissolved in water, essential for fish and root health in aquatic systems.

### E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)**
A measure of a solution's ability to conduct electricity, used to estimate nutrient concentration.

**Ecosystem**
A community of living organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment.

**Ecosystem Services**
Benefits humans receive from ecosystems, including clean water, pollination, and climate regulation.

**Erosion**
The wearing away of soil by wind, water, or other forces.

**Eutrophication**
The enrichment of water with nutrients (especially nitrogen and phosphorus) causing excessive plant/algae growth and oxygen depletion.

**Externality**
A cost or benefit that affects parties not directly involved in a transaction, not reflected in market prices.

### F

**Farmers Market**
A market where farmers sell produce directly to consumers.

**Fertigation**
The application of fertilizers through an irrigation system.

**Food Desert**
An area, often urban, with limited access to affordable and nutritious food.

**Food Hub**
A facility that aggregates and distributes locally produced food.

**Food Miles**
The distance food travels from where it is grown to where it is consumed.

**Food Security**
The condition in which all people have reliable access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.

**Food Sovereignty**
The right of peoples to define their own food and agriculture systems.

**Food System**
All processes and infrastructure involved in feeding a population: production, processing, distribution, consumption, and waste management.

**Food Waste**
Edible food that is discarded at any stage of the food supply chain.

### G

**Genetic Diversity**
The variety of genes within a species, important for adaptation and resilience.

**Greenhouse**
A structure with walls and roof made largely of transparent material for growing plants under controlled conditions.

**Greenhouse Gases (GHG)**
Gases that trap heat in the atmosphere, including CO2, methane, and nitrous oxide.

**Grow Bed**
The container in aquaponics/hydroponics where plants grow, filled with media or holding nutrient solution.

### H

**Heirloom Variety**
Traditional plant varieties that have been passed down through generations, typically open-pollinated.

**Hydroponics**
A method of growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions in water.

### I

**Industrial Agriculture**
Large-scale, mechanized farming focused on maximizing output through intensive use of inputs.

**Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**
An approach combining biological, cultural, physical, and chemical tools to minimize pest damage with minimal risk.

### L

**Local Food**
Food grown and consumed within a defined geographic area, typically 100-250 miles.

### M

**Methane (CH4)**
A potent greenhouse gas produced by livestock digestion, rice paddies, and decomposing organic matter.

**Microgreens**
Young vegetable greens harvested when the first true leaves have developed, typically 7-21 days after germination.

**Monoculture**
The agricultural practice of growing a single crop species over a large area for many consecutive years.

### N

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)**
A hydroponic method where a thin film of nutrient solution flows over plant roots in channels.

**Nitrate (NO3)**
The final, plant-usable form of nitrogen in the nitrogen cycle; relatively non-toxic to fish.

**Nitrite (NO2)**
An intermediate nitrogen compound in the cycling process; toxic to fish at low levels.

**Nitrogen Cycle**
The biological process by which nitrogen is converted between various chemical forms as it circulates through ecosystems.

**Nitrous Oxide (N2O)**
A greenhouse gas with 265 times the warming potential of CO2, primarily from fertilizer use.

### O

**Organic Agriculture**
Farming methods that avoid synthetic chemicals and emphasize natural processes.

### P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)**
The portion of light spectrum (400-700nm) used by plants for photosynthesis.

**Peak Phosphorus**
The point in time when maximum global phosphorus extraction is reached, estimated 2030-2040.

**Permaculture**
A design system for creating sustainable human environments based on observing natural ecosystems.

**Pesticide**
A substance used to kill, repel, or control pests including insects, weeds, and fungi.

**pH**
A measure of acidity/alkalinity on a scale of 0-14, with 7 being neutral.

**Photosynthesis**
The process by which plants convert light energy, water, and CO2 into glucose and oxygen.

**Polyculture**
Growing multiple crop species together in the same space.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**
The measure of light intensity for plant growth, measured in μmol/m²/s.

### R

**Recirculating Aquaculture System (RAS)**
Fish farming systems that filter and reuse water continuously.

**Regenerative Agriculture**
Farming practices that restore and enhance ecosystem health, particularly soil health.

**Resilience**
The ability of a system to absorb disturbances and reorganize while maintaining essential function.

**Runoff**
Water that flows over land surfaces, potentially carrying pollutants into waterways.

### S

**Salinization**
The accumulation of salts in soil, often from irrigation, making it unsuitable for crops.

**Soil Degradation**
The decline in soil quality caused by improper use or poor management.

**Soil Erosion**
The removal of topsoil by wind, water, or human activity faster than it can be replaced.

**Supply Chain**
The network of organizations, resources, and processes involved in creating and delivering a product.

**Sustainability**
Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

**Sustainable Agriculture**
Farming practices that meet current food needs while protecting resources for future generations.

### T

**Three Pillars of Sustainability**
The framework identifying environmental, economic, and social factors as equally important for sustainability.

**Topsoil**
The upper layer of soil, rich in organic matter and nutrients, essential for plant growth.

**Traceability**
The ability to track the origin and journey of food products through the supply chain.

### U

**Urban Agriculture**
Growing food within cities, including rooftops, vacant lots, community gardens, and indoor farms.

### V

**Value Chain**
All activities involved in creating value for a product, from raw material to end consumer.

**Vertical Farming**
Growing crops in vertically stacked layers, typically in controlled indoor environments.

### W

**Water Footprint**
The total volume of freshwater used to produce goods or services.

**Watershed**
An area of land where all water drains to a common outlet.
