## Lesson Content

### 3.1 Material Quality and Selection

#### Lumber for Raised Beds

**Best Choices:**

| Wood Type | Cost | Lifespan | Notes |
|-----------|------|----------|-------|
| **Cedar** | $$$ | 10-15 years | Naturally rot-resistant, beautiful |
| **Redwood** | $$$$ | 15-20 years | Best quality, expensive, regional |
| **Cypress** | $$$ | 10-15 years | Good alternative, regional (South) |
| **Pine (untreated)** | $ | 3-5 years | Affordable starter, plan to replace |
| **Composite lumber** | $$$$ | 20+ years | No rot, no splinters, expensive |

**Dimensions to Buy:**
- 2"×12" boards (actual: 1.5"×11.25") for 12" tall beds
- 2"×6" boards (actual: 1.5"×5.5") for 6" tall beds or double-stack
- 4"×4" posts for corner supports (actual: 3.5"×3.5")

```
WOOD GRADE SELECTION

Appearance Grade (for visible projects)
├─ Clear/Select: Few knots, beautiful
├─ Premium: Some character
└─ Standard: Most economical

Structural Grade (for strength)
├─ #1 Grade: Highest strength
├─ #2 Grade: Good for most projects ✓
└─ #3 Grade: Economy, okay for raised beds

Choose #2 grade for best value/quality balance
```

**Avoid:**
- Pressure-treated lumber (before 2004 contains arsenic)
- Railroad ties (creosote contamination)
- Painted or unknown wood (lead, chemicals)

#### Hardware and Fasteners

**Screws vs. Nails:**

| Feature | Screws | Nails |
|---------|--------|-------|
| **Strength** | High holding power | Good shear strength |
| **Adjustability** | Can remove/adjust | Permanent |
| **Speed** | Slower | Faster |
| **Best For** | Raised beds, precise work | General construction |

**Recommended:**
- 3" exterior-grade screws (deck screws)
- Coated or stainless (rust-resistant)
- Quantity: 16-24 per raised bed

#### Soil and Growing Media

**Raised Bed Soil Mix Recipe:**

```
IDEAL RAISED BED MIX

┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  40% Quality Topsoil         │ ← Structure, minerals
├──────────────────────────────┤
│  30% Compost                 │ ← Nutrients, biology
├──────────────────────────────┤
│  30% Aeration                │ ← Perlite, vermiculite,
│     Amendment                 │   or coconut coir
└──────────────────────────────┘

Alternative: "Mel's Mix"
├─ 1/3 peat moss or coco coir
├─ 1/3 vermiculite
└─ 1/3 blended compost (5+ types)
```

**Purchasing Options:**

| Method | Cost | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|------|
| **Bagged mix** | $$$ | Convenient, consistent | Expensive for large beds |
| **Bulk soil delivery** | $$ | Better value, less work | Minimum order (often 1+ yard) |
| **Make your own** | $ | Cheapest, customizable | Requires sourcing, mixing |

**Volume Needed:**
- 4'×6'×1' bed = 24 cu ft = ~12 bags (2 cu ft bags)
- Or 0.89 cubic yards (round up to 1 yard for bulk)

#### Hydroponic Components

**Essential Items:**

| Component | Source | Price Range |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Food-grade container** | Hardware store, online | $10-40 |
| **Net pots (2-3")** | Hydro supplier | $0.25-1 each |
| **Growing medium** | Hydro supplier | $15-30/bag |
| **Nutrients (A+B)** | Hydro supplier | $20-50/set |
| **pH test kit** | Hydro supplier, pool store | $10-25 |
| **pH adjuster** | Hydro supplier | $10-20 |

**Quality Indicators:**
- Containers: Food-grade, opaque (light-blocking)
- Nutrients: Hydroponic-specific (not soil fertilizer)
- pH kit: Liquid or digital (not paper strips)

### 3.2 Sourcing Strategies

#### Where to Buy

```
SUPPLIER COMPARISON

Big Box Stores (Home Depot, Lowe's)
├─ Pros: Convenient, full stock, easy returns
├─ Cons: Higher prices, limited selection
└─ Best for: Hardware, basic lumber, tools

Local Lumber Yards
├─ Pros: Better quality, advice, delivery
├─ Cons: Less convenient hours, variable stock
└─ Best for: Quality lumber, bulk orders

Farm/Feed Stores
├─ Pros: Agricultural knowledge, fair prices
├─ Cons: Limited building supplies
└─ Best for: Soil amendments, tools, greenhouse supplies

Online Hydroponic Suppliers
├─ Pros: Specialty items, good prices
├─ Cons: Shipping costs, can't inspect
└─ Best for: Hydroponic equipment, nutrients, seeds

Local Classifieds (Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace)
├─ Pros: Cheap/free, recycling
├─ Cons: Time investment, variable quality
└─ Best for: Containers, used materials, tools

Freecycle/Buy Nothing Groups
├─ Pros: Free! Community
├─ Cons: Availability unpredictable
└─ Best for: Containers, pallets, pots
```

#### Cost-Saving Strategies

**New Materials:**
1. **Buy in bulk** - Split orders with friends
2. **Shop sales** - Spring and fall clearances
3. **Price match** - Many stores will match competitors
4. **Use coupons** - Sign up for store emails
5. **Buy off-season** - Cheaper when demand is low

**Used/Recycled Options:**

| Item Needed | Free/Cheap Alternative |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **Raised bed lumber** | Pallet wood (heat-treated only), old fence boards |
| **Containers** | 5-gal buckets (bakery, restaurant), food-grade drums |
| **Greenhouse frame** | PVC from plumbing surplus, cattle panels |
| **Pots** | Yogurt containers, nursery donations |
| **Tools** | Garage sales, estate sales, tool libraries |
| **Compost** | Make your own, municipal programs |

**Safety Note:** Only use food-grade containers for hydroponics/food growing. Avoid containers that held chemicals.

#### Specialty Item Sourcing

**Hydroponic Nutrients:**
- Online: Amazon, Hydroponics retailers
- Local: Hydroponic shops (if available)
- Alternatives: Garden centers (limited selection)

**Seeds:**
- Local: Garden centers, feed stores
- Online: Johnny's, Baker Creek, High Mowing Seeds
- Seed swaps: Local gardening groups

**Growing Media (hydroponics):**
- Clay pebbles: Hydroponic suppliers, Amazon
- Rockwool: Hydroponic suppliers
- Coco coir: Garden centers, pet stores (reptile bedding)

### 3.4 Quality vs. Cost Trade-offs

#### When to Splurge

**Spend More On:**
1. **Foundation/structure** - Failure is expensive to fix
2. **Items you'll use for years** - Quality tools, pumps
3. **Hard-to-access later** - Underground pipes, electrical
4. **Safety-critical** - Electrical components, sharp tools

**Examples:**
- Cedar lumber lasts 3× longer than pine (worth it for permanent beds)
- Quality pump pays for itself in reliability
- Good soil is foundation of success

#### When to Save

**Save Money On:**
1. **Temporary/experimental** - First-time builds, testing
2. **Easy to replace** - Pots, small tools
3. **Aesthetic choices** - Decorative items
4. **Tools used once** - Rent or borrow

**Examples:**
- Pine lumber fine for learning/temporary beds
- Used containers perfect for experiments
- Borrow specialty tools for one-time use

### 3.5 Shopping Timeline

#### Strategic Purchase Sequence

```
BUYING TIMELINE

4-6 Weeks Before Build
├─ Research and price comparison
├─ Order specialty items (long shipping)
└─ Watch for sales

2-3 Weeks Before
├─ Purchase lumber (inspect before buying)
├─ Order hydroponic equipment
└─ Arrange bulk soil delivery

1 Week Before
├─ Buy hardware and fasteners
├─ Gather any borrowed tools
└─ Pick up last-minute items

Build Weekend
├─ Final check - do you have everything?
└─ Quick run for forgot items
```

#### Inspection Tips

**Lumber Inspection:**
- □ Check for straight (sight down length)
- □ Avoid excessive knots (weak points)
- □ Look for cracks/splits (especially ends)
- □ Moisture: Should be dry, not wet
- □ Choose best pieces in stack

**Container Inspection:**
- □ Food-grade symbol (for hydroponics)
- □ No cracks or damage
- □ Opaque/light-blocking
- □ Lid fits snugly
- □ Easy to drill/modify
