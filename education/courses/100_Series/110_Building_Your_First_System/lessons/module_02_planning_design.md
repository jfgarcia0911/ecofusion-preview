## Lesson Content

### 2.1 Site Analysis Fundamentals

#### Sun Exposure Assessment

**Why It Matters:** Most vegetables need 6-8 hours of direct sunlight daily.

```
SUN EXPOSURE TRACKING

Full Sun (6-8+ hours)
├─ Tomatoes, peppers, squash
├─ Fruiting vegetables
└─ Most herbs

Partial Sun (4-6 hours)
├─ Lettuce, greens
├─ Root vegetables
└─ Cool season crops

Shade (2-4 hours)
├─ Limited options
├─ Leafy greens only
└─ Consider supplemental lighting
```

**How to Assess:**

| Time | Task |
|------|------|
| **Morning (9 AM)** | Mark sunny spots |
| **Midday (12 PM)** | Note peak sun areas |
| **Afternoon (3 PM)** | Check late-day sun |
| **Evening (6 PM)** | Note evening light |

**Sun Mapping Exercise:**
1. Sketch your space
2. Mark structures, trees, fences
3. Track sun movement over one day
4. Identify full sun zones (your best spots)

**Seasonal Considerations:**
- Summer: Sun is higher, more intense
- Winter: Sun is lower, less intense
- Trees: Deciduous lose leaves (more winter sun)
- Buildings: Longer shadows in winter

#### Water Access

**Critical Questions:**
1. How close is the nearest water source?
2. Can you run a hose to your growing area?
3. Is outdoor water available year-round?
4. Do you need frost-free faucets?
5. Is rainwater catchment possible?

```
WATER ACCESS PLANNING

Ideal: <25 feet from hose spigot
├─ Easy daily watering
└─ Multiple trips manageable

Moderate: 25-50 feet
├─ Install drip irrigation
└─ Or extend permanent hose

Difficult: 50+ feet
├─ Must install water line
├─ Rainwater collection system
└─ Or choose drought-tolerant system
```

#### Drainage and Soil

**Drainage Test:**
1. Dig hole 12" deep
2. Fill with water
3. Time how long to drain

**Results:**
- <2 hours = Excellent drainage
- 2-4 hours = Good drainage
- 4-8 hours = Moderate (amend soil)
- >8 hours = Poor (raised beds essential)

**Soil Testing:**
- pH test (6.0-7.0 ideal for most vegetables)
- Texture test (squeeze test for clay/sand/loam)
- Contamination check (if urban or unknown history)

#### Accessibility

**Plan for Easy Access:**

| Factor | Ideal | Acceptable | Problematic |
|--------|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Distance from house** | <50 ft | 50-100 ft | >100 ft |
| **Path** | Paved/mulched | Grass | Mud/obstacles |
| **Gate width** | 48"+ | 36" | <36" |
| **Slope** | Flat | <10% | >10% |

**Accessibility Checklist:**
- □ Can you see the system from your house? (Out of sight = out of mind)
- □ Is the path lit at night?
- □ Can you access it in wet weather?
- □ Is it close enough for daily monitoring?
- □ Can you bring tools and supplies easily?

#### Wind and Weather

**Wind Assessment:**
- Observe prevailing wind direction
- Note strong wind events
- Identify windbreaks (buildings, fences, trees)

**Wind Protection Strategies:**

```
WIND PROTECTION

Problem: Strong prevailing winds
Solution Options:
├─ Place system on protected side of building
├─ Install fence or windbreak
├─ Use low-profile systems
└─ Stake plants securely
```

**Microclimates:**
- South-facing walls = Warmer
- North-facing walls = Cooler
- Near concrete/pavement = Heat island
- Under eaves = Rain shadow (drier)

### 2.2 Design Principles

#### Raised Bed Design Specifications

**Optimal Dimensions:**

```
RAISED BED DIMENSIONS

Width: 3-4 feet (can reach center from sides)
Length: 4-8 feet (structural strength, materials)
Height: 12-18 inches (root depth, less bending)

    ←──── 4 ft ────→
  ┌──────────────────┐  ▲
  │                  │  │
  │    Growing       │  │ 12-18"
  │    Space         │  │
  │                  │  │
  └──────────────────┘  ▼
  ←──── 6 ft ────→
  (Example: 4'x6'x12")
```

**Spacing Between Beds:**
- Minimum: 18" (wheelbarrow width)
- Comfortable: 24-30"
- Accessible: 36-48"

**Orientation:**
- North-South orientation = Even sun exposure
- East-West orientation = More south side sun

**Materials Selection:**

| Material | Pros | Cons | Lifespan |
|----------|------|------|----------|
| **Cedar** | Rot-resistant, natural, beautiful | Expensive | 10-15 years |
| **Pine (untreated)** | Affordable, available | Rots faster | 3-5 years |
| **Composite** | Long-lasting, no rot | Expensive, synthetic | 20+ years |
| **Concrete blocks** | Permanent, sturdy | Heavy, less aesthetic | 50+ years |
| **Galvanized steel** | Durable, modern look | Can heat up, pricey | 20+ years |

**Avoid:** Treated lumber (older CCA contains arsenic), railroad ties (creosote)

#### Hydroponic System Design

**Kratky System Specifications:**

```
KRATKY SYSTEM COMPONENTS

Net Pot (2-3")
     │
     ┤ Plant stem
     │
     ▼ Roots grow into solution
┌─────────────┐
│             │ ▲ Air gap (essential)
│ ░░░░░░░░░░ │ │ Nutrient solution
│ ░░░░░░░░░░ │ │ Level decreases
└─────────────┘ ▼
Container (5-20 gallons)

Components needed:
├─ Light-blocking container
├─ Net pots with lids
├─ Growing medium (clay pellets, rockwool)
├─ Hydroponic nutrients (A+B formula)
├─ pH test kit
└─ Seedlings or rockwool cubes
```

**Container Selection:**
- 5-gallon bucket = 1-2 large plants or 4-6 lettuce
- 10-gallon tote = 6-8 lettuce heads
- 20-gallon tote = 12-15 lettuce heads

**Scaling:** Start with 2-4 containers to learn before building large systems.

#### Small Greenhouse Design

**Size Recommendations:**

| Purpose | Minimum Size | Ideal Size |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Seedlings only** | 4'x6' | 6'x8' |
| **Season extension** | 6'x8' | 8'x10' |
| **Year-round growing** | 8'x10' | 10'x12'+ |

**Critical Features:**

```
GREENHOUSE ESSENTIALS

┌─────────────────────────┐
│  Vents (top & bottom)   │ ← Air circulation
│         ┌─┐             │
│        ╱   ╲            │
│       │ ☀️ │            │ ← Light transmission
│       │     │           │
│  🌱 🌱 🌱  │           │ ← Growing space
│  ─────────  │           │
└─────────────┴───────────┘
      │
      └── Stable foundation

Essential Elements:
├─ Ventilation (roof and wall vents)
├─ Door (wide enough for equipment)
├─ Level foundation
├─ Good light transmission (>85%)
└─ Anchor points (wind resistance)
```

**Orientation:**
- Long axis East-West (max winter sun)
- South-facing for cold climates
- Some shade for hot climates

### 2.3 Creating Your Design

#### Step-by-Step Design Process

**Step 1: Site Sketch**
- Draw to scale (1/4" = 1 foot common)
- Include existing features
- Mark sun patterns
- Note utilities

**Step 2: Place Your System**
- Choose best sun location
- Ensure water access
- Allow access paths
- Consider expansion

**Step 3: Add Details**
- Exact dimensions
- Material specifications
- Plumbing/electrical needs
- Access points

**Step 4: Create Cut List**
- Lumber dimensions and quantities
- Hardware needs
- Soil/media volumes
- Component parts

#### Calculating Quantities

**Soil Volume for Raised Beds:**

```
Formula: Length × Width × Depth = Volume

Example: 4' × 6' × 1.5' = 36 cubic feet

Conversion: 36 cu ft ÷ 27 = 1.33 cubic yards

Bags: 36 cu ft ÷ 2 cu ft per bag = 18 bags needed
```

**Lumber for Raised Bed:**

```
4' × 6' × 12" tall bed:

Sides:
├─ 2 pieces 2"×12"×6' (long sides)
└─ 2 pieces 2"×12"×4' (short sides)

Stakes (interior corners):
└─ 4 pieces 2"×2"×18" (or 4"×4"×18")

Hardware:
├─ 16-24 exterior screws (3")
└─ (Optional) Corner brackets
```

**Hydroponic Nutrient Solution:**

```
5-gallon container:
├─ 4.5 gallons water (leave air gap)
├─ 9 ml Part A nutrient
└─ 9 ml Part B nutrient
(Typically 2ml per gallon of each)
```

### 2.4 Design Considerations by System Type

#### Container Garden Layout

**Spacing Guide:**

| Container Size | Spacing | Crops |
|---------------|---------|-------|
| **1 gallon** | 8-12" apart | Herbs, lettuce |
| **3 gallon** | 12-18" apart | Peppers, small tomatoes |
| **5 gallon** | 18-24" apart | Tomatoes, eggplant |
| **10+ gallon** | 24-36" apart | Large tomatoes, squash |

**Arrangement Tips:**
- Taller plants on north side (don't shade others)
- Group by water needs
- Consider aesthetics (you'll see it daily)

#### Aquaponics Basics

**Simple Ratio:** 1 gallon fish tank per 1 square foot grow bed

```
BASIC AQUAPONICS LAYOUT

50-gallon fish tank
     │
     │ Pump (120 GPH)
     ▼
12 sq ft grow bed (e.g., 2'×6')
     │
     │ Drain (gravity)
     ▼
Return to fish tank

Components:
├─ Fish tank (50-100 gal to start)
├─ Grow bed with media (12-24 sq ft)
├─ Water pump (240-480 GPH)
├─ Plumbing (1/2" or 3/4" PVC)
└─ Bell siphon or constant flow
```

### 2.5 Common Design Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Too Big First Time** | Overwhelming, expensive, abandonment | Start 50% smaller than you think |
| **Poor Sun Location** | Low yields, leggy plants | Sun tracking before placement |
| **No Water Access** | Daily hassle, inconsistent watering | Plan irrigation or choose close spot |
| **Inadequate Drainage** | Root rot, system failure | Proper drainage holes/design |
| **Can't See from House** | Out of sight, out of mind neglect | Visible location = better care |
| **Too Far to Walk** | Won't visit daily | Close and convenient |
| **No Expansion Plan** | Boxed in when you want to grow | Leave room for future additions |
| **Ignoring Microclimates** | Fighting your environment | Work with your conditions |
