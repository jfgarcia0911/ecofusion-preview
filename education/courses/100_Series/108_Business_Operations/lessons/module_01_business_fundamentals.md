### 1.1 Is Farming Right for Your Business?

#### Reality Check: Farming as a Business

```
THE TRUTH ABOUT FARMING BUSINESSES

ADVANTAGES
✓ Growing market demand for local food
✓ Premium prices for quality produce
✓ Year-round production potential (CEA)
✓ Lower water and land requirements
✓ Sustainable and fulfilling work
✓ Direct customer relationships

CHALLENGES
⚠ High startup costs
⚠ Thin profit margins initially
⚠ Requires technical knowledge
⚠ Daily commitment (plants/fish need care)
⚠ Market development takes time
⚠ Competition from conventional farms
⚠ Seasonal demand fluctuations

HONEST ASSESSMENT:
    Not a "get rich quick" scheme
    Requires passion + business savvy
    Plan for 2-3 years to profitability
    Expect to reinvest early profits
```

#### Business Viability Questions

```
BEFORE YOU START - ASK YOURSELF:

PASSION & COMMITMENT
□ Am I willing to work 50-60+ hours/week initially?
□ Can I handle physical labor?
□ Am I comfortable with uncertainty?
□ Will I enjoy this in 5 years?

RESOURCES
□ Do I have startup capital ($10k-$100k+)?
□ Can I sustain 1-2 years with little income?
□ Do I have space/land access?
□ Can I access necessary utilities?

SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE
□ Do I understand growing systems?
□ Am I willing to learn business skills?
□ Can I market and sell effectively?
□ Do I have financial management skills?

MARKET
□ Is there demand in my area?
□ Can I differentiate from competitors?
□ Do I have access to customers?
□ Can I command premium prices?

If you answered "no" to many questions:
    → Start small (side business)
    → Partner with someone who complements your skills
    → Get more education/experience first
```

### 1.2 Business Models for Sustainable Farming

#### Scale-Based Models

| Model | Description | Investment | Revenue Potential | Best For |
|-------|-------------|------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Home Micro** | Hobby-scale, personal use + extras | $500-$5,000 | $0-$5,000/yr | Beginners, testing |
| **Side Business** | Part-time, supplemental income | $5,000-$25,000 | $5,000-$30,000/yr | Keep day job |
| **Market Garden** | Full-time small-scale | $25,000-$75,000 | $30,000-$75,000/yr | Local markets |
| **Commercial Small** | Full-time, employees | $75,000-$250,000 | $75,000-$250,000/yr | Restaurants, stores |
| **Commercial Large** | Multi-acre, full operation | $250,000-$1M+ | $250,000-$1M+/yr | Regional distribution |

#### Market-Based Models

```
BUSINESS MODEL OPTIONS

1. DIRECT-TO-CONSUMER (D2C)
   Revenue: Farmers markets, farm stand, CSA
   Pros: Higher margins, customer relationships
   Cons: Time intensive, limited volume
   Margins: 60-80%

2. RESTAURANT/CHEF SALES
   Revenue: High-end restaurants, catering
   Pros: Premium prices, consistent orders
   Cons: Demanding specs, may require delivery
   Margins: 50-70%

3. RETAIL/GROCERY
   Revenue: Local stores, co-ops
   Pros: Larger volume, steady orders
   Cons: Lower margins, strict requirements
   Margins: 30-50%

4. WHOLESALE/DISTRIBUTOR
   Revenue: Food distributors
   Pros: Highest volume, predictable
   Cons: Lowest margins, quality requirements
   Margins: 20-40%

5. VALUE-ADDED PRODUCTS
   Revenue: Processed goods (pesto, salad mixes)
   Pros: Higher margins, shelf stability
   Cons: Regulations, equipment needs
   Margins: 70-90%

6. AGRITOURISM/EDUCATION
   Revenue: Tours, classes, workshops
   Pros: Additional income stream
   Cons: Time commitment, liability
   Margins: 80-95%

BEST APPROACH: Diversified revenue streams
    Example: 40% D2C, 30% restaurant, 20% retail, 10% education
```

### 1.4 Business Structure and Legal Entities

#### Legal Structure Options

| Structure | Ownership | Liability | Taxes | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Individual | Unlimited personal | Personal return | Solo, testing concept |
| **Partnership** | 2+ individuals | Shared personal | Pass-through | Partners with trust |
| **LLC** | 1+ members | Limited | Flexible | Most small farms |
| **S-Corporation** | Shareholders | Limited | Pass-through | Growing operations |
| **C-Corporation** | Shareholders | Limited | Double taxation | Large operations |
| **Cooperative** | Members | Limited | Member level | Community farms |

```
CHOOSING YOUR STRUCTURE

START HERE: Most sustainable farms choose LLC

LLC Advantages:
    ✓ Personal asset protection
    ✓ Simple taxation (pass-through)
    ✓ Flexible management
    ✓ Professional credibility
    ✓ Relatively easy to form

Steps to Form LLC:
    1. Choose business name (check availability)
    2. File Articles of Organization (state)
    3. Create Operating Agreement
    4. Get EIN from IRS
    5. Register for state taxes
    6. Obtain business licenses
    7. Open business bank account

Cost: $100-$500 (varies by state)
Time: 2-4 weeks
```

INSURANCE

FOOD SAFETY

□ Organic certification (if claiming organic)

### 1.5 Essential Business Functions

#### The Business Operations Framework

```
CORE BUSINESS FUNCTIONS

PRODUCTION (40-50% of time)
    • Growing plants
    • Fish husbandry (aquaponics)
    • Harvesting
    • Quality control
    • System maintenance

SALES & MARKETING (20-30%)
    • Customer acquisition
    • Order management
    • Delivery/distribution
    • Customer service
    • Brand building

FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT (10-15%)
    • Bookkeeping
    • Invoicing
    • Expense tracking
    • Tax compliance
    • Financial planning

ADMINISTRATION (10-15%)
    • Record keeping
    • Compliance
    • Inventory management
    • Scheduling
    • Communications

STRATEGIC PLANNING (5-10%)
    • Business development
    • Product development
    • Process improvement
    • Long-term planning
```

#### Roles and Responsibilities

```
SMALL FARM STAFFING (Evolution)

PHASE 1: Solo Operator
    YOU do everything
    Time: 50-60+ hours/week
    Revenue: $0-$50k/year

PHASE 2: Part-time Help
    YOU: Production, sales, strategy
    PART-TIME: Harvest, markets
    Time: 40-50 hours/week
    Revenue: $50k-$100k/year

PHASE 3: Full-time Employee(s)
    YOU: Management, sales, strategy
    MANAGER: Production oversight
    STAFF: Labor, harvest, fulfillment
    Time: 40-50 hours/week (you)
    Revenue: $100k-$250k+/year

PHASE 4: Multiple Employees
    YOU: Strategy, key accounts
    MANAGER(S): Operations, production
    STAFF: Specialized roles
    Time: 30-40 hours/week (you)
    Revenue: $250k+/year
```

### 1.6 Creating Your Mission and Vision

#### Mission Statement Framework

```
MISSION STATEMENT COMPONENTS

What we do: ____________________
For whom: ____________________
How we're different: ____________________
Why it matters: ____________________

GOOD EXAMPLE:
    "We provide ultra-fresh, pesticide-free greens
    to health-conscious families and chefs through
    sustainable aquaponic farming, strengthening
    our local food system while protecting the
    environment."

BAD EXAMPLE:
    "We grow food."
    (Too vague, no differentiation)

YOUR MISSION:
    ______________________________________
    ______________________________________
    ______________________________________
```

#### Vision and Values

```
VISION STATEMENT
    Where you want to be in 5-10 years

Example:
    "To become the region's leading sustainable
    farm, serving 500+ families with year-round
    local produce while demonstrating that
    environmentally-responsible farming is
    economically viable."

CORE VALUES (Choose 3-5)
    □ Sustainability
    □ Quality
    □ Community
    □ Transparency
    □ Innovation
    □ Education
    □ Integrity
    □ Health
```
