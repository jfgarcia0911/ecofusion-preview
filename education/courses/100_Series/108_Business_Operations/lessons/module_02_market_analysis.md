## Lesson Content

### 2.1 Understanding Market Research

#### Why Market Research Matters

```
THE COST OF SKIPPING MARKET RESEARCH

Without Research:
    ❌ Grow what you like (not what sells)
    ❌ Price based on costs (not value)
    ❌ Assume customers will come
    ❌ Compete on price only
    ❌ High failure rate

With Research:
    ✓ Grow what customers want
    ✓ Price for profitable margins
    ✓ Target right customers
    ✓ Compete on differentiation
    ✓ Higher success rate

Investment: 20-40 hours of research
Return: Avoid costly mistakes, faster profitability
```

#### Market Size Calculation

```
MARKET SIZING EXAMPLE: LETTUCE

Step 1: Market Population
    Local area: 75,000 people
    Average household: 2.5 people
    = 30,000 households

Step 2: Target Segment
    Income $60k+: 40% = 12,000 households
    Interest in local: 30% = 3,600 households

Step 3: Purchase Behavior
    Buy lettuce weekly: 80% = 2,880 households
    Average heads/week: 2

Step 4: Total Potential Demand
    2,880 × 2 heads/week = 5,760 heads/week

Step 5: Realistic Market Share
    Year 1: 2% = 115 heads/week
    Year 3: 5% = 288 heads/week
    Year 5: 10% = 576 heads/week

Step 6: Revenue Projection
    Selling price: $4/head
    Year 1: 115 × $4 × 52 weeks = $23,920
    Year 5: 576 × $4 × 52 weeks = $119,808
```

### 2.5 Pricing Strategy

#### Pricing Approaches

```
PRICING METHODS

COST-PLUS PRICING
    Production cost per unit: $________
    + Desired margin (%): _____
    = Selling price: $________

    Example:
        Lettuce costs $1.50 to produce
        Want 60% margin
        $1.50 ÷ (1 - 0.60) = $3.75 price

VALUE-BASED PRICING
    What is it worth to customer?
    Based on:
        • Quality premium
        • Convenience
        • Local/sustainable value
        • Uniqueness

    Example:
        Store organic: $3.00
        Your superior quality: +$1.00
        Local premium: +$0.50
        = $4.50 price point

COMPETITIVE PRICING
    Competitor A: $4.00
    Competitor B: $3.50
    Your price: $4.25 (justify premium)

RECOMMENDED: Combination approach
    Check costs (must be profitable)
    Check value (customer willingness)
    Check competition (positioning)
    → Choose optimal price
```

### 2.6 Distribution Channel Analysis

#### Channel Options

```
DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL EVALUATION

FARMERS MARKETS
    Pros:
        ✓ Direct customer contact
        ✓ Test products/pricing
        ✓ Build brand awareness
        ✓ Immediate cash
    Cons:
        ⚠ Time intensive (full day)
        ⚠ Weather dependent
        ⚠ Seasonal in many areas
        ⚠ Limited volume
    Margins: 70-80%

CSA (Community Supported Agriculture)
    Pros:
        ✓ Upfront payment
        ✓ Predictable demand
        ✓ Customer loyalty
        ✓ Reduced marketing
    Cons:
        ⚠ Variety requirements
        ⚠ Customer management
        ⚠ Distribution logistics
        ⚠ Seasonal model
    Margins: 60-70%

RESTAURANT DIRECT
    Pros:
        ✓ Larger orders
        ✓ Consistent schedule
        ✓ Premium pricing
        ✓ Prestige/marketing
    Cons:
        ⚠ Exacting standards
        ⚠ Delivery required
        ⚠ Payment terms (30 days)
        ⚠ Relationship dependent
    Margins: 50-65%

RETAIL/GROCERY
    Pros:
        ✓ High volume potential
        ✓ Steady orders
        ✓ Professional relationship
    Cons:
        ⚠ Lower margins
        ⚠ Strict requirements
        ⚠ Liability insurance needed
        ⚠ Packaging/labeling costs
    Margins: 35-50%

WHOLESALE DISTRIBUTOR
    Pros:
        ✓ Highest volume
        ✓ Single customer (simple)
        ✓ Predictable orders
    Cons:
        ⚠ Lowest margins
        ⚠ Quality requirements
        ⚠ May require GAP cert
        ⚠ Price pressure
    Margins: 25-40%
```
