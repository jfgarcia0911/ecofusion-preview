# Quiz 08: Troubleshooting Water Quality Issues

**Course:** 112 - Water Chemistry Foundations
**Module:** 08 - Troubleshooting Water Quality Issues
**Duration:** 20 minutes
**Passing Score:** 80% (8/10 correct)

## Instructions
Select the best answer for each question. Only one answer is correct per question.

---

## Questions

### Question 1
What is the first step in troubleshooting water quality issues?
- a) Add chemicals immediately
- b) Test all relevant water parameters
- c) Remove all fish
- d) Drain the system

### Question 2
If ammonia levels are high in an established system, what is a likely cause?
- a) Too much aeration
- b) Overfeeding, overstocking, or inadequate biofiltration
- c) Too many plants
- d) Excessive light

### Question 3
What should you do if you detect a nitrite spike?
- a) Ignore it
- b) Reduce feeding, increase aeration, and consider partial water changes
- c) Add more fish
- d) Turn off all equipment

### Question 4
What can cause a sudden pH drop?
- a) Too much alkalinity
- b) Insufficient buffering capacity or excessive nitrification
- c) Too few fish
- d) Underfeeding

### Question 5
If plants show signs of nutrient deficiency despite adequate fish feeding, what might be the issue?
- a) Too much light
- b) pH is out of range affecting nutrient availability
- c) Too much oxygen
- d) Water is too clean

### Question 6
What indicates inadequate dissolved oxygen?
- a) Fish are active and feeding well
- b) Fish gasping at the surface and lethargy
- c) Plants growing rapidly
- d) Water is perfectly clear

### Question 7
How can you address cloudy or turbid water?
- a) Add more food
- b) Improve mechanical filtration and reduce organic load
- c) Stop all water flow
- d) Add soil to the system

### Question 8
What might cause algae blooms in your system?
- a) Complete darkness
- b) Excess nutrients combined with light exposure
- c) No water present
- d) Extremely low temperatures

### Question 9
If fish show signs of stress but water parameters test normal, what should you investigate?
- a) Nothing, if tests are normal
- b) Temperature fluctuations, dissolved oxygen levels, or disease
- c) Only the fish color
- d) The time of day

### Question 10
What is the best approach to preventing water quality problems?
- a) Reactive management after problems occur
- b) Proactive monitoring, regular maintenance, and gradual adjustments
- c) Never testing water
- d) Changing all water daily

---

## Answer Key

1. **b** - Test all relevant water parameters
2. **b** - Overfeeding, overstocking, or inadequate biofiltration
3. **b** - Reduce feeding, increase aeration, and consider partial water changes
4. **b** - Insufficient buffering capacity or excessive nitrification
5. **b** - pH is out of range affecting nutrient availability
6. **b** - Fish gasping at the surface and lethargy
7. **b** - Improve mechanical filtration and reduce organic load
8. **b** - Excess nutrients combined with light exposure
9. **b** - Temperature fluctuations, dissolved oxygen levels, or disease
10. **b** - Proactive monitoring, regular maintenance, and gradual adjustments
