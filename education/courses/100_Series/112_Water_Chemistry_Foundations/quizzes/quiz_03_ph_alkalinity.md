**Course:** 112 - Water Chemistry Foundations
**Module:** 03 - pH and Alkalinity Management
**Duration:** 20 minutes

## Instructions
Select the best answer for each question. Only one answer is correct per question.

## Questions

### Question 1
What is the optimal pH range for most aquaponic systems?
- a) 4.0-5.0
- b) 6.0-7.0
- c) 8.0-9.0
- d) 9.0-10.0

### Question 2
Why is alkalinity important in aquaculture systems?
- a) It changes water color
- b) It buffers against rapid pH fluctuations
- c) It increases water temperature
- d) It has no importance

### Question 3
What happens to pH during nitrification?
- a) pH increases
- b) pH stays exactly the same
- c) pH decreases (becomes more acidic)
- d) pH becomes neutral

### Question 4
Which substance can be used to raise pH in aquaculture systems?
- a) Vinegar
- b) Lemon juice
- c) Potassium hydroxide or calcium carbonate
- d) Salt

### Question 5
What can cause a pH crash in an aquaponic system?
- a) Too much aeration
- b) Insufficient alkalinity to buffer against acid production
- c) Too many plants
- d) Excessive lighting

### Question 6
How does high pH affect ammonia toxicity?
- a) High pH makes ammonia less toxic
- b) High pH increases the toxic form of ammonia (NH3)
- c) pH has no effect on ammonia toxicity
- d) High pH removes all ammonia

### Question 7
What is the relationship between pH and nutrient availability?
- a) pH has no effect on nutrients
- b) pH affects the solubility and availability of nutrients
- c) Only temperature affects nutrients
- d) Nutrients control pH exclusively

### Question 8
Which method is best for adjusting pH?
- a) Making large sudden changes
- b) Making gradual adjustments over time
- c) Changing pH daily
- d) Ignoring pH fluctuations

### Question 9
What unit is alkalinity typically measured in?
- a) Degrees Fahrenheit
- b) Milligrams per liter (mg/L) or ppm as CaCO3
- c) Volts
- d) Watts

### Question 10
Why should pH be monitored regularly?
- a) To waste time
- b) To ensure optimal conditions for fish, plants, and bacteria
- c) To increase costs
- d) pH never changes so monitoring is unnecessary

---

## Answer Key

1. **b** - 6.0-7.0
2. **b** - It buffers against rapid pH fluctuations
3. **c** - pH decreases (becomes more acidic)
4. **c** - Potassium hydroxide or calcium carbonate
5. **b** - Insufficient alkalinity to buffer against acid production
6. **b** - High pH increases the toxic form of ammonia (NH3)
7. **b** - pH affects the solubility and availability of nutrients
8. **b** - Making gradual adjustments over time
9. **b** - Milligrams per liter (mg/L) or ppm as CaCO3
10. **b** - To ensure optimal conditions for fish, plants, and bacteria
