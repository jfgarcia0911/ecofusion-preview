### 1.2 The Water Molecule

#### Molecular Structure

```
WATER MOLECULE (H₂O)

        H
        │
        │ 104.5° angle
    H ──O
        ↑
    Oxygen atom
    (negative pole)

Hydrogen atoms
(positive poles)

POLAR MOLECULE
   δ+ means partial positive charge
   δ- means partial negative charge

        δ+
        H
        │
    δ+ H─O δ-

This polarity makes water a
UNIVERSAL SOLVENT
```

**Key Properties from Molecular Structure:**

| Property | Cause | Importance |
|----------|-------|------------|
| **Polarity** | Uneven electron distribution | Dissolves ionic compounds |
| **Hydrogen bonding** | Attraction between molecules | Surface tension, cohesion |
| **High specific heat** | Strong molecular bonds | Temperature stability |
| **High heat of vaporization** | Energy to break bonds | Cooling effect |
| **Density anomaly** | Ice less dense than liquid | Ice floats (protects aquatic life) |

### 1.3 Water as a Solvent

#### Dissolution Process

```
HOW WATER DISSOLVES SALT (NaCl)

SOLID SALT CRYSTAL           DISSOLVED IN WATER
       ↓                           ↓
┌─────────────┐           ┌─────────────────┐
│  Na+ Cl-    │           │  H₂O H₂O        │
│  Cl- Na+    │    →→→    │   │   │         │
│  Na+ Cl-    │           │  Na+ Cl-        │
│  Cl- Na+    │           │ (hydrated ions) │
└─────────────┘           └─────────────────┘

Water molecules surround and separate ions
    ↓
  δ-O attracts Na+ (positive ion)
  δ+H attracts Cl- (negative ion)
    ↓
Ions become HYDRATED (surrounded by water)
```

**What Dissolves in Water:**

**Readily Soluble:**
- Ionic compounds (salts): NaCl, KNO₃, CaCl₂
- Polar molecules: Sugars, alcohols
- Acids and bases: HCl, NaOH
- Many gases: O₂, CO₂, NH₃

**Poorly Soluble:**
- Nonpolar molecules: Oils, fats
- Some minerals: CaCO₃ (limestone)
- Some gases: N₂ (nitrogen)

#### Solutions, Suspensions, and Colloids

```
TYPES OF MIXTURES IN WATER

SOLUTION (True solution)
• Dissolved particles <1 nm
• Homogeneous
• Clear (light passes through)
• Example: Salt water, nutrient solution

COLLOID
• Particles 1-1000 nm
• Homogeneous appearance
• Tyndall effect (scatters light)
• Example: Milk, some organic matter

SUSPENSION
• Particles >1000 nm
• Heterogeneous
• Settles over time
• Example: Muddy water, algae bloom
```

### 1.4 Basic Chemical Concepts

#### Concentration and Units

```
CONCENTRATION EXPRESSIONS

PPM (Parts Per Million)
1 ppm = 1 mg/L (for water)
       = 1 g/1,000 L
       = 0.0001%

Example: 5 ppm nitrate = 5 mg of NO₃ per liter of water

PERCENTAGE (%)
1% = 10,000 ppm
   = 10 g/L
   = 1 g/100 mL

MOLARITY (M)
Moles of solute per liter of solution
Used in chemistry, less common in aquaculture/hydroponics
```

**Common Concentration Conversions:**

| Unit | Equivalent | Example Use |
|------|------------|-------------|
| **mg/L** | = ppm (for water) | Dissolved oxygen, nutrients |
| **g/L** | = 1,000 ppm | Fertilizer concentration |
| **%** | = 10,000 ppm = 10 g/L | Stock solutions |
| **ppb** | = 0.001 ppm | Trace elements, contaminants |

#### Ions and Ionic Compounds

```
IONS IN WATER

CATIONS (Positive Ions)         ANIONS (Negative Ions)
    ↓                                  ↓
Calcium    Ca²⁺                 Nitrate      NO₃⁻
Magnesium  Mg²⁺                 Phosphate    PO₄³⁻
Potassium  K⁺                   Sulfate      SO₄²⁻
Sodium     Na⁺                  Chloride     Cl⁻
Ammonium   NH₄⁺                 Bicarbonate  HCO₃⁻
Iron       Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺            Hydroxide    OH⁻

NUTRIENT SALTS dissolve to release ions:
Calcium nitrate: Ca(NO₃)₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻
Potassium sulfate: K₂SO₄ → 2K⁺ + SO₄²⁻
```

**Important Ion Concepts:**

| Concept | Explanation | Importance |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Charge balance** | Total positive = Total negative | Water remains electrically neutral |
| **Ion pairing** | Opposite ions attract | Affects availability, solubility |
| **Hydration** | Water surrounds ions | Keeps ions separated, dissolved |
| **Valence** | Number of charges | Determines combining ratios |

### 1.5 Chemical Reactions in Water

#### Common Reaction Types

**1. Acid-Base Reactions**
```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water

Example:
HNO₃ + KOH → KNO₃ + H₂O
(Nitric acid + Potassium hydroxide → Potassium nitrate + Water)

Used for pH adjustment in growing systems
```

**2. Precipitation Reactions**
```
Two soluble salts → Insoluble precipitate

Example:
Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃ ↓
(Calcium + Carbonate → Calcium carbonate precipitate)

Can cause scaling in pipes, reduce nutrient availability
```

**3. Redox (Oxidation-Reduction) Reactions**
```
Electron transfer between substances

Example (Nitrification):
NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻
(Ammonium oxidized to nitrite, then to nitrate)

Critical for biological filtration in aquaponics
```

### 1.6 Water Quality Parameters Overview

#### The "Big Six" Water Parameters

```
ESSENTIAL WATER QUALITY PARAMETERS

1. TEMPERATURE (°F or °C)
   Affects: Everything
   Range: Species-dependent

2. pH (0-14 scale)
   Affects: Nutrient availability, toxicity
   Range: 6.0-7.5 (most systems)

3. DISSOLVED OXYGEN (mg/L or ppm)
   Affects: Fish/root respiration
   Range: >5 mg/L for fish

4. AMMONIA (NH₃/NH₄⁺, mg/L)
   Affects: Toxicity to fish
   Range: <1 mg/L, ideally <0.5

5. NITRITE (NO₂⁻, mg/L)
   Affects: Fish toxicity
   Range: <0.5 mg/L, ideally 0

6. NITRATE (NO₃⁻, mg/L)
   Affects: Plant nutrition, fish (high levels)
   Range: 5-150 mg/L depending on system
```

**Interconnections:**

```
PARAMETER INTERACTIONS

TEMPERATURE
    ↓
Affects all chemical/biological rates
    ↓
┌───────────┬───────────┬────────────┐
│           │           │            │
↓           ↓           ↓            ↓
pH      Dissolved   Ammonia    Bacterial
change   Oxygen    toxicity    activity
         (↓ as      (↑ as       (↑ as
         temp ↑)   temp ↑)    temp ↑)

Everything is connected!
```

### 1.7 Source Water Considerations

#### Types of Water Sources

| Source | Typical Characteristics | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------------------------|------|------|
| **Municipal (Tap)** | Treated, chlorinated, variable chemistry | Reliable, safe for humans | Chlorine/chloramine, variable quality |
| **Well** | Groundwater, mineral content varies | Often good quality, no chlorine | May have high minerals, testing needed |
| **Surface (Pond/River)** | Variable, organic matter, contaminants | Free or low cost | Requires treatment, variable |
| **Rainwater** | Very pure, low minerals | Low EC, no chlorine | Requires storage, filtration |
| **RO (Reverse Osmosis)** | Ultra-pure, all minerals removed | Consistent baseline | Expensive, minerals must be added |

#### Initial Water Analysis

```
BASELINE WATER TESTING CHECKLIST

BEFORE SYSTEM STARTUP:
□ pH
□ EC/TDS (total dissolved solids)
□ Hardness (calcium, magnesium)
□ Alkalinity
□ Chlorine/Chloramine
□ Heavy metals (if concerned)
□ Nitrate (if present)
□ Iron
□ Sulfur (if smells)

WHY BASELINE MATTERS:
• Know what you're starting with
• Identify potential problems early
• Understand amendments needed
• Track changes over time
• Troubleshoot issues
```

### 1.8 The Nitrogen Cycle (Introduction)

#### Foundation for Aquaponics

```
THE NITROGEN CYCLE (Simplified)

Fish waste          Beneficial Bacteria      Plant food
    ↓                      ↓                     ↓
┌─────────┐         ┌──────────┐          ┌──────────┐
│Ammonia  │ ──────► │ Nitrite  │ ───────► │ Nitrate  │
│ (NH₃/   │ Nitroso-│  (NO₂⁻)  │ Nitro-  │  (NO₃⁻)  │
│  NH₄⁺)  │ monas   │          │ bacter  │          │
└─────────┘         └──────────┘          └──────────┘
   TOXIC                TOXIC              NUTRIENT
   to fish              to fish            for plants

This process is THE FOUNDATION of aquaponics
```

**Why This Matters:**

| Stage | Form | Fish Impact | Plant Impact | Management Goal |
|-------|------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Ammonia** | NH₃/NH₄⁺ | Highly toxic | Can use small amounts | Keep <1 ppm |
| **Nitrite** | NO₂⁻ | Toxic | Not used | Keep <0.5 ppm |
| **Nitrate** | NO₃⁻ | Low toxicity (high conc.) | Primary N source | 5-150 ppm |

### 1.9 Water Chemistry and System Health

#### Effects on Fish

```
WATER CHEMISTRY → FISH HEALTH

OPTIMAL CHEMISTRY          POOR CHEMISTRY
       ↓                         ↓
┌─────────────┐           ┌─────────────┐
│• Normal     │           │• Stress     │
│  respiration│           │• Disease    │
│• Good       │           │  suscept-   │
│  appetite   │           │  ibility ↑  │
│• Active     │           │• Mortality  │
│  swimming   │           │• Poor       │
│• Growth     │           │  growth     │
└─────────────┘           └─────────────┘
```

**Fish Stress Indicators from Water Chemistry:**
- Gasping at surface (low O₂)
- Lethargy (ammonia, nitrite, pH extremes)
- Clamped fins (stress)
- Red gills (ammonia/nitrite)
- Erratic swimming (pH shock, toxins)

#### Effects on Plants

```
WATER CHEMISTRY → PLANT HEALTH

OPTIMAL CHEMISTRY          POOR CHEMISTRY
       ↓                         ↓
┌─────────────┐           ┌─────────────┐
│• Nutrient   │           │• Nutrient   │
│  available  │           │  lockout    │
│• Vigorous   │           │• Chlorosis  │
│  growth     │           │  (yellowing)│
│• Dark green │           │• Stunting   │
│  leaves     │           │• Tip burn   │
│• Good yield │           │• Wilting    │
└─────────────┘           └─────────────┘
```

**Plant Problems from Water Chemistry:**
- Yellowing leaves (nitrogen deficiency, wrong pH)
- Tip burn (excess salts, EC too high)
- Stunted growth (nutrient imbalance)
- Purple leaves (phosphorus deficiency)
- Interveinal chlorosis (iron deficiency at high pH)

## Vocabulary
