### 4.1 What is Dissolved Oxygen (DO)?

```
DISSOLVED OXYGEN (DO)

Oxygen gas (O₂) dissolved in water

AIR                          WATER
21% oxygen       →        Dissolved O₂
(Gas)                      (Aqueous)

┌────────────┐            ┌────────────┐
│  O₂ O₂     │            │  O₂        │
│  O₂ O₂     │   →→→→→   │      O₂    │
│  O₂ O₂     │            │  O₂        │
└────────────┘            └────────────┘
Atmospheric               Dissolved
(Abundant)                (Limited)

DO is measured in:
• mg/L (milligrams per liter)
• ppm (parts per million) - same as mg/L
• % saturation (relative to maximum)
```

**Why DO Matters:**
- **Fish respiration** - Extract O₂ through gills
- **Root respiration** - Roots need O₂ for energy
- **Beneficial bacteria** - Aerobic bacteria need O₂
- **Organic decomposition** - Breakdown of waste
- **Chemical reactions** - Oxidation processes

### 4.2 Oxygen Solubility and Temperature

#### The Inverse Relationship

```
TEMPERATURE vs DISSOLVED OXYGEN

As temperature ↑, dissolved oxygen ↓

COLD WATER (50°F / 10°C)     WARM WATER (85°F / 29°C)
     ↓                             ↓
Can hold ~11 mg/L               Can hold ~7 mg/L
     ↓                             ↓
More oxygen                     Less oxygen
available                       available

┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Temperature-DO Relationship          │
│                                      │
│ 12 mg/L│         ▓▓▓                 │
│        │      ▓▓▓                    │
│ 10mg/L │   ▓▓▓                       │
│        │▓▓▓                          │
│  8mg/L │                       ▓▓▓   │
│        │                    ▓▓▓      │
│  6mg/L │                 ▓▓▓         │
│        └─────────────────────────────│
│        40°F    60°F    80°F   100°F  │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Saturation Table (at sea level, freshwater):**

| Temp (°F) | Temp (°C) | 100% Saturation (mg/L) |
|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| 50 | 10 | 11.3 |
| 59 | 15 | 10.1 |
| 68 | 20 | 9.1 |
| 77 | 25 | 8.2 |
| 86 | 30 | 7.5 |
| 95 | 35 | 6.9 |

**Critical Point:** In summer (warm water), oxygen is LEAST available when organisms need it MOST (higher metabolic rates).

### 4.3 Factors Affecting Dissolved Oxygen

#### Major Factors

```
FACTORS INFLUENCING DO LEVELS

INCREASE DO                 DECREASE DO
     ↓                           ↓
┌─────────────┐           ┌─────────────┐
│• Cool temp  │           │• Warm temp  │
│• Aeration   │           │• Stagnation │
│• Water flow │           │• Overcrowding│
│• Plants     │           │• Waste      │
│  (daytime)  │           │  accumulation│
│• Low bioload│           │• Algae death│
│• Altitude   │           │• High salt  │
│  (low)      │           │  (salinity) │
└─────────────┘           └─────────────┘
```

**Factor Details:**

| Factor | Effect on DO | Explanation |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | ↑Temp = ↓DO | Solubility decreases |
| **Salinity** | ↑Salt = ↓DO | Saltwater holds less O₂ |
| **Altitude** | ↑Altitude = ↓DO | Lower atmospheric pressure |
| **Bioload** | ↑Fish/waste = ↓DO | More O₂ consumption |
| **Photosynthesis** | Plants ADD O₂ (day) | Releases O₂ during day |
| **Respiration** | All organisms USE O₂ | 24/7 consumption |
| **Aeration** | Increases DO | Gas exchange at surface |
| **Organic matter** | Decomposition USES O₂ | Bacterial breakdown |

### 4.4 Oxygen Requirements

#### For Fish

```
FISH DO REQUIREMENTS (mg/L)

CRITICAL   STRESSED   ADEQUATE   OPTIMAL   EXCELLENT
   2          4          5          7          9+

TILAPIA    ├──────────●────────────────────┤
           Tolerant   Prefer    Good growth

TROUT                         ├────────●────┤
                          Need    Thrive
                          high DO

CATFISH    ├─────────────●─────────────────┤
           Tolerant      Prefer

BASS               ├──────────●─────────────┤
                  Need      Optimal
                  5+ mg/L
```

**Species-Specific Needs:**

| Species | Minimum DO | Optimal DO | Notes |
|---------|------------|------------|-------|
| **Tilapia** | 3 mg/L | 5-7 mg/L | Most tolerant warm-water fish |
| **Catfish** | 4 mg/L | 5-8 mg/L | Tolerant, but growth suffers <5 |
| **Trout** | 6 mg/L | 7-10 mg/L | High DO needed, cold water |
| **Bass** | 5 mg/L | 6-9 mg/L | Moderate requirements |
| **Carp** | 3 mg/L | 5-7 mg/L | Very tolerant |

**Signs of Low DO in Fish:**
- Gasping at surface
- Gulping air
- Gathering near aerator/waterfall
- Reduced feeding
- Lethargy
- Increased disease

#### For Plants (Roots)

```
ROOT OXYGEN NEEDS

SOIL GROWING               HYDROPONICS
     ↓                          ↓
Oxygen from air            Must dissolve
in soil pores              in water
     ↓                          ↓
Less critical              VERY CRITICAL

MINIMUM: 3-4 mg/L for root health
OPTIMAL: 6-8 mg/L for best growth

LOW DO SYMPTOMS IN PLANTS:
• Wilting (despite wet media)
• Slow growth
• Root rot (pythium)
• Yellowing leaves
• Reduced nutrient uptake
```

### 4.5 Measuring Dissolved Oxygen

#### Testing Methods

**1. Chemical Test Kits (Winkler Method)**
```
ADVANTAGES               DISADVANTAGES
• Accurate (±0.2 mg/L)  • Time-consuming
• No calibration        • Multiple steps
• Inexpensive           • Chemical reagents
• Good for occasional   • 10-15 minutes per test
  testing
```

**2. Electronic DO Meters**
```
ADVANTAGES               DISADVANTAGES
• Fast (instant)        • Expensive ($$$)
• Easy to read          • Requires calibration
• Very accurate         • Membrane maintenance
  (±0.1 mg/L)          • Battery/power needed
• Best for frequent
  monitoring
```

**3. Optical DO Sensors**
```
ADVANTAGES               DISADVANTAGES
• No membrane           • Most expensive
• Low maintenance       • Technology dependent
• Very accurate         • May drift over time
• Long-term monitoring
```

#### Proper DO Meter Use

```
DO METER PROCEDURE

BEFORE MEASURING:
1. Calibrate meter
   • 100% air saturation method, OR
   • 0% and 100% two-point calibration
   • Calibrate weekly-monthly

2. Check probe
   • Membrane intact (if applicable)
   • No air bubbles
   • Electrolyte level (if applicable)

DURING MEASUREMENT:
3. Collect sample or measure in situ
   • Representative location
   • Avoid surface (not representative)

4. Move probe gently
   • Stirring action needed
   • Don't create bubbles

5. Wait for stable reading
   • 30-60 seconds
   • Note temperature (auto or manual)

6. Record both:
   • DO concentration (mg/L)
   • Temperature (°F or °C)
```

#### Passive Aeration

```
LOW-ENERGY OXYGENATION

SURFACE AGITATION        WATERFALLS
       ↓                      ↓
┌─────────────┐         ┌─────────────┐
│  ~ ~ ~ ~    │         │    ╱╲       │
│  ~ ~ ~ ~    │         │   ╱  ╲      │
│  Waters     │         │  ╱    ╲     │
│  surface    │         │ ╱Water ╲    │
│  movement   │         │╱  falls ╲   │
└─────────────┘         └─────────────┘
Gas exchange at         Maximum surface
surface area           area contact

TRICKLE FILTERS         VENTURI
       ↓                      ↓
Media with water        Air sucked into
trickling through       water flow
```

**Passive Method Comparison:**

| Method | O₂ Addition | Cost | Use Case |
|--------|-------------|------|----------|
| **Surface agitation** | Low | $ | Small systems |
| **Waterfall** | Moderate | $$ | Medium systems, dual purpose |
| **Trickle filter** | Moderate-High | $$$ | Biological + aeration |
| **Venturi** | Moderate | $$ | Inline with pump |

#### Active Aeration

```
HIGH-ENERGY OXYGENATION

AIR STONES                   AIR DIFFUSERS
     ↓                            ↓
┌──────────┐                ┌──────────┐
│  ○○○○    │                │ ~ ~ ~ ~  │
│  ○○○○    │                │ ~~~~~~~~ │
│  ○○○○    │                │ Fine     │
│  [▓▓▓]   │                │ bubbles  │
│  Stone   │                │ [══════] │
└──────────┘                │ Diffuser │
Coarse bubbles              └──────────┘
Large bubbles               More efficient

PURE OXYGEN (O₂)            PADDLEWHEEL
       ↓                          ↓
Injected directly           Surface splashing
95-99% pure oxygen          + water circulation
Most efficient              Pond aeration
```

**Active Method Comparison:**

| Method | Efficiency | Energy Use | Cost | Best For |
|--------|-----------|------------|------|----------|
| **Air stones** | Low | Moderate | $ | Small tanks |
| **Air diffusers** | Moderate | Moderate | $$ | Medium tanks |
| **Blowers** | Moderate | High | $$$ | Large systems |
| **Pure O₂** | Very High | Low | $$$$ | Intensive RAS |
| **Paddlewheel** | Moderate | High | $$$ | Ponds |

### 4.8 Temperature Management

#### Temperature Effects

```
TEMPERATURE IMPACTS

METABOLIC RATE              OXYGEN SOLUBILITY
      ↓                             ↓
Warmer = Faster            Warmer = Less DO
      ↓                             ↓
┌──────────────┐           ┌──────────────┐
│• Fish eat    │           │• Less O₂     │
│  more        │           │  available   │
│• Grow faster │           │• Fish need   │
│• Need more   │           │  MORE        │
│  O₂          │           │• Supply-     │
│• Bacteria    │           │  demand      │
│  more active │           │  mismatch    │
└──────────────┘           └──────────────┘
       ↓                           ↓
        SUMMER = HIGHEST RISK
```

**Safe Temperature Ranges:**

| System | Optimal Temp | Critical Concern |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| **Warm-water fish** | 75-85°F (24-29°C) | DO decreases above 85°F |
| **Cool-water fish** | 65-75°F (18-24°C) | Stress above 75°F |
| **Cold-water fish** | 50-65°F (10-18°C) | Mortality above 70°F |
| **Hydroponics** | 65-75°F (18-24°C) | Root rot risk >75°F |

### 4.9 Troubleshooting DO Problems

```
PROBLEM: Low Dissolved Oxygen

SYMPTOMS:
• Fish gasping at surface
• Gathering at aerator
• Reduced feeding
• Plant wilting
• Slow growth

CAUSES:
❌ Insufficient aeration
❌ High temperature
❌ Overcrowding
❌ Excess organic matter
❌ Equipment failure
❌ Algae die-off

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. Increase aeration (emergency aerator)
2. Reduce feeding (less waste/consumption)
3. Partial water change (add O₂-rich water)
4. Cool water if possible (shade, ice)
5. Check for equipment failures

LONG-TERM SOLUTIONS:
□ Upgrade aeration system
□ Reduce stocking density
□ Improve water flow
□ Add backup aerator + alarm
□ Install chiller if chronic heat issue
□ Regular maintenance schedule
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Dissolved Oxygen (DO)** | Amount of oxygen gas dissolved in water (mg/L) |
| **Saturation** | Maximum DO water can hold at given temp/pressure |
| **Aeration** | Process of adding oxygen to water |
| **Bioload** | Total biomass of organisms consuming oxygen |
| **Aerobic** | Requiring oxygen (organisms, processes) |
| **Anaerobic** | Not requiring oxygen; can be toxic |
| **Hypoxia** | Low oxygen condition |
| **Gas Exchange** | Movement of gases (O₂, CO₂) across water surface |
