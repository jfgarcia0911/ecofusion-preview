## Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary purpose of ventilation in grow spaces?

a) Make noise
b) Exchange air, control temperature/humidity, provide fresh CO₂ ✓
c) Just for looks
d) Ventilation has no purpose

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Ventilation brings in fresh air (CO₂), removes excess heat and humidity, and exchanges stale air for optimal plant health.*

---

### Question 2
What is air exchange rate?

a) How often grow space air is completely replaced ✓
b) Cost of electricity
c) Fan speed setting
d) Temperature change rate

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: Air exchange rate (e.g., 1 exchange per minute) indicates how frequently the entire room volume is replaced with fresh air.*

---

### Question 3
What is the difference between active and passive ventilation?

a) There is no difference
b) Active uses fans; passive uses natural air movement ✓
c) Active is always worse
d) Passive requires electricity

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Active ventilation uses exhaust fans to force air exchange. Passive relies on natural convection and vents—slower and less controllable.*

---

### Question 4
Where should exhaust fans be located?

a) At floor level
b) Near the ceiling where hot air accumulates ✓
c) Outside the building
d) Location doesn't matter

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Hot air rises, so exhaust fans near the ceiling effectively remove warm, humid air. Intake vents are typically lower, drawing cooler air.*

---

### Question 5
What is horizontal airflow (HAF)?

a) Fans that blow air sideways across canopy ✓
b) Vertical air movement only
c) Outside ventilation
d) Air conditioning

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: HAF fans circulate air horizontally at canopy level, preventing stagnant air pockets, strengthening stems, and distributing CO₂ and temperature evenly.*

---

### Question 6
Why is air circulation important even without ventilation?

a) It's not important
b) Prevents mold, strengthens plants, distributes CO₂ ✓
c) Only for cooling
d) To annoy plants

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Constant gentle air movement prevents humidity pockets (disease), strengthens stems (thigmomorphogenesis), and ensures even CO₂/temperature distribution.*

---

### Question 7
What is a negative pressure environment?

a) Bad vibes in the grow room
b) Exhaust exceeds intake, drawing air in through passive vents ✓
c) Positive air pressure
d) No air movement

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Negative pressure (exhaust > intake) prevents uncontrolled air/odor/pest escape. Air is pulled in through filtered intakes, exits through exhaust.*

---

### Question 8
What should intake air pass through?

a) Nothing—bring air straight in
b) Filters to remove pests, dust, pathogens ✓
c) Heaters only
d) Plants first

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Filtering intake air (HEPA, MERV filters) prevents introduction of pests, mold spores, dust, and pathogens into the controlled environment.*

---

### Question 9
How much air movement should plants experience?

a) Hurricane-force winds
b) Gentle breeze that gently moves leaves ✓
c) Completely still air always
d) Tornadic conditions

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Gentle air movement (leaves slightly rustling) is ideal. Too strong damages plants; too little causes stagnant air and disease.*

---

### Question 10
When is sealed environment with recirculation preferred?

a) Never seal rooms
b) When using CO₂ enrichment to prevent waste ✓
c) In all situations always
d) Only in winter

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Sealed rooms with recirculating air conditioning allow CO₂ enrichment without waste. Dehumidification handles moisture without venting enriched air.*
