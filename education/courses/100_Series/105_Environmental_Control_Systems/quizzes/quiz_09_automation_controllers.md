## Questions

### Question 1
What is an environmental controller?

a) Person who manages the facility
b) Device that automates environmental systems based on sensor inputs ✓
c) Temperature-only thermostat
d) Light switch

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Environmental controllers integrate multiple sensors and control outputs (HVAC, lights, CO₂, dehumidifiers) based on programmed setpoints and logic.*

---

### Question 2
What is a setpoint?

a) Location where you set equipment
b) Target value for a parameter (e.g., 72°F) ✓
c) Electrical outlet
d) Plant spacing

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: A setpoint is the target value for environmental parameters. Controllers activate equipment to maintain conditions at or near setpoints.*

---

### Question 3
What is hysteresis (deadband)?

a) Historical data analysis
b) Range around setpoint before controller acts to prevent rapid cycling ✓
c) Broken controller
d) Type of sensor

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Hysteresis prevents equipment from rapidly turning on/off. E.g., heat at 68°F, turn off at 72°F, preventing constant cycling at 70°F setpoint.*

---

### Question 4
What can be automated in grow facilities?

a) Only irrigation
b) Lights, HVAC, irrigation, CO₂, nutrient dosing, curtains ✓
c) Nothing should be automated
d) Only temperature

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Modern controllers can automate lighting schedules, climate control, irrigation/fertigation, CO₂ injection, shade curtains, and more.*

---

### Question 5
What is the benefit of automation?

a) No benefits
b) Consistency, labor savings, optimized conditions, remote control ✓
c) Makes growing harder
d) Only increases costs

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Automation provides precise control, reduces labor, maintains consistent conditions 24/7, and allows remote management and alerts.*

---

### Question 6
What is a timer used for?

a) Telling time only
b) Controlling equipment on scheduled intervals (lights, irrigation) ✓
c) Measuring plant age
d) No use in growing

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Timers automate lighting photoperiods, irrigation cycles, and other time-based operations, ensuring consistency without manual intervention.*

---

### Question 7
What is the difference between analog and digital controllers?

a) No difference
b) Analog uses dials/simple circuits; digital uses microprocessors and programming ✓
c) Analog is always better
d) Digital doesn't work

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Analog controllers (thermostats, timers) are simple and cheap. Digital controllers offer multi-parameter control, programming, data logging, and connectivity.*

---

### Question 8
What does PID control stand for?

a) Plant Identification Database
b) Proportional-Integral-Derivative control algorithm ✓
c) Pest Insect Detection
d) Programmable Irrigation Device

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: PID is an advanced control algorithm that adjusts output based on error magnitude (P), duration (I), and rate of change (D) for precise control.*

---

### Question 9
What is IoT in agriculture?

a) Inside Or Tractor
b) Internet of Things—connected sensors and devices ✓
c) Irrigation Optimization Tool
d) It's Okay Though

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: IoT connects sensors, controllers, and devices to networks, enabling remote monitoring, data analytics, and integration with farm management software.*

---

### Question 10
Why have manual overrides on automated systems?

a) To disable useful automation
b) For emergency control and maintenance access ✓
c) Manual control is always better
d) Overrides serve no purpose

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Manual overrides allow emergency intervention (fire, equipment malfunction), maintenance, and testing without reprogramming the entire automation system.*
