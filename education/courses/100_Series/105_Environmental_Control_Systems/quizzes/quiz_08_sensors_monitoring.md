## Questions

### Question 1
What environmental parameters should be monitored in CEA?

a) Only temperature
b) Temperature, humidity, CO₂, light, EC, pH ✓
c) Just look at the plants
d) Monitoring isn't necessary

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Comprehensive monitoring includes temperature, humidity/VPD, CO₂, light (PPFD), nutrient EC, and pH for complete environmental control.*

---

### Question 2
Where should temperature sensors be placed?

a) On the floor
b) At canopy level, shaded from direct light/heat ✓
c) Outside the facility
d) In the office

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Sensors at canopy height in shade measure what plants actually experience. Avoid placement near heat sources, AC vents, or direct light.*

---

### Question 3
What does a data logger do?

a) Cuts down trees
b) Records sensor data over time for analysis ✓
c) Only displays current readings
d) Waters the plants

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Data loggers record environmental parameters over time, allowing trend analysis, troubleshooting, and optimization of growing conditions.*

---

### Question 4
Why is sensor calibration important?

a) It's not important
b) Ensures accuracy and prevents costly mistakes from bad data ✓
c) Just for fun
d) Sensors never need calibration

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Sensors drift over time. Regular calibration (pH monthly, EC quarterly, CO₂ semi-annually) ensures data accuracy for decision-making.*

---

### Question 5
What is the benefit of remote monitoring?

a) No benefit
b) Check conditions and receive alerts from anywhere ✓
c) More expensive with no value
d) Only works locally

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Remote monitoring (smartphone apps, web dashboards) allows 24/7 oversight, immediate alerts for problems, and peace of mind when off-site.*

---

### Question 6
What should trigger an alert/alarm?

a) Nothing—never use alerts
b) Parameters exceeding safe ranges (temperature, humidity, etc.) ✓
c) Everything constantly
d) Only total system failure

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Set alerts for high/low temperature, humidity extremes, CO₂ depletion, power loss, or equipment failure—anything threatening crop safety.*

---

### Question 7
How often should you check data?

a) Once per year
b) Daily for critical parameters, continuous monitoring ideal ✓
c) Never check data
d) Only when problems are visible

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Check critical parameters daily minimum. Automated continuous monitoring with data logging catches problems early, often before visible plant damage.*

---

### Question 8
What is a PAR meter used for?

a) Measuring water quality
b) Measuring photosynthetically active light (PPFD) ✓
c) Measuring air pressure
d) Counting plants

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: PAR/quantum meters measure PPFD (μmol/m²/s) to ensure plants receive adequate light intensity and to map uniformity across growing area.*

---

### Question 9
What is sensor redundancy?

a) Useless extra sensors
b) Multiple sensors for critical parameters as backup ✓
c) Too many sensors always
d) Sensors that repeat themselves

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Redundant sensors (multiple temp/humidity sensors) ensure system continues functioning if one fails, preventing false alarms or missed alerts.*

---

### Question 10
How can data analysis improve operations?

a) Data has no value
b) Identifies trends, optimizes conditions, troubleshoots problems ✓
c) Only for large operations
d) Makes things more complicated

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Analyzing historical data reveals patterns (daily/seasonal), optimizes setpoints, diagnoses problems, and validates the ROI of environmental control investments.*
