## What is Controlled Environment Agriculture?

### Definition

**Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA)** is a technology-based approach to food production that aims to provide optimal growing conditions throughout the development of the crop. Production takes place within an enclosed structure where the environment can be monitored and adjusted.

### The Six Environmental Factors

Every CEA system must manage these interconnected factors:

1. **Temperature** - Air and root zone temperatures
2. **Humidity** - Water vapor in the air
3. **Light** - Intensity, spectrum, and duration
4. **CO₂** - Carbon dioxide concentration
5. **Airflow** - Air circulation and exchange
6. **Nutrition** - Delivered through growing medium or solution

```

║         │  HUMIDITY   │ │  LEVELS   │ │   OUTPUT    │                    ║
║         └──────┬──────┘ └─────┬─────┘ └──────┬──────┘                    ║
```

## Module Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **CEA** is technology-based agriculture that controls growing conditions
2. **Facility types** range from greenhouses to fully enclosed indoor farms
3. **Six factors** must be managed: temperature, humidity, light, CO₂, airflow, and nutrition
4. **Benefits** include consistency, efficiency, and reduced risk
5. **Factors interact** - changing one affects others
6. **Economics** must balance capital investment against operating costs and revenue
