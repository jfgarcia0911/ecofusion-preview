# Glossary of Environmental Control Terms
## Course 105: Environmental Control Systems

---

## A

**Air Exchange** - The process of replacing indoor air with outdoor air, measured in exchanges per minute or hour.

**Aspirated Box** - A sensor housing with a small fan that draws air past the sensor for more accurate temperature/humidity readings.

---

## B

**BTU (British Thermal Unit)** - A unit of heat; the amount of heat required to raise 1 pound of water by 1°F. Used to rate heating and cooling equipment.

**Boundary Layer** - The thin layer of stagnant air that surrounds leaves, affecting gas exchange and transpiration.

---

## C

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)** - Technology-based approach to food production within enclosed structures where environmental conditions are monitored and adjusted.

**CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute)** - A measure of air volume flow used to rate fans and ventilation equipment.

**CMH (Ceramic Metal Halide)** - Also called LEC (Light Emitting Ceramic); a type of HID light with broad spectrum output similar to sunlight.

**CO₂ Enrichment** - Adding carbon dioxide to growing environments above ambient levels (400 ppm) to increase photosynthesis rates.

**COP (Coefficient of Performance)** - A measure of heat pump efficiency; the ratio of heating/cooling output to electrical input.

---

## D

**Deadband** - A range around a setpoint where no action is taken, preventing rapid equipment cycling.

**Dehumidifier** - Equipment that removes moisture from air by condensing water vapor on cold coils.

**Dew Point** - The temperature at which air becomes saturated and water begins to condense.

**DIF (Day/Night Differential)** - The difference between day and night temperatures; affects plant morphology and stretch.

**DLI (Daily Light Integral)** - The total amount of photosynthetically active light received in a 24-hour period, measured in mol/m²/day.

---

## E

**Efficacy** - A measure of how efficiently a light fixture converts electricity into PAR light, measured in μmol/J.

**Evaporative Cooling** - Cooling method using water evaporation to reduce air temperature; effective in dry climates.

---

## H

**HAF (Horizontal Air Flow)** - A system of fans creating circular air movement within a growing space for uniform conditions.

**Heat Load** - The amount of heat that must be added or removed to maintain desired temperature.

**Heat Pump** - A device that moves heat from one location to another; provides both heating and cooling with high efficiency.

**HID (High Intensity Discharge)** - A category of lamps including HPS and MH that produce light by electrical arc through gas.

**HPS (High Pressure Sodium)** - A type of HID light with yellow/orange spectrum, commonly used for flowering.

**HVAC** - Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning systems.

---

## I

**Infiltration** - Uncontrolled air leakage into or out of a structure through gaps and openings.

**Inverter (Variable Speed)** - Technology allowing compressors and fans to adjust speed rather than just on/off, improving efficiency.

---

## L

**Latent Heat** - Heat absorbed or released during phase changes (like evaporation), not reflected in temperature change.

**LED (Light Emitting Diode)** - Solid-state lighting technology offering high efficiency and spectral control.

**LEC (Light Emitting Ceramic)** - See CMH.

---

## M

**MERV Rating** - Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value; measures air filter effectiveness at capturing particles.

**MH (Metal Halide)** - A type of HID light with blue/white spectrum, commonly used for vegetative growth.

**Mini-Split** - A ductless air conditioning/heat pump system with separate indoor and outdoor units.

---

## N

**Negative Pressure** - When air pressure inside a space is lower than outside, causing air to flow inward through openings.

**NDIR (Non-Dispersive Infrared)** - The sensing technology used in most CO₂ monitors.

---

## P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)** - Light wavelengths from 400-700nm that plants use for photosynthesis.

**Photoperiod** - The duration of light exposure in a 24-hour cycle; affects flowering in many plant species.

**PPF (Photosynthetic Photon Flux)** - The total number of PAR photons emitted by a light source per second, measured in μmol/s.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)** - Light intensity at a specific point, measured in μmol/m²/s.

---

## R

**Radiant Heat** - Heat transferred directly through electromagnetic radiation without heating the air.

**RH (Relative Humidity)** - The percentage of water vapor in air relative to the maximum the air can hold at that temperature.

---

## S

**Saturation Vapor Pressure (SVP)** - The maximum amount of water vapor air can hold at a given temperature.

**SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio)** - Rating for air conditioner efficiency over a cooling season; higher is better.

**Setpoint** - The target value for a controlled parameter (temperature, humidity, CO₂, etc.).

**Short-Day Plant** - A plant that flowers when day length is shorter than a critical period (usually 12-14 hours).

**Staging** - Using multiple levels of heating/cooling that activate progressively as needed.

**Static Pressure** - Resistance to airflow in a duct system, measured in inches of water column.

**Stomata** - Tiny pores on leaf surfaces that open and close to control gas exchange and transpiration.

---

## T

**Thermal Mass** - Materials that absorb and store heat, moderating temperature fluctuations.

**Thermostat** - A device that controls heating/cooling equipment based on temperature readings.

**Ton (of cooling)** - A unit of cooling capacity equal to 12,000 BTU/hr.

**Transpiration** - The process of water evaporating from plant leaves through stomata.

---

## U

**U-Value** - A measure of heat transfer through a material; lower values indicate better insulation.

**Uniformity Ratio** - The ratio of minimum to maximum PPFD in a lit area; higher is better (target >0.70).

---

## V

**Ventilation** - The process of exchanging indoor air with outdoor air, either naturally (passive) or mechanically (active).

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)** - The difference between the amount of moisture in the air and the amount the air can hold when saturated; indicates the "drying power" of air.

---

## Z

**Zone** - A discrete area with independent environmental controls.
