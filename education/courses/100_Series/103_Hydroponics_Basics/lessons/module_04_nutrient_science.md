## Lesson Content

### 4.1 Plant Nutrition Fundamentals

#### What Plants Need to Grow

Plants create their own food through photosynthesis, but they need raw materials (nutrients) to build structures, produce energy, and carry out metabolic processes.

```
PLANT NUTRITION OVERVIEW

ENERGY SOURCE
    ↓
☀️ LIGHT (photosynthesis)
    ↓
BUILDING BLOCKS
    ↓
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ From Air/Water:                             │
│   • Carbon (C) - from CO₂                   │
│   • Hydrogen (H) - from H₂O                 │
│   • Oxygen (O) - from H₂O and CO₂           │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    +
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ From Nutrient Solution (14 elements):       │
│   • Macronutrients (needed in large amounts)│
│   • Micronutrients (needed in tiny amounts) │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    ↓
COMPLETE NUTRITION = HEALTHY PLANT GROWTH
```

#### The 17 Essential Elements

| Category | Elements | Source |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **From Air & Water** | Carbon (C), Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O) | CO₂ and H₂O |
| **Primary Macronutrients** | Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K) | Nutrient solution |
| **Secondary Macronutrients** | Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), Sulfur (S) | Nutrient solution |
| **Micronutrients** | Iron (Fe), Manganese (Mn), Boron (B), Zinc (Zn), Copper (Cu), Molybdenum (Mo), Chlorine (Cl), Nickel (Ni) | Nutrient solution |

### 4.2 Macronutrients - Primary Elements

#### Nitrogen (N)

```
NITROGEN - THE GROWTH ELEMENT

Chemical Symbol: N
Typical Range: 100-250 ppm

FUNCTION:
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Chlorophyll production (green)   │
│ • Protein synthesis                │
│ • Amino acid formation             │
│ • Vegetative growth                │
│ • Cell division and expansion      │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

DEFICIENCY SYMPTOMS:
    Old leaves → Yellow (chlorosis)
    Stunted growth
    Pale, weak plants

TOXICITY SYMPTOMS:
    Dark green, lush foliage
    Delayed maturity
    Weak stems, lodging
```

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Mobility in Plant** | Mobile (symptoms show in old leaves first) |
| **Forms Used** | Nitrate (NO₃⁻), Ammonium (NH₄⁺) |
| **Primary Function** | Vegetative growth, protein formation |
| **Deficiency Indicator** | Yellowing of older leaves |
| **High Demand Period** | Vegetative growth stage |

---

#### Phosphorus (P)

```
PHOSPHORUS - THE ENERGY ELEMENT

Chemical Symbol: P
Typical Range: 30-90 ppm

FUNCTION:
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Energy transfer (ATP)            │
│ • Root development                 │
│ • Flowering and fruiting           │
│ • Photosynthesis                   │
│ • DNA/RNA synthesis                │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

DEFICIENCY SYMPTOMS:
    Purple/red discoloration
    Poor root development
    Delayed maturity
    Dark green leaves

TOXICITY SYMPTOMS:
    Rare in hydroponics
    May lock out other nutrients
```

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Mobility in Plant** | Mobile (symptoms show in old leaves) |
| **Forms Used** | Phosphate (H₂PO₄⁻, HPO₄²⁻) |
| **Primary Function** | Energy transfer, root/flower development |
| **Deficiency Indicator** | Purple stems, dark leaves |
| **High Demand Period** | Flowering, fruiting, root development |

---

#### Potassium (K)

```
POTASSIUM - THE REGULATOR ELEMENT

Chemical Symbol: K
Typical Range: 150-350 ppm

FUNCTION:
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Enzyme activation                │
│ • Water regulation (stomata)       │
│ • Disease resistance               │
│ • Fruit quality and flavor         │
│ • Sugar/starch transport           │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

DEFICIENCY SYMPTOMS:
    Leaf edge burn (necrosis)
    Weak stems
    Poor fruit quality
    Starts on older leaves

TOXICITY SYMPTOMS:
    Difficult to achieve
    May cause Ca/Mg deficiency
```

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Mobility in Plant** | Mobile |
| **Forms Used** | K⁺ (potassium ion) |
| **Primary Function** | Overall plant health, stress resistance |
| **Deficiency Indicator** | Burnt leaf edges |
| **High Demand Period** | Throughout growth, especially fruiting |

### 4.3 Macronutrients - Secondary Elements

#### Calcium (Ca)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Typical Range** | 80-200 ppm |
| **Mobility** | Immobile (symptoms in new growth) |
| **Functions** | Cell wall strength, membrane structure, enzyme activation |
| **Deficiency Signs** | Blossom end rot, tip burn, deformed new growth |
| **Notes** | Critical for fruiting plants, often deficient in pure RO water |

```
CALCIUM DEFICIENCY EXAMPLES

TOMATOES              LETTUCE
    🍅                   🥬
     │                    │
Blossom End Rot      Tip Burn
(dark, sunken         (brown leaf
 bottom)               edges)
```

---

#### Magnesium (Mg)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Typical Range** | 25-75 ppm |
| **Mobility** | Mobile (old leaves affected first) |
| **Functions** | Chlorophyll center atom, enzyme activation, phosphorus metabolism |
| **Deficiency Signs** | Interveinal chlorosis (veins stay green, tissue yellows) |
| **Notes** | Often deficient in coco coir systems |

---

#### Sulfur (S)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Typical Range** | 25-75 ppm |
| **Mobility** | Intermediate mobility |
| **Functions** | Protein synthesis, amino acids, enzyme function |
| **Deficiency Signs** | Similar to nitrogen (yellowing), but in younger leaves |
| **Notes** | Usually adequate in complete hydroponic formulas |

### 4.4 Micronutrients (Trace Elements)

#### Overview of Micronutrients

Needed in very small amounts, but absolutely essential. Deficiencies are less common with quality hydroponic nutrients.

```
MICRONUTRIENT REQUIREMENTS

ELEMENT          TYPICAL RANGE       FUNCTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Iron (Fe)        1-5 ppm            Chlorophyll, enzymes
Manganese (Mn)   0.5-2 ppm          Photosynthesis, enzymes
Boron (B)        0.3-1 ppm          Cell division, calcium use
Zinc (Zn)        0.3-1 ppm          Enzyme systems, auxins
Copper (Cu)      0.05-0.2 ppm       Photosynthesis, enzymes
Molybdenum (Mo)  0.04-0.08 ppm      Nitrogen metabolism
Chlorine (Cl)    <1 ppm             Photosynthesis, osmosis
Nickel (Ni)      <0.1 ppm           Nitrogen metabolism

⚠️ More is NOT better - toxicity occurs quickly!
```

#### Iron (Fe) - Most Common Micronutrient Issue

```
IRON IN HYDROPONICS

WHY IMPORTANT:
    • Chlorophyll synthesis (not part of it, but needed)
    • Electron transfer in photosynthesis
    • Enzyme component

DEFICIENCY:
    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │  Interveinal Chlorosis      │
    │  (YOUNG leaves yellow,      │
    │   veins stay green)         │
    │                             │
    │       🌿 ← Affected         │
    │      Young leaf             │
    │    (yellow between veins)   │
    └─────────────────────────────┘

CAUSES:
    • High pH (>6.5) locks out iron
    • Poor chelation
    • Imbalanced nutrients

SOLUTION:
    • Lower pH to 5.5-6.0
    • Use chelated iron (Fe-DTPA, Fe-EDDHA)
    • Foliar spray for quick fix
```

#### Chelation - Making Micronutrients Available

| Chelate Type | pH Range | Best For | Stability |
|--------------|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Fe-DTPA** | 5.5-6.5 | Hydroponics | Good |
| **Fe-EDDHA** | 4.0-9.0 | High pH situations | Excellent |
| **Fe-EDTA** | 4.0-6.0 | Lower pH only | Moderate |

**What is Chelation?**
Chelates are organic molecules that "wrap around" metal ions, keeping them soluble and available to plants even when pH or other conditions would normally cause precipitation.

### 4.5 Understanding N-P-K Ratios

#### Reading Fertilizer Labels

```
FERTILIZER LABEL EXAMPLE

┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│  HYDROPONIC GROW FORMULA            │
│                                     │
│        20 - 10 - 20                 │
│         │    │    │                 │
│         N    P    K                 │
│                                     │
│  Guaranteed Analysis:               │
│  Total Nitrogen (N).......... 20%   │
│  Available Phosphate (P₂O₅).. 10%   │
│  Soluble Potash (K₂O)........ 20%   │
│                                     │
│  Also Contains:                     │
│  Calcium (Ca)................ 3%    │
│  Magnesium (Mg).............. 1.5%  │
│  Sulfur (S).................. 2%    │
│  Micronutrients.............. <1%   │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Common N-P-K Ratios for Different Stages

| Growth Stage | N-P-K Ratio | Purpose | Example Use |
|--------------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **Seedling** | 1-1-1 or 2-1-2 | Balanced, gentle | Initial growth |
| **Vegetative** | 3-1-2 or 20-10-20 | High nitrogen | Leafy growth |
| **Transition** | 1-1-1 or 15-15-15 | Balanced | Pre-flowering |
| **Flowering** | 1-3-2 or 5-15-10 | High phosphorus | Flower initiation |
| **Fruiting** | 1-2-3 or 8-15-36 | High P and K | Fruit development |
| **Leafy Greens** | 2-1-3 or 16-8-24 | Moderate N, higher K | Lettuce, herbs |

### 4.6 Complete vs. Incomplete Formulas

#### Complete Nutrient Formulas

```
COMPLETE HYDROPONIC NUTRIENT

Must contain all 14 essential mineral elements:

PRIMARY MACROS          SECONDARY MACROS
    N                       Ca
    P                       Mg
    K                       S

MICRONUTRIENTS
    Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl, Ni

✓ Ready to use
✓ Properly balanced
✓ pH buffered
✓ Chelated micronutrients
```

#### Incomplete Formulas - Require Supplementation

| Type | Missing Elements | Must Add |
|------|------------------|----------|
| **General Purpose Garden Fertilizer** | Often lacks micros, Ca, Mg | Cal-Mag, micronutrient mix |
| **Dry Fertilizer Blend** | May lack Ca (incompatible in powder) | Calcium nitrate separately |
| **Organic Liquid** | Variable, often low in micros | Depends on analysis |

### 4.7 Nutrient Mobility and Deficiency Diagnosis

#### Mobile vs. Immobile Nutrients

```
NUTRIENT MOBILITY IN PLANTS

MOBILE NUTRIENTS                 IMMOBILE NUTRIENTS
(Symptoms in OLD leaves)         (Symptoms in NEW leaves)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    N                                Ca
    P                                S
    K                                Fe
    Mg                               Mn
    Cl                               B
    Ni                               Zn
    Mo                               Cu

Why it matters:
Old leaves = Plant is robbing nutrients from old tissue
New leaves = Nutrient can't move to where needed
```

#### Visual Deficiency Guide

| Symptom | Location | Likely Deficiency |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| **General yellowing (chlorosis)** | Old leaves | Nitrogen (N) |
| **Interveinal yellowing** | Old leaves | Magnesium (Mg) |
| **Interveinal yellowing** | New leaves | Iron (Fe) |
| **Purple/red discoloration** | Old leaves | Phosphorus (P) |
| **Burnt leaf edges** | Old leaves | Potassium (K) |
| **Blossom end rot (tomatoes)** | Fruit | Calcium (Ca) |
| **Tip burn (lettuce)** | New leaves | Calcium (Ca) |
| **Twisted, deformed new growth** | New leaves | Calcium (Ca) or Boron (B) |
| **Dead growing tips** | New leaves | Calcium (Ca) or Boron (B) |

### 4.8 Nutrient Interactions and Antagonism

#### How Nutrients Interact

```
NUTRIENT INTERACTIONS

SYNERGISTIC (Help Each Other)
    N + S  → Both needed for proteins
    Ca + B → Calcium uptake improved by boron
    P + Zn → Work together in many processes

ANTAGONISTIC (Compete/Inhibit)
    K ↔ Ca, Mg  → Excess K blocks Ca and Mg uptake
    Ca ↔ Mg     → High Ca can reduce Mg uptake
    Fe ↔ Mn, Zn → Competition at high levels
    P ↔ Fe, Zn  → High P can lock out micronutrients
```

#### The Mulder's Chart Concept

High levels of one nutrient can induce deficiency of another, even when the second nutrient is present in adequate amounts.

| Excess | Can Induce Deficiency Of |
|--------|-------------------------|
| **Nitrogen** | Potassium, Boron, Copper |
| **Potassium** | Calcium, Magnesium, Boron |
| **Calcium** | Magnesium, Potassium, Boron |
| **Phosphorus** | Iron, Zinc, Copper |
| **Iron** | Manganese |

**Key Lesson:** Balanced nutrition is more important than high levels of any single element.

### 4.9 Organic Hydroponics

#### Can Hydroponics Be Organic?

```
ORGANIC HYDROPONICS CHALLENGES

TRADITIONAL ORGANIC FERTILIZERS
    ├── Compost tea
    ├── Fish emulsion
    ├── Seaweed extract
    └── Worm castings

CHALLENGES IN HYDROPONICS:
    ✗ Particles clog systems
    ✗ Inconsistent nutrient content
    ✗ Biological oxygen demand
    ✗ Pathogen risk
    ✗ System fouling

ORGANIC-APPROVED OPTIONS:
    ✓ Hydrolyzed fish
    ✓ Amino acid-based nutrients
    ✓ Mineral-based (some certifiers)
    ✓ Biological additives (carefully filtered)
```

#### Certification Status

- **USDA Organic:** Does NOT allow hydroponic certification (as of 2023, ongoing debate)
- **Other Certifiers:** Some allow "container growing" with organic inputs
- **Biological Hydroponics:** Emerging field using beneficial microbes

### 4.11 Advanced Concepts

#### Liebig's Law of the Minimum

```
LIEBIG'S BARREL ANALOGY

    Water Level = Plant Growth

    ┌───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┐
    │   │   │   │   │   │   │
    │   │   │   │   │   │   │  ← All nutrients adequate
    │   │   │   │   │   │   │
    │   │   │   │   │ P │   │  ← P is limiting
    │   │   │   │   │ h │   │     (shortest stave)
    │   │   │   │   │ o │   │
    │ N │ K │Ca │Mg │ s │ Fe│  ← Other nutrients OK
    └───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┘
         ~~~~                 ← Growth limited by P

    Plant growth is limited by the LEAST available
    (most deficient) essential nutrient.

    Adding more N, K, Ca won't help if P is limiting!
```

#### Electrical Conductivity (EC) and Nutrient Strength

| EC (mS/cm) | TDS (ppm 500 scale) | TDS (ppm 700 scale) | Typical Use |
|------------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| 0.8-1.2 | 400-600 | 560-840 | Seedlings, young plants |
| 1.2-2.0 | 600-1000 | 840-1400 | Vegetative growth |
| 2.0-2.8 | 1000-1400 | 1400-1960 | Fruiting plants |
| 2.8-3.5 | 1400-1750 | 1960-2450 | Heavy feeders (tomatoes) |

**Note:** EC measures total dissolved ions, not individual nutrients.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Macronutrient** | Element needed by plants in large quantities (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) |
| **Micronutrient** | Element needed in trace amounts but still essential |
| **N-P-K Ratio** | The proportion of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in fertilizer |
| **Chlorosis** | Yellowing of plant tissue due to nutrient deficiency |
| **Necrosis** | Death of plant tissue, often appearing as brown/black spots |
| **Mobile Nutrient** | Can be relocated within plant from old to new growth |
| **Immobile Nutrient** | Cannot move once deposited in plant tissue |
| **Chelate** | Organic molecule that keeps metal ions soluble and available |
| **Liebig's Law** | Plant growth limited by the most deficient nutrient |
| **Antagonism** | When excess of one nutrient inhibits uptake of another |
