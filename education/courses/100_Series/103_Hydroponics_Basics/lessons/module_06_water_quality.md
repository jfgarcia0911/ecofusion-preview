## Lesson Content

### 6.1 pH Fundamentals

#### What is pH?

pH measures the acidity or alkalinity of a solution on a scale of 0-14.

```
THE pH SCALE

    0  ← ACIDIC (high H⁺ concentration)
    │
    1  ← Battery acid
    2  ← Lemon juice
    3  ← Vinegar
    4  ← Tomatoes
    5  ←─┐
    6  ← │ IDEAL HYDROPONIC RANGE
    7  ← │ (5.5 - 6.5)
    │  ←─┘
    8  ← Baking soda
    9  ← Hand soap
   10  ← Milk of magnesia
   11  ← Ammonia
   12  ← Bleach
   13  ←
   14  ← ALKALINE/BASIC (low H⁺, high OH⁻)

Each number is 10× difference in H⁺ concentration!
pH 5 is 10× more acidic than pH 6
pH 4 is 100× more acidic than pH 6
```

#### Why pH Matters in Hydroponics

```
NUTRIENT AVAILABILITY BY pH

       pH: 4.0  5.0  5.5  6.0  6.5  7.0  7.5  8.0
           │    │    │    │    │    │    │    │
Nitrogen   ─────█████████████████──────────────
Phosphorus ──────────█████████████─────────────
Potassium  ─────█████████████████████──────────
Calcium    ────────────██████████████████──────
Magnesium  ────────────██████████████████──────
Sulfur     ─────█████████████████████──────────
Iron       ─────█████████──────────────────────
Manganese  ─────████████────────────────────────
Boron      ──────────██████████████────────────
Zinc       ─────████████████──────────────────
Copper     ─────████████████──────────────────
Molybdenum ──────────────────███████████──────

           │              │
      TOO ACIDIC    OPTIMAL RANGE    TOO ALKALINE
                   (5.5 - 6.5)

Key Insight:
  • Lower pH (5.5-6.0): Better micronutrient availability
  • Higher pH (6.0-6.5): Better macronutrient availability
  • Target 5.8-6.2 for best overall availability
```

### 6.2 Target pH Ranges

#### General Guidelines

| System/Crop Type | Optimal pH | Acceptable Range | Notes |
|------------------|------------|------------------|-------|
| **Most Hydroponics** | 5.8-6.2 | 5.5-6.5 | Sweet spot for availability |
| **Leafy Greens** | 5.5-6.0 | 5.0-6.5 | Slightly lower OK |
| **Fruiting Plants** | 6.0-6.3 | 5.8-6.5 | Higher for Ca availability |
| **Herbs** | 5.5-6.0 | 5.0-6.5 | Similar to leafy greens |
| **Strawberries** | 5.5-6.0 | 5.3-6.2 | Prefer slightly acidic |

#### pH Fluctuation is Normal (and Beneficial!)

```
IDEAL pH PATTERN OVER WEEK

pH
7.0 ┤
6.5 ┤        ╱╲      ╱╲
6.2 ┤     ╱─╯  ╲──╱─╯  ╲─
5.8 ┤   ╱╯              ╲╮
5.5 ┤ ─╯                 ╰─
5.0 ┤
    └──────────────────────→ Time
    Mon  Tue  Wed  Thu  Fri

Natural drift allows plants to access
different nutrients at different times

ACTION NEEDED:
  • pH < 5.3 → Adjust UP
  • pH > 6.8 → Adjust DOWN
  • Between 5.5-6.5 → Leave alone!
```

**Don't Chase pH!** Small fluctuations within the range are normal and healthy.

### 6.3 Measuring pH

#### pH Measurement Tools

```
pH MEASUREMENT OPTIONS

1. LIQUID INDICATOR (pH Test Kit)
   ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ Add drops → Compare  │
   │ to color chart       │
   └──────────────────────┘
   Cost: $10-20
   Accuracy: ±0.5 pH
   Pros: Cheap, no calibration
   Cons: Subjective, less accurate

2. pH TEST STRIPS
   ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ Dip → Compare color  │
   └──────────────────────┘
   Cost: $5-15
   Accuracy: ±0.5-1.0 pH
   Pros: Very cheap, quick
   Cons: Least accurate

3. DIGITAL pH METER (Basic)
   ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ Probe → Digital      │
   │ readout              │
   └──────────────────────┘
   Cost: $15-50
   Accuracy: ±0.1 pH
   Pros: Easy to read
   Cons: Requires calibration

4. PROFESSIONAL pH METER
   ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ Replaceable probe    │
   │ Auto-calibration     │
   └──────────────────────┘
   Cost: $100-500+
   Accuracy: ±0.01 pH
   Pros: Very accurate, durable
   Cons: Expensive

RECOMMENDATION:
  Start: pH test kit or basic digital meter
  Long-term: Mid-range digital meter ($30-80)
```

#### pH Meter Calibration

```
CALIBRATION PROCEDURE

REQUIRED MATERIALS:
  □ pH 7.0 calibration solution
  □ pH 4.0 calibration solution
  □ Distilled water for rinsing
  □ Soft cloth for drying probe

STEPS:
1. Rinse probe with distilled water
2. Place in pH 7.0 solution
3. Adjust meter to read 7.0
4. Rinse probe
5. Place in pH 4.0 solution
6. Adjust meter to read 4.0
7. Rinse and store in storage solution

FREQUENCY:
  • Before first use (new meter)
  • Weekly for regular use
  • After any inaccurate reading
  • When solution is old

⚠️ NEVER store probe dry!
   Use storage solution or pH 7 buffer
```

### 6.4 pH Adjustment Methods

#### Lowering pH (Making More Acidic)

```
pH DOWN OPTIONS

PHOSPHORIC ACID (most common)
  • H₃PO₄
  • Adds phosphorus (bonus!)
  • Safe when diluted
  • Most popular choice

NITRIC ACID
  • HNO₃
  • Adds nitrogen
  • Good for vegetative growth
  • More aggressive

CITRIC ACID (organic)
  • Natural, food-grade
  • Biodegradable
  • Less stable (pH drift)
  • OK for organic systems

SULFURIC ACID (advanced)
  • H₂SO₄
  • Very strong - use carefully!
  • Adds sulfur
  • Commercial operations

VINEGAR (emergency only)
  • Acetic acid
  • Not recommended long-term
  • Can support bacterial growth
  • Use only if stranded!
```

---

#### Raising pH (Making More Alkaline)

```
pH UP OPTIONS

POTASSIUM HYDROXIDE (best)
  • KOH
  • Adds potassium (bonus!)
  • Safe when diluted
  • Preferred choice

POTASSIUM SILICATE
  • K₂SiO₃
  • Adds silica (strengthens plants)
  • Raises pH significantly
  • Dual purpose

POTASSIUM CARBONATE
  • K₂CO₃
  • Milder than KOH
  • Good for small adjustments

SODIUM HYDROXIDE (avoid)
  • NaOH (lye)
  • Adds unwanted sodium
  • Can accumulate over time
  • Not recommended

BAKING SODA (emergency)
  • Sodium bicarbonate
  • Adds sodium (not ideal)
  • Temporary solution only
```

### 6.5 Safe pH Adjustment Procedure

#### Step-by-Step Process

```
pH ADJUSTMENT SAFETY PROTOCOL

PREPARATION:
□ Wear gloves and eye protection
□ Work in well-ventilated area
□ Have clean water nearby
□ Keep children/pets away

PROCESS:

1. MEASURE current pH
   Record: ______

2. CALCULATE adjustment needed
   Target: 5.8-6.2
   Current: ______
   Change needed: ______

3. DILUTE pH adjuster
   ┌────────────────────────────────┐
   │ NEVER add concentrated acid/   │
   │ base directly to reservoir!    │
   └────────────────────────────────┘

   Mix small amount in cup of water:
   • Start with 1ml per 10 gallons
   • Mix in separate container

4. ADD diluted solution slowly
   Pour while stirring reservoir

5. WAIT 15-30 minutes
   Allow circulation and mixing

6. RE-MEASURE pH
   Adjust again if needed

7. RECORD results
   Learn your system's needs

HOW MUCH TO ADD?
  • Start conservative (1ml per 10 gal)
  • Each system different
  • Learn through experience
  • Record amounts for future reference
```

#### Common pH Adjustment Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Adding undiluted pH adjuster** | pH shock to plants, localized extremes | Always pre-dilute in separate container |
| **Adjusting too much at once** | pH swing, plant stress | Make small adjustments, wait, re-test |
| **Not waiting after adjustment** | Inaccurate reading | Wait 15-30 min for equilibration |
| **Chasing perfect pH** | Constant fluctuation, stress | Allow natural drift in 5.5-6.5 range |
| **Using sodium-based products** | Salt accumulation over time | Use potassium-based pH Up |

### 6.6 Water Sources and Quality

#### Common Water Sources

```
WATER SOURCE COMPARISON

MUNICIPAL TAP WATER
├── Pros: Convenient, usually safe, contains minerals
├── Cons: Chlorine/chloramine, variable quality, hardness
├── EC: 0.1-0.6 mS/cm (varies widely)
└── Best For: Most home growers (if tested)

WELL WATER
├── Pros: Free, no chlorine
├── Cons: Unknown composition, hardness, pathogens
├── EC: 0.2-1.5+ mS/cm (highly variable)
└── Best For: Rural growers (MUST test first)

RAINWATER
├── Pros: Pure, free, soft
├── Cons: Contamination from roof, storage issues
├── EC: ~0.0-0.1 mS/cm
└── Best For: Sustainable systems with good collection

REVERSE OSMOSIS (RO)
├── Pros: Pure, consistent, removes everything
├── Cons: Wastes water (3:1 ratio), removes beneficial minerals
├── EC: 0.0-0.05 mS/cm
└── Best For: Problem water, precise control

DISTILLED WATER
├── Pros: Pure, consistent
├── Cons: Expensive, not practical for large systems
├── EC: 0.0 mS/cm
└── Best For: Small systems, topping off
```

### 6.7 Water Hardness and Alkalinity

#### Understanding Hardness

```
WATER HARDNESS

Hardness = Dissolved calcium and magnesium

CLASSIFICATION:
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Soft:      0-60 ppm (0-3.5 grains/gal) │
│ Moderate:  61-120 ppm (3.5-7 gpg)      │
│ Hard:      121-180 ppm (7-10.5 gpg)    │
│ Very Hard: 180+ ppm (10.5+ gpg)        │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

EFFECTS ON HYDROPONICS:

SOFT WATER
  ✓ Need to add Ca/Mg (Cal-Mag)
  ✓ More control over nutrients
  ✗ Requires supplementation

HARD WATER
  ✓ Contains Ca/Mg already
  ✗ May need less Ca-based nutrients
  ✗ Can cause pH drift
  ✗ May clog emitters/spray heads

VERY HARD WATER
  ✗ Usually requires RO treatment
  ✗ Excess Ca/Mg locks out other nutrients
  ✗ Scaling in equipment
```

---

#### Alkalinity vs. pH

```
ALKALINITY ≠ pH (But They're Related)

pH
  └─► Current acidity/alkalinity level
      "Where you are NOW"

ALKALINITY
  └─► Buffering capacity
      "Resistance to pH change"
      "How hard to adjust pH"

Example:
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Low Alkalinity Water (soft)            │
│   pH: 7.0                              │
│   → Easy to adjust to 5.8              │
│   → pH may drift quickly               │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ High Alkalinity Water (hard)           │
│   pH: 7.5                              │
│   → Requires more acid to reach 5.8    │
│   → pH drifts back up (buffered)       │
│   → Constant pH adjustment needed      │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

High alkalinity = More pH Down required
```

| Alkalinity (ppm CaCO₃) | Classification | Hydroponic Impact |
|------------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **0-40** | Low | Easy pH control, may need buffering |
| **40-120** | Moderate | Good for hydroponics |
| **120-180** | High | Requires more pH Down |
| **180+** | Very High | Consider RO or acidification |

### 6.8 Chlorine and Chloramine

#### Why They Matter

Municipal water is treated with disinfectants that can harm beneficial microbes and, in high concentrations, plant roots.

```
CHLORINE vs. CHLORAMINE

CHLORINE (Cl₂)
├── Traditional disinfectant
├── Concentration: 0.5-2 ppm typical
├── Volatile (evaporates)
├── Easy to remove
└── Removal: Let sit 24-48 hours or aerate

CHLORAMINE (NH₂Cl)
├── Chlorine + Ammonia
├── More stable (doesn't evaporate)
├── Harder to remove
├── Increasingly common in cities
└── Removal: Requires chemical treatment or RO

TOLERANCE:
  Low levels (<1 ppm) generally OK
  High levels (>2 ppm) can inhibit growth
  Beneficial bacteria sensitive to both
```

---

#### Removal Methods

```
CHLORINE/CHLORAMINE REMOVAL

METHOD 1: AGING (Chlorine only)
  1. Fill container with water
  2. Let sit uncovered 24-48 hours
  3. Optional: Use air stone to speed up
  ✓ Free
  ✗ Takes time, doesn't work for chloramine

METHOD 2: ASCORBIC ACID (Vitamin C)
  • 50 mg per 100 gallons
  • Neutralizes chlorine and chloramine
  • Cheap and effective
  ✓ Works on both
  ✓ Safe for plants

METHOD 3: SODIUM THIOSULFATE
  • Aquarium dechlorinator
  • Very effective
  • Follow product directions
  ✓ Fast acting
  ✓ Inexpensive

METHOD 4: ACTIVATED CARBON FILTER
  • Install on water line
  • Removes chlorine (not chloramine well)
  • Requires replacement
  ✓ Automatic
  ✗ Limited chloramine removal

METHOD 5: REVERSE OSMOSIS
  • Removes everything including both
  • Most thorough
  ✓ Complete removal
  ✗ Expensive, wastes water

FIND OUT WHAT YOUR WATER HAS:
  • Call water utility
  • Check annual water quality report
  • Test kit available
```

### 6.9 Reverse Osmosis Systems

#### When to Use RO

```
CONSIDER RO IF:

✓ EC > 0.5 mS/cm (250 ppm)
✓ Very hard water (>180 ppm hardness)
✓ High sodium/chloride
✓ Unknown well water composition
✓ Recurring pH problems
✓ Want maximum control
✓ Growing high-value crops

SKIP RO IF:

✗ Water EC < 0.3 mS/cm
✗ Low hardness, good quality
✗ Budget constraints
✗ Water conservation concerns
✗ Small system (buy distilled instead)
```

#### RO System Basics

```
RO SYSTEM COMPONENTS

Source Water
    ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ Sediment Filter │ → Removes particles
└─────────────────┘
    ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ Carbon Filter   │ → Removes chlorine, organics
└─────────────────┘
    ↓
┌─────────────────┐
│ RO Membrane     │ → Removes 95-99% dissolved solids
└─────────────────┘
    ↓          ↓
  Pure      Waste
  Water     Water
  (25%)     (75%)

WASTE RATIO:
  • Typically 3:1 or 4:1 (waste:product)
  • Makes 25 gallons pure, wastes 75 gallons
  • Use waste water for garden, etc.

STORAGE:
  • Pressure tank or large container
  • Slow production (50-100 gallons/day typical)
  • Plan ahead!
```

### 6.11 pH and EC Drift Patterns

#### Understanding Drift

```
TYPICAL DRIFT PATTERNS & CAUSES

PATTERN 1: pH ↑, EC ↓
├── Plants consuming nutrients faster than water
├── Solution becoming dilute
└── Action: Add concentrated nutrients

PATTERN 2: pH ↓, EC ↑
├── Plants consuming water faster than nutrients
├── Solution becoming concentrated
└── Action: Add plain water

PATTERN 3: pH ↑, EC stable
├── Plant nutrient uptake balanced
├── Alkaline water source
└── Action: Adjust pH down, normal

PATTERN 4: pH ↓, EC stable
├── Bacterial activity
├── Organic matter breakdown
└── Action: Check for root issues, clean system

PATTERN 5: pH stable, EC ↑
├── Water evaporation
├── Reservoir too small
└── Action: Top off with water more frequently

PATTERN 6: Both stable
├── Perfect! (rare)
└── Action: Keep doing what you're doing!
```

### 6.13 Troubleshooting Water Quality Issues

#### Common Problems and Solutions

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| **Can't stabilize pH** | High alkalinity | Use RO, add more pH Down, check nutrients |
| **pH drifts up constantly** | Hard water, high alkalinity | Acidify source water, use RO |
| **pH drifts down constantly** | Bacterial growth, root rot | Clean system, check roots, H₂O₂ treatment |
| **White scale on equipment** | Hard water, mineral buildup | Descale with vinegar, consider RO |
| **Slimy reservoir** | Bacteria, algae | Light-proof reservoir, H₂O₂, clean |
| **Plants look healthy but grow slow** | High EC from source water | Dilute with RO or distilled |
| **Clogged drippers/sprayers** | Hard water, algae | Filter, use RO, regular cleaning |
