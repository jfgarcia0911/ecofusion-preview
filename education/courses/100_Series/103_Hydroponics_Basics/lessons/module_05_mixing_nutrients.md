## Lesson Content

### 5.1 Understanding EC and TDS

#### What is EC (Electrical Conductivity)?

EC measures the ability of water to conduct electricity. More dissolved ions (nutrients) = higher conductivity.

```
ELECTRICAL CONDUCTIVITY (EC)

Pure Water               Nutrient Solution
  (No ions)                (With ions)

    ≈≈≈≈≈≈                    ≈≈≈≈≈≈
      No                      (+) (-)
   conductivity            (+) (-) (+)
                          (-) (+) (-)
    EC = 0                  Ions conduct
                            electricity

                            EC = 1.5 mS/cm

Units:
  • mS/cm (millisiemens per centimeter)
  • μS/cm (microsiemens) = mS/cm × 1000
  • Example: 1.5 mS/cm = 1500 μS/cm
```

#### What is TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)?

TDS measures the total concentration of dissolved substances in water, typically expressed in parts per million (ppm).

```
TDS CONVERSION SCALES

EC meters convert EC to TDS using a conversion factor:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ EC (mS/cm)  ×  Conversion  =  TDS (ppm)         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Common Scales:
  • 500 scale: TDS = EC × 500 (US, common)
  • 700 scale: TDS = EC × 700 (European)
  • 640 scale: TDS = EC × 640 (less common)

Example:
  EC = 2.0 mS/cm
  TDS (500 scale) = 2.0 × 500 = 1000 ppm
  TDS (700 scale) = 2.0 × 700 = 1400 ppm

⚠️ Always know which scale your meter uses!
```

#### EC vs. TDS - Which Should You Use?

| Measurement | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|-------------|------|------|----------------|
| **EC (mS/cm)** | Universal, no conversion confusion | Less intuitive for beginners | Preferred for consistency |
| **TDS (ppm)** | Easy to understand (parts per million) | Scale confusion (500 vs 700) | OK if you know your scale |

**Best Practice:** Use EC for recording and communication. It eliminates confusion between TDS scales.

### 5.2 Target EC Ranges

#### By Growth Stage

```
EC RANGES BY GROWTH STAGE

SEEDLINGS / CLONES
├── EC: 0.8-1.2 mS/cm
├── TDS: 400-600 ppm (500 scale)
└── Reason: Delicate roots, low nutrient demand

VEGETATIVE GROWTH
├── EC: 1.2-2.0 mS/cm
├── TDS: 600-1000 ppm (500 scale)
└── Reason: Rapid growth, high nitrogen demand

TRANSITION (Pre-Flowering)
├── EC: 1.8-2.2 mS/cm
├── TDS: 900-1100 ppm (500 scale)
└── Reason: Shifting nutrient ratios

FLOWERING / FRUITING
├── EC: 2.0-2.8 mS/cm
├── TDS: 1000-1400 ppm (500 scale)
└── Reason: High P-K demand, heavy feeding

RIPENING / LATE STAGE
├── EC: 1.5-2.0 mS/cm
├── TDS: 750-1000 ppm (500 scale)
└── Reason: Reduced feeding, quality focus
```

#### By Crop Type

| Crop | Vegetative EC | Flowering/Production EC | Notes |
|------|---------------|------------------------|-------|
| **Lettuce** | 1.2-1.8 | N/A (harvested young) | Keep moderate, quality > size |
| **Basil/Herbs** | 1.4-2.0 | N/A | Can tolerate higher EC |
| **Tomatoes** | 2.0-2.5 | 2.5-3.5 | Heavy feeders, high EC |
| **Peppers** | 1.8-2.2 | 2.2-3.0 | Similar to tomatoes |
| **Cucumbers** | 1.7-2.5 | 2.0-2.8 | Fast growers |
| **Strawberries** | 1.2-1.8 | 1.4-2.0 | Lower EC than tomatoes |
| **Microgreens** | 1.0-1.5 | N/A (harvested quickly) | Light feeding |

### 5.3 Nutrient System Types

#### One-Part (All-in-One) Systems

```
ONE-PART NUTRIENT SYSTEMS

┌────────────────────────┐
│  Single Bottle         │
│  All nutrients mixed   │
│  "Shake and pour"      │
└────────────────────────┘
         ↓
    Add to water

ADVANTAGES:
✓ Simple, beginner-friendly
✓ No mixing required
✓ Hard to make mistakes

DISADVANTAGES:
✗ Less customizable
✗ Some nutrients may precipitate in bottle
✗ Often less concentrated
✗ Same ratio all growth stages
```

**Best For:** Beginners, single-crop systems, simplicity

---

#### Two-Part Systems

```
TWO-PART NUTRIENT SYSTEMS

┌─────────────┐    ┌─────────────┐
│  Part A     │    │  Part B     │
│  Calcium    │    │  Phosphate  │
│  Nitrate    │    │  Sulfate    │
│  Iron       │    │  Magnesium  │
│  Micros     │    │  Potassium  │
└─────────────┘    └─────────────┘
      ↓                  ↓
   Mix separately into water
   (never mix A+B directly!)

WHY TWO PARTS?
  Ca + PO₄ → Calcium phosphate (precipitate)

  Separating prevents precipitation in bottle
```

**Mixing Procedure:**
1. Fill reservoir with water
2. Add Part A, mix thoroughly
3. Add Part B, mix thoroughly
4. Check EC, adjust as needed

**Best For:** Most hydroponic growers, good balance of simplicity and flexibility

---

#### Three-Part Systems

```
THREE-PART NUTRIENT SYSTEMS

┌───────────┐  ┌───────────┐  ┌───────────┐
│  Grow     │  │  Micro    │  │  Bloom    │
│  (N-rich) │  │  (Micros) │  │  (P-K)    │
└───────────┘  └───────────┘  └───────────┘
      ↓             ↓              ↓
        Add to water separately

CUSTOMIZE RATIOS BY STAGE:

VEGETATIVE:
  3 parts Grow + 2 parts Micro + 1 part Bloom

FLOWERING:
  1 part Grow + 2 parts Micro + 3 parts Bloom

LEAFY GREENS:
  2 parts each (balanced)
```

**Advantages:**
- Maximum customization
- Adjust ratios for growth stage
- Popular commercial brands (General Hydroponics Flora series)

**Best For:** Experienced growers, multi-stage crops, different crop types

---

#### Dry (Powder) Nutrients

```
DRY NUTRIENT SYSTEMS

Typically 2-5 separate powders:
  • Calcium Nitrate (Part A)
  • Master Blend (Part B - NPK + micros)
  • Epsom Salt (Magnesium Sulfate)
  • Optional: Potassium Sulfate, etc.

ADVANTAGES:
  ✓ Much cheaper per gallon
  ✓ Concentrated, less shipping
  ✓ Long shelf life
  ✓ Professional-grade

DISADVANTAGES:
  ✗ More complex mixing
  ✗ Requires scale for measuring
  ✗ Dusty (wear mask!)
  ✗ More room for error
```

**Best For:** Large systems, cost-conscious growers, experienced users

### 5.4 Proper Mixing Procedures

#### Step-by-Step Mixing Process

```
NUTRIENT MIXING PROCEDURE

1. PREPARE
   □ Clean reservoir/container
   □ Gather nutrients, meters, PPE
   □ Have clean water ready

2. ADD WATER FIRST
   □ Fill to desired volume
   □ Check starting EC (source water)
   □ Note temperature

3. ADD NUTRIENTS
   □ Add Part A (or Calcium Nitrate)
   □ Mix/stir thoroughly (1-2 minutes)
   □ Add Part B (or Master Blend)
   □ Mix thoroughly again
   □ Add any supplements (Epsom salt, etc.)
   □ Mix thoroughly

4. MEASURE
   □ Wait 5-10 minutes for equilibration
   □ Measure EC
   □ Measure pH
   □ Record both values

5. ADJUST
   □ Adjust EC if needed (add nutrients or water)
   □ Adjust pH (we'll cover this in Module 6)
   □ Re-measure after adjustments

6. DEPLOY
   □ Add solution to system
   □ Monitor plants
   □ Record results
```

### 5.5 Calculating Dilution Ratios

#### Understanding Concentration

Most liquid nutrients are concentrated and require dilution:

```
DILUTION CALCULATIONS

Concentration Ratios:
  • 1:100 = 1 part nutrient : 100 parts water
  • 1:200 = 1 part nutrient : 200 parts water
  • "5 ml per gallon" = specific dosage

Example 1:
  Product says "5 ml per gallon"
  You have 10 gallons
  Amount needed = 5 ml × 10 = 50 ml

Example 2:
  You want EC 1.5 mS/cm
  Starting water EC = 0.2 mS/cm
  Product adds ~0.5 EC per 5ml/gallon
  Target increase = 1.5 - 0.2 = 1.3 EC
  Dosage = (1.3 ÷ 0.5) × 5ml = 13ml per gallon
```

#### Stock Solution Method (for Dry Nutrients)

```
STOCK SOLUTION MIXING

Instead of weighing powder each time:

1. Make Concentrated Stock Solutions
   Example: 100:1 concentration

   For Part A (Calcium Nitrate):
   ├── 100 grams powder
   └── 1 liter water

2. Use Stock Solutions
   Add 10ml stock = equivalent to 1g powder

3. Benefits
   ✓ Faster mixing
   ✓ More accurate
   ✓ Less dust exposure

⚠️ Label clearly! Store safely!
```

### 5.6 Adjusting for Growth Stages

#### Progressive Feeding Schedule Example

```
8-WEEK CROP FEEDING SCHEDULE

Week  Stage          EC      N-P-K Ratio  Notes
────────────────────────────────────────────────
1     Seedling       1.0     1-1-1        Gentle start
2     Veg (early)    1.2     3-1-2        Increase N
3     Veg (mid)      1.6     3-1-2        Peak growth
4     Veg (late)     2.0     2-1-2        Prepare transition
5     Transition     2.0     1-1-2        Balanced

Adjustments:
  • Increase EC gradually (0.2-0.4 per week)
  • Watch for burn or deficiency signs
  • Each crop is different - adjust as needed
```

| 1-2 | 1.2 | 2-1-2 | Establishment |
| 3-5 | 1.6 | 2-1-3 | Production EC |
| 6+ | 1.6 | 2-1-3 | Maintain, harvest |

### 5.7 Common Mixing Mistakes

#### Critical Errors to Avoid

```
❌ NEVER DO THIS:

1. Mix Part A and Part B together BEFORE adding to water
   → Creates precipitate, nutrients lock out

2. Add nutrients to empty reservoir, then add water
   → Concentrated spots can damage plants

3. Use hot water
   → Can damage some nutrient components
   → Changes pH unpredictably

4. Forget to mix/stir
   → Stratification, inconsistent strength

5. Overfeed seedlings
   → Nutrient burn, stunted growth

6. Mix nutrients in exact same spot in reservoir
   → Local precipitation possible

7. Skip calibration of meters
   → Inaccurate readings, problems downstream
```

#### Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| **White precipitate in solution** | A+B mixed directly, or high pH | Remix properly, check pH |
| **EC won't rise** | Meter not calibrated, or maxed out | Calibrate meter, dilute and re-measure |
| **EC too high** | Too much concentrate | Dilute with water |
| **EC drifts up over time** | Plants using water faster than nutrients | Top off with lower EC water |
| **EC drifts down over time** | Plants using nutrients faster than water | Add nutrients, check for issues |
| **Cloudiness in solution** | Bacterial growth, or precipitation | Check pH, cleanliness; may need to dump |

### 5.8 Water Quality Considerations

#### Starting Water EC/TDS

```
SOURCE WATER QUALITY

RAINWATER / RO / DISTILLED
├── EC: ~0.0-0.1 mS/cm
├── TDS: 0-50 ppm
└── Action: Add all nutrients from scratch

MUNICIPAL WATER (Soft)
├── EC: 0.1-0.3 mS/cm
├── TDS: 50-150 ppm
└── Action: Account for starting EC

MUNICIPAL WATER (Hard)
├── EC: 0.3-0.6 mS/cm
├── TDS: 150-300 ppm
└── Action: May need less Ca/Mg

WELL WATER (Variable)
├── EC: 0.2-1.0+ mS/cm
├── TDS: 100-500+ ppm
└── Action: Get water test, adjust accordingly

⚠️ Measure starting water EC before adding nutrients!
   Subtract from target EC to determine nutrient addition
```

#### Adjusting for Source Water

**Example:**
- Target EC: 1.6 mS/cm
- Source water EC: 0.3 mS/cm
- Nutrients should add: 1.6 - 0.3 = 1.3 mS/cm

### 5.9 Safety and Handling

#### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

```
SAFETY WHEN MIXING NUTRIENTS

REQUIRED PPE:
  □ Gloves (nitrile or latex)
  □ Eye protection (splash goggles)
  □ Dust mask (for powders)
  □ Apron or old clothes

SAFE PRACTICES:
  ✓ Mix in well-ventilated area
  ✓ Have clean water nearby
  ✓ Keep nutrients out of reach of children/pets
  ✓ Label everything clearly
  ✓ Store concentrates safely
  ✓ Don't eat/drink while mixing
  ✓ Wash hands after handling

SPILL RESPONSE:
  • Liquid: Wipe up, rinse area
  • Powder: Gently sweep, don't create dust
  • Skin contact: Rinse with water
  • Eye contact: Flush 15 min, seek medical help
```

### 5.10 Nutrient Solution Maintenance

#### How Often to Change Solution

```
SOLUTION CHANGE FREQUENCY

SMALL SYSTEMS (< 20 gallons)
  └── Change every 1-2 weeks

MEDIUM SYSTEMS (20-100 gallons)
  └── Change every 2-3 weeks

LARGE SYSTEMS (100+ gallons)
  └── Change every 3-4 weeks

FACTORS AFFECTING FREQUENCY:
  • Plant size/uptake rate
  • System volume
  • EC drift pattern
  • pH stability
  • Visible issues (algae, cloudiness)

BETWEEN CHANGES:
  □ Top off with water as needed
  □ Monitor EC daily
  □ Adjust EC back to target
  □ Monitor pH (Module 6)
```

#### Top-Off Strategies

| EC Trend | What It Means | Top-Off Strategy |
|----------|---------------|------------------|
| **EC Rising** | Plants using water faster than nutrients | Add plain water or very dilute solution |
| **EC Stable** | Balanced uptake | Add solution at current EC |
| **EC Falling** | Plants using nutrients faster than water | Add higher EC solution or fresh batch |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **EC (Electrical Conductivity)** | Measure of dissolved ions in solution, expressed in mS/cm or μS/cm |
| **TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)** | Concentration of dissolved substances, typically in ppm |
| **ppm (Parts Per Million)** | Measurement of concentration (1 ppm = 1 mg per liter) |
| **Stock Solution** | Highly concentrated nutrient solution diluted before use |
| **Precipitation** | Formation of solid particles in solution (usually unwanted) |
| **Dilution Ratio** | Proportion of concentrate to water (e.g., 1:100) |
| **Top-Off** | Adding water or nutrients to maintain reservoir level and strength |
| **EC Drift** | Change in EC over time as plants consume water and nutrients |
| **Two-Part System** | Nutrients separated into A and B to prevent precipitation |
| **Calibration** | Adjusting meters to known standards for accuracy |
