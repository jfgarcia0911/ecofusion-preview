### 8.2 Crop Profiles - Leafy Greens

#### Lettuce

```
LETTUCE - THE PERFECT HYDROPONIC CROP

VARIETIES:
  • Butterhead (Bibb, Boston)
  • Romaine/Cos
  • Leaf (Red/Green Oak, Lollo)
  • Crisphead (Iceberg - less common)

GROWING PARAMETERS:
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Seed to Harvest:  28-45 days           │
│ Temperature:      60-70°F              │
│ pH:               5.5-6.0              │
│ EC:               1.2-1.8 mS/cm        │
│ Light (DLI):      12-16 mol/m²/day     │
│ Spacing:          6-9" (15-23 cm)      │
│ Yield:            4-6 oz per head      │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

TIMELINE:
  Day 0:    Seed
  Day 7:    Germination complete
  Day 14:   Transplant to system
  Day 28-35: Baby leaf harvest
  Day 35-45: Full head harvest

TIPS:
  ✓ Keep cool (lettuce bolts in heat)
  ✓ Harvest in morning for crispness
  ✓ Succession plant every 7-10 days
  ✓ Watch for tip burn (calcium deficiency)
```

#### Tomato Management Techniques

```
PRUNING INDETERMINATE TOMATOES

SINGLE STEM METHOD (Most Common):
        Main Stem
            │
            │  🍅 Fruit clusters
    ────────┼────────
    │       │       │
  Remove  Keep    Remove
  sucker  main    sucker
          stem

WHY PRUNE?
  ✓ Directs energy to fruit
  ✓ Improves air circulation
  ✓ Easier to manage
  ✓ Better fruit size
  ✓ Faster ripening

SUPPORT:
  • String/tomato clips
  • Lean and lower method
  • Trellising
  • Height: 6-8 feet needed

POLLINATION:
  Indoor tomatoes need help!
  • Shake plants daily
  • Use electric toothbrush on flowers
  • Fan providing gentle air movement
  • Bumblebees (greenhouse)

COMMON ISSUES:
  • Blossom End Rot → Add calcium, check pH
  • Leaf curl → Temperature stress, overwatering
  • Yellow leaves → Nitrogen deficiency or normal aging
  • Blossom drop → Too hot (>85°F)
```

### 8.4 Germination Techniques

#### Seed Starting Methods

```
GERMINATION METHODS FOR HYDROPONICS

METHOD 1: ROCKWOOL CUBES (Most Popular)
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Pre-soak in pH 5.5 water 24 hours  │
│ 2. Drain excess water                  │
│ 3. Place 1-2 seeds per cube           │
│ 4. Cover with humidity dome            │
│ 5. Keep warm (70-75°F)                 │
│ 6. Remove dome after germination       │
│ 7. Transplant when roots visible       │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

METHOD 2: RAPID ROOTERS / STARTER PLUGS
  • Pre-made compressed peat/coir
  • Neutral pH, ready to use
  • Excellent success rates
  • More expensive than rockwool

METHOD 3: SEED STARTING TRAYS → TRANSPLANT
  • Start in traditional seed starting mix
  • Wash roots carefully when transplanting
  • Risk of shocking plant
  • OK for robust crops (tomatoes, peppers)

METHOD 4: PAPER TOWEL METHOD
  • Good for pre-germination
  • Transfer to medium once sprouted
  • Risky (easy to damage radicle)
  • Not recommended for beginners
```

#### Germination Conditions by Crop

| Crop | Temp (°F) | Days to Germinate | Light Needed | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------------------|--------------|-------|
| **Lettuce** | 60-70 | 2-7 | No (until sprouted) | Cool preference |
| **Basil** | 70-80 | 5-10 | No | Needs warmth |
| **Tomato** | 70-80 | 5-10 | No | Reliable |
| **Pepper** | 75-85 | 10-21 | No | Slow, needs heat |
| **Cucumber** | 70-80 | 3-7 | No | Fast |
| **Spinach** | 50-70 | 6-14 | No | Cool season |
| **Cilantro** | 55-70 | 7-10 | No | Erratic |

**General Rules:**
- Most seeds germinate in darkness (until sprouted)
- Provide light immediately after cotyledons emerge
- Keep moist but not waterlogged
- Use humidity dome for first few days
- Warmth speeds germination

### 8.5 Transplanting

#### When to Transplant

```
TRANSPLANT READINESS INDICATORS

TOO EARLY:
  ✗ Just cotyledons (seed leaves)
  ✗ No true leaves
  ✗ Weak, spindly stem
  ✗ No visible roots

JUST RIGHT:
  ✓ 2-4 true leaves developed
  ✓ Strong, healthy stem
  ✓ Roots visible at bottom/sides of cube
  ✓ Active growth

TOO LATE:
  ✗ Root-bound in cube
  ✗ Yellowing leaves (nutrient deficiency)
  ✗ Stunted growth
  ✗ Roots circling, brown

TYPICAL TIMING:
  Lettuce:       10-14 days after germination
  Basil:         14-21 days
  Tomatoes:      14-21 days
  Peppers:       21-28 days
```

---

#### Transplanting Procedure

```
STEP-BY-STEP TRANSPLANTING

1. PREPARE SYSTEM
   □ Clean net pots
   □ Fill with clay pebbles or chosen media
   □ Ensure solution at proper EC/pH
   □ System running smoothly

2. PREPARE SEEDLINGS
   □ Water seedlings 1 hour before
   □ Handle by leaves, not stem
   □ Keep roots moist

3. TRANSPLANT
   ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
   │     Net Pot Cross-Section       │
   │                                 │
   │   ═════════════════════         │
   │   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ Clay      │
   │   ░░░▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░ pebbles   │
   │   ░░░▓ Cube ▓░░░░░░░            │
   │   ░░░▓ or  ▓░░░░░░░             │
   │   ░░░▓ plug ▓░░░░░░░            │
   │   ═══╪══════╪═══════ Lid        │
   │      │ Roots │                  │
   │      └───────┘                  │
   │   Roots should reach solution   │
   └─────────────────────────────────┘

   □ Place cube/plug in center of net pot
   □ Fill around with media
   □ Top of cube level with or slightly below media
   □ Ensure roots can reach solution

4. ACCLIMATE
   □ Keep light lower first 2-3 days
   □ Monitor closely
   □ Expect slight transplant shock
   □ Growth resumes within 3-5 days

5. THIN IF NEEDED
   □ If multiple seedlings per cube
   □ Keep strongest, remove others
   □ Use scissors (don't pull roots)
```

### 8.7 Growth Stage Management

#### Vegetative Stage

```
VEGETATIVE GROWTH MANAGEMENT

GOALS:
  • Strong root development
  • Healthy leaf production
  • Compact, bushy structure

NUTRIENTS:
  • Higher nitrogen (N)
  • Moderate P-K
  • Example: 3-1-2 ratio

LIGHTING:
  • Blue-heavy spectrum
  • 14-18 hour photoperiod
  • Moderate to high intensity

TRAINING TECHNIQUES:

TOPPING (Tomatoes, Peppers)
    Before:           After:
       │               ╱ ╲
       │              ╱   ╲
       │         New shoots
      ═══             ═══

PRUNING (Basil, Herbs)
    Pinch terminal bud
    → Bushier growth

LST (Low Stress Training)
    Gently bend stems
    → Even canopy
```

---

#### Flowering/Fruiting Stage

```
FLOWERING & FRUITING MANAGEMENT

TRANSITION INDICATORS:
  • Flower buds forming
  • Pre-flowers visible
  • Vegetative growth slowing

NUTRIENTS:
  • Reduce nitrogen
  • Increase phosphorus (P)
  • Increase potassium (K)
  • Maintain calcium
  • Example: 1-3-2 ratio

LIGHTING:
  • More red spectrum
  • 12-16 hour photoperiod (crop dependent)
  • High intensity for fruiting

SUPPORT:
  • Stakes, trellises
  • String support
  • Tomato clips
  • Plan before needed!

POLLINATION (Indoor):
  • Hand pollination
  • Vibration (electric toothbrush)
  • Fans for air movement
  • Some crops self-pollinate, others need help
```

#### Harvest Best Practices

```
HARVESTING GUIDELINES

TIMING
  ✓ Harvest in morning (cooler, crisper)
  ✓ After lights on 2-3 hours (full turgor)
  ✗ Not midday (plants stressed)
  ✗ Not right before lights out

TOOLS
  □ Clean, sharp scissors or knife
  □ Harvest basket/container
  □ Gloves (if preferred)
  □ Clean hands

TECHNIQUE
  ✓ Cut cleanly (don't tear)
  ✓ Handle gently (prevent bruising)
  ✓ Keep produce cool
  ✓ Wash before storage
  ✓ Remove damaged leaves/fruit

POST-HARVEST
  • Lettuce: Rinse, spin dry, refrigerate
  • Herbs: Refrigerate in water or plastic
  • Tomatoes: Room temp (never refrigerate)
  • Peppers: Refrigerate in plastic bag

STORAGE LIFE:
  Lettuce:      5-7 days refrigerated
  Basil:        3-5 days (best fresh)
  Tomatoes:     1-2 weeks room temp
  Peppers:      1-2 weeks refrigerated
```

### 8.11 Common Crop Problems

#### Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Possible Cause | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| **Leggy seedlings** | Insufficient light | Increase light intensity, lower lights |
| **Yellowing lower leaves** | Nitrogen deficiency or normal aging | Check EC, add nutrients if young plant |
| **Purple stems/leaves** | Phosphorus deficiency or cold | Increase temperature, check pH |
| **Leaf tip burn (lettuce)** | Calcium deficiency | Add Cal-Mag, check pH, lower EC |
| **Blossom end rot (tomato)** | Calcium deficiency, inconsistent watering | Add calcium, maintain consistent moisture |
| **Slow growth** | Low temperature, low light, or low nutrients | Adjust environment and nutrition |
| **Bolting (lettuce)** | Too warm | Lower temperature, harvest sooner |
| **No fruit set (tomato)** | No pollination or too hot | Vibrate flowers, reduce temperature |
| **Small fruit** | Overcrowded or underfed | Thin plants, increase EC |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Succession Planting** | Staggered planting schedule for continuous harvest |
| **Cotyledons** | First seed leaves that emerge during germination |
| **True Leaves** | Leaves that develop after cotyledons, species-specific shape |
| **Transplant Shock** | Temporary stress when moving seedlings to new environment |
| **Bolting** | Premature flowering and seed production (unwanted in lettuce) |
| **Determinate** | Plant with fixed growth size (bush tomatoes) |
| **Indeterminate** | Plant with continuous growth (vining tomatoes) |
| **Topping** | Cutting main stem to encourage branching |
| **Pinching** | Removing growing tip to promote bushier growth |
| **DLI** | Daily Light Integral - total light received per day |
