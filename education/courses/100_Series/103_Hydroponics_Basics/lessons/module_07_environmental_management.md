### 7.1 Light Fundamentals

#### Understanding Light for Plant Growth

Plants use light energy for photosynthesis. Not all light is created equal!

```
THE ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM

        Useful for Photosynthesis
              ↓         ↓
UV ─── Blue ─── Green ─── Red ─── Far Red ─── IR
280nm  400nm   500nm   600nm   700nm    800nm

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHOTOSYNTHETICALLY ACTIVE RADIATION (PAR)      │
│ 400-700 nanometers                             │
│                                                │
│ Blue (400-500nm)                               │
│   • Vegetative growth                          │
│   • Compact, bushy plants                      │
│   • Stomata regulation                         │
│                                                │
│ Green (500-600nm)                              │
│   • Penetrates deeper into canopy              │
│   • Less efficient but still used              │
│                                                │
│ Red (600-700nm)                                │
│   • Flowering and fruiting                     │
│   • Stem elongation                            │
│   • High photosynthesis efficiency             │
│                                                │
│ Far Red (700-800nm)                            │
│   • Shade avoidance                            │
│   • Flowering signals (photoperiod)            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 7.2 Light Measurement

#### Key Metrics

```
LIGHT MEASUREMENT UNITS

LUMENS (lm)
  └─► Human eye brightness
      NOT useful for plants! ✗

FOOT-CANDLES (fc) or LUX
  └─► Illuminance for human vision
      Misleading for plant growth ✗

PAR (μmol/m²/s)
  └─► Photosynthetically Active Radiation
      Instantaneous light intensity
      THIS is what matters! ✓

DLI (mol/m²/day)
  └─► Daily Light Integral
      Total light per day
      Best for planning! ✓✓

CONVERSION:
  DLI = PAR × photoperiod (hours) × 0.0036

  Example:
    PAR = 400 μmol/m²/s
    Photoperiod = 16 hours
    DLI = 400 × 16 × 0.0036 = 23 mol/m²/day
```

---

#### DLI Requirements by Crop

```
DAILY LIGHT INTEGRAL (DLI) TARGETS

LOW LIGHT CROPS (10-15 mol/m²/day)
├── Lettuce
├── Leafy greens
├── Microgreens
└── Herbs (low light tolerant)

MEDIUM LIGHT CROPS (15-20 mol/m²/day)
├── Herbs (basil, parsley)
├── Kale, chard
├── Strawberries
└── Cucumbers (minimum)

HIGH LIGHT CROPS (20-30+ mol/m²/day)
├── Tomatoes
├── Peppers
├── Cucumbers (optimal)
├── Cannabis
└── Fruiting crops

OUTDOOR SUNLIGHT:
  • Clear summer day: 40-60 DLI
  • Cloudy day: 10-20 DLI
  • Winter: 5-15 DLI

INDOOR TARGETS:
  Most crops: 12-18 DLI with supplemental lighting
```

| Crop Type | Minimum DLI | Optimal DLI | Maximum DLI |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Lettuce** | 12 | 14-16 | 18 |
| **Basil** | 12 | 16-18 | 22 |
| **Tomatoes** | 20 | 25-30 | 35+ |
| **Peppers** | 18 | 22-28 | 32 |
| **Cucumbers** | 18 | 24-28 | 32 |
| **Strawberries** | 12 | 15-20 | 25 |

### 7.4 Photoperiod (Day Length)

#### Light Duration Requirements

```
PHOTOPERIOD CATEGORIES

SHORT-DAY PLANTS
  • Flower when nights are LONG (days short)
  • Examples: Chrysanthemums, Cannabis (flowering)
  • Typical: <12 hours light

DAY-NEUTRAL PLANTS
  • Flower regardless of photoperiod
  • Examples: Tomatoes, Peppers, Lettuce
  • Typical: 12-18 hours light

LONG-DAY PLANTS
  • Flower when nights are SHORT (days long)
  • Examples: Spinach, Radish
  • Typical: >14 hours light

HYDROPONICS PHOTOPERIODS:
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Leafy Greens:     14-16 hours          │
│ Herbs:            14-18 hours          │
│ Fruiting Plants:  16-18 hours (veg)    │
│                   12 hours (flower)    │
│ Microgreens:      12-16 hours          │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

⚠️ More light ≠ always better!
   Plants need dark period for respiration
   18+ hours can stress some plants
```

### 7.5 Temperature Management

#### Optimal Temperature Ranges

```
TEMPERATURE EFFECTS ON PLANT GROWTH

TOO COLD (<50°F / 10°C)
  ✗ Slow growth
  ✗ Nutrient uptake reduced
  ✗ Risk of chilling injury
  ✗ Disease susceptibility

COOL (50-65°F / 10-18°C)
  ○ Slow but steady growth
  ○ Good for cool-season crops
  ○ Lower nutrient demand

OPTIMAL (65-75°F / 18-24°C)
  ✓ Maximum growth rate
  ✓ Best nutrient uptake
  ✓ Healthy development
  ✓ Most crops thrive

WARM (75-85°F / 24-29°C)
  ○ Faster metabolism
  ○ Higher water/nutrient demand
  ○ Some crops stressed
  ○ Disease pressure increases

TOO HOT (>85°F / >29°C)
  ✗ Heat stress
  ✗ Reduced photosynthesis
  ✗ Blossom drop
  ✗ Wilting, damage
```

---

#### Temperature Targets by Crop

| Crop | Day Temp (°F) | Night Temp (°F) | Notes |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Lettuce** | 60-70 | 55-65 | Prefers cooler |
| **Basil** | 70-80 | 65-70 | Warm-loving |
| **Tomatoes** | 70-80 | 60-70 | DIF = 10-15°F |
| **Peppers** | 75-85 | 65-75 | Heat-loving |
| **Cucumbers** | 75-80 | 65-70 | Warm preference |
| **Strawberries** | 65-75 | 55-65 | Moderate temps |
| **Herbs (general)** | 65-75 | 60-70 | Most versatile |

**DIF (Day-Night Temperature Difference):**
- 10-15°F difference promotes healthy growth
- Cooler nights allow respiration and energy storage
- Constant temperature can stress plants

#### Root Zone Temperature

```
ROOT ZONE TEMPERATURE

WHY IT MATTERS:
  • Affects nutrient uptake
  • Influences oxygen availability
  • Impacts beneficial bacteria
  • Disease risk (Pythium loves warm water)

OPTIMAL RANGES:

┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Most Crops:       65-72°F (18-22°C)    │
│                                        │
│ Lettuce:          60-68°F (15-20°C)    │
│ (cooler preference)                    │
│                                        │
│ Warm crops:       68-75°F (20-24°C)    │
│ (tomatoes, peppers)                    │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

PROBLEMS:
  <60°F (15°C):  Slow growth, poor uptake
  >75°F (24°C):  Low dissolved oxygen
  >80°F (27°C):  Root disease risk HIGH

COOLING OPTIONS:
  • Insulated reservoirs
  • Water chillers (expensive but effective)
  • Frozen water bottles (small systems)
  • Shade reservoir
  • Bury reservoir underground

HEATING OPTIONS:
  • Aquarium heaters
  • Heat mats
  • Warm grow room
```

### 7.6 Humidity Management

#### Understanding Relative Humidity (RH)

```
RELATIVE HUMIDITY (RH)

RH = (Actual water vapor / Maximum possible at temp) × 100%

Warm air holds MORE water vapor than cold air!

Example:
  70°F at 50% RH = comfortable
  90°F at 50% RH = feels dry
  50°F at 50% RH = feels damp

PLANT EFFECTS:

LOW HUMIDITY (<40%)
  ✗ Excessive transpiration
  ✗ Water stress
  ✗ Leaf tip burn
  ✗ Slower growth
  ✓ Less disease pressure

OPTIMAL (50-70%)
  ✓ Healthy transpiration
  ✓ Good nutrient uptake
  ✓ Strong growth
  ✓ Manageable disease risk

HIGH HUMIDITY (>80%)
  ✗ Reduced transpiration
  ✗ Poor nutrient transport
  ✗ Fungal disease risk HIGH
  ✗ Mold, mildew
  ✓ Seedling/clone propagation
```

---

#### Humidity Targets by Growth Stage

```
HUMIDITY BY GROWTH STAGE

SEEDLINGS / CLONES
├── Target: 70-80% RH
├── High humidity supports delicate roots
└── Dome/cover maintains humidity

VEGETATIVE GROWTH
├── Target: 50-70% RH
├── Promotes healthy transpiration
└── Good balance

FLOWERING / FRUITING
├── Target: 40-60% RH
├── Lower RH prevents bud/fruit rot
└── Especially important for dense flowers

HARVEST / DRYING
├── Target: 45-55% RH
└── Prevents mold in post-harvest
```

```
  ✓ Space plants for airflow
```

### 7.7 Air Circulation and Ventilation

#### Why Air Movement Matters

```
BENEFITS OF AIR CIRCULATION

PLANT HEALTH
  ✓ Strengthens stems (thigmomorphogenesis)
  ✓ Prevents hot/cold spots
  ✓ Reduces disease pressure
  ✓ Improves transpiration
  ✓ Even CO₂ distribution

ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL
  ✓ Prevents humidity buildup
  ✓ Distributes heat from lights
  ✓ Prevents temperature stratification
  ✓ Removes stale air

DISEASE PREVENTION
  ✓ Prevents fungal growth
  ✓ Dries leaf surfaces
  ✓ Reduces pest habitat
```

---

#### Circulation System Design

```
AIR CIRCULATION SETUP

OSCILLATING FANS
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  Fan                   │
│                   ↕                    │
│        Gentle breeze                   │
│        across canopy                   │
│                                        │
│  🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱            │
│                                        │
│  Target: Leaves gently rustling        │
│  NOT: Strong wind bending plants       │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

VENTILATION SYSTEM
    Passive                Active
    ────────              ────────
    Passive Intake        Inline Fan Exhaust
         ↓                      ↑
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │   🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱  🌱    │
    │                         │
    │   Grow Room/Tent        │
    │                         │
    └─────────────────────────┘
         ↑                    ↓
    Intake Fan           Passive Exhaust
    (optional)              (optional)

AIR CHANGES PER HOUR:
  Small systems: 20-30 ACH
  Large systems: 1-5 ACH
  With CO₂: Minimal (sealed)

CALCULATION:
  CFM needed = (Room Volume × ACH) / 60

  Example:
    10' × 10' × 8' = 800 cubic feet
    Target: 30 ACH
    CFM = (800 × 30) / 60 = 400 CFM fan
```

### 7.8 Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) Enrichment

#### CO₂ and Photosynthesis

```
CO₂ IN PHOTOSYNTHESIS

Photosynthesis Equation:
  6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  (carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen)

ATMOSPHERIC CO₂
  └─► ~400-420 ppm (0.04%)
      Normal outdoor air

ENHANCED CO₂
  └─► 800-1500 ppm
      Can increase growth 20-40%

⚠️ REQUIREMENTS for CO₂ benefits:
  • High light intensity (DLI >20)
  • Optimal temperature (75-85°F)
  • Adequate nutrients
  • Sealed or semi-sealed space
  • Otherwise: WASTED MONEY
```

---

#### When to Use CO₂ Enrichment

```
CO₂ DECISION TREE

Is your grow space sealed/semi-sealed?
    │
    ├─► NO → Don't use CO₂ (it escapes)
    │         Focus on ventilation
    │
    └─► YES
         ↓
    Is your light intensity high (>600 μmol/m²/s)?
         │
         ├─► NO → Don't use CO₂ (limiting factor is light)
         │         Upgrade lights first
         │
         └─► YES
              ↓
    Are temperature and nutrients optimized?
              │
              ├─► NO → Fix those first
              │
              └─► YES
                   ↓
              Consider CO₂ enrichment!
              Expected gain: 20-40% yield

SIMPLE RULE:
  High-tech system with sealed room = CO₂ beneficial
  Basic system with ventilation = CO₂ wasted
```

---

#### CO₂ Delivery Methods

| Method | CO₂ Level Control | Cost | Best For |
|--------|------------------|------|----------|
| **Compressed CO₂ Tank** | Excellent (with controller) | $$$ | Professional |
| **CO₂ Generator (burner)** | Good (with controller) | $$ | Large spaces |
| **CO₂ Bags/Buckets** | Poor (passive) | $ | Hobby, supplemental |
| **Fermentation** | Very poor | $ | Minimal benefit |
| **Dry Ice** | Poor (temporary) | $$ | Not practical |

**Safety Note:** High CO₂ levels (>5000 ppm) are dangerous to humans. Use monitors and alarms in sealed grow rooms!

### 7.11 Troubleshooting Environmental Issues

#### Common Problems and Solutions

| Symptom | Environmental Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------------------|----------|
| **Leggy, stretched plants** | Insufficient light | Increase light intensity or duration |
| **Slow growth** | Low temperature or light | Raise temperature, add light |
| **Wilting despite wet roots** | High temperature, low humidity | Cool down, increase humidity |
| **Leaf curl, crispy edges** | Too much light or heat | Reduce intensity, improve cooling |
| **Powdery mildew** | High humidity, poor circulation | Lower RH, add fans |
| **Blossom drop (tomatoes)** | Temperature too high | Cool to <85°F |
| **Purple leaves** | Cold stress | Increase temperature |
| **Interveinal chlorosis** | Light too intense (bleaching) | Raise lights, reduce intensity |

### 7.12 Energy Efficiency

#### Reducing Environmental Control Costs

```
ENERGY SAVING STRATEGIES

LIGHTING (Largest expense)
  ✓ Use efficient LEDs
  ✓ Match DLI to crop needs (don't over-light)
  ✓ Use reflective surfaces
  ✓ Clean fixtures regularly
  ✓ Timer controls

HEATING/COOLING
  ✓ Insulate grow space
  ✓ Seal air leaks
  ✓ Use LED lights (less heat)
  ✓ Passive cooling (night air)
  ✓ Programmable thermostats

VENTILATION
  ✓ Variable speed fans
  ✓ Timer controls
  ✓ Seal room if using CO₂
  ✓ Heat recovery (advanced)

HUMIDITY
  ✓ Proper ventilation reduces need
  ✓ Drain/remove standing water
  ✓ Energy-efficient dehumidifiers

SAMPLE ENERGY USE:
  4' × 4' tent, LED lighting
  • Lights: 300W × 16h = 4.8 kWh/day
  • Fans: 50W × 24h = 1.2 kWh/day
  • Total: ~6 kWh/day = $0.60-1.20/day
           (depending on electricity rates)
```
