## Questions

### Question 1
What does N-P-K stand for on fertilizer labels?

a) Nickel, Platinum, Krypton
b) Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium ✓
c) Nitrate, Phosphate, Kalium
d) Natural, Pure, Kosher

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: N-P-K represents Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium—the three primary macronutrients plants need in largest quantities.*

---

### Question 2
Which nutrient is primarily responsible for leaf growth?

a) Phosphorus
b) Nitrogen ✓
c) Potassium
d) Calcium

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Nitrogen (N) is essential for vegetative growth, chlorophyll production, and leaf development. It's the "growth" nutrient.*

---

### Question 3
What does phosphorus (P) promote in plants?

a) Leaf growth
b) Root development and flowering ✓
c) Disease resistance only
d) Water uptake

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Phosphorus is critical for root development, energy transfer (ATP), flowering, and fruiting. It's especially important during reproductive stages.*

---

### Question 4
How many essential nutrients do plants require?

a) 3 (N-P-K only)
b) 6
c) 17 ✓
d) 50+

**Correct Answer: c**

*Explanation: Plants require 17 essential elements: 3 from air/water (C, H, O), 6 macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S), and 8 micronutrients (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl, Ni).*

---

### Question 5
What are micronutrients?

a) Nutrients for microscopic plants
b) Elements needed in very small quantities ✓
c) Nutrients that don't matter
d) Only vitamins

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Micronutrients (iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron, molybdenum, chlorine, nickel) are needed in trace amounts but are still essential for plant health.*

---

### Question 6
What role does calcium play in plants?

a) Energy production
b) Cell wall structure and strength ✓
c) Photosynthesis only
d) No significant role

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Calcium is crucial for cell wall structure, membrane function, and preventing disorders like blossom end rot in tomatoes and tip burn in lettuce.*

---

### Question 7
What is a "complete" nutrient solution?

a) One that fills the reservoir completely
b) Contains all 17 essential plant nutrients ✓
c) Only contains N-P-K
d) Any fertilizer solution

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: A complete nutrient solution provides all 17 essential elements in proper ratios, not just the primary macronutrients.*

---

### Question 8
Why is iron often chelated in hydroponic nutrients?

a) To make it yellow
b) To keep it available and prevent precipitation ✓
c) To reduce its concentration
d) It doesn't need to be chelated

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Iron chelation (binding to organic molecules) keeps iron soluble and available to plants, preventing it from precipitating out at higher pH levels.*

---

### Question 9
What happens if plants don't get enough magnesium?

a) Leaves turn purple
b) Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins) ✓
c) Excessive growth
d) Nothing—magnesium is optional

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Magnesium is the central atom in chlorophyll. Deficiency causes yellowing between leaf veins (interveinal chlorosis), typically in older leaves first.*

---

### Question 10
Can you use regular garden fertilizer for hydroponics?

a) Yes, always
b) No, hydroponic fertilizers are formulated for direct uptake without soil ✓
c) Only in emergencies
d) Garden fertilizer is better

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Hydroponic nutrients are water-soluble and immediately available. Garden fertilizers often require soil biology to break down and may contain insoluble components.*
