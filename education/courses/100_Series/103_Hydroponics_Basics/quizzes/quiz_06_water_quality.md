## Questions

### Question 1
What is the ideal pH range for most hydroponic crops?

a) 3.0-4.0
b) 5.5-6.5 ✓
c) 7.5-8.5
d) 9.0-10.0

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Most hydroponic crops thrive at pH 5.5-6.5, where nutrient availability is optimized. Outside this range, nutrients can become unavailable (nutrient lockout).*

---

### Question 2
What happens if pH is too high (above 7.0)?

a) Iron and micronutrients become unavailable ✓
b) All nutrients become more available
c) Plants grow faster
d) Nothing changes

**Correct Answer: a**

*Explanation: High pH causes micronutrient lockout, especially iron, manganese, and zinc, leading to deficiency symptoms even when nutrients are present.*

---

### Question 3
How do you lower pH in hydroponics?

a) Add baking soda
b) Add pH Down (phosphoric or nitric acid) ✓
c) Add lime
d) Add potassium carbonate

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: pH Down products (phosphoric acid, nitric acid, citric acid) lower pH. Add slowly, mix well, and retest before making further adjustments.*

---

### Question 4
What is water hardness?

a) How frozen the water is
b) Concentration of calcium and magnesium carbonates ✓
c) How fast water flows
d) Water pressure

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Water hardness measures dissolved calcium and magnesium, typically as carbonates. Hard water affects pH stability and can contribute calcium/magnesium to plants.*

---

### Question 5
Why should you remove chlorine from tap water?

a) Chlorine is beneficial to plants
b) Chlorine can harm beneficial microbes and roots ✓
c) Chlorine makes water taste bad to plants
d) You don't need to remove it

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Chlorine can damage plant roots and kill beneficial microorganisms. Remove by letting water sit 24 hours or using dechlorinator.*

---

### Question 6
What is chloramine?

a) A type of plant nutrient
b) More stable chlorine compound than chlorine ✓
c) A beneficial bacteria
d) A pH buffer

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Chloramine is chlorine bonded to ammonia, used in some water systems because it's more stable. It doesn't evaporate like chlorine and requires chemical removal.*

---

### Question 7
What does a reverse osmosis (RO) system do?

a) Adds minerals to water
b) Removes dissolved solids, producing pure water ✓
c) Adds oxygen to water
d) Heats the water

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: RO filters remove dissolved minerals, salts, and contaminants, producing near-pure water (0-10 ppm). This provides a clean slate for adding precise nutrients.*

---

### Question 8
When is RO water most beneficial?

a) Never—tap water is always better
b) When source water has high EC, hardness, or contaminants ✓
c) Only for outdoor systems
d) For watering soil gardens

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: RO is beneficial when tap water has high dissolved solids, extreme hardness, or contaminants that interfere with nutrient management.*

---

### Question 9
What is alkalinity in water?

a) Same as pH
b) Water's buffering capacity to resist pH changes ✓
c) How basic the water tastes
d) Amount of sodium

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Alkalinity is buffering capacity (usually from carbonates/bicarbonates). High alkalinity makes pH harder to adjust and maintain in desired range.*

---

### Question 10
How often should you test pH in hydroponics?

a) Once when you set up the system
b) Daily, especially in small systems ✓
c) Once per year
d) Never test pH

**Correct Answer: b**

*Explanation: Test pH daily in small systems, every few days in larger systems. pH fluctuates due to plant uptake, nutrient dosing, and microbial activity.*
