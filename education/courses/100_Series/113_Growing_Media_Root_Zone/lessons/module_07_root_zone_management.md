### 7.1 Irrigation Management for Healthy Roots

#### The Water-Air Balance

**Roots need BOTH water and oxygen**

```
ROOT ZONE WATER CYCLE

IMMEDIATELY AFTER IRRIGATION
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Water: 70-80%   ████████│ Saturated
│ Air: 10-20%     ██░░░░░░│ Low oxygen
│ Solids: 10%     █░░░░░░░│
└─────────────────────────┘
    Risk: Root suffocation (if prolonged)
              ↓
        DRAINAGE (minutes)
              ↓
CONTAINER CAPACITY (Target)
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Water: 40-60%   ████████│ Ideal
│ Air: 20-40%     ████████│ Good oxygen
│ Solids: 10%     ██░░░░░░│
└─────────────────────────┘
    Maintain this state!
              ↓
        PLANT UPTAKE (hours/days)
              ↓
BEFORE NEXT IRRIGATION
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Water: 15-25%   ████░░░░│ Adequate
│ Air: 50-60%     ████████│ High oxygen
│ Solids: 10%     ██░░░░░░│
└─────────────────────────┘
    Time to irrigate again!
```

#### When to Irrigate

**Methods to Determine Irrigation Timing:**

```
IRRIGATION TIMING METHODS

METHOD 1: WEIGHT/LIFT TEST
├─ Lift container when saturated (learn weight)
├─ Lift daily to assess water depletion
├─ Irrigate when 40-50% of water depleted
└─ Best for: Small operations, hand watering

METHOD 2: VISUAL/FEEL
├─ Check media surface (dry on top)
├─ Insert finger 1-2 inches deep
├─ Media should feel moist but not wet
└─ Best for: Hand watering, experienced growers

METHOD 3: MOISTURE SENSORS
├─ Tensiometers measure water tension
├─ Capacitance sensors measure water content
├─ Set threshold for irrigation trigger
└─ Best for: Automated systems, large operations

METHOD 4: CALCULATED ET
├─ Estimate water loss based on climate
├─ Factor in crop size and stage
├─ Apply replacement + leaching fraction
└─ Best for: Advanced growers, greenhouses
```

**Irrigation Frequency by System:**

| System | Frequency | Media AFP Target |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Hand watering | 1x/day | 15-25% |
| Drip (timer) | 2-4x/day | 20-30% |
| Ebb & flood | 2-6x/day | 25-35% |
| NFT/DWC | Continuous | N/A (hydroponic) |

#### How Much to Apply

**Irrigation Volume Formula:**
```
Volume to Apply = Container Capacity + Leaching Fraction

Example for 1-gallon container:
├─ Container capacity: 0.5 gallon
├─ Leaching fraction (20%): 0.1 gallon
└─ Total to apply: 0.6 gallon per irrigation
```

**Leaching Fraction:**
```
Leaching Fraction (%) = Drainage / Water Applied × 100

Target: 10-30% depending on water quality

Example:
Applied 1000 ml water
Drained 200 ml
LF = 200/1000 × 100 = 20% ✓
```

#### Leaching Requirements

**Why Leach?**

```
SALT ACCUMULATION WITHOUT LEACHING

Week 1: EC = 1.5 dS/m    [████░░░░░░]
        Apply fertilizer, no drainage
        ↓
Week 2: EC = 2.5 dS/m    [████████░░]
        Salts accumulate
        ↓
Week 3: EC = 3.8 dS/m    [██████████]
        Plant stress begins
        ↓
Week 4: EC = 5.2 dS/m    [██████████]
        Severe damage!

WITH PROPER LEACHING (20%)
Week 1-4: EC = 1.5-2.0   [████░░░░░░]
          Salts wash out, stable EC
```

**Leaching Guidelines:**

| Water EC | Recommended Leaching % |
|----------|----------------------|
| < 0.5 dS/m (RO, rain) | 10-15% |
| 0.5-1.0 dS/m (good well) | 15-20% |
| 1.0-2.0 dS/m (moderate) | 20-30% |
| > 2.0 dS/m (poor quality) | 30-50% or treat water |

#### Irrigation Water Quality

**Key Parameters:**

```
IRRIGATION WATER TESTING

ESSENTIAL TESTS
├─ EC (total salts)         Target: < 0.8 dS/m
├─ pH                       Target: 5.5-7.0
├─ Alkalinity               Target: < 150 ppm CaCO₃
└─ Sodium (Na)              Target: < 50 ppm

IMPORTANT TESTS
├─ Calcium (Ca)             Target: 40-100 ppm
├─ Magnesium (Mg)           Target: 10-30 ppm
├─ Iron (Fe)                Target: < 2 ppm
├─ Bicarbonates             Target: < 150 ppm
└─ Chloride (Cl)            Target: < 70 ppm

OCCASIONAL TESTS
├─ Sulfur, Boron, Heavy metals
└─ Microbiological (if surface water)
```

**High Alkalinity Problem:**

```
HIGH ALKALINITY EFFECTS

Well water: 250 ppm CaCO₃ alkalinity
         ↓
Gradually raises media pH
         ↓
Week 1: pH 6.0  ✓
Week 4: pH 6.5  ✓
Week 8: pH 7.2  ⚠
Week 12: pH 7.8 ✗ (nutrient lockout!)

SOLUTION: Acid injection
├─ Use sulfuric or citric acid
├─ Neutralize alkalinity
└─ Maintain water pH 5.5-6.0
```

### 7.2 Root Zone Diseases and Prevention

#### Common Root Diseases

```
MAJOR ROOT PATHOGENS

PYTHIUM
├─ Symptoms: Brown, mushy roots; wilting
├─ Favored by: Overwatering, poor drainage, warm temps
├─ Prevention: Proper irrigation, drainage, sanitation
└─ Treatment: Fungicides, dry down, improve drainage

PHYTOPHTHORA
├─ Symptoms: Black root rot, crown rot, wilting
├─ Favored by: Wet media, poor drainage
├─ Prevention: Avoid overwatering, use clean media
└─ Treatment: Fungicides, media replacement

FUSARIUM
├─ Symptoms: Wilting, brown vascular tissue, root decay
├─ Favored by: Warm temperatures, stressed plants
├─ Prevention: Sanitation, resistant varieties
└─ Treatment: Difficult; remove infected plants

RHIZOCTONIA
├─ Symptoms: Brown lesions on stems/roots, damping off
├─ Favored by: Cool, wet conditions
├─ Prevention: Avoid deep planting, overwatering
└─ Treatment: Fungicides, improve air circulation
```

#### Disease Prevention Strategies

```
INTEGRATED ROOT DISEASE MANAGEMENT

CULTURAL PRACTICES
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Proper irrigation (avoid excess) │
│ ✓ Good drainage (AFP 20-30%)       │
│ ✓ Adequate air circulation         │
│ ✓ Sanitation (clean pots, media)   │
│ ✓ Avoid wounding roots             │
│ ✓ Optimize fertilization           │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

BIOLOGICAL CONTROLS
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Beneficial microbes              │
│   - Trichoderma spp.               │
│   - Bacillus spp.                  │
│   - Mycorrhizae                    │
│ ✓ Compost tea (beneficial bacteria)│
└────────────────────────────────────┘

CHEMICAL CONTROLS (when needed)
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ✓ Preventive fungicide drenches    │
│ ✓ Rotate active ingredients        │
│ ✓ Follow label directions          │
│ ✓ Use as part of IPM program       │
└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Healthy vs. Unhealthy Roots

```
ROOT HEALTH ASSESSMENT

HEALTHY ROOTS                UNHEALTHY ROOTS
┌────────────────┐          ┌────────────────┐
│ • White/cream  │          │ • Brown/black  │
│ • Firm texture │          │ • Mushy/slimy  │
│ • Fresh smell  │          │ • Foul odor    │
│ • Branching    │          │ • Few branches │
│ • Growing tips │          │ • Dead tips    │
│ • Fill pot     │          │ • Sparse       │
└────────────────┘          └────────────────┘
      GOOD!                      PROBLEM!

Visual check: Remove plant from container monthly
```

#### Monitoring for Disease

**Weekly Inspection Checklist:**
```
□ Check 5-10 plants per area/crop
□ Look for wilting (even when wet)
□ Check for stunting or yellowing
□ Pull sample plants to inspect roots
□ Note any foul odors
□ Check for brown/black roots
□ Monitor EC and pH
□ Record observations
```

### 7.3 Root Zone Temperature Management

#### Optimal Temperature Ranges

```
ROOT ZONE TEMPERATURE EFFECTS

°F    °C     Plant Response
────────────────────────────────────
< 50   <10   Very slow growth, nutrient uptake reduced
50-60  10-15 Slow growth, cool-season crops OK
60-70  15-21 Good growth for most crops
70-75  21-24 OPTIMAL for warm-season crops
75-80  24-27 Good for tropicals, watch for disease
80-90  27-32 Heat stress, increased disease risk
> 90   >32   Severe stress, root damage
```

**Crop-Specific Targets:**

| Crop Type | Optimal Root Zone Temp |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Lettuce, greens | 60-68°F (15-20°C) |
| Tomatoes, peppers | 68-75°F (20-24°C) |
| Cucumbers | 70-75°F (21-24°C) |
| Tropical plants | 72-78°F (22-26°C) |
| Seedlings | 70-75°F (21-24°C) |

#### Temperature Effects

```
ROOT ZONE TEMPERATURE IMPACTS

TOO COLD (< 60°F)             TOO HOT (> 80°F)
┌──────────────────┐         ┌──────────────────┐
│ • Slow growth    │         │ • Heat stress    │
│ • Poor nutrient  │         │ • Low oxygen     │
│   uptake         │         │ • Disease risk   │
│ • Root diseases  │         │ • Rapid drying   │
│   (Pythium)      │         │ • Nutrient burn  │
│ • Purple leaves  │         │ • Algae growth   │
└──────────────────┘         └──────────────────┘
```

#### Managing Root Zone Temperature

**Cooling Strategies:**

```
LOWERING ROOT ZONE TEMPERATURE

FOR CONTAINERS
├─ Shade containers (white/reflective)
├─ Elevate containers (air circulation)
├─ Evaporative cooling (pad & fan)
├─ Increase irrigation frequency
└─ Grow on benches (not floor)

FOR MEDIA
├─ Use light-colored containers
├─ Increase media air porosity
├─ Apply cool irrigation water
└─ Mulch surface

FOR ENVIRONMENT
├─ Greenhouse shading
├─ Evaporative cooling
├─ Ventilation
└─ Fog/mist systems
```

**Warming Strategies:**

```
RAISING ROOT ZONE TEMPERATURE

FOR CONTAINERS
├─ Bottom heat mats (seedlings)
├─ Heated benches
├─ Floor heating pipes
└─ Dark-colored containers

FOR ENVIRONMENT
├─ Heat greenhouse air
├─ Insulate from cold floor
├─ Close vents at night
└─ Thermal mass (water barrels)

FOR IRRIGATION
├─ Temper cold water
├─ Irrigate during warmest part of day
└─ Reduce irrigation frequency
```

### 7.4 Fertigation: Integrating Nutrition and Irrigation

#### Fertilizer Injection Systems

```
FERTIGATION METHODS

CONSTANT FEED
├─ Every irrigation contains fertilizer
├─ Low concentration (50-150 ppm N)
├─ Most precise nutrient control
├─ Best for: Inert media, frequent irrigation
└─ Equipment: Injector, stock tanks

PERIODIC FEED
├─ Fertilize 1-3× per week
├─ Higher concentration (150-300 ppm N)
├─ Alternate with clear water
├─ Best for: Peat/coco media, moderate irrigation
└─ Equipment: Injector or hose-end applicator

SLOW-RELEASE + WATER
├─ Incorporate CRF in media
├─ Supplement with liquid as needed
├─ Low labor
├─ Best for: Long-term crops, outdoor
└─ Equipment: None (CRF only) or basic injector
```

#### Fertilizer Concentration Guidelines

```
NUTRIENT CONCENTRATION BY GROWTH STAGE

SEEDLINGS/CUTTINGS
├─ N: 50-100 ppm
├─ EC: 0.7-1.2 dS/m
└─ Frequency: Every irrigation or 2-3×/week

YOUNG PLANTS
├─ N: 100-150 ppm
├─ EC: 1.2-1.8 dS/m
└─ Frequency: Every irrigation

MATURE VEGETATIVE
├─ N: 150-200 ppm
├─ EC: 1.8-2.5 dS/m
└─ Frequency: Every irrigation

FLOWERING/FRUITING
├─ N: 150-250 ppm (varies by crop)
├─ EC: 2.0-3.0 dS/m
├─ Adjust NPK ratio (less N, more K)
└─ Frequency: Every irrigation
```

#### Preventing Fertilizer Problems

```
COMMON FERTIGATION ISSUES

ISSUE: Salt buildup (high EC)
├─ Cause: Insufficient leaching, over-fertilization
├─ Solution: Increase leaching %, reduce fertilizer
└─ Prevention: Regular EC monitoring

ISSUE: Nutrient deficiency (low EC)
├─ Cause: Under-fertilization, excessive leaching
├─ Solution: Increase fertilizer concentration
└─ Prevention: Monitor plant growth and EC

ISSUE: pH drift
├─ Cause: Fertilizer type, water alkalinity
├─ Solution: Use pH-appropriate fertilizer, acid inject
└─ Prevention: Test water, monitor media pH

ISSUE: Clogged emitters
├─ Cause: Precipitates, algae, debris
├─ Solution: Clean/replace emitters, filter water
└─ Prevention: Flush lines, use clean fertilizer
```

### 7.5 Troubleshooting Root Zone Problems

#### Diagnostic Process

```
ROOT ZONE PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

STEP 1: OBSERVE SYMPTOMS
┌────────────────────────────┐
│ • Wilting?                 │
│ • Yellowing/discoloration? │
│ • Stunted growth?          │
│ • Leaf burn?               │
└────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
STEP 2: INSPECT ROOTS
┌────────────────────────────┐
│ • Remove plant from pot    │
│ • Check root color/texture │
│ • Smell for odors          │
│ • Note root distribution   │
└────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
STEP 3: TEST MEDIA
┌────────────────────────────┐
│ • pH (pour-through)        │
│ • EC (pour-through)        │
│ • Moisture level           │
│ • Temperature              │
└────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
STEP 4: IDENTIFY CAUSE
┌────────────────────────────┐
│ See troubleshooting table  │
└────────────────────────────┘
         ↓
STEP 5: IMPLEMENT SOLUTION
```

#### Common Problems and Solutions

| Symptom | Root Appearance | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|----------------|--------------|----------|
| **Wilting (wet media)** | Brown, mushy | Root rot (overwatering) | Improve drainage, reduce irrigation |
| **Wilting (dry media)** | Dry, brittle | Underwatering | Increase irrigation frequency |
| **Yellowing, slow growth** | White but sparse | Nutrient deficiency | Increase fertilizer |
| **Leaf burn, dark green** | Brown tips | Salt accumulation | Leach with clear water, reduce fertilizer |
| **Iron deficiency** | White/healthy | High pH | Lower pH to 5.5-6.0 |
| **Stunted, purple color** | Healthy | Low temperature | Increase root zone temperature |
| **Rapid collapse** | Black, foul smell | Pythium/Phytophthora | Fungicide drench, improve drainage |

### 7.6 Best Management Practices

```
ROOT ZONE MANAGEMENT CHECKLIST

DAILY
□ Monitor irrigation system operation
□ Check for wilting or water stress
□ Observe plant growth/color

WEEKLY
□ Test pH and EC (pour-through)
□ Inspect roots on sample plants
□ Adjust fertilizer as needed
□ Check root zone temperature

BI-WEEKLY
□ Leach if EC is high
□ Clean irrigation equipment
□ Review fertigation program

MONTHLY
□ Lab test media (full analysis)
□ Deep-clean irrigation systems
□ Evaluate root health thoroughly
□ Update records and adjust program

SEASONALLY
□ Replace or sterilize media
□ Calibrate injection equipment
□ Test water quality
□ Review and revise protocols
```

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Leaching Fraction** | Percentage of applied water that drains through media |
| **Container Capacity** | Water content after gravity drainage stops |
| **Fertigation** | Application of fertilizer through irrigation system |
| **Root Zone** | Area where active roots grow and take up water/nutrients |
| **Pythium** | Water mold causing root rot in overwatered conditions |
| **Alkalinity** | Water's resistance to pH change, measured as ppm CaCO₃ |
| **Injector** | Device that adds concentrated fertilizer to irrigation water |
| **Stock Solution** | Concentrated fertilizer solution for injection |
| **Evapotranspiration (ET)** | Combined water loss from soil and plant |
| **Tensiometer** | Device measuring soil water tension |
