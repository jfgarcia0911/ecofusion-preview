### 3.1 pH: The Master Variable

#### What is pH?

**pH** is a measure of acidity or alkalinity on a scale of 0-14:
- pH < 7 = **Acidic**
- pH = 7 = **Neutral**
- pH > 7 = **Alkaline** (or basic)

```
pH SCALE

0  ─┬─  VERY ACIDIC
    │   Battery acid (1)
2   │
    │   Lemon juice (2)
4   │
    │   Peat moss (4-5)
5   │   ┌────────────────────────┐
    │   │  IDEAL RANGE FOR       │
6   │   │  MOST CROPS (5.5-6.5)  │
    │   └────────────────────────┘
7   │   Neutral - Pure water
    │
8   │   Baking soda (9)
    │
10  │
    │   Bleach (12)
12  │
    │
14 ─┴─  VERY ALKALINE
```

#### Why pH Matters

**pH controls nutrient availability** - even if nutrients are present, plants can't absorb them at wrong pH.

```
NUTRIENT AVAILABILITY vs pH

Nutrient    4.0   4.5   5.0   5.5   6.0   6.5   7.0   7.5   8.0
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Nitrogen         ░░░░░ ████████████████████ ░░░░
Phosphorus       ░░░░  ████████████  ░░░░░░░░░░
Potassium        ░░░░░░████████████████████████
Calcium          ░░░   ░░░░ █████████████████░░
Magnesium        ░░░   ░░░░ █████████████████░░
Sulfur           ░░░░░ ████████████░░░░░░░░░░░░
Iron             ██████████ ░░░░░░   ░░░
Manganese        ███████████░░░░░░   ░░░
Boron            ░░░░  ████████████████ ░░░░░░░
Copper           ███████████░░░░░░░  ░░░
Zinc             █████████████░░░░░  ░░░

                     ↑
              OPTIMAL RANGE
              (5.5 - 6.5)

Legend: ████ = Available    ░░░░ = Limited/Unavailable
```

**Key Observations:**
- Most nutrients available at pH 5.5-6.5
- Iron becomes unavailable above pH 6.5
- Phosphorus limited below pH 5.5 and above pH 7.0
- Calcium and magnesium limited below pH 5.5

#### Optimal pH by Crop Type

| Crop Category | Optimal pH Range | Examples |
|---------------|------------------|----------|
| **Most vegetables** | 5.8-6.5 | Tomatoes, lettuce, peppers |
| **Most herbs** | 6.0-7.0 | Basil, parsley, cilantro |
| **Acid-loving plants** | 4.5-5.5 | Blueberries, azaleas |
| **Alkaline-tolerant** | 6.5-7.5 | Asparagus, cabbage |
| **Hydroponic general** | 5.5-6.5 | Most crops |

### 3.2 Managing pH in Growing Media

#### Testing pH

**Two Methods:**

**1. pH Meter Method** (Most Accurate)
```
POUR-THROUGH pH TESTING

Step 1: Water media thoroughly
        ┌─────────────┐
        │   Water     │
        │     ↓       │
        │  ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒  │  Media
        └──────┬──────┘
               ↓
Step 2: Collect leachate after 30-60 min
        ┌─────────────┐
        │  Collection │  Test this
        │    Cup      │  with pH meter
        └─────────────┘

Target: 5.5-6.5 for most crops
```

**2. Slurry Method**
- Mix 1 part media : 2 parts distilled water
- Let sit 30 minutes
- Measure pH of liquid
- More stable reading than direct insertion

#### Adjusting pH

**To LOWER pH (make more acidic):**

| Amendment | Application Rate | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Elemental sulfur** | 1-3 lb/yd³ | Slow acting (weeks) |
| **Iron sulfate** | 2-5 lb/yd³ | Faster than sulfur |
| **Aluminum sulfate** | 3-8 lb/yd³ | Fast but can add toxicity |
| **Acidic fertilizers** | Use ammonium forms | Gradual effect |
| **Citric acid** | 1-2 tsp/gal water | Temporary, for irrigation |

**To RAISE pH (make more alkaline):**

| Amendment | Application Rate | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Dolomitic limestone** | 3-10 lb/yd³ | Adds Ca and Mg |
| **Calcitic limestone** | 3-10 lb/yd³ | Adds Ca only |
| **Hydrated lime** | 1-3 lb/yd³ | Fast acting, use carefully |
| **Wood ash** | 2-5 lb/yd³ | Variable, adds K |
| **Potassium bicarbonate** | 1-2 tsp/gal water | Temporary, for irrigation |

```
pH ADJUSTMENT TIMELINE

FAST (Hours-Days)
├─ Hydrated lime
├─ Aluminum sulfate
└─ Acid/base in irrigation water

MEDIUM (Days-Weeks)
├─ Iron sulfate
├─ Wood ash
└─ Ammonium fertilizers

SLOW (Weeks-Months)
├─ Elemental sulfur
├─ Limestone (calcitic/dolomitic)
└─ Composted materials
```

#### pH Buffering

**Buffering capacity** = Resistance to pH change

**High Buffering:**
- Peat moss (due to organic acids)
- Compost
- Coco coir (moderate)

**Low Buffering:**
- Perlite
- Sand
- Rock wool

```
BUFFERED vs UNBUFFERED MEDIA

HIGH BUFFER (Peat)           LOW BUFFER (Perlite)
pH: 5.5                      pH: 7.0

Add acid ↓                   Add acid ↓
pH: 5.4 (small change)       pH: 4.0 (large change)

Add base ↓                   Add base ↓
pH: 5.7 (resists change)     pH: 9.0 (rapid change)

= STABLE                     = UNSTABLE
```

**Management Implications:**
- High buffer = More stable, harder to adjust
- Low buffer = Less stable, easier to adjust
- Blends balance stability with adjustability

### 3.3 Electrical Conductivity (EC) and Salinity

#### What is EC?

**Electrical Conductivity (EC)** measures the salt concentration in media or water. Higher salt = higher EC.

**Units:**
- **dS/m** (deciSiemens per meter) - most common
- **mS/cm** (milliSiemens per centimeter) - same value
- **μS/cm** (microSiemens per centimeter) - 1000× smaller
- **ppm** or **TDS** (parts per million, Total Dissolved Solids)

**Conversion:** 1 dS/m = 1 mS/cm ≈ 640-700 ppm (varies by salt type)

```
EC SCALE FOR GROWING MEDIA

EC (dS/m)    Interpretation          Impact
────────────────────────────────────────────────
0.0-0.5      Very low salts          Nutrient deficiency risk
0.5-1.0      Low                     OK for seedlings
1.0-2.0      Optimal for most crops  ████ Target zone
2.0-3.0      Moderately high         Monitor sensitive crops
3.0-4.0      High                    Stress for many crops
4.0-6.0      Very high               Growth reduction
> 6.0        Excessive               Severe damage/death
```

#### Why EC Matters

**EC indicates:**
1. Fertilizer concentration
2. Salt buildup
3. Over/under-fertilization
4. Irrigation water quality

**Effects of Salt Levels:**

| EC Level | Plant Response |
|----------|---------------|
| **Too Low (<0.5 dS/m)** | Nutrient deficiency, yellowing, slow growth |
| **Optimal (1.0-2.0 dS/m)** | Healthy growth, good color, maximum yield |
| **Moderately High (2.0-3.5)** | Slightly reduced growth, dark green leaves |
| **High (3.5-5.0)** | Stunted growth, leaf burn, reduced yield |
| **Very High (>5.0)** | Severe stress, wilting, plant death |

#### Salt Tolerance by Crop

```
CROP SALT TOLERANCE

SENSITIVE (EC < 2.0 dS/m)
├─ Lettuce
├─ Strawberries
├─ Beans
└─ Carrots

MODERATELY TOLERANT (EC 2.0-4.0)
├─ Tomatoes
├─ Cucumbers
├─ Peppers
└─ Most herbs

TOLERANT (EC 4.0-6.0)
├─ Beets
├─ Spinach
├─ Asparagus
└─ Some flowers

HIGHLY TOLERANT (EC > 6.0)
├─ Barley (grain)
├─ Date palm
└─ Saltbush
```

#### Measuring EC

**Pour-Through Method:**

```
EC MEASUREMENT PROCEDURE

1. Water container to saturation
2. Wait 30-60 minutes
3. Place saucer under container
4. Apply 50-100ml water to top
5. Collect leachate in saucer
6. Measure EC of leachate

┌──────────────┐
│   Add water  │ ← 50-100ml
│      ↓       │
│   ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒   │ Media
└──────┬───────┘
       ↓ Leachate
┌──────────────┐
│ ▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒▒  │ ← Test this
└──────────────┘
```

**Interpretation:**

| Leachate EC | Action Needed |
|-------------|---------------|
| **< 1.0 dS/m** | Increase fertilizer concentration |
| **1.0-2.5 dS/m** | Optimal - maintain current program |
| **2.5-4.0 dS/m** | Reduce fertilizer or leach with water |
| **> 4.0 dS/m** | Immediate leaching required |

#### Managing Salinity

**Preventing Salt Buildup:**
1. Use quality water (low EC)
2. Apply correct fertilizer rates
3. Ensure adequate drainage (20-30% leaching)
4. Monitor EC regularly

**Correcting High Salinity:**

```
LEACHING PROCEDURE

High salt media (EC 5.0 dS/m)
        ↓
Apply 2-3× container volume of
low-EC water (< 0.5 dS/m)
        ↓
Allow complete drainage
        ↓
Test leachate EC
        ↓
If still high (> 3.0), repeat
        ↓
Resume normal fertilization
at reduced rate
```

**Leaching Formula:**
```
Leaching Requirement (%) = ECw / (5 × ECe - ECw)

Where:
ECw = EC of irrigation water
ECe = EC of saturated media extract (threshold for crop)
```

### 3.4 Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)

#### What is CEC?

**Cation Exchange Capacity** is the ability of media to hold and exchange positively charged nutrients (cations).

**Common Cations:**
- Calcium (Ca²⁺)
- Magnesium (Mg²⁺)
- Potassium (K⁺)
- Ammonium (NH₄⁺)
- Sodium (Na⁺)

**Units:** milliequivalents per 100 grams (meq/100g)

```
HOW CEC WORKS

MEDIA PARTICLE with negative sites
    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │         ⊖ ⊕ Ca²⁺    │  Cations attracted
    │   ⊖ ⊕ Mg²⁺          │  to negative charges
    │         ⊖ ⊕ K⁺      │  on media surface
    │   ⊖ ⊕ K⁺            │
    │         ⊖ ⊕ NH₄⁺    │  HELD but EXCHANGEABLE
    └─────────────────────┘
              ↕ Exchange
    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │   Soil Solution     │  Nutrients in water
    │   Ca²⁺  K⁺  Mg²⁺   │  can swap with held
    │   NH₄⁺  K⁺  Ca²⁺   │  cations
    └─────────────────────┘
              ↑
         Taken by roots
```

#### CEC Values of Media Materials

| Material | CEC (meq/100g) | Nutrient Retention |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Vermiculite** | 100-150 | Excellent |
| **Sphagnum peat** | 100-200 | Excellent |
| **Coco coir** | 60-130 | Very good |
| **Compost** | 40-100 | Good-very good |
| **Pine bark** | 10-40 | Fair-good |
| **Rock wool** | 0-5 | Very poor |
| **Perlite** | 0-2 | Very poor |
| **Sand** | 1-5 | Very poor |

```
CEC COMPARISON

High CEC (>80)              Low CEC (<20)
┌──────────────┐           ┌──────────────┐
│ Peat, Coir   │           │ Perlite,Sand │
│              │           │              │
│ ⊖⊖⊖⊖⊖⊖⊖⊖⊖  │           │ ⊖  ⊖    ⊖   │
│ Many binding │           │ Few binding  │
│ sites        │           │ sites        │
│              │           │              │
│ Holds        │           │ Nutrients    │
│ nutrients    │           │ wash away    │
│ for later    │           │ quickly      │
└──────────────┘           └──────────────┘
= Buffered                 = Requires frequent
= Forgiving               = feeding
```

#### Practical Implications of CEC

**High CEC Media (Peat, Coco, Compost):**

**Advantages:**
- Retains nutrients against leaching
- Buffers against over-fertilization
- More forgiving for beginners
- Reduces fertilizer waste

**Disadvantages:**
- Can bind nutrients too tightly
- Slower to adjust nutrient levels
- May need higher initial fertilization

**Low CEC Media (Perlite, Rock Wool, Sand):**

**Advantages:**
- Precise nutrient control
- Quick to adjust feeding program
- No nutrient tie-up

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires frequent fertilization
- Nutrients leach easily
- Less forgiving of errors
- Higher fertilizer costs

**Management Strategy:**

| CEC Level | Fertilization Approach |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **High (>80)** | Less frequent, moderate concentration |
| **Medium (20-80)** | Regular schedule, balanced approach |
| **Low (<20)** | Frequent, dilute concentration (constant feed) |

### 3.5 Other Chemical Considerations

#### Starting Nutrient Content

**Most soilless media are INERT** (contain no nutrients).

**Exceptions:**

| Material | Notable Nutrients | Management |
|----------|------------------|------------|
| **Compost** | Variable NPK, micronutrients | Test before use, reduce fertilizer |
| **Coco coir** | High K, some Ca, Mg | May need Ca/Mg supplement |
| **Some barks** | May release N during decomposition | Monitor N levels |
| **Worm castings** | Moderate NPK | Reduce fertilizer rates |

#### Toxic Elements

**Watch for:**

| Element | Source | Problem | Solution |
|---------|--------|---------|----------|
| **Sodium (Na)** | Poor water, some composts | Salt stress, reduces Ca uptake | Use low-Na water |
| **Chloride (Cl)** | Tap water, some fertilizers | Leaf burn, osmotic stress | Avoid Cl-based fertilizers |
| **Boron (B)** | Some composts, fly ash | Toxic at low levels | Test materials |
| **Heavy metals** | Contaminated composts | Toxic accumulation | Use certified materials |
| **Fluoride (F)** | Some water sources | Tip burn in sensitive plants | Avoid fluoridated water |

#### Organic Acids in Peat

**Peat moss contains organic acids** that:
- Create low pH (3.5-4.5)
- Provide buffering capacity
- Can bind micronutrients (especially Fe)

**Management:**
- Always add limestone to peat (3-10 lb/yd³)
- Target pH 5.5-6.5 for most crops
- May need micronutrient supplementation

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **pH** | Measure of acidity/alkalinity on 0-14 scale |
| **Electrical Conductivity (EC)** | Measure of salt concentration in media or water |
| **Salinity** | Total salt content, measured by EC |
| **Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)** | Ability to hold and exchange positively charged nutrients |
| **Buffering Capacity** | Resistance to pH change |
| **Leaching** | Applying excess water to flush salts from media |
| **Pour-Through Method** | Testing technique to measure pH/EC of media solution |
| **Cation** | Positively charged ion (Ca²⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, NH₄⁺) |
| **Anion** | Negatively charged ion (NO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻, SO₄²⁻) |
| **Inert Media** | Material with no CEC or nutrient content |
| **Dolomitic Limestone** | pH amendment providing calcium and magnesium |
| **Leachate** | Solution collected after draining through media |
