# Glossary: Regenerative Agriculture Systems

**Course 405: Regenerative Agriculture Systems**

---

## A

**Acidification** - Environmental impact category measuring the potential for emissions to lower pH of soil and water, primarily from ammonia (NH₃), nitrogen oxides (NOx), and sulfur dioxide (SO₂).

**Active Carbon (POXC)** - Permanganate Oxidizable Carbon; a measure of labile soil carbon that indicates recent organic matter additions and microbial activity.

**Aerated Compost Tea** - Liquid extract of compost produced by steeping compost in aerated water with microbial foods, creating a biologically active solution for plant and soil application.

**Agroecology** - The integration of ecological principles into agricultural system design and management; the science of sustainable agriculture.

**Agroforestry** - Intentional integration of trees with crops and/or livestock to create multi-functional landscapes with environmental and economic benefits.

**Alley Cropping** - Agroforestry system with rows of trees or shrubs with crops grown in the alleys between rows.

**Allocation (LCA)** - Method of partitioning environmental impacts among multiple products from a single system (e.g., fish and vegetables from aquaponics).

**Anaerobic Digestion** - Biological process in which microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas (methane and CO₂) and digestate.

**Aquifer Recharge** - Process of water infiltrating into underground aquifers, can be natural or managed through infiltration basins and swales.

---

## B

**Banker Plants** - Host plants that maintain non-pest prey populations to support beneficial insects in biocontrol programs.

**Biochar** - Stable carbon-rich material produced by pyrolysis (heating without oxygen) of organic materials, used as soil amendment for carbon sequestration and soil improvement.

**Biodiversity** - The variety of life at all levels: genetic diversity within species, species diversity within ecosystems, and ecosystem diversity across landscapes.

**Biogas** - Gaseous fuel composed primarily of methane (CH₄) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) produced by anaerobic digestion of organic matter.

**Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD)** - Amount of oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water; indicator of water quality and organic pollution.

**Biomimicry** - Design approach that emulates nature's forms, processes, and ecosystems to solve human challenges.

**Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL)** - Larvae of Hermetia illucens used for bioconversion of organic waste into protein (for animal feed) and frass (fertilizer).

**Bulk Density** - Mass of dry soil per unit volume, indicating degree of compaction; lower values indicate better soil structure.

---

## C

**Carbon Negative** - System or process that removes more carbon from the atmosphere than it emits; net carbon sink.

**Carbon Sequestration** - Long-term storage of carbon in soils, biomass, or geological formations, removing CO₂ from the atmosphere.

**Cascade Design** - Principle of using outputs at successively lower quality levels as inputs for appropriate processes, maximizing resource utilization.

**Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)** - Soil's ability to hold and exchange positively charged nutrients (cations); higher CEC indicates greater nutrient holding capacity.

**Closed-Loop System** - Production system where waste outputs are captured and recycled as inputs, minimizing external inputs and waste discharge.

**Composting** - Aerobic biological decomposition of organic matter into stable humus-like material (compost) for soil amendment.

**Conservation Biological Control** - Enhancing naturally occurring beneficial organisms through habitat provision and management practices rather than releases or imports.

**Constructed Wetland** - Engineered wetland system for wastewater treatment using natural processes of vegetation, soils, and microorganisms.

**Context-Based Management** - Decision-making approach that adapts practices to local conditions rather than applying universal solutions.

**Coppice** - Forestry practice of cutting trees/shrubs to ground level to stimulate new shoot growth, used for biomass production.

---

## D

**Detritivore** - Organism that feeds on dead organic matter (detritus), contributing to decomposition and nutrient cycling.

**Digestate** - Nutrient-rich material remaining after anaerobic digestion, used as fertilizer.

**Dry Matter** - Weight of material after all moisture has been removed; used for standardized comparisons of organic materials.

---

## E

**Ecological Outcome Verification (EOV)** - Monitoring protocol that verifies actual ecological improvements rather than just practice adoption; used in Land to Market certification.

**Economic Allocation** - LCA allocation method based on the market value of co-products.

**Ecosystem Services** - Benefits humans obtain from ecosystems, including provisioning (food), regulating (climate, water), supporting (nutrient cycling), and cultural services.

**Eutrophication** - Over-enrichment of water bodies with nutrients (primarily nitrogen and phosphorus), leading to excessive algae growth and oxygen depletion.

**Exudates** - Organic compounds (sugars, amino acids, organic acids) released by plant roots to feed soil microorganisms.

---

## F

**Feedstock** - Raw material input to a process (e.g., organic waste fed to compost or digester).

**First-Flush Diverter** - Device that diverts the initial, most contaminated portion of rainwater runoff away from storage tanks.

**Forest Garden (Food Forest)** - Multi-layered perennial planting designed to mimic forest structure while producing food, fiber, and other products.

**Frass** - Insect excrement and leftover material; specifically refers to waste from black soldier fly larvae rearing used as fertilizer.

**Functional Diversity** - Variety of ecological roles or functions performed by organisms in an ecosystem.

**Functional Unit** - Quantified performance of a product system for use as a reference basis in Life Cycle Assessment (e.g., "1 kg of tomatoes").

**Fungal:Bacterial Ratio (F:B)** - Ratio of fungal to bacterial biomass in soil, indicating successional stage and decomposition characteristics.

---

## G

**Glomalin** - Sticky protein produced by mycorrhizal fungi that helps bind soil particles into stable aggregates and stores carbon.

**Green Manure** - Crop grown primarily to be incorporated into soil to add organic matter and nutrients; often a cover crop.

**Greywater** - Wastewater from sinks, showers, and laundry (excludes toilet water); can be treated and reused for irrigation.

**Gross Margin** - Revenue minus variable costs, expressed as percentage of revenue; indicates profitability before fixed costs.

---

## H

**Holistic Management** - Decision-making framework developed by Allan Savory that considers whole-system goals encompassing quality of life, production, and future resource base.

**Humus** - Stable, highly decomposed organic matter in soil that contributes to soil structure, water holding capacity, and nutrient retention.

---

## I

**Infiltration** - Movement of water from surface into soil; measured as rate (inches or mm per hour).

**Integration** - Deliberate connection of system components to create beneficial relationships and resource flows.

**Ion Exchange** - Process of selectively capturing charged nutrients (ions) from solution using natural or synthetic materials.

---

## K

**Keyline Design** - Water management system based on topography that distributes water across landscapes to maximize infiltration and productivity.

---

## L

**Land to Market** - Regenerative agriculture verification program administered by Savory Institute that verifies ecological outcomes through soil health and landscape monitoring.

**Leaching** - Downward movement of water-soluble nutrients beyond root zone, often resulting in nutrient loss and groundwater contamination.

**Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)** - Comprehensive methodology for evaluating environmental impacts of products/systems from "cradle to grave" following ISO 14040/14044 standards.

**Liquid Carbon Pathway** - Process where plants transfer photosynthesized sugars to roots as exudates, feeding soil microbes that produce stable carbon compounds and soil aggregates.

---

## M

**Mass Balance** - Accounting of all material or nutrient inputs, outputs, and changes in storage for a defined system; based on conservation of mass principle.

**Material Flow Analysis (MFA)** - Systematic assessment of flows and stocks of materials within a system over time.

**Microarthropods** - Small arthropods (mites, springtails, etc.) that fragment organic matter and graze on microbes in soil.

**Mineralization** - Microbial conversion of organic nutrients into inorganic, plant-available forms (e.g., organic N to NH₄⁺ or NO₃⁻).

**Monitoring, Reporting, Verification (MRV)** - System for tracking, documenting, and third-party confirmation of environmental outcomes, especially for carbon credits.

**Multistrata** - Multiple vegetation layers (canopy, understory, shrub, herbaceous, ground cover) as in forest gardens and agroforestry.

**Mycorrhizal Fungi** - Beneficial fungi that form symbiotic relationships with plant roots, exchanging nutrients (especially phosphorus) for plant sugars.

---

## N

**Nematodes** - Microscopic roundworms in soil; include beneficial bacterial-feeders, fungal-feeders, and predators, as well as some root-feeding pests.

**Net Primary Productivity** - Rate of plant biomass production in an ecosystem; often higher in diverse regenerative systems.

**Nitrification** - Microbial conversion of ammonium (NH₄⁺) to nitrite (NO₂⁻) and then nitrate (NO₃⁻).

**Nitrogen Fixation** - Biological conversion of atmospheric nitrogen gas (N₂) into plant-available forms by bacteria in legume root nodules or free-living organisms.

**No-Till** - Agricultural practice of growing crops without mechanical soil disturbance (tillage), preserving soil structure and biology.

**Nutrient Cycling** - Movement of nutrients through biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components of ecosystems via processes like mineralization, uptake, and decomposition.

**Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE)** - Ratio of nutrients in harvested products to total nutrient inputs, expressed as percentage.

---

## O

**Organic Matter (OM)** - Material of biological origin in soil, including living organisms, fresh residues, and decomposed humus.

---

## P

**Parasitoid** - Insect that lays eggs in or on host insects, with larvae consuming the host; important biological control agents.

**Particulate Organic Matter (POM)** - Partially decomposed organic matter in soil; intermediate stage between fresh residues and humus.

**Pathogen** - Organism that causes disease in plants, animals, or humans.

**Perennial** - Plant that lives for more than two years, as opposed to annuals; critical for regenerative systems due to continuous living roots.

**Permanganate Oxidizable Carbon (POXC)** - See Active Carbon.

**Permaculture** - Design system based on ecological patterns and principles, emphasizing permanent (perennial) agriculture and culture.

**pH** - Measure of acidity/alkalinity on scale of 0-14; most agricultural plants prefer 6.0-7.5.

**Polyculture** - Growing multiple crop species in the same space simultaneously or in rotation; opposite of monoculture.

**Protozoa** - Single-celled organisms that graze on bacteria in soil, releasing plant-available nitrogen.

**Pyrolysis** - Thermal decomposition of organic material at high temperature in absence of oxygen, producing biochar, bio-oil, and syngas.

---

## R

**Regenerative Agriculture** - Farming and grazing practices that actively restore and improve ecosystem health, focusing on soil regeneration, biodiversity, water cycles, and ecosystem services.

**Regenerative Organic Certification (ROC)** - Certification standard with three pillars: soil health, animal welfare, and social fairness; builds on USDA Organic baseline.

**Resilience** - Ability of a system to maintain function and recover from disturbances.

**Reverse Osmosis** - Water filtration process using semi-permeable membrane to remove dissolved solids, concentrating nutrients in reject stream.

**Riparian Buffer** - Vegetated area along waterways that filters runoff, stabilizes banks, and provides wildlife habitat.

---

## S

**Sensitivity Analysis** - Testing how changes in key variables (revenue, costs, yields) affect outcomes in financial models.

**Shannon Diversity Index** - Mathematical measure of species diversity considering both richness and evenness.

**Silvopasture** - Agroforestry practice integrating trees, forage, and livestock grazing.

**Slake Test** - Simple test of aggregate stability where dry soil is placed in water to observe dispersion.

**Soil Food Web** - Complex community of soil organisms and their feeding relationships, including bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and larger organisms.

**Soil Health** - Capacity of soil to function as a living ecosystem supporting plants, animals, and humans; encompasses physical, chemical, and biological properties.

**Soil Organic Carbon (SOC)** - Carbon component of soil organic matter; approximately 58% of total organic matter weight.

**Soil Respiration** - Carbon dioxide released from soil due to microbial and root respiration; indicator of biological activity.

**Struvite** - Mineral (magnesium ammonium phosphate) that can be precipitated from wastewater to recover nitrogen and phosphorus.

**Substrate-Induced Respiration** - Method of measuring microbial biomass by providing substrate and measuring CO₂ production rate.

**Sustainable Agriculture** - Farming practices that maintain resource base and productivity indefinitely without degradation.

**Swale** - Shallow depression or ditch on contour designed to slow water runoff and promote infiltration.

**System Boundary** - Defined limits of what is included in a system analysis (LCA, material flow analysis, etc.).

**Systems Thinking** - Approach to understanding interconnections, feedback loops, and emergent properties of complex systems.

---

## T

**Thermophilic** - Heat-loving; refers to microorganisms that thrive at high temperatures (45-70°C) during hot composting phase.

**Tillage** - Mechanical disturbance of soil; practices range from intensive (moldboard plow) to none (no-till).

**Total Suspended Solids (TSS)** - Solid particles suspended in water; indicator of water quality.

**Trophic Level** - Position in food chain/web (producers, primary consumers, secondary consumers, etc.).

---

## V

**Valorization** - Process of creating value from waste materials; converting waste into useful products.

**Vermicompost** - Compost produced by earthworm digestion of organic matter; premium soil amendment rich in nutrients and beneficial microbes.

**Vermiculture** - Raising earthworms for production purposes (composting, protein, etc.).

---

## W

**Water Footprint** - Total volume of freshwater used to produce goods/services, including blue (surface/ground), green (rain), and grey (pollution) water.

**Water Holding Capacity** - Amount of water soil can retain against gravity; increases with organic matter content.

**Watershed** - Land area that drains to a common water body; unit for managing water resources and ecological health.

---

## Z

**Zero-Waste** - System design goal where all material outputs are captured and utilized, eliminating waste to landfill.

**Zone (Permaculture)** - Planning tool that organizes landscape based on frequency of use and need for attention, from Zone 0 (home) to Zone 5 (wilderness).
