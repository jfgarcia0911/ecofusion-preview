## Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary distinction between sustainable and regenerative agriculture?

A) Sustainable uses organic inputs while regenerative allows synthetic inputs
B) Sustainable maintains current resource levels while regenerative actively improves ecosystems
C) Sustainable focuses on profit while regenerative focuses on people
D) There is no meaningful distinction between the two approaches

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sustainable agriculture aims to maintain current conditions without depleting resources, while regenerative agriculture goes further by actively improving and restoring ecosystem health, soil quality, biodiversity, and water cycles over time.

---

### Question 2
Which of the following is NOT one of the six core principles of regenerative agriculture?

A) Minimize soil disturbance
B) Maximize monoculture efficiency
C) Maximize biodiversity
D) Maintain living roots year-round

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The six core principles are: minimize soil disturbance, maximize soil cover, maximize biodiversity, maintain living roots, integrate animals, and use context-based management. Monoculture contradicts the biodiversity principle.

---

### Question 3
In the holistic management framework, what are the three components of a holistic goal?

A) Profit, people, and planet
B) Quality of life, forms of production, and future resource base
C) Soil health, water quality, and biodiversity
D) Short-term gains, medium-term stability, and long-term sustainability

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** According to Allan Savory's holistic management framework, a holistic goal consists of: quality of life (what matters to stakeholders), forms of production (what will be produced), and future resource base (desired ecosystem state).

---

### Question 4
What is a key advantage of integrating CEA with outdoor regenerative systems?

A) CEA eliminates the need for outdoor production
B) Complementary production enables year-round supply and risk diversification
C) Outdoor systems are only useful for composting CEA waste
D) Integration is primarily for aesthetic purposes

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Integrating CEA with outdoor systems creates synergies including: year-round vs. seasonal production, consistent quality vs. lower-cost volume, risk diversification, closed-loop nutrient cycling, and complementary resource utilization.

---

### Question 5
Which paradigm shift characterizes the transition from industrial to regenerative agriculture?

A) Simplification → Complexity
B) Perennial crops → Annual crops
C) Organic matter building → Soil mining
D) Local markets → Global markets

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Regenerative agriculture shifts from industrial simplification (monocultures, standardization) to ecological complexity (polycultures, diverse communities, integrated systems) that mimics natural ecosystems.

---

### Question 6
In a systems thinking approach, what is "emergence"?

A) The gradual appearance of new pests
B) System properties that exceed the sum of individual parts
C) The process of bringing products to market
D) Emergency response protocols for farm incidents

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Emergence is a key concept in systems thinking where the whole system exhibits properties and behaviors that cannot be predicted by examining individual components alone—the system is greater than the sum of its parts.

### Question 7
According to the lesson, approximately how much economic value do global pollination services provide annually?

A) $12-30 million
B) $1.25-3 billion
C) $125-300 billion
D) $1.25-3 trillion

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Global pollination services are estimated to provide $125-300 billion in economic value annually, highlighting the critical importance of pollinators and biodiversity to agricultural productivity.

### Question 8
What is the primary goal of "conservation biological control" in regenerative systems?

A) Purchasing and releasing beneficial insects in large numbers
B) Enhancing existing beneficial insect populations through habitat and management
C) Using synthetic pesticides selectively
D) Importing exotic natural enemies for pest control

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Conservation biological control focuses on enhancing naturally occurring beneficial insect populations through habitat provision, reduced pesticide use, alternative food sources, and appropriate management—rather than relying on releases or imports.

---

### Question 9
Which integration strategy involves using waste from one subsystem as input for another?

A) Functional complementarity
B) Temporal separation
C) Material flow integration
D) Competitive exclusion

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Material flow integration is the strategy of connecting subsystems so that outputs (including "waste") from one become valuable inputs for another, creating closed-loop nutrient cycling and resource efficiency.

---

### Question 10
On the conventional-sustainable-regenerative spectrum, what is the typical carbon impact trajectory?

A) All three approaches are carbon neutral
B) Conventional: net emitter; Sustainable: carbon neutral; Regenerative: carbon negative
C) Only regenerative agriculture produces any carbon emissions
D) Carbon impact is the same across all three approaches

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Conventional agriculture is typically a net carbon emitter (releases more than it sequesters), sustainable agriculture aims for carbon neutrality (balance), while regenerative agriculture seeks to be carbon negative (sequesters more than it releases) through soil building and ecosystem restoration.
