# Lesson 2: Soil Health and Carbon Sequestration

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Understand the structure and function of the soil food web
2. Identify and measure key soil health indicators
3. Explain carbon sequestration mechanisms in agricultural soils
4. Design and implement soil organic matter building strategies
5. Develop microbial inoculation and enhancement programs
6. Apply soil health principles to integrated CEA-outdoor systems

## Introduction

Soil health is the foundation of regenerative agriculture. Healthy soil is not merely a growing medium but a living ecosystem teeming with billions of organisms that create the conditions for plant growth, nutrient cycling, water management, and carbon storage. Understanding and enhancing soil biology is critical for building regenerative systems that sequester carbon, increase resilience, and improve productivity over time.

For operations integrating CEA with outdoor regenerative systems, soil health principles inform composting strategies, media amendments, and the design of closed-loop nutrient cycling systems.

## 1. Soil Biology and the Soil Food Web

### The Soil Food Web Structure

```
Soil Food Web Hierarchy

TROPHIC LEVEL          ORGANISMS                    FUNCTION
=============          =========                    ========

[Primary Producers]
                       Plants                       - Photosynthesize
                       Algae, Mosses                - Produce exudates
                                                    - Create biomass

        |
        v (exudates, litter)

[Decomposers]
                       Bacteria                     - Decompose organic matter
                       Fungi                        - Cycle nutrients
                       Actinomycetes                - Produce antibiotics

        |
        v (consume)

[First Consumers]
                       Protozoa                     - Graze bacteria
                       Nematodes                    - Release nutrients
                       Microarthropods              - Fragment organic matter

        |
        v (consume)

[Second Consumers]
                       Predatory nematodes          - Control populations
                       Predatory mites              - Maintain balance
                       Springtails                  - Aerate soil

        |
        v (consume)

[Third Consumers]
                       Larger arthropods            - Mix soil layers
                       Earthworms                   - Create structure
                       Beetles                      - Final decomposition
```

### Key Functional Groups

**1. Bacteria (10^9 per gram of soil)**

Types and Functions:
- **Decomposers**: Break down simple compounds (sugars, proteins)
- **Mutualists**: N-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium, Azotobacter)
- **Pathogens**: Disease-causing organisms (usually <1%)
- **Actinomycetes**: Produce antibiotics, decompose complex compounds

Bacterial Characteristics:
- Rapid reproduction (20 min doubling time)
- Prefer young, labile organic matter
- Dominant in disturbed, bacterial-dominated systems
- Nutrient cycling: Fast, readily available nutrients

**2. Fungi (meters of hyphae per gram of soil)**

Types and Functions:
- **Saprophytic**: Decompose complex organic matter (lignin, cellulose)
- **Mycorrhizal**: Partner with plant roots for nutrient exchange
  - Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF): 80% of plants
  - Ectomycorrhizal: Trees and woody plants
- **Pathogenic**: Small percentage, controlled by diversity

Fungal Characteristics:
- Slower growth, longer-lived structures
- Decompose complex, recalcitrant organic matter
- Dominant in undisturbed, perennial systems
- Create soil aggregates and stability

**3. Protozoa (10,000-100,000 per gram)**

Functions:
- Graze on bacteria (consume 1000s per day)
- Release plant-available nitrogen (30-40% of consumption)
- Regulate bacterial populations
- Improve nutrient cycling efficiency

Types: Flagellates, amoebae, ciliates

**4. Nematodes (10-100 per gram)**

Functional Groups:
- **Bacterial-feeders**: Most abundant, rapid nutrient cycling
- **Fungal-feeders**: Slower cycling, stable nutrients
- **Predatory**: Consume other nematodes, balance populations
- **Root-feeders**: Usually <10%, pathogenic potential
- **Omnivores**: Generalists

**5. Microarthropods**

Functions:
- Fragment organic matter (increase surface area)
- Graze on fungi and bacteria
- Distribute microbes throughout soil
- Create pore spaces and aeration

Types: Mites, springtails, beetles

**6. Earthworms**

Functions:
- Mix soil layers (bioturbation)
- Create stable aggregates
- Improve water infiltration
- Enhance nutrient availability

Types:
- Epigeic: Surface litter feeders
- Endogeic: Topsoil feeders
- Anecic: Deep burrowers (most beneficial)

### Fungal to Bacterial Ratio (F:B)

```
Fungal:Bacterial Ratio by Ecosystem

Ecosystem Type          F:B Ratio    Characteristics
==============          =========    ===============
Row crops               0.1-0.3      Bacterial-dominated
                                     Annual disturbance
                                     Fast nutrient cycling

Grasslands/Pasture      0.3-1.0      Balanced systems
                                     Perennial roots
                                     Moderate cycling

Deciduous forest        5-10         Fungal-dominated
                                     Undisturbed
                                     Slow, stable cycling

Coniferous forest       10-100       Highly fungal
                                     Acid soils
                                     Very stable

Optimal Regenerative:   1-5          Diverse, resilient
                                     Carbon storage
                                     Nutrient efficiency
```

**Managing F:B Ratio:**
- Increase fungi: Woody mulch, reduce tillage, perennials
- Increase bacteria: Green manures, compost, disturbance
- Balance: Diverse inputs, minimal disturbance, integration

## 2. Carbon Sequestration Mechanisms

### The Soil Carbon Cycle

```
Soil Carbon Dynamics

ATMOSPHERE (CO2: 400+ ppm)
        |
        v (photosynthesis)

PLANT BIOMASS
- Shoots: 40-60% C
- Roots: 40-60% C
        |
        +---> Exudates (10-40% of photosynthate)
        |         |
        |         v
        |     ROOT ZONE CARBON
        |     - Feeds soil food web
        |     - Creates aggregates
        |     - Fast cycling
        |
        v (senescence, harvest)

LITTER & RESIDUE
        |
        v (decomposition)

SOIL ORGANIC MATTER (SOM)
        |
        +---> PARTICULATE OM (POM)
        |     - Partially decomposed
        |     - 2-50 years residence
        |     - Labile carbon pool
        |
        +---> HUMUS
        |     - Highly processed
        |     - 50-1000 years residence
        |     - Slow carbon pool
        |
        +---> RECALCITRANT C
              - Charcoal, aggregates
              - 1000+ years residence
              - Stable carbon pool
```

### Carbon Sequestration Pathways

**1. Liquid Carbon Pathway**

Process:
1. Plants photosynthesize, produce sugars
2. 10-40% of sugars → root exudates
3. Exudates feed soil microbes
4. Microbes produce glomalin and other compounds
5. Stable carbon compounds coat soil particles
6. Aggregates form, protecting carbon from decomposition

Key Players:
- Mycorrhizal fungi (glomalin production)
- Root exudates (continuous carbon input)
- Soil aggregates (physical protection)

Management:
- Maintain living roots year-round
- Minimize soil disturbance
- Support mycorrhizal networks

**2. Particulate Organic Matter Pathway**

Process:
1. Plant litter and residue on soil surface
2. Soil fauna fragment material
3. Fungi and bacteria decompose
4. Partially decomposed material incorporated
5. Carbon temporarily stored in POM fraction

Management:
- Maximize surface residue cover
- Diverse plant materials (C:N ratios)
- Reduce tillage to preserve POM

**3. Mineral-Associated Organic Matter**

Process:
1. Dissolved organic carbon from decomposition
2. Binds to clay particles
3. Protected from decomposition by mineral associations
4. Very stable, long-term carbon storage

Management:
- Build soil aggregates
- Increase clay-humus complex
- Add diverse organic amendments

**4. Aggregate Formation and Stabilization**

```
Soil Aggregate Hierarchy

MACRO-AGGREGATES (>250 μm)
    Binding Agents: Roots, fungal hyphae
    Carbon: POM, fresh residue
    Stability: Temporary (months-years)
        |
        v (compression, wetting/drying)

MICRO-AGGREGATES (53-250 μm)
    Binding Agents: Microbial products, glomalin
    Carbon: Processed OM, humus
    Stability: Persistent (years-decades)
        |
        v (strong bonding)

SILT+CLAY+OM COMPLEXES (<53 μm)
    Binding Agents: Polyvalent cations, clay-humus
    Carbon: Mineral-associated OM
    Stability: Very stable (decades-centuries)
```

## 3. Soil Health Indicators and Assessment

### Physical Indicators

**1. Soil Structure and Aggregation**

Test: Slake Test
- Place dry soil aggregate in water
- Observe stability over 5 minutes
- Score: 0 (disperses immediately) to 5 (no change)

Interpretation:
- Score 0-1: Poor structure, high erosion risk
- Score 2-3: Moderate structure, needs improvement
- Score 4-5: Excellent structure, stable aggregates

**2. Water Infiltration**

Test: Single-Ring Infiltrometer
- Insert ring 3-6 inches into soil
- Add water, measure infiltration rate
- Record rate over time (inches/hour)

Targets:
- Poor: <0.5 in/hr
- Fair: 0.5-2.0 in/hr
- Good: 2.0-4.0 in/hr
- Excellent: >4.0 in/hr

**3. Soil Compaction**

Test: Penetrometer
- Insert probe into soil
- Measure resistance (psi)
- Map compaction layers

Interpretation:
- <150 psi: No restriction
- 150-300 psi: Root growth slowed
- >300 psi: Root growth severely restricted

**4. Bulk Density**

Measurement:
- Collect known volume core sample
- Dry and weigh
- Calculate: Bulk Density = Dry Weight / Volume

Targets:
- Sandy soils: <1.6 g/cm³
- Loam soils: <1.4 g/cm³
- Clay soils: <1.2 g/cm³

### Chemical Indicators

**1. Organic Matter Content**

Methods:
- Loss on Ignition (LOI)
- Walkley-Black (wet oxidation)
- Spectroscopy (VNIR, MIR)

Targets:
- Poor: <2%
- Fair: 2-4%
- Good: 4-6%
- Excellent: >6%

**2. pH**

Target Ranges by Crop Type:
- Most vegetables: 6.0-7.0
- Blueberries, potatoes: 4.5-5.5
- Legumes: 6.5-7.5
- General range: 6.0-7.0

**3. Nutrient Levels**

Test: Standard soil test + micronutrients
- N, P, K (primary macronutrients)
- Ca, Mg, S (secondary macronutrients)
- Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo (micronutrients)

Interpretation: Compare to crop-specific sufficiency ranges

**4. Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)**

Indicates:
- Nutrient holding capacity
- Buffering capacity
- Soil fertility potential

Typical Ranges:
- Sandy soils: 5-15 meq/100g
- Loam soils: 10-25 meq/100g
- Clay soils: 20-40 meq/100g
- Organic soils: 40-100+ meq/100g

### Biological Indicators

**1. Microbial Biomass**

Methods:
- Chloroform fumigation extraction
- Substrate-induced respiration
- Phospholipid fatty acid (PLFA) analysis

Targets:
- Bacteria: 100-1000 μg C/g soil
- Fungi: 100-500 μg C/g soil
- Total: 500-2000 μg C/g soil

**2. Soil Respiration**

Test: Solvita CO2 Burst
- Measure CO2 evolved from rewetted soil
- Indicates microbial activity

Interpretation:
- <20 ppm: Very low activity
- 20-50 ppm: Low activity
- 50-100 ppm: Moderate activity
- >100 ppm: High activity

**3. Active Carbon**

Test: Permanganate Oxidizable Carbon (POXC)
- Measures labile carbon fraction
- Indicator of recent management effects

Targets:
- <300 ppm: Low
- 300-500 ppm: Medium
- 500-800 ppm: High
- >800 ppm: Very high

**4. Biological Diversity**

Methods:
- Visual observation (earthworms, arthropods)
- Microscopy (bacteria, fungi, protozoa)
- Molecular methods (DNA sequencing)

Assessment:
- Earthworm count: >10 per shovel = excellent
- Fungal hyphae: Visible under microscope = good
- Protozoa: Multiple types = diverse system

## 4. Soil Organic Matter Building Strategies

### Strategy 1: Maximize Photosynthesis

**Approaches:**
1. **Extend Growing Season**
   - Cover crops in off-season
   - Perennial crops and pastures
   - Season extension structures

2. **Increase Diversity**
   - Multi-species cover crop mixes
   - Crop rotations
   - Intercropping and polycultures

3. **Optimize Plant Health**
   - Adequate nutrition
   - Water management
   - Pest and disease control

**Expected Impact**: +0.3-0.8% OM per year

### Strategy 2: Minimize Soil Disturbance

**Tillage Impact on SOM:**

```
Tillage Effects on Soil Carbon

INTENSIVE TILLAGE           NO-TILL
================           =======

- Disrupts aggregates      - Preserves aggregates
- Exposes OM to air        - Protects OM
- Kills fungal networks    - Builds fungal networks
- Oxidizes carbon          - Accumulates carbon
- Fast nutrient release    - Slow nutrient release

Carbon Loss Rate:          Carbon Gain Rate:
-2% to -5% per year       +0.5% to +1.5% per year
```

**Reduced Tillage Strategies:**
1. No-till with high-residue crops
2. Strip-till (disturb planting zone only)
3. Shallow tillage (<4 inches)
4. Zone or keyline subsoiling (without inversion)
5. Permanent bed systems

**Expected Impact**: +0.3-0.6% OM per year

### Strategy 3: Organic Amendment Application

**Compost Application:**

Calculation Example:
- Apply 20 tons/acre compost (30% OM, 15% C)
- Total carbon applied: 20 × 0.30 × 0.15 = 0.9 tons C/acre
- Stabilization efficiency: 20-30%
- Stable carbon added: 0.18-0.27 tons C/acre/year

**Amendment Types and Characteristics:**

| Amendment | C:N Ratio | OM % | Stability | Application Rate |
|-----------|-----------|------|-----------|------------------|
| Finished compost | 15-25:1 | 30-60% | High | 5-20 tons/acre |
| Vermicompost | 10-15:1 | 40-70% | High | 2-10 tons/acre |
| Biochar | >100:1 | 80-90% | Very high | 1-5 tons/acre |
| Manure (raw) | 10-30:1 | 20-40% | Low | 10-30 tons/acre |
| Green waste | 25-40:1 | 50-70% | Medium | 10-30 tons/acre |
| Wood chips | 100-500:1 | 50-60% | High | 2-6 inches depth |

**Expected Impact**: +0.5-1.5% OM per year

### Strategy 4: Living Root Systems

**Continuous Living Roots:**

Benefits:
1. Constant exudate production (carbon pumping)
2. Mycorrhizal network maintenance
3. Soil structure preservation
4. Year-round nutrient cycling

Implementation:
- Perennial crops and pastures
- Multi-species cover crop cocktails
- Relay cropping and intercropping
- Living mulches

**Root Exudation Rates:**
- 10-40% of photosynthate to roots
- 30-60% of root carbon as exudates
- Result: 3-24% of total photosynthate to soil
- Annual rate: 500-2000 kg C/ha/year

**Expected Impact**: +0.4-1.0% OM per year

### Strategy 5: Animal Integration

**Grazing Management for Soil Building:**

```
Adaptive Multi-Paddock Grazing

TRADITIONAL                 REGENERATIVE
===========                ============

Low density (1-5 AU/acre)  High density (50-200 AU/acre)
Long duration (30-365 days) Short duration (1-3 days)
Continuous grazing         Rotational grazing
Selective grazing          Even utilization
Bare soil and erosion      Soil cover and building

Soil Impact:               Soil Impact:
- SOM decline             - SOM increase
- Compaction              - Improved structure
- Nutrient export         - Nutrient distribution
- Root damage             - Root stimulation

Carbon Result:             Carbon Result:
-0.5 t C/ha/year          +1.0-3.0 t C/ha/year
```

**Mechanisms:**
1. Hoof action (seed-soil contact, litter incorporation)
2. Manure and urine (fertility, microbial food)
3. Grazing stimulation (root exudation, tillering)
4. Trampling (litter breakdown, soil coverage)

**Expected Impact**: +0.5-2.0% OM per year

### Integrated Soil Building Program

**Year 1-2: Foundation**
- Baseline soil testing
- Reduce/eliminate tillage
- Establish cover crops
- Begin compost application (5-10 tons/acre)

**Year 3-5: Building**
- Diverse cover crop mixes
- Animal integration (if applicable)
- Perennial strips or alley cropping
- Continued amendments (3-5 tons/acre)

**Year 6-10: Optimization**
- Fine-tune management based on monitoring
- Scale successful practices
- Maximize living roots and diversity
- Reduced amendment needs (maintenance only)

**Expected 10-Year SOM Increase: 2-5%**

## 5. Microbial Inoculation and Enhancement

### Beneficial Microorganism Types

**1. Mycorrhizal Fungi**

Types:
- Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)
- Ectomycorrhizal Fungi

Functions:
- Nutrient uptake (especially P, Zn, Cu)
- Water acquisition and drought tolerance
- Disease suppression
- Soil aggregation (glomalin production)

Application:
- Inoculate seeds or transplants
- Apply to soil at planting
- Rate: 10-20 spores per seed/transplant
- Avoid high P conditions (>50 ppm Olsen P)

**2. Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria**

Types:
- Rhizobium (legume-specific)
- Azotobacter (free-living)
- Azospirillum (associative)

Functions:
- Convert atmospheric N2 to plant-available forms
- Reduce fertilizer requirements
- Improve soil fertility

Application:
- Inoculate legume seeds before planting
- Apply to soil or foliar spray for free-living types
- Rate: 10^6-10^9 CFU per seed

**3. Phosphate-Solubilizing Bacteria**

Types:
- Bacillus spp.
- Pseudomonas spp.

Functions:
- Solubilize bound phosphorus
- Improve P availability
- Reduce P fertilizer requirements

Application:
- Soil drench or root dip
- Rate: 10^7-10^9 CFU/ml

**4. Biocontrol Agents**

Types:
- Trichoderma (fungi)
- Bacillus subtilis (bacteria)
- Streptomyces (actinomycetes)

Functions:
- Suppress plant pathogens
- Induce systemic resistance
- Compete for resources

Application:
- Seed treatment, soil drench, or foliar spray
- Rate: 10^6-10^8 CFU/g or ml

### Compost Tea and Extract Production

**Compost Tea (Aerated):**

Recipe:
- 5 gallons water (chlorine-free)
- 2-3 cups high-quality compost
- 1-2 Tbsp molasses or kelp
- Aerate for 24-48 hours

Microbial Counts (target):
- Bacteria: >1000 μg/ml
- Fungi: >10 μg/ml
- Protozoa: >10,000/ml

Application:
- Foliar spray: 5-20 gallons/acre
- Soil drench: 10-50 gallons/acre
- Frequency: Monthly during growing season

**Compost Extract (Non-aerated):**

Recipe:
- 5 gallons water
- 2-3 cups compost
- Steep for 3-7 days (no aeration)
- Strain before application

Use:
- Similar to compost tea but lower microbial activity
- More nutrient extraction

### Indigenous Microorganism (IMO) Cultivation

**IMO Collection:**

1. **IMO-1: Collection**
   - Fill wooden box with cooked rice
   - Place in forest or healthy soil area
   - Cover with breathable cloth
   - Collect after 3-7 days (white mold growth)

2. **IMO-2: Multiplication**
   - Mix IMO-1 with equal parts brown sugar
   - Store in container, burp daily
   - Ready in 7-10 days

3. **IMO-3: Scaling**
   - Mix IMO-2 with bran or grain (1:10 ratio)
   - Add moisture (60%)
   - Ferment for 5-7 days

4. **IMO-4: Field Application**
   - Mix IMO-3 with soil (1:10 ratio)
   - Apply to field or add to compost
   - Rate: 100-500 lbs/acre

**Applications:**
- Compost inoculation
- Soil amendment
- Liquid fertilizer base
- Anaerobic digestion starter

### Effective Microorganism (EM) Technology

**EM-1 (Mother Culture):**

Contains:
- Lactic acid bacteria
- Photosynthetic bacteria
- Yeasts
- Actinomycetes
- Fermenting fungi

**Activation (EM-A):**

Recipe:
- 1 part EM-1
- 1 part molasses
- 18 parts water (chlorine-free)
- Ferment for 7-14 days (pH <3.5)

**Applications:**
- Soil drench: 1:500-1:1000 dilution
- Foliar spray: 1:500 dilution
- Compost accelerator: 1:100 dilution
- Bokashi: Undiluted with bran

## Practical Application: Integration with CEA

### Soil Health for Outdoor Components

**1. Outdoor Aquaponics Integration**
- Use compost tea to enhance biofilter microbiology
- Apply IMO to fish waste composting
- Build soil health in planted wetlands for water treatment

**2. Cover Crop Production**
- Grow biomass for CEA compost inputs
- Sequester carbon in outdoor areas
- Provide beneficial insect habitat

**3. Soil-Based Growing Areas**
- High tunnels and greenhouses with soil floors
- Seasonal outdoor production
- Nursery and propagation areas

### Microbiology in Soilless Systems

**Application to CEA:**
1. **Growing Media Inoculation**
   - Add mycorrhizae to coco coir or peat-based media
   - Incorporate compost (5-10% by volume)
   - Use vermicompost tea as root drench

2. **Nutrient Solution Enhancement**
   - Low levels of beneficial bacteria (Bacillus, Pseudomonas)
   - Monitor and manage populations
   - Avoid pathogen buildup

3. **Biofilter Optimization (Aquaponics)**
   - Diverse nitrifying communities
   - Regular compost tea additions
   - Maintain aerobic conditions

**Caution**: Balance between beneficial biology and pathogen risk in closed recirculating systems.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Soil is a living ecosystem** with complex food webs that cycle nutrients, build structure, and sequester carbon
2. **Carbon sequestration** occurs through multiple pathways, primarily via root exudates, residue decomposition, and aggregate formation
3. **Soil health assessment** requires physical, chemical, and biological indicators for comprehensive evaluation
4. **Building soil organic matter** requires integrated strategies: maximizing photosynthesis, minimizing disturbance, applying amendments, maintaining living roots, and integrating animals
5. **Microbial inoculation** can enhance soil function through mycorrhizae, nitrogen-fixers, biocontrol agents, and diverse microbial communities
6. **Integration with CEA** creates opportunities for compost production, microbial management, and closed-loop nutrient cycling
