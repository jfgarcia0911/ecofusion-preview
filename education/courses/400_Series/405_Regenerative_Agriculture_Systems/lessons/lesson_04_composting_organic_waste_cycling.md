# Lesson 4: Composting and Organic Waste Cycling

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Design and manage advanced composting systems for diverse feedstocks
2. Implement vermicomposting systems at various scales
3. Integrate black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) production for waste valorization
4. Optimize anaerobic digestion for energy and nutrient recovery
5. Produce high-quality compost extracts and teas for plant and soil health
6. Calculate waste processing capacity and nutrient recovery rates

## Introduction

Organic waste cycling is a cornerstone of regenerative agriculture, transforming what conventional systems consider "waste" into valuable inputs for soil building, plant nutrition, and ecosystem health. Advanced composting and waste valorization systems enable farms to close nutrient loops, reduce external inputs, improve soil health, and create additional revenue streams. This lesson explores cutting-edge approaches to composting, vermicomposting, insect-based bioconversion, anaerobic digestion, and the production of biologically active soil amendments.

## 1. Advanced Composting Science and Engineering

### Composting Fundamentals

**Aerobic Decomposition Process:**

```
Composting Phases

Phase 1: MESOPHILIC (20-40°C, Days 1-3)
- Bacteria dominate
- Rapid consumption of sugars, proteins
- Temperature rises quickly
- Oxygen demand high

Phase 2: THERMOPHILIC (40-70°C, Days 3-14)
- Heat-loving bacteria
- Breakdown of cellulose, hemicellulose
- Pathogen destruction (>55°C for 3+ days)
- Weed seed kill (>60°C)
- Maximum oxygen and moisture requirements

Phase 3: COOLING (40-20°C, Days 14-30)
- Mesophilic bacteria return
- Fungi colonize
- Actinomycetes produce antibiotics
- Humus formation begins

Phase 4: CURING/MATURATION (20°C, Months 1-6)
- Fungi dominant
- Stable humus formation
- Nutrient stabilization
- Phytotoxin breakdown
- Plant growth enhancement
```

### Critical Parameters

**1. Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio (C:N)**

```
C:N Ratio Management

MATERIAL TYPE         C:N RATIO    FUNCTION IN PILE
=============         =========    ================
Sawdust, wood chips   200-500:1    Carbon source (browns)
Straw                 80-100:1     Carbon, structure
Leaves (dry)          40-80:1      Carbon, moderate structure
Food waste            15-20:1      Nitrogen, moisture
Grass clippings       15-25:1      Nitrogen, moisture
Manure (varies)       10-30:1      Nitrogen, nutrients
Coffee grounds        20:1         Nitrogen, moisture

OPTIMAL PILE C:N: 25-30:1

Blending Formula:
C:N_mix = (W1 × C:N1 + W2 × C:N2) / (W1 + W2)

Example:
50 lbs straw (80:1) + 50 lbs food waste (15:1)
= (50×80 + 50×15) / 100 = 47.5:1 (Too high)

Adjust: 30 lbs straw + 70 lbs food waste
= (30×80 + 70×15) / 100 = 34.5:1 (Better)
```

**2. Moisture Content**

Optimal Range: 50-60%
- <40%: Microbial activity slows
- >65%: Anaerobic conditions, odors

**Squeeze Test:**
- Too dry: Doesn't hold shape, dusty
- Optimal: Holds shape, few drops of water
- Too wet: Water streams out

**3. Oxygen Availability**

Target: >5% O2 throughout pile

Management:
- Passive aeration: Perforated pipes, chimney effect
- Active aeration: Forced air (blowers)
- Turning: Mechanical or manual mixing

**Turning Schedule:**
- Days 1-7: Every 2-3 days (high oxygen demand)
- Days 7-21: Every 5-7 days (moderate demand)
- Days 21+: Every 2-4 weeks (low demand)

**4. Temperature**

```
Temperature Monitoring and Management

TEMPERATURE   CONDITION           ACTION
===========   =========           ======
<40°C         Inactive            - Check C:N ratio
                                  - Check moisture
                                  - Check pile size

40-55°C       Active mesophilic   - Monitor, maintain conditions
                                  - May need turning for heat

55-70°C       Active thermophilic - Ideal for pathogen kill
                                  - Monitor to prevent >70°C
                                  - Turn if needed to cool

>70°C         Too hot             - Turn immediately
                                  - Add dry materials
                                  - Bacteria dying off

<20°C         Curing/finished     - Minimal management
                                  - Allow maturation
```

**5. pH**

Typical progression:
- Initial: 6.0-7.0
- Thermophilic: 8.0-9.0 (ammonia production)
- Finished: 6.5-8.0 (buffered, stable)

### Compost Quality Assessment

**Physical Properties:**
- Color: Dark brown to black
- Odor: Earthy, pleasant
- Texture: Crumbly, friable
- Particle size: <½ inch
- Moisture: 30-50%

**Chemical Properties:**
- C:N ratio: 10-15:1 (finished)
- pH: 6.5-8.0
- Soluble salts (EC): <4.0 dS/m
- Organic matter: >40%
- Nutrients: Variable by feedstock

**Biological Properties:**
- Stable (low respiration rate)
- Mature (low phytotoxicity)
- Diverse microbial community

**Maturity Tests:**
1. **Germination Test**: >80% germination vs. control
2. **Dewar Self-Heating Test**: <20°C temperature rise in 24 hours
3. **Solvita Maturity Index**: >6 (scale 1-8)

## 2. Vermicomposting Systems Design

### Vermiculture Fundamentals

**Species Selection:**

| Species | Temperature Range | Processing Rate | Applications |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Eisenia fetida (Red wigglers) | 15-25°C | High | General vermicomposting |
| Eisenia andrei | 15-25°C | Very high | Commercial operations |
| Lumbricus rubellus | 10-20°C | Medium | Cool climates |
| Eudrilus eugeniae (African nightcrawler) | 20-30°C | Very high | Warm climates, tropical |

**Worm Biology:**
- Consume 50-100% of body weight per day
- Double population every 60-90 days
- Produce castings (worm poop) = vermicompost
- Optimal stocking: 1-2 lbs worms per sq ft

### Vermicomposting System Designs

**1. Flow-Through Systems (Commercial)**

```
Continuous Flow-Through Vermicomposter

[Feed Layer - fresh material] ←─── Continuous feeding
         ↓
[Active Zone - worms working]
         ↓
[Mature Zone - mostly castings]
         ↓
[Harvesting Grate] ───→ Castings removed
         ↓
[Collection Bin]

Advantages:
- Continuous processing
- Minimal worm disturbance
- Efficient harvesting
- Scalable

Dimensions (example):
- Width: 4-8 feet
- Length: 20-100 feet
- Depth: 2-3 feet
```

**2. Wedge Systems**

Process:
1. Start pile on one side
2. Feed only on one edge
3. Worms migrate to fresh food
4. Harvest finished side

**3. Bin Systems (Small-Scale)**

```
Three-Bin Rotation System

[Bin 1]        [Bin 2]        [Bin 3]
Active    →    Migrating  →   Harvesting
(feeding)      (finishing)    (castings)

Cycle: 2-3 months per bin
Continuous production
```

### Feedstock Management

**Suitable Materials:**
- Fruit and vegetable scraps
- Coffee grounds and filters
- Tea bags (remove staples)
- Crushed eggshells
- Shredded cardboard/paper
- Aged manure (not fresh)

**Avoid:**
- Meat, dairy, oils (attract pests, odors)
- Citrus (may repel worms in large amounts)
- Onions, garlic (can be toxic)
- Highly acidic or alkaline materials

**Pre-Processing:**
- Chop/shred to 1-2 inch pieces
- Pre-compost hot materials (3-7 days)
- Balance C:N (25-35:1 optimal)
- Maintain 70-80% moisture

### Vermicompost Quality

**Properties:**
- 5x more N, P, K than regular compost
- High in plant growth hormones
- Rich in beneficial microorganisms
- Enhanced disease suppression
- Excellent soil structure improvement

**Application Rates:**
- Potting mix: 10-30%
- Field application: 2-10 tons/acre
- Topdressing: ¼-½ inch layer
- Compost tea: 1-5% solution

## 3. Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL) Production

### BSFL Biology and Benefits

**Hermetia illucens Characteristics:**
- Adults don't feed (no pest potential)
- Larvae process wide range of organics
- Produce valuable protein (40-45%)
- Produce oil (30-35% fat)
- Process 2-4x their body weight daily
- Reduce waste volume by 50-80%

**System Benefits:**
- Fast processing (2-3 weeks)
- High-value outputs (feed, oil, fertilizer)
- Pathogen reduction
- Odor reduction
- Works with challenging feedstocks

### BSFL Production System

```
BSFL Life Cycle Production

[Adult Colony]
    ↓ (eggs)
[Egg Collection] → 500-900 eggs per female
    ↓
[Egg Hatching] → 3-4 days
    ↓
[Larvae Rearing] → 14-21 days
    ↓
[Pre-Pupae Migration]
    ↓
    +→ [Harvest for Feed/Processing]
    +→ [Adult Production]

Temperature Requirements:
- Larvae: 25-35°C optimal
- Adults: 25-30°C
- Relative humidity: 60-70%
```

**Rearing System Design:**

```
Modular BSFL Rearing Unit

[Feeding Tray]
- Area: 2' × 4' per module
- Depth: 4-6 inches
- Material: Food-grade plastic

[Ramp for Pre-Pupae Migration]
- Slope: 30-40 degrees
- Textured surface
- Leads to collection bucket

[Collection Bucket]
- Larvae self-harvest
- 80-90% separation efficiency

Stocking Rate: 5-10 larvae per cm²
Feeding Rate: 100-200 mg per larva per day
```

**Feedstock Management:**

Suitable:
- Food waste (all types)
- Manure (poultry, swine, cattle)
- Brewery/distillery waste
- Spent growing media
- Plant processing waste

Management:
- Feed daily or every 2-3 days
- Maintain 60-70% moisture
- Monitor temperature (prevent >40°C)
- Control odor with C:N balance

### BSFL Products and Uses

**1. Larvae/Pre-Pupae**
- Animal feed (poultry, fish, swine)
- High protein (40-45% dry weight)
- Good amino acid profile
- Calcium-rich

**2. Frass (Larval Waste)**
- Excellent fertilizer
- NPK varies by feedstock
- Beneficial microorganisms
- Soil amendment

**3. Larval Oil**
- Feed additive
- Biodiesel feedstock
- Industrial applications

## 4. Anaerobic Digestion Integration

### Anaerobic Digestion Process

```
Anaerobic Digestion Stages

Stage 1: HYDROLYSIS
- Complex organics → Simple molecules
- Enzymes break down polymers
- Rate-limiting for most substrates

Stage 2: ACIDOGENESIS
- Simple molecules → Organic acids
- Also produce H₂, CO₂, NH₃
- pH may drop temporarily

Stage 3: ACETOGENESIS
- Organic acids → Acetic acid, H₂, CO₂
- Bacteria sensitive to pH and temperature

Stage 4: METHANOGENESIS
- Acetic acid → CH₄ + CO₂
- H₂ + CO₂ → CH₄
- Produces biogas (50-70% CH₄)
```

### Digester System Types

**1. Plug Flow Digesters**

Suitable for: Dairy manure, high-solids (11-13%)

```
[Feed Input] → [Reactor (horizontal)] → [Effluent Output]
                      ↑
                  [Biogas Collection]

Retention time: 15-20 days
Temperature: 35-40°C (mesophilic)
```

**2. Complete Mix Digesters**

Suitable for: Variable feedstocks, 3-10% solids

```
       [Biogas]
           ↑
    [Mixed Reactor]
       (heated)
    ↑           ↓
[Feed In]   [Effluent Out]

Retention time: 15-30 days
Mixing: Mechanical or biogas recirculation
```

**3. Covered Lagoon Digesters**

Suitable for: Liquid manure, warm climates

**4. Dry Anaerobic Digestion**

Suitable for: Food waste, yard waste, 20-40% solids

### Biogas Production and Use

**Biogas Composition:**
- Methane (CH₄): 50-70%
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂): 30-50%
- Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S): 0-3%
- Water vapor: Saturated
- Trace gases: N₂, O₂, NH₃

**Energy Content:**
- Raw biogas: 20-25 MJ/m³
- Purified methane: 35-40 MJ/m³

**Uses:**
1. **Heat**: Boiler, space heating, greenhouse heating
2. **Electricity**: Generator, CHP (combined heat and power)
3. **Fuel**: Upgraded to biomethane for vehicles
4. **CO₂**: Capture for CEA enrichment

**Yield Estimates:**

| Substrate | Biogas Yield (m³/ton VS) | CH₄ Content |
|-----------|-------------------------|-------------|
| Cow manure | 200-300 | 55-65% |
| Pig manure | 300-500 | 60-70% |
| Poultry manure | 350-550 | 60-70% |
| Food waste | 400-600 | 50-60% |
| Grass silage | 500-700 | 52-55% |
| Spent growing media | 200-400 | 50-60% |

### Digestate Management

**Digestate Characteristics:**
- Stabilized organic matter
- High available nutrients (especially N as NH₄⁺)
- Pathogen reduced
- Odor reduced vs. raw manure
- pH: 7.5-8.5

**Separation:**
- Solid fraction: Compost, bedding, soil amendment
- Liquid fraction: Fertilizer, irrigation water

**Application:**
- Direct field application
- Further composting
- CEA nutrient solution supplement (carefully managed)

## 5. Compost Extract and Tea Production

### Compost Extract (Non-Aerated)

**Production Method:**
1. Mix 1:5-1:10 compost:water ratio
2. Steep 3-14 days without aeration
3. Strain through mesh or burlap
4. Apply within 24 hours

**Characteristics:**
- Nutrient extraction focus
- Lower microbial activity than tea
- More shelf-stable
- Soluble nutrients and humic substances

### Compost Tea (Aerated)

**Production Method:**

```
Compost Tea Brewing System

[Brewing Vessel - 5-500 gallons]
    |
    +─ [Compost in mesh bag - 2-5% by volume]
    +─ [Microbial foods - molasses, kelp, etc.]
    +─ [Water - chlorine-free]
    +─ [Air stones/diffusers] ← Air pump

Process:
1. Fill vessel with chlorine-free water
2. Add compost in mesh bag
3. Add microbial foods
4. Aerate for 24-48 hours
5. Monitor temperature (<30°C), dissolved oxygen (>6 ppm)
6. Assess quality (microscopy)
7. Apply within 4-6 hours of completion
```

**Microbial Food Additions:**

| Ingredient | Amount (per 5 gal) | Purpose |
|------------|-------------------|---------|
| Molasses | 1-2 Tbsp | Bacterial food (simple sugars) |
| Kelp meal | 1-2 Tbsp | Fungal food, micronutrients |
| Fish hydrolysate | 1-2 Tbsp | Protein, diverse food source |
| Humic acid | 1 Tbsp | Nutrient source, microbial food |
| Rock dust | 2-4 Tbsp | Minerals, fungal food |

**Quality Assessment:**

Microscopy (400x magnification):
- Bacteria: >1000 μg/ml
- Flagellates: >10,000/ml
- Beneficial fungi: >5-10 μg/ml
- Ciliates: >1000/ml
- Nematodes: >10/ml
- No ciliates or foul odor = poor quality

**Application:**

Rates:
- Foliar: 5-20 gallons/acre
- Soil drench: 10-50 gallons/acre
- Frequency: Monthly during growing season

Timing:
- Early morning or evening (low UV)
- When stomata open (for foliar)
- Before stress events (inoculation)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Advanced composting** requires careful management of C:N ratio, moisture, oxygen, temperature, and pH for optimal microbial activity and quality outputs
2. **Vermicomposting** produces premium quality amendments and can process sensitive materials unsuitable for thermophilic composting
3. **BSFL systems** valorize diverse waste streams into high-value protein, oil, and fertilizer products
4. **Anaerobic digestion** generates renewable energy while processing high-moisture organics and producing nutrient-rich digestate
5. **Compost extracts and teas** provide soluble nutrients and beneficial microorganisms for plant and soil health
6. **Integrated waste processing** combines multiple technologies to maximize waste valorization and create diverse revenue streams
