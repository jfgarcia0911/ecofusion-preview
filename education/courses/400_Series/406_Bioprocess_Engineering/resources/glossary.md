# Bioprocess Engineering Glossary

## A

**Acetogenesis** - The third stage of anaerobic digestion where volatile fatty acids are converted to acetate, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide by acetogenic bacteria.

**Acidogenesis** - The second stage of anaerobic digestion where simple organic compounds are fermented to volatile fatty acids, CO₂, and H₂.

**Activated Sludge** - A suspended-growth biological wastewater treatment process using aerobic microorganisms to oxidize organic matter.

**Aeration** - The process of bringing air or oxygen into contact with water or other liquids to dissolve oxygen for biological processes.

**Aerobic** - Requiring oxygen; describes processes or organisms that need oxygen to function.

**Alkalinity** - The buffering capacity of water, typically measured as mg/L CaCO₃; critical for pH stability in biological systems.

**Anammox** - Anaerobic ammonia oxidation; a process where ammonia and nitrite are converted directly to nitrogen gas without requiring organic carbon.

**Anaerobic** - Occurring in the absence of oxygen; describes processes or organisms that function without oxygen.

**Anaerobic Digestion (AD)** - The biological breakdown of organic matter in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas (methane and carbon dioxide) and digestate.

**Anoxic** - Containing no dissolved oxygen but containing oxidized compounds (like nitrate) that can serve as electron acceptors.

**Aquaponics** - An integrated system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant growing) in a recirculating environment.

**Arrhenius Equation** - Mathematical relationship describing the temperature dependence of reaction rates and microbial growth.

**Aspect Ratio** - The ratio of height to diameter (H/D) in a bioreactor vessel.

**Autotroph** - An organism that produces complex organic compounds from simple inorganic molecules, often using CO₂ as the carbon source.

## B

**Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)** - A bacterium that produces insecticidal crystal proteins; widely used as a biological pesticide.

**Baffle** - A plate or barrier installed in a bioreactor to improve mixing by disrupting flow patterns.

**Batch Reactor** - A closed system operated with all materials added at the beginning; no continuous input or output during operation.

**Beer-Lambert Law** - Mathematical relationship describing light attenuation through a medium; used to model light penetration in algae cultures.

**Bioaugmentation** - The addition of cultured microorganisms to improve the performance of a biological process.

**Biocontrol Agent** - A beneficial organism used to control pests or diseases; includes bacteria, fungi, viruses, and nematodes.

**Biofilm** - A structured community of microorganisms adhering to a surface and embedded in a self-produced polymer matrix.

**Biofilter** - A biological reactor that removes pollutants (ammonia, organics, odors) through microbial metabolism.

**Biogas** - A gaseous mixture produced by anaerobic digestion, primarily composed of methane (55-70%) and carbon dioxide (30-45%).

**Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD)** - The amount of oxygen consumed by microorganisms during the aerobic decomposition of organic matter in water.

**Biomass** - The total mass of living microorganisms in a system; measured as dry weight, volatile solids, or optical density.

**Bioprocess** - A process that uses living cells or their components (enzymes, organelles) to obtain desired products.

**Bioreactor** - A vessel or container in which a biological reaction or process is carried out.

**Biorefinery** - An integrated facility that converts biomass into multiple value-added products (fuels, chemicals, materials).

**Biosafety Level (BSL)** - A set of biocontainment precautions required for working with biological agents; ranges from BSL-1 (minimal risk) to BSL-4 (highest risk).

**Biostimulant** - A substance or microorganism that stimulates plant growth, nutrient uptake, or stress tolerance.

**Bubble Column** - A cylindrical vessel where gas is bubbled through liquid to achieve mixing and mass transfer without mechanical agitation.

**Buswell Equation** - A stoichiometric formula for calculating theoretical biogas production from organic compounds.

## C

**C:N Ratio (Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio)** - The ratio of carbon to nitrogen in organic materials; critical for optimizing composting and anaerobic digestion (optimal ~25-30:1 for composting).

**Carrier Media** - Solid support material (plastic, ceramic, etc.) that provides surface area for biofilm growth in attached-growth bioreactors.

**Cell Yield (YX/S)** - The mass of cells produced per unit mass of substrate consumed; dimensionless or g cells/g substrate.

**Chemolithoautotroph** - An organism that obtains energy from inorganic chemical reactions and uses CO₂ as carbon source (e.g., nitrifying bacteria).

**Chemostat** - A continuous stirred-tank reactor operated with constant fresh medium input and culture removal, maintaining steady-state conditions.

**Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)** - The amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize organic matter in water; used as a measure of organic content.

**CHP (Combined Heat and Power)** - A system that simultaneously generates electricity and useful heat from a single fuel source (often biogas).

**Compost** - The stabilized, humus-like product of aerobic decomposition of organic matter.

**Composting** - The biological decomposition of organic matter under controlled aerobic conditions.

**Continuous Culture** - A cultivation method with continuous input of fresh medium and removal of culture, potentially achieving steady-state conditions.

**CSTR (Continuously Stirred Tank Reactor)** - A reactor with continuous input and output flows, ideally completely mixed with uniform conditions throughout.

## D

**Denitrification** - The biological reduction of nitrate (NO₃⁻) to nitrogen gas (N₂) under anoxic conditions using organic carbon as electron donor.

**Denitrifying Bacteria** - Facultative anaerobic bacteria that reduce nitrate to nitrogen gas when oxygen is absent.

**Digestate** - The solid and liquid material remaining after anaerobic digestion; rich in nutrients and used as fertilizer.

**Dilution Rate (D)** - In continuous culture, the flow rate divided by reactor volume (F/V); units of h⁻¹.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)** - The concentration of oxygen dissolved in water; critical parameter for aerobic bioprocesses; measured in mg/L or % saturation.

**Doubling Time (td)** - The time required for a microbial population to double during exponential growth; td = ln(2)/μ.

## E

**EBCT (Empty Bed Contact Time)** - The theoretical time required for liquid to pass through a reactor based on empty volume and flow rate.

**Effluent** - The liquid flowing out of a reactor or treatment system.

**Exponential Phase** - The growth phase where microorganisms divide at their maximum rate (μmax); also called log phase.

**Extracellular** - Outside the cell; extracellular products are secreted into the surrounding medium.

## F

**Facultative Anaerobe** - An organism that can grow with or without oxygen; preferentially uses oxygen when available.

**Fed-Batch Culture** - A cultivation method where substrate is added periodically or continuously while product and biomass remain in the reactor.

**Fermentation** - Metabolic process where organic compounds are metabolized in the absence of an external electron acceptor (anaerobic); also used broadly for any industrial-scale microbial process.

**Flocculation** - The aggregation of particles into larger clumps (flocs) to facilitate settling or separation.

**Fluidized Bed** - A reactor where solid particles (often with attached biofilm) are suspended by upward flow of liquid or gas.

**Free Ammonia (NH₃)** - The unionized form of ammonia; toxic to aquatic life and some microorganisms at concentrations >1-10 mg/L.

## G

**Geometric Similarity** - In scale-up, maintaining the same dimensional ratios (e.g., H/D) at different scales.

**Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)** - Guidelines and regulations for producing safe, high-quality products through proper facility design, operation, and quality control.

**Growth-Associated Product** - A product formed primarily during active cell growth.

**Growth Rate (Specific, μ)** - The rate of biomass increase per unit biomass; μ = (1/X)(dX/dt); units of h⁻¹.

## H

**Half-Saturation Constant (Ks)** - In Monod kinetics, the substrate concentration at which growth rate equals half the maximum (μ = μmax/2).

**Haldane Model** - An extension of Monod kinetics that accounts for substrate inhibition at high concentrations.

**Henry's Law** - Relates the partial pressure of a gas to its dissolved concentration in liquid; C* = H × P.

**Heterotroph** - An organism that requires organic compounds for carbon and energy.

**HRT (Hydraulic Retention Time)** - The average time that liquid remains in a reactor; HRT = V/Q.

**Humification** - The process of forming humic substances (complex, stable organic compounds) during composting maturation.

**Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR)** - The flow rate per unit surface area; common in trickling filters; units of m³/m²/day or m/day.

**Hydrolysis** - The first stage of anaerobic digestion where complex organic polymers are broken down into simple monomers by hydrolytic bacteria.

## I

**Impeller** - A rotating device that provides mixing and circulation in a stirred tank reactor.

**Influent** - The liquid flowing into a reactor or treatment system.

**Inhibition** - Reduction in microbial growth or activity due to presence of toxic or inhibitory substances.

**Inoculum** - A small volume of cultured microorganisms used to start a new culture or bioprocess.

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** - The discount rate at which the net present value of a project equals zero; used to evaluate investment profitability.

**Intracellular** - Inside the cell; intracellular products must be extracted through cell disruption.

## K

**KLa (Volumetric Mass Transfer Coefficient)** - A measure of the rate of gas transfer (typically oxygen) from bubbles to liquid; units of h⁻¹.

**Kinetics** - The study of reaction or process rates; microbial kinetics describes growth, substrate consumption, and product formation rates.

**Kolmogorov Microscale** - The smallest eddy size in turbulent flow; important for assessing shear stress on cells.

## L

**Lag Phase** - The initial phase of batch culture where cells adapt to new conditions before exponential growth begins.

**LCA (Life Cycle Assessment)** - A method for evaluating the environmental impacts of a product or process throughout its entire life cycle.

**Leachate** - Liquid that has percolated through solid material (compost, landfill) and extracted dissolved or suspended substances.

**Lineweaver-Burk Plot** - A linear transformation of the Monod equation (1/μ vs. 1/S) used to estimate μmax and Ks.

**Luedeking-Piret Equation** - Model describing product formation as growth-associated and/or non-growth-associated: qp = α×μ + β.

## M

**Macronutrient** - Essential nutrients required in large amounts (C, N, P, S, K, Mg, Ca).

**Maintenance Coefficient (ms or mO)** - The rate of substrate or oxygen consumption for non-growth cellular maintenance functions.

**Mass Balance** - Accounting for all inputs, outputs, generation, and consumption of a substance in a system.

**Mass Transfer** - The movement of substances from one phase to another (e.g., oxygen from gas bubbles to liquid).

**MBBR (Moving Bed Biofilm Reactor)** - A biofilm reactor where plastic carrier media move freely in the reactor, providing surface area for biofilm growth.

**Mesophilic** - Organisms or processes that function optimally at moderate temperatures (20-40°C).

**Methanogenesis** - The fourth and final stage of anaerobic digestion where methane is produced by methanogenic archaea.

**Methanogen** - An archaeon that produces methane as a metabolic byproduct; strictly anaerobic.

**Micronutrient** - Essential nutrients required in small amounts (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mo, B, etc.).

**Monod Equation** - Mathematical model relating specific growth rate to substrate concentration: μ = μmax × S/(Ks + S).

## N

**Net Present Value (NPV)** - The sum of discounted future cash flows minus initial investment; used to evaluate project profitability.

**Nitrification** - The biological oxidation of ammonia to nitrite and then nitrate by nitrifying bacteria under aerobic conditions.

**Nitrifying Bacteria** - Chemolithoautotrophic bacteria that obtain energy from oxidizing ammonia or nitrite.

**Nitrobacter** - Genus of bacteria that oxidizes nitrite (NO₂⁻) to nitrate (NO₃⁻) in the second step of nitrification.

**Nitrosomonas** - Genus of bacteria that oxidizes ammonia (NH₃) to nitrite (NO₂⁻) in the first step of nitrification.

**Non-Growth-Associated Product** - A product formed primarily during stationary phase when growth has ceased.

**NPDES (National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System)** - EPA permit program regulating wastewater discharge to surface waters.

## O

**Obligate Aerobe** - An organism that requires oxygen for growth and cannot survive in its absence.

**Obligate Anaerobe** - An organism that cannot tolerate oxygen and grows only in its absence.

**OLR (Organic Loading Rate)** - The mass of organic matter (VS or COD) fed per unit reactor volume per time; common units: kg VS/m³/day.

**Optical Density (OD)** - A measure of light absorbance used to estimate cell concentration; commonly measured at 600 nm (OD₆₀₀).

**Organic Loading Rate** - See OLR.

**ORP (Oxidation-Reduction Potential)** - A measure of the oxidizing or reducing power of a solution; measured in mV; indicates aerobic (+) vs. anaerobic (-) conditions.

**OTR (Oxygen Transfer Rate)** - The rate at which oxygen is transferred from gas phase to liquid phase; units of g O₂/L/h.

**OUR (Oxygen Uptake Rate)** - The rate at which organisms consume oxygen; units of g O₂/L/h.

## P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)** - Light in the 400-700 nm wavelength range used by photosynthetic organisms.

**PAT (Process Analytical Technology)** - A framework for real-time process monitoring and control to ensure quality.

**Pathogen** - A disease-causing microorganism.

**PBR (Photobioreactor)** - A bioreactor designed to cultivate photosynthetic organisms (algae, cyanobacteria) with controlled light delivery.

**pH** - A measure of acidity/alkalinity; pH = -log[H⁺]; scale of 0-14 with 7 being neutral.

**Photoautotroph** - An organism that uses light for energy and CO₂ as carbon source (e.g., algae, cyanobacteria).

**Photosynthesis** - The process by which organisms convert light energy, CO₂, and water into organic compounds and oxygen.

**PID Controller** - Proportional-Integral-Derivative controller; common feedback control algorithm.

**Pilot Scale** - Intermediate scale between laboratory and commercial production; used to validate process before full-scale investment.

**Plug Flow** - Flow pattern where fluid elements move through the reactor without back-mixing; first-in, first-out.

**Power Number (Np)** - Dimensionless number relating power consumption to impeller design and operating conditions.

## Q

**qp (Specific Product Formation Rate)** - The rate of product formation per unit biomass; units of g product/g cells/h.

**qO₂ (Specific Oxygen Uptake Rate)** - The rate of oxygen consumption per unit biomass; units of g O₂/g cells/h.

**qs (Specific Substrate Uptake Rate)** - The rate of substrate consumption per unit biomass; units of g substrate/g cells/h.

## R

**RAS (Recirculating Aquaculture System)** - A closed-loop fish production system with water treatment and recirculation.

**Recirculation Ratio** - In trickling filters or other systems, the ratio of recirculated flow to influent flow.

**Redfield Ratio** - The atomic ratio C:N:P = 106:16:1 found in phytoplankton and used as a guide for nutrient stoichiometry.

**Redox Potential** - See ORP.

**Respirometry** - The measurement of oxygen consumption or carbon dioxide production rates to assess biological activity.

**Reynolds Number (Re)** - Dimensionless number characterizing flow regime; Re > 10,000 indicates turbulent flow in bioreactors.

**Rotating Biological Contactor (RBC)** - A biofilm reactor where media discs rotate, alternately exposing biofilm to air and wastewater.

## S

**SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)** - Computer systems for monitoring and controlling industrial processes.

**Scale-up** - The process of increasing a bioprocess from laboratory to commercial scale.

**SDNR (Specific Denitrification Rate)** - The rate of nitrate removal per unit biomass; units of mg NO₃⁻-N/g VSS/h.

**Seed Culture** - A small-scale culture used to inoculate a larger fermentation.

**Soft Sensor** - A software algorithm that estimates difficult-to-measure variables from available online measurements.

**Solid-State Fermentation (SSF)** - Fermentation on moist solid substrates with minimal free water.

**Specific Growth Rate (μ)** - See Growth Rate.

**SRT (Solids Retention Time)** - The average time that solids (biomass) remain in a system; units of days.

**Stationary Phase** - Growth phase where cell growth rate equals death rate, resulting in constant cell concentration.

**Steady State** - Condition where all state variables remain constant over time; input rates equal output rates.

**Stirred Tank Reactor (STR)** - A bioreactor with mechanical agitation for mixing.

**Stoichiometry** - The quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical or biological reaction.

**Struvite** - Magnesium ammonium phosphate (MgNH₄PO₄·6H₂O); a slow-release fertilizer that can be precipitated from waste streams.

**Submerged Fermentation (SmF)** - Fermentation where microorganisms grow suspended in liquid medium.

**Substrate** - The substance consumed by microorganisms for growth and energy.

**Substrate Inhibition** - Reduction in growth rate at high substrate concentrations.

## T

**TAN (Total Ammonia Nitrogen)** - The sum of ammonia (NH₃) and ammonium (NH₄⁺); units of mg N/L.

**Thermophilic** - Organisms or processes that function optimally at high temperatures (45-70°C).

**Tip Speed** - The velocity of the outer edge of an impeller; v = π×N×D.

**Total Solids (TS)** - The dry weight of material after evaporating all moisture; expressed as % or mg/L.

**Trickling Filter** - An attached-growth bioreactor where wastewater trickles over fixed media colonized by biofilm.

**Turbidostat** - A continuous culture system where biomass concentration is kept constant by controlling dilution rate.

## U

**Upflow Velocity** - The velocity of upward-flowing liquid; critical for fluidized bed reactors.

## V

**Valorization** - The process of converting waste materials into valuable products.

**VFA (Volatile Fatty Acids)** - Short-chain organic acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate) produced during anaerobic digestion; indicators of process stability.

**Volatile Solids (VS)** - The portion of total solids that can be volatilized (combusted) at high temperature; approximates organic content.

**Volumetric Productivity** - The mass of product formed per unit reactor volume per time; units of g/L/day.

**VVM (Volume of gas per Volume of Medium per Minute)** - A measure of aeration rate; e.g., 1 VVM = 1 m³ air per m³ medium per minute.

## W

**Washout** - In continuous culture, the condition where dilution rate exceeds maximum growth rate, causing biomass to be removed faster than it grows (X → 0).

**Wastewater** - Water containing dissolved or suspended contaminants from domestic, industrial, or agricultural sources.

## X

**X (Biomass Concentration)** - The concentration of cells in a culture; units of g/L, g dry weight/L, or cells/mL.

## Y

**Yield Coefficient** - The ratio of mass of product to mass of substrate consumed; common yields:
- YX/S: cell yield on substrate
- YX/O: cell yield on oxygen
- YP/S: product yield on substrate

## Z

**Zero-Discharge System** - A system that recycles or reuses all water with no liquid discharge to the environment.

**Zone of Inhibition** - In bioassays, a clear area indicating growth inhibition by an antimicrobial agent.
