## Part B: Case Study Analysis (40 points)

### Case Study: TechGrow Vertical Farm Project

**Background:**
TechGrow Corporation is planning a 60,000 sf vertical farm to produce 3 million lbs/year of leafy greens and herbs. The facility will be located in an urban area and will feature advanced automation and LED lighting. Project budget is $40M with a 28-month target completion.

**Project Charter Summary:**
- **Sponsor:** CEO, TechGrow Corp
- **Business Case:** NPV of $35M over 10 years at 12% discount rate, IRR of 24%
- **Key Objectives:**
  - Complete by Month 28 (Dec 31, 2026)
  - Budget of $40M (±10%)
  - Achieve 3M lbs/year capacity
  - Energy consumption <14 kWh/lb

**Current Situation (Month 14 - Midpoint):**

**Schedule Status:**
- Baseline completion: Month 28
- Current forecast: Month 31 (+3 months delay)
- Critical path: HVAC equipment delayed 8 weeks (vendor manufacturing issues)
- Building shell complete (on time)
- MEP rough-in 60% complete (2 weeks behind)

**Cost Status:**
- Budget at Completion (BAC): $40M
- Planned Value (PV): $20M (should have spent 50%)
- Earned Value (EV): $18.5M (46.25% complete)
- Actual Cost (AC): $19.8M (spent 49.5% of budget)

**Quality/Technical:**
- Building inspections: 95% first-time pass rate (target: 95%)
- One safety incident (minor, no lost time)
- Chiller specifications changed mid-project (additional cooling capacity needed)
- Growing system design still being refined based on grower input

**Stakeholder Issues:**
- CFO concerned about cost trajectory
- Head Grower frustrated with design changes
- Neighbors complaining about construction noise (early morning deliveries)
- Investor requesting monthly updates instead of quarterly

## Answer Key - Part B: Case Study

### Question 1: Earned Value Analysis (10 points)

**Calculations:**
a) SV = EV - PV = $18.5M - $20M = **-$1.5M** (behind schedule)
b) CV = EV - AC = $18.5M - $19.8M = **-$1.3M** (over budget)
c) SPI = EV / PV = $18.5M / $20M = **0.925** (completing work at 92.5% of planned rate)
d) CPI = EV / AC = $18.5M / $19.8M = **0.934** (getting $0.93 value per $1 spent)
e) EAC = BAC / CPI = $40M / 0.934 = **$42.8M** (forecasted overrun of $2.8M)
f) VAC = BAC - EAC = $40M - $42.8M = **-$2.8M** (projected $2.8M over budget)

**Assessment:**
Project is in **YELLOW/CAUTION** status:
- Both schedule and cost performance are below acceptable thresholds (target ≥0.95)
- Project forecasted to be 7% over budget ($2.8M overrun)
- Schedule delay of 3 months threatens business case (market window, financing costs)
- Requires immediate corrective action

*Grading: 2 points per calculation, 2 points for assessment*

### Question 2: Schedule Recovery Plan (10 points)

**Sample Answer:**

**Recovery Actions:**

1. **Crash HVAC Installation (Parallel Action)**
   - Add second mechanical crew for HVAC installation
   - Work extended hours (10-hour days vs. 8-hour)
   - Schedule impact: Recover 4 weeks
   - Cost impact: $200K (additional labor and overtime premium)
   - Justification: HVAC is on critical path, crashing has direct impact on completion date

2. **Fast-Track Commissioning Activities**
   - Begin pre-functional testing during construction (rather than waiting for 100% complete)
   - Conduct factory acceptance tests concurrently with site work
   - Overlap commissioning agent engagement
   - Schedule impact: Recover 2 weeks
   - Cost impact: $50K (additional commissioning agent time)
   - Justification: Low cost for significant schedule benefit

3. **Negotiate Accelerated Chiller Delivery**
   - Pay premium for expedited manufacturing
   - Use air freight instead of ocean freight
   - Implement vendor penalties/incentives for on-time delivery
   - Schedule impact: Recover 2 weeks of the 8-week delay
   - Cost impact: $150K (expedite fees and air freight premium)
   - Justification: Chillers are critical path constraint, must address root cause

**Recommended Combination:**
- Implement Actions 1, 2, and 3 together
- Total schedule recovery: 8 weeks (gets back to Month 28 target)
- Total cost: $400K (well within 10% budget tolerance)
- Net result: On-time completion, $2.4M under the forecasted $42.8M ($40.4M final cost, 1% over original budget)

*Grading: 3 points for actions, 3 points for analysis, 4 points for recommendation and justification*

### Question 3: Risk Management (10 points)

**Sample Answer:**

**Risk 1: Further HVAC Equipment Delays**
- Description: Chiller delivery delayed beyond current 8-week delay due to manufacturing or shipping issues
- Probability: MEDIUM (30-50%) - Vendor having known production issues
- Impact: HIGH (adds 4+ weeks to critical path, jeopardizes recovery plan)
- Risk Score: 12 (High)
- Response Strategy: MITIGATE + TRANSFER
- Actions:
  1. Identify backup chiller vendor (pre-qualified)
  2. Include liquidated damages clause in vendor contract ($10K/week penalty)
  3. Weekly status calls with vendor to monitor progress
  4. Consider rental chillers as contingency (expensive but available)

**Risk 2: Growing System Design Not Finalized**
- Description: Continued design changes to growing system delay procurement and installation
- Probability: MEDIUM (40%) - Grower still providing input mid-project
- Impact: MEDIUM (2-4 week delay, potential rework costs $100-300K)
- Risk Score: 8 (Medium-High)
- Response Strategy: MITIGATE
- Actions:
  1. Freeze growing system design by end of Month 15 (hard deadline)
  2. Conduct design sprint with grower to finalize in 2-week intensive session
  3. Any changes after freeze go to Phase 2 (post-commissioning)
  4. Get grower sign-off in writing on final design

**Risk 3: Budget Overrun Exceeds Contingency**
- Description: Current forecast of $2.8M overrun exceeds available contingency; additional funding may not be approved
- Probability: MEDIUM (40%) - Trending toward overrun based on CPI
- Impact: HIGH (project halt, scope reduction, quality compromises)
- Risk Score: 12 (High)
- Response Strategy: MITIGATE
- Actions:
  1. Implement cost reduction initiatives (value engineering remaining work) - target $500K savings
  2. Improve cost performance (CPI) through productivity improvements and tighter controls
  3. Prepare contingent funding request to board with justification
  4. Identify non-critical scope that could be deferred to Phase 2 if necessary

*Grading: 3 points per risk (description, assessment, response), 1 point for overall quality*

### Question 4: Stakeholder Management (10 points)

**Sample Answer:**

**CFO (Concerned about cost trajectory):**
- Strategy: MANAGE CLOSELY - High power, high interest
- Communication: Bi-weekly one-on-one meetings + monthly written financial report
- Content: Detailed cost tracking, EVM analysis, recovery plan progress, cost reduction initiatives
- Address concern: Present concrete corrective action plan, commit to specific cost targets, demonstrate control

**Head Grower (Frustrated with design changes):**
- Strategy: COLLABORATE - Key end-user, critical to success
- Communication: Weekly design coordination meetings + dedicated session to finalize design
- Content: Design freeze timeline, change control process explanation, Phase 2 enhancement plan
- Address concern:
  - Acknowledge frustration and validate that input is valuable
  - Explain that continued changes jeopardize project (cost and schedule)
  - Conduct intensive 2-week design sprint to incorporate all feedback and finalize
  - Establish clear change control: changes after freeze require sponsor approval and go to Phase 2
  - Ensure grower understands impact of late changes on project success

**Neighbors (Construction noise complaints):**
- Strategy: KEEP INFORMED - Lower power but can influence regulators/permits
- Communication: Monthly community newsletter + complaint hotline
- Content: Construction schedule updates, noise mitigation efforts, anticipated completion
- Address concern:
  - Adjust delivery schedules (no deliveries before 8 AM)
  - Provide advance notice of high-noise activities
  - Designate community liaison for complaint response (24-hour turnaround)
  - Consider noise barriers if feasible

**Investor (Requesting monthly updates):**
- Strategy: MANAGE CLOSELY - High power, high interest
- Communication: Shift from quarterly to monthly updates (email summary + optional call)
- Content: Executive dashboard (1-page), progress photos, key metrics, major decisions upcoming
- Address concern: Respect their need for information while keeping concise and focused
