# Quiz 2: Project Initiation & Feasibility Analysis

**Course:** 413 - CEA Project Management
**Module:** 2 - Project Initiation & Feasibility Analysis
**Time Limit:** 20 minutes
**Passing Score:** 80% (8/10 correct)

---

## Questions

**1. What is the primary purpose of a feasibility study in CEA project development?**

A) To design the final facility
B) To determine if the project is technically viable and financially sound before committing major resources
C) To hire contractors
D) To satisfy regulatory requirements only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Feasibility studies evaluate technical viability, financial returns, market conditions, and risks to make informed go/no-go decisions before committing to full design and construction.

---

**2. In a business case for a vertical farm, what financial metric is most commonly used to evaluate investment attractiveness?**

A) Total revenue only
B) Net Present Value (NPV) and Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
C) Number of employees
D) Building square footage

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NPV and IRR account for time value of money and compare returns to investment costs, providing key metrics for investment decisions and comparing alternatives.

---

**3. What is a typical payback period target for CEA facility investments?**

A) 1-2 years
B) 3-5 years
C) 7-12 years
D) 20+ years

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** CEA facilities have high capital costs requiring 7-12 year payback periods, though investors often target shorter periods (5-8 years) depending on technology and market conditions.

---

**4. What site selection criterion is MOST critical for vertical farming facilities?**

A) Proximity to agricultural land
B) Access to reliable, affordable electricity and proximity to target markets
C) Scenic views
D) Low land costs only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Vertical farms require substantial electricity for lighting and climate control, and proximity to markets minimizes transportation costs and ensures product freshness.

---

**5. In preliminary engineering for CEA projects, what level of design detail is appropriate?**

A) Complete construction drawings
B) Conceptual design with major systems sized at ±30-40% accuracy
C) No technical detail needed
D) Equipment quotes only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Preliminary engineering provides conceptual designs with systems sized to ±30-40% accuracy, sufficient for cost estimation and feasibility without full design investment.

---

**6. What is a fatal flaw analysis in CEA project feasibility?**

A) Identifying equipment defects
B) Identifying issues that would make the project non-viable (zoning, permitting, market access)
C) Testing structural integrity
D) Evaluating employee performance

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Fatal flaw analysis identifies potential showstoppers early (regulatory barriers, environmental issues, market limitations) that would make the project infeasible regardless of design.

---

**7. For a 50,000 sf vertical farm feasibility study, what is a typical capital cost estimate range (all-in, turnkey)?**

A) $1-2 million
B) $5-10 million
C) $10-20 million
D) $50-100 million

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** At $200-400/sf typical vertical farm costs, a 50,000 sf facility ranges $10-20 million including building, equipment, installation, engineering, and contingency.

---

**8. What market analysis component is essential for CEA project feasibility?**

A) Competitor analysis and pricing research
B) Demand assessment and distribution channel evaluation
C) Customer willingness to pay for local, fresh produce
D) All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Comprehensive market analysis includes demand assessment, competitive analysis, pricing, distribution channels, and customer preferences—all critical for revenue projections.

---

**9. What is the recommended contingency percentage for conceptual-level CEA project cost estimates?**

A) 5-10%
B) 15-20%
C) 25-35%
D) 50%+

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Conceptual estimates have high uncertainty, requiring 25-35% contingency to account for unknowns, design changes, and market variations before detailed engineering.

---

**10. At Gate 1 (feasibility approval), what decision-making criteria should be applied?**

A) Does the project meet financial hurdle rates (IRR, NPV)?
B) Are technical risks manageable?
C) Does it align with strategic objectives?
D) All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Gate 1 decisions require multi-criteria evaluation: financial returns, technical feasibility, strategic fit, market opportunity, and risk assessment before proceeding to design.
