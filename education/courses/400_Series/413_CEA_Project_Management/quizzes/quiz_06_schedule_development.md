## Questions

**1. What is the critical path in project scheduling?**

A) The shortest path through the project
B) The longest duration path determining minimum project completion time
C) The most expensive activities
D) The path with most resources

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The critical path is the sequence of activities with zero float that determines the minimum project duration—delays on critical path activities directly delay project completion.

---

**2. What does "float" or "slack" represent in project scheduling?**

A) Floating equipment
B) The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project or successor activities
C) Budget flexibility
D) Extra workers

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Float is schedule flexibility—activities with float can be delayed without impacting project completion. Critical path activities have zero float.

---

**3. For a CEA facility construction, what is a realistic timeline from groundbreaking to substantial completion?**

A) 1-3 months
B) 6-12 months
C) 12-18 months
D) 3-5 years

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Typical CEA construction spans 12-18 months including site work, building construction, equipment installation, and commissioning, though complexity and size affect duration.

---

**4. What is the Finish-to-Start (FS) dependency relationship?**

A) Activities must finish at the same time
B) Successor activity cannot start until predecessor activity finishes
C) Activities start simultaneously
D) No relationship exists

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** FS is the most common dependency: Activity B cannot start until Activity A finishes (e.g., electrical can't be installed until walls are framed).

---

**5. What scheduling technique estimates activity duration using optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic scenarios?**

A) Guessing
B) Three-point estimating (PERT)
C) Top-down estimating
D) Historical data only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Three-point estimating (Program Evaluation Review Technique) uses weighted average: (Optimistic + 4×Most Likely + Pessimistic) ÷ 6, accounting for uncertainty.

---

**6. What is resource leveling in project scheduling?**

A) Demolishing the site
B) Adjusting activity start dates to smooth resource usage and avoid overallocation
C) Paying all workers equally
D) Removing activities

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Resource leveling adjusts non-critical activities to eliminate resource conflicts and smooth demand, often extending project duration but avoiding overallocation.

---

**7. Calculate the expected duration using three-point estimating: Optimistic = 10 days, Most Likely = 15 days, Pessimistic = 26 days**

A) 15 days
B) 16 days
C) 17 days
D) 20 days

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Expected Duration = (10 + 4×15 + 26) ÷ 6 = (10 + 60 + 26) ÷ 6 = 96 ÷ 6 = 16 days

---

**8. What is fast-tracking in schedule compression?**

A) Increasing budget
B) Performing activities in parallel that were originally planned in sequence
C) Reducing scope
D) Eliminating quality checks

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Fast-tracking overlaps activities normally done sequentially to reduce duration, increasing risk but maintaining scope (vs. crashing which adds resources).

---

**9. For effective schedule monitoring, what frequency of updates is recommended during CEA construction?**

A) Annual
B) Monthly
C) Weekly or bi-weekly
D) Only at milestones

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Construction schedules should be updated weekly or bi-weekly to track progress, identify delays early, adjust resource allocation, and communicate status to stakeholders.

---

**10. What does a Schedule Performance Index (SPI) of 0.85 indicate?**

A) Project is ahead of schedule
B) Project is earning only 85% of planned value—behind schedule
C) 85% of work is complete
D) Budget is over by 15%

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SPI = Earned Value / Planned Value. SPI < 1.0 indicates behind schedule. SPI = 0.85 means progressing at 85% of planned rate.
