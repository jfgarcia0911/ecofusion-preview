# Module 3: Stakeholder Identification & Management

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Identify all stakeholders for CEA capital projects
- Conduct stakeholder analysis using power/interest matrices
- Develop targeted engagement strategies for different stakeholder groups
- Create communication plans that meet diverse stakeholder needs
- Manage stakeholder expectations throughout the project lifecycle
- Navigate conflicts between competing stakeholder interests

### Detailed Stakeholder Analysis Framework

**Assess Each Stakeholder:**

1. **Interest Level**
   - How much does project outcome affect them?
   - What do they care about most?
   - How engaged are they currently?

2. **Power/Influence**
   - Can they impact project success?
   - What decisions do they control?
   - Who do they influence?

3. **Attitude**
   - Champion (actively promotes project)
   - Supporter (favorable, passive)
   - Neutral (no strong opinion)
   - Skeptic (concerns, needs convincing)
   - Blocker (opposed, could derail project)

4. **Engagement Needs**
   - Information: What do they need to know?
   - Timing: When do they need it?
   - Medium: How should we communicate?
   - Frequency: How often?

- Concerned about public safety, environmental impact
- Wants projects that benefit community (jobs, tax revenue)
- Accountable to city council and public

POWER/INFLUENCE: High
- Can approve, delay, or deny permits
- Can impose conditions on approval
- Can influence public perception through recommendations
- Controls critical path timeline (permitting phase)

CURRENT ATTITUDE: Neutral/Slightly Skeptical
- Unfamiliar with CEA technology (previous applications limited)
- Concerned about industrial use in mixed-use zone
- Questions about energy use, traffic, visual impact
- Open to learning but needs education and reassurance

INTERESTS & CONCERNS:
Primary Interests:
✓ Compliance with zoning and building codes
✓ Public safety (fire, structural, electrical)
✓ Minimal neighborhood impact (traffic, noise, aesthetics)
✓ Economic benefit to community (jobs, tax revenue)

Key Concerns:
! Precedent-setting approval (what about next applicant?)
! Adequate parking and loading/unloading areas
! Energy demand impact on grid
! Wastewater discharge composition and volume

ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY: Consult & Collaborate
- Schedule pre-application meeting (educate on CEA)
- Provide detailed application with all supporting studies
- Proactively address concerns (traffic study, energy plan, etc.)
- Offer site tour of similar facility
- Present at public hearing with compelling narrative
- Maintain responsive communication throughout process

COMMUNICATION PLAN:
- Frequency: Bi-weekly during permit phase
- Medium: In-person meetings, formal submittals, email
- Key Messages: Safety, community benefit, environmental stewardship
- Success Metric: Permit approval within 6 months, minimal conditions

RISK ASSESSMENT:
- Risk: Permit denial or significant delay
- Probability: Medium (20-30%)
- Impact: High (critical path item)
- Mitigation: Early engagement, thorough application, community support

### Executive Sponsor & Steering Committee

```
ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY: EXECUTIVE SPONSOR
+===================================================================+
| STAKEHOLDER: Executive Sponsor / CEO                              |
| INTEREST: Strategic success, financial returns, reputation        |
| POWER: Very high - approves budget, major decisions               |
+===================================================================+

COMMUNICATION PLAN:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ WEEKLY:                                                         │
│ - Brief email update (1 page max)                               │
│   • Accomplishments this week                                   │
│   • Planned activities next week                                │
│   • Issues/decisions needed (if any)                            │
│   • Budget/schedule status (traffic light: 🟢🟡🔴)              │
│                                                                 │
│ MONTHLY:                                                        │
│ - In-person meeting (30-60 min)                                 │
│   • Detailed progress review                                    │
│   • Financial performance vs. baseline                          │
│   • Risk register review                                        │
│   • Upcoming milestones and decisions                           │
│   • Strategic issues or opportunities                           │
│                                                                 │
│ QUARTERLY:                                                      │
│ - Steering committee presentation                               │
│   • Comprehensive project dashboard                             │
│   • Earned value analysis                                       │
│   • Lessons learned and course corrections                      │
│   • Lookahead (next 90 days)                                    │
│                                                                 │
│ AD HOC:                                                         │
│ - Immediate notification of major issues                        │
│ - Decision requests (48-hour turnaround when possible)          │
│ - Celebration of major milestones                               │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

KEY MESSAGES:
✓ "Project is on track" (or specific plan to get back on track)
✓ "We are delivering value for the investment"
✓ "Risks are identified and managed"
✓ "We need your decision/support on [X]"

ENGAGEMENT TIPS:
- Be concise - respect their time
- Lead with bottom line, provide backup detail
- No surprises - flag issues early
- Come with solutions, not just problems
- Quantify everything possible
- Celebrate wins, acknowledge team

### Regulatory Agencies

**Engagement Approach: Consult & Collaborate**

```
REGULATORY STAKEHOLDER STRATEGY
+===================================================================+
| STAKEHOLDER: Regulatory Agencies (Building, Planning, Env., etc.) |
| INTEREST: Code compliance, public safety, environmental protection|
| POWER: High - can approve/deny, impose conditions                 |
+===================================================================+

ENGAGEMENT PHASES:

PHASE 1: PRE-APPLICATION (Critical!)
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Objectives:                                                     │
│ - Educate on CEA technology (often unfamiliar)                  │
│ - Identify concerns and requirements early                      │
│ - Build rapport and credibility                                 │
│ - Clarify application requirements and process                  │
│                                                                 │
│ Activities:                                                     │
│ • Schedule pre-application meeting                              │
│ • Prepare educational materials (CEA overview, similar projects)│
│ • Present conceptual design                                     │
│ • Listen actively to concerns                                   │
│ • Take detailed notes on requirements                           │
│ • Ask about precedents and comparable projects                  │
│ • Discuss timeline and review process                           │
│                                                                 │
│ Follow-up:                                                      │
│ • Summarize meeting in writing                                  │
│ • Confirm requirements and process                              │
│ • Address questions that arose                                  │
│ • Offer additional information or facility tours                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

PHASE 2: APPLICATION PREPARATION
- Assemble complete application package (avoid RFIs)
- Include all required supporting studies:
  • Traffic impact analysis (if required)
  • Environmental assessment
  • Stormwater management plan
  • Energy demand letter from utility
  • Fire protection plan
  • Structural calculations
  • Photometric study (lighting)
- Proactively address concerns raised in pre-app meeting
- Have professional consultants prepare technical studies
- Review complete package before submission

PHASE 3: REVIEW PROCESS
- Respond promptly to RFIs (Requests for Information)
- Provide complete, well-organized responses
- Offer to meet in person for complex items
- Be flexible and collaborative on conditions
- Keep communication professional and respectful
- Maintain regular check-ins on status

PHASE 4: PUBLIC HEARINGS (if required)
- Prepare compelling presentation
- Emphasize community benefits:
  • Local jobs (construction + operations)
  • Tax revenue
  • Fresh, local food
  • Sustainable agriculture
  • Educational opportunities
- Address concerns directly and honestly
- Bring supporters (customers, neighbors, employees)
- Professional graphics and handouts
- Anticipate opposition and prepare responses

PHASE 5: POST-APPROVAL
- Thank reviewers for their work
- Comply with all conditions of approval
- Keep regulators informed of construction progress
- Schedule inspections proactively
- Maintain good relationship for future projects

RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT:
✓ Treat regulators as partners, not adversaries
✓ Respect their authority and responsibilities
✓ Educate, don't lecture
✓ Be patient - CEA is new to many jurisdictions
✓ Document everything in writing
✓ Build credibility through professionalism
```

**Engagement Approach: Collaborate**

```
CUSTOMER STAKEHOLDER STRATEGY
+===================================================================+
| STAKEHOLDER: Customers (Retailers, Restaurants, Food Service)     |
| INTEREST: Reliable supply, quality, pricing, partnership          |

- Conduct formal market research (surveys, interviews)

- Build relationships that can withstand startup challenges

CONSTRUCTION PHASE:
- Quarterly updates on project progress
- Facility tours during construction
- Involve in crop trials and variety selection
- Confirm delivery logistics and timing
- Manage expectations on startup timeline

COMMISSIONING/STARTUP:
- Weekly updates as first harvest approaches
- Invite for "first harvest" events
- Be transparent about initial challenges
- Under-promise, over-deliver on early shipments
- Solicit feedback and adjust quickly

ONGOING OPERATIONS:
- Regular business reviews (quarterly)
- Collaborative crop planning
- Transparency on production challenges
- Quick response to quality issues
- Continuous improvement partnerships

COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES:
✓ Transparency builds trust
✓ Involve customers as partners, not just buyers
✓ Manage expectations realistically (especially during startup)
✓ Responsiveness to concerns
✓ Celebrate successes together

## 5. Managing Stakeholder Conflicts

### Common Conflicts in CEA Projects

```
TYPICAL STAKEHOLDER CONFLICTS
+===================================================================+
| CONFLICT TYPE                  | COMPETING INTERESTS             |
+================================+=================================+
| COST vs. QUALITY               | - CFO wants to cut costs        |
|                                | - Grower wants premium systems  |
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| SPEED vs. THOROUGHNESS         | - Sponsor wants fast delivery   |
|                                | - Engineers want thorough design|
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| AESTHETICS vs. COST            | - Architect wants premium facade|
|                                | - CFO wants basic building      |
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| AUTOMATION vs. JOBS            | - Investors want full automation|
|                                | - Community wants local jobs    |
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| STANDARDIZATION vs. INNOVATION | - Lender wants proven technology|
|                                | - Grower wants latest innovations|
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
| SHORT-TERM vs. LONG-TERM       | - Sponsor wants quick payback   |
|                                | - Ops wants future flexibility  |
+--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
```

### Conflict Resolution Framework

**Step 1: Identify Root Cause**
- What are the underlying interests (not just positions)?
- Are there misunderstandings or missing information?
- Is this a values conflict or a resource conflict?

**Step 2: Gather Perspectives**
- Interview stakeholders individually
- Listen actively without judgment
- Clarify interests and constraints
- Identify areas of agreement

**Step 3: Analyze Trade-offs**
- Quantify impacts where possible
- Consider both short and long-term effects
- Assess alignment with project objectives
- Identify decision criteria

**Step 4: Generate Options**
- Brainstorm creative solutions
- Look for win-win possibilities
- Consider phased or hybrid approaches
- Explore precedents from other projects

**Step 5: Facilitate Decision**
- Present analysis objectively
- Clarify decision-making authority
- Advocate for project objectives
- Document decision and rationale
- Communicate to all affected parties

**Step 6: Monitor & Follow-up**
- Ensure decision is implemented
- Check in with dissenting parties
- Watch for unintended consequences
- Be willing to revisit if necessary

## Key Takeaways

1. **Stakeholder identification must be comprehensive** - Don't overlook "quiet" stakeholders who may emerge later with significant influence.

2. **Power/Interest analysis guides engagement strategy** - Invest time where it matters most, but don't ignore any group completely.

3. **Early engagement prevents problems** - Especially with regulators and community, proactive outreach builds trust and avoids delays.

4. **Communication must be tailored** - One size does not fit all. Adapt message, medium, and frequency to each stakeholder group.

5. **Transparency builds trust** - Share bad news early and often. Stakeholders respect honesty more than unrealistic optimism.

6. **Conflicts are inevitable** - Develop skill in navigating competing interests. The PM often serves as mediator.

7. **Relationships matter** - Projects are temporary, but relationships last. Invest in building genuine partnerships.
