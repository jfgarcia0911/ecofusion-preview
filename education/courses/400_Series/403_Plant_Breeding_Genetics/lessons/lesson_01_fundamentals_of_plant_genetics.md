## 1. DNA Structure and Gene Expression

### 1.1 The Molecular Basis of Heredity

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the hereditary material that carries genetic information from one generation to the next.

**DNA Structure:**
```
    5' End
     |
    Sugar-Phosphate Backbone
     |
    Base Pairs (A-T, G-C)
     |
    Double Helix Structure
     |
    3' End

    A = Adenine (pairs with T)
    T = Thymine (pairs with A)
    G = Guanine (pairs with C)
    C = Cytosine (pairs with G)
```

**Key Components:**
- **Nucleotides**: Building blocks consisting of:
  - Deoxyribose sugar
  - Phosphate group
  - Nitrogenous base (A, T, G, or C)
- **Base Pairing**: Complementary base pairing maintains double helix structure
- **Antiparallel Strands**: 5' to 3' and 3' to 5' orientation

### 1.2 Gene Structure in Plants

Plants have nuclear DNA, chloroplast DNA (cpDNA), and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA).

**Gene Components:**
```
[Promoter]--[5' UTR]--[Exon]--[Intron]--[Exon]--[3' UTR]--[Terminator]
     |          |        |        |        |        |          |
  Controls   Regulation Coding  Non-coding Coding  Regulation  Stop
  expression            region   region   region              signal
```

**Nuclear Genome:**
- Contains most genes affecting plant traits
- Diploid (2n) in most species
- Target for breeding programs

**Organellar Genomes:**
- Chloroplast DNA: ~120-200 kb, maternally inherited
- Mitochondrial DNA: Larger, more variable
- Important for photosynthetic efficiency in CEA

### 1.3 Central Dogma: DNA → RNA → Protein

```
DNA (Genome)
    |
    | Transcription (in nucleus)
    ↓
mRNA (Messenger RNA)
    |
    | Processing (splicing, capping, poly-A tail)
    ↓
Mature mRNA
    |
    | Translation (in ribosomes)
    ↓
Protein (Functional molecules)
    |
    ↓
Trait Expression
```

**Key Processes:**

1. **Transcription**: DNA → RNA
   - RNA polymerase reads DNA template
   - Produces pre-mRNA in eukaryotes
   - Occurs in nucleus

2. **RNA Processing**:
   - Splicing removes introns
   - 5' cap and 3' poly-A tail added
   - Alternative splicing creates protein diversity

3. **Translation**: RNA → Protein
   - mRNA read in codons (3-nucleotide units)
   - tRNA brings amino acids
   - Ribosomes synthesize proteins

### 1.4 Gene Expression and Regulation

**Levels of Gene Regulation:**
- Transcriptional control (primary level)
- Post-transcriptional modifications
- Translational control
- Post-translational modifications

**Relevance to CEA:**
- Light quality affects gene expression (photomorphogenesis)
- Photoperiod influences flowering genes
- Nutrient availability regulates metabolic genes
- Temperature affects developmental gene expression

## 2. Cell Division: Mitosis and Meiosis

### 2.1 Mitosis: Somatic Cell Division

Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells for growth and maintenance.

**Phases of Mitosis:**
```
Interphase (G1, S, G2)
    |
    ↓
Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase → Cytokinesis
    |          |           |           |            |
Chromatin  Chromosomes  Sister      Nuclear   Two identical
condenses  align at    chromatids   envelope   daughter cells
           equator     separate     reforms
```

**Detailed Stages:**

1. **Interphase**:
   - G1: Cell growth, normal metabolism
   - S: DNA replication (chromosome duplication)
   - G2: Preparation for division

2. **Prophase**:
   - Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes
   - Each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids
   - Nuclear envelope begins to break down
   - Spindle apparatus forms

3. **Metaphase**:
   - Chromosomes align at cell equator (metaphase plate)
   - Spindle fibers attach to centromeres
   - Checkpoint ensures proper attachment

4. **Anaphase**:
   - Sister chromatids separate
   - Move to opposite poles of cell
   - Cell elongates

5. **Telophase**:
   - Nuclear envelopes reform around each set of chromosomes
   - Chromosomes decondense
   - Spindle apparatus disassembles

6. **Cytokinesis**:
   - Plant cells form cell plate
   - Division into two daughter cells

**Breeding Significance:**
- Maintains chromosome number in vegetative propagation
- Ensures genetic stability in tissue culture
- Allows cloning of superior genotypes

### 2.2 Meiosis: Gamete Formation

Meiosis produces four haploid cells (gametes) with genetic variation through recombination.

**Meiosis Overview:**
```
Diploid Parent Cell (2n)
         |
         ↓
    Meiosis I (Reductional Division)
         |
         ↓
Two Haploid Cells (n)
         |
         ↓
    Meiosis II (Equational Division)
         |
         ↓
Four Haploid Gametes (n)
(Each genetically unique)
```

**Meiosis I Stages:**

1. **Prophase I** (longest phase):
   - Chromatin condenses
   - Homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis)
   - Crossing over occurs (genetic recombination)
   - Nuclear envelope breaks down

2. **Metaphase I**:
   - Homologous pairs align at equator
   - Independent assortment occurs
   - Random orientation creates variation

3. **Anaphase I**:
   - Homologous chromosomes separate
   - Sister chromatids remain together
   - Move to opposite poles

4. **Telophase I and Cytokinesis**:
   - Two haploid cells form
   - Chromosome number reduced by half

**Meiosis II Stages:**

Similar to mitosis but in haploid cells:
- Prophase II → Metaphase II → Anaphase II → Telophase II
- Sister chromatids separate
- Results in four haploid gametes

**Key Differences Between Mitosis and Meiosis:**

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Number of divisions | One | Two |
| Number of daughter cells | Two | Four |
| Chromosome number | Diploid (2n) | Haploid (n) |
| Genetic variation | Identical | Variation |
| Occurs in | Somatic cells | Germ cells |
| Purpose | Growth, repair | Sexual reproduction |
| Crossing over | No | Yes (Prophase I) |

### 2.3 Crossing Over and Recombination

**Mechanism:**
```
Before Crossing Over:
Chromosome A: [====Gene1====|====Gene2====]
Chromosome B: [====gene1====|====gene2====]

After Crossing Over:
Chromosome A': [====Gene1====|====gene2====]
Chromosome B': [====gene1====|====Gene2====]

Result: New gene combinations
```

**Significance for Breeding:**
- Creates new allele combinations
- Breaks linkage between genes
- Increases genetic variation
- Allows selection of favorable trait combinations
- Frequency affects breeding strategy

**Recombination Frequency:**
- Genes close together: Low recombination
- Genes far apart: High recombination
- Basis for genetic mapping

## 3. Chromosome Structure and Behavior

### 3.1 Chromosome Organization

**Chromosome Structure:**
```
        Centromere
            |
    p arm   |   q arm
    (short) | (long)
       \    |    /
        \   |   /
    [========X========]
         Sister Chromatids
              |
         Telomeres (ends)
```

**Components:**
- **Centromere**: Attachment point for spindle fibers
  - Metacentric: Central position
  - Submetacentric: Off-center
  - Acrocentric: Near one end
  - Telocentric: At the end

- **Telomeres**: Protective caps at chromosome ends
  - TTAGGG repeats in plants
  - Prevent chromosome degradation

- **Chromatin**: DNA-protein complex
  - Euchromatin: Loosely packed, transcriptionally active
  - Heterochromatin: Tightly packed, transcriptionally inactive

### 3.2 Chromosome Number and Ploidy

**Ploidy Levels:**

| Term | Symbol | Description | Example |
|------|--------|-------------|---------|
| Haploid | n | One set of chromosomes | Gametes |
| Diploid | 2n | Two sets (normal) | Most plants |
| Triploid | 3n | Three sets | Seedless watermelon |
| Tetraploid | 4n | Four sets | Many crop plants |
| Polyploid | >2n | Multiple sets | Common in plants |

**Common CEA Crop Chromosome Numbers:**

- Lettuce (Lactuca sativa): 2n = 18
- Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum): 2n = 24
- Basil (Ocimum basilicum): 2n = 72
- Strawberry (Fragaria × ananassa): 2n = 56 (octoploid)
- Cucumber (Cucumis sativus): 2n = 14

**Polyploidy in Breeding:**
- Creates larger cells and organs
- Can increase disease resistance
- May improve stress tolerance
- Often results in seedlessness
- Induced with colchicine treatment

### 3.3 Karyotyping

Visual representation of an organism's complete chromosome set.

**Karyotype Analysis:**
```
Chromosome Pair 1  [======X======]  [======X======]
Chromosome Pair 2  [====X====]      [====X====]
Chromosome Pair 3  [========X========] [========X========]
        ...              ...              ...
Chromosome Pair n  [===X===]        [===X===]

Analysis reveals:
- Chromosome number
- Structural abnormalities
- Ploidy level
```

**Applications in CEA Breeding:**
- Verify chromosome number in breeding lines
- Detect polyploidy
- Identify chromosomal aberrations
- Confirm hybrid status

## 4. Genetic Variation and Mutation

### 4.1 Sources of Genetic Variation

**Natural Sources:**

1. **Mutation**: Changes in DNA sequence
2. **Recombination**: Crossing over during meiosis
3. **Independent Assortment**: Random chromosome distribution
4. **Gene Flow**: Introduction of alleles from other populations
5. **Random Genetic Drift**: Chance changes in allele frequencies

### 4.2 Types of Mutations

**Point Mutations:**
```
Original DNA:  ATG GAC TTC GGA
               ↓
Mutant DNA:    ATG GAT TTC GGA (One base changed)
                      ↑
```

**Categories:**
- **Silent mutation**: No amino acid change
- **Missense mutation**: Different amino acid
- **Nonsense mutation**: Premature stop codon
- **Frameshift mutation**: Insertion/deletion changes reading frame

**Chromosomal Mutations:**

1. **Deletion**: Loss of chromosome segment
   ```
   Normal: [====A====B====C====D====]
   Mutant: [====A====B========D====]  (C deleted)
   ```

2. **Duplication**: Extra copy of segment
   ```
   Normal: [====A====B====C====]
   Mutant: [====A====B====B====C====]  (B duplicated)
   ```

3. **Inversion**: Segment reversed
   ```
   Normal: [====A====B====C====D====]
   Mutant: [====A====C====B====D====]  (B-C inverted)
   ```

4. **Translocation**: Segment moved to different chromosome
   ```
   Chromosome 1: [====A====B====]  →  [====A====]
   Chromosome 2: [====X====Y====]  →  [====X====Y====B====]
   ```

### 4.3 Induced Mutations in Breeding

**Mutagenesis Methods:**

1. **Physical Mutagens**:
   - Gamma rays
   - X-rays
   - Fast neutrons
   - UV radiation

2. **Chemical Mutagens**:
   - EMS (Ethyl methanesulfonate)
   - Sodium azide
   - Colchicine (for polyploidy)

**TILLING (Targeting Induced Local Lesions IN Genomes):**
- Create large mutant populations
- Screen for specific gene mutations
- Non-GMO approach to trait modification
- Useful for CEA trait development

**Mutation Breeding Success Stories:**
- Compact plant architecture
- Disease resistance
- Altered flowering time
- Improved nutritional content

### 4.4 Spontaneous Mutation Rates

**Typical Mutation Rates:**
- Per nucleotide per generation: 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁹
- Per gene per generation: 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁶
- Higher in some genomic regions
- Affected by environmental stress

**Implications for CEA:**
- Source of novel variation
- Somaclonal variation in tissue culture
- Selection pressure in controlled environments
- Monitoring for off-types

## 5. CEA-Relevant Genetic Concepts

### 5.1 Genotype vs. Phenotype

**Definitions:**
- **Genotype**: Genetic makeup (DNA sequence)
- **Phenotype**: Observable characteristics
- **Environment**: Growing conditions

**Relationship:**
```
Phenotype = Genotype + Environment + (G × E Interaction)

Example in CEA:
Plant Height = Genetics + (Light + Nutrients + Temperature + ...)
```

**Genotype × Environment Interaction:**
- Same genotype performs differently in different environments
- Critical consideration in CEA breeding
- LED spectrum may reveal different trait expression than sunlight
- Temperature and humidity effects vary by genotype

### 5.2 Alleles and Dominance

**Allele Types:**
- **Dominant (A)**: Expressed in heterozygotes
- **Recessive (a)**: Only expressed in homozygotes
- **Codominant**: Both alleles expressed equally
- **Incomplete dominance**: Intermediate phenotype

**Genotype Notation:**
- Homozygous dominant: AA
- Heterozygous: Aa
- Homozygous recessive: aa

### 5.3 Gene Expression in CEA Environments

**Light-Regulated Genes:**
- Phytochromes (red/far-red light receptors)
- Cryptochromes (blue light receptors)
- Phototropins (phototropism)
- Zeitlupe family (circadian clock)

**LED Spectrum Effects:**
```
Blue Light (450nm)
    ↓
Activates Cryptochromes
    ↓
Influences: Stomatal opening, phototropism, anthocyanin synthesis

Red Light (660nm)
    ↓
Activates Phytochrome
    ↓
Influences: Germination, flowering, shade avoidance

Far-Red Light (730nm)
    ↓
Inactivates Phytochrome
    ↓
Influences: Stem elongation, flowering time
```

**Temperature-Sensitive Genes:**
- Vernalization genes (FLC, VRN)
- Heat shock proteins
- Cold acclimation genes

**Nutrient-Responsive Genes:**
- Nitrogen assimilation (NRT, NIA, NIR genes)
- Phosphate transporters
- Iron uptake mechanisms

### 5.4 Epigenetics in CEA

**Epigenetic Mechanisms:**
- DNA methylation
- Histone modifications
- Small RNA regulation

**Significance:**
- Gene expression changes without DNA sequence changes
- Can be environmentally induced
- Some changes heritable
- Reset during meiosis (mostly)

**CEA Applications:**
- Stress memory in plants
- Priming for disease resistance
- Acclimation to LED lighting
- Potential for selection
