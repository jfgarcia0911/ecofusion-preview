# Glossary: Plant Breeding & Genetics for CEA

## Comprehensive Technical Terminology Reference

**Course 403 - Expert Level**

---

## A

**Additive Gene Action**
- Effects of alleles that combine cumulatively to influence a trait
- Each allele contributes a fixed amount to the phenotype
- Basis for predictable response to selection

**Allele**
- Alternative form of a gene at a specific locus
- Example: A and a are alleles of the same gene

**Androgenesis**
- Development of a plant from male gametes (pollen)
- Used in doubled haploid production via anther culture

**Aneuploidy**
- Abnormal chromosome number (not a multiple of the basic set)
- Example: 2n+1 (trisomy), 2n-1 (monosomy)

**Anthocyanin**
- Red/purple pigment in plants
- Antioxidant compound
- Production influenced by blue light in CEA

**Apomixis**
- Asexual reproduction through seeds
- Clonal seeds (genetically identical to mother plant)
- Rare but valuable if engineered into hybrids

**Aquaponics**
- Integration of aquaculture (fish) and hydroponics
- Fish waste provides nutrients for plants
- Breeding considerations: pH tolerance, nutrient efficiency

---

## B

**Backcross (BC)**
- Cross of hybrid offspring back to one of the parents
- Used to introgress specific genes into elite backgrounds
- BC1, BC2, BC3... indicate successive backcross generations

**Base Editing**
- CRISPR technology that converts one DNA base to another
- C→T or A→G conversions
- No double-strand break required

**BLUP (Best Linear Unbiased Prediction)**
- Statistical method for estimating breeding values
- Accounts for relatedness and environmental effects
- Used in genomic selection

**Bolting**
- Premature flowering/seed stalk formation in leafy greens
- Undesirable in CEA production
- Late-bolting trait critical for lettuce, spinach

**Breeder Seed**
- Highest purity seed maintained by plant breeder
- Source for all subsequent seed multiplication
- Small quantity, maximum genetic purity

**Breeding Value**
- Genetic worth of an individual as a parent
- Measured by performance of its offspring
- Central concept in quantitative genetics

**Broad-Sense Heritability (H²)**
- Proportion of phenotypic variance due to all genetic factors
- H² = VG / VP
- Includes additive, dominance, and epistatic variance

---

## C

**Callus**
- Undifferentiated mass of cells in tissue culture
- Can regenerate into whole plants
- Risk of somaclonal variation if maintained long-term

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
- Plant production in enclosed structures with environmental control
- Includes vertical farms, plant factories, advanced greenhouses
- Enables year-round production with optimized conditions

**CentiMorgan (cM)**
- Unit of genetic map distance
- 1 cM = 1% recombination frequency
- Named after geneticist Thomas Hunt Morgan

**Chlorophyll Fluorescence**
- Re-emission of light by chlorophyll molecules
- Fv/Fm ratio indicates photosynthetic health
- Non-destructive stress detection method

**Codominance**
- Both alleles expressed equally in heterozygote
- Example: AB blood type
- Molecular markers often codominant

**Combining Ability**
- Performance of a line in hybrid combinations
- General combining ability (GCA): average performance
- Specific combining ability (SCA): specific pair performance

**CRISPR/Cas9**
- Gene editing system using guide RNA and Cas9 nuclease
- Enables precise genome modifications
- Revolutionary tool for crop improvement

**Cultivar**
- Cultivated variety
- Assemblage of plants selected for specific characteristics
- Maintains these characteristics when propagated

**Cytoplasmic Male Sterility (CMS)**
- Maternally inherited male sterility
- Mitochondrial genes cause pollen sterility
- Used in hybrid seed production (three-line system)

---

## D

**Day-Neutral**
- Plants that flower regardless of photoperiod
- Preferred for year-round CEA production
- Contrasts with short-day and long-day plants

**Diploid (2n)**
- Two complete sets of chromosomes
- Normal chromosome number for most plants
- Gametes are haploid (n)

**Dominant**
- Allele expressed in heterozygote
- Masks recessive allele
- Uppercase letter notation (A)

**Doubled Haploid (DH)**
- Completely homozygous line from haploid
- Created by chromosome doubling
- Instant homozygosity (vs. 7-8 generations selfing)

**Downy Mildew**
- Major disease of lettuce and basil in CEA
- High humidity favors infection
- Dm genes provide resistance

---

## E

**Epistasis**
- Interaction between genes at different loci
- One gene masks or modifies expression of another
- Can create modified Mendelian ratios

**Essentially Derived Variety (EDV)**
- Variety predominantly derived from initial variety
- Retains essential characteristics
- May require license from initial variety owner

**Euchromatin**
- Loosely packed chromatin
- Transcriptionally active
- Contains most expressed genes

**Explant**
- Tissue sample used to initiate culture
- Examples: leaf segments, shoot tips, anthers

---

## F

**F1 Hybrid**
- First generation from cross of two different parents
- Often exhibits hybrid vigor (heterosis)
- Uniform and predictable

**Foreground Selection**
- Marker-assisted selection for target gene
- Primary gene being introgressed
- Complemented by background selection

**Freedom to Operate (FTO)**
- Analysis of IP landscape before commercialization
- Ensures no infringement of patents or PVP
- Critical due diligence

---

## G

**Gamete**
- Reproductive cell (pollen or egg)
- Haploid (n chromosomes)
- Fuses at fertilization to form diploid zygote

**GBS (Genotyping-by-Sequencing)**
- Discovers and genotypes SNPs simultaneously
- Reduced-representation sequencing
- Cost-effective for species without reference genome

**General Combining Ability (GCA)**
- Average performance of line in hybrid combinations
- Reflects additive gene effects
- Predicts hybrid performance

**Gene Pyramiding**
- Combining multiple genes for same trait
- Example: Stacking disease resistance genes
- Marker-assisted selection enables efficient pyramiding

**Genetic Map**
- Positions of markers/genes on chromosomes
- Based on recombination frequency
- Measured in centiMorgans (cM)

**Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS)**
- Scanning genome for marker-trait associations
- Uses natural populations
- Higher resolution than biparental QTL mapping

**Genomic Selection (GS)**
- Selection based on genome-wide marker data
- Calculates genomic estimated breeding values (GEBV)
- Enables selection without phenotyping

**Genotype**
- Genetic makeup of an organism
- Allele composition at one or more loci
- Underlies phenotype (along with environment)

**Genotype × Environment Interaction (G×E)**
- Differential performance of genotypes across environments
- Crossover interaction: ranking changes
- Important consideration in multi-environment testing

**Germplasm**
- Genetic resources (seeds, plants, tissues)
- Foundation for breeding programs
- Includes landraces, cultivars, wild relatives

**Gibberellins (GA)**
- Plant hormones promoting stem elongation
- GA-deficient mutants are dwarf
- Target for compact architecture breeding

---

## H

**Haploid (n)**
- One set of chromosomes
- Chromosome number in gametes
- Can be doubled to create DH lines

**Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium**
- Stable allele frequencies in large random-mating populations
- p² + 2pq + q² = 1
- Baseline for population genetics

**Heritability**
- Proportion of phenotypic variation due to genetics
- Predicts response to selection
- See Broad-sense (H²) and Narrow-sense (h²)

**Heterochromatin**
- Tightly packed chromatin
- Transcriptionally inactive
- Includes centromeric and telomeric regions

**Heterosis (Hybrid Vigor)**
- Superior performance of F1 hybrid over parents
- Exploited in hybrid crop production
- Genetic basis: dominance, overdominance, epistasis

**Heterozygote**
- Individual with two different alleles at a locus
- Example: Aa
- Contrasts with homozygote

**Homologous Chromosomes**
- Chromosome pairs with same genes
- One from each parent
- Pair during meiosis

**Homozygote**
- Individual with two identical alleles at a locus
- Example: AA or aa
- True-breeding for that locus

**Hybrid**
- Offspring from genetically different parents
- F1 hybrid: first generation cross
- Often exhibits heterosis

**Hydroponics**
- Growing plants without soil in nutrient solution
- Various systems: NFT, DWC, aeroponics
- Dominant method in CEA

---

## I

**Ideotype**
- Model of ideal plant for specific environment
- Guides breeding objectives
- Defines target trait combinations

**Inbred Line**
- Homozygous line from repeated self-pollination
- Used as hybrid parents
- Requires 7-8 generations to achieve 99% homozygosity

**Incomplete Dominance**
- Heterozygote shows intermediate phenotype
- Example: Pink flowers from red × white
- Genotypic and phenotypic ratios both 1:2:1

**Independent Assortment**
- Random distribution of alleles at different loci
- Mendel's Second Law
- Applies to unlinked genes

**Independent Culling Levels**
- Multi-trait selection with minimum standards for each trait
- Individual must meet all criteria
- Prevents extreme deficiency in any trait

**Indeterminate**
- Unlimited growth (no terminal flower)
- Example: Vining tomatoes
- Contrasts with determinate (bush) types

**Internode**
- Stem segment between nodes (leaf attachment points)
- Dwarfing genes reduce internode length
- Critical for compact CEA varieties

---

## K

**Karyotype**
- Visual representation of complete chromosome set
- Ordered by size and morphology
- Used to detect chromosomal abnormalities

---

## L

**LED (Light Emitting Diode)**
- Energy-efficient lighting for CEA
- Specific wavelengths (red, blue, green, far-red)
- Breeding for LED optimization critical

**Linkage**
- Genes on same chromosome tend to be inherited together
- Reduces independent assortment
- Recombination can break linkage

**Linkage Drag**
- Undesirable genes linked to target gene
- Brought along during introgression
- Minimized through recombinant selection

**Linkage Disequilibrium (LD)**
- Non-random association of alleles at different loci
- Decays over generations due to recombination
- Determines GWAS resolution

**Locus (plural: Loci)**
- Position on chromosome where gene is located
- Can have multiple alleles

---

## M

**Marker-Assisted Selection (MAS)**
- Selection based on DNA markers linked to genes
- Enables early selection without phenotyping
- Accelerates breeding

**Meiosis**
- Cell division producing four haploid gametes
- Includes crossing over and independent assortment
- Creates genetic variation

**Mendelian Inheritance**
- Patterns discovered by Gregor Mendel
- Laws of segregation and independent assortment
- Basis of genetics

**Meristem Culture**
- Culturing apical meristem (0.1-0.5 mm)
- Produces virus-free plants
- Exploits low virus concentration in meristem

**Micropropagation**
- Rapid clonal multiplication via tissue culture
- Four stages: establishment, multiplication, rooting, acclimatization
- Critical for producing certified planting material

**Mitosis**
- Cell division producing two identical daughter cells
- For growth and maintenance
- No genetic variation created

**Molecular Marker**
- DNA-based marker for genes or traits
- Types: RFLP, SSR, SNP, etc.
- Essential for MAS and genomic selection

**Mutation**
- Change in DNA sequence
- Source of new genetic variation
- Can be spontaneous or induced

---

## N

**Narrow-Sense Heritability (h²)**
- Proportion of phenotypic variance due to additive genetic effects
- h² = VA / VP
- Predicts response to selection

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)**
- Hydroponic system with thin film of nutrient solution
- Roots in shallow channel
- Common in CEA lettuce production

**NHEJ (Non-Homologous End Joining)**
- DNA repair pathway after double-strand break
- Error-prone, creates insertions/deletions
- Used in CRISPR for gene knockout

---

## O

**Organogenesis**
- Formation of organs (shoots, roots) from undifferentiated cells
- Two pathways: direct or via callus
- Contrasts with somatic embryogenesis

**Outcrossing**
- Cross-pollination between different individuals
- Increases genetic diversity
- Contrasts with self-pollination

**Overdominance**
- Heterozygote superior to both homozygotes
- Hypothesized mechanism of heterosis
- Example: Aa better than AA or aa

---

## P

**PAM (Protospacer Adjacent Motif)**
- Short DNA sequence required for Cas9 binding
- NGG for SpCas9
- Limits where CRISPR can target

**Pedigree**
- Record of ancestry and descent
- Essential in pedigree breeding method
- Documents selection history

**Pedigree Method**
- Breeding method with individual plant selection and records
- Labor-intensive but informative
- Ideal for multiple trait improvement

**Phenotype**
- Observable characteristics of organism
- Phenotype = Genotype + Environment
- What breeder selects on

**Phenotyping**
- Measuring observable traits
- Can be manual or high-throughput
- Critical for breeding progress

**Photoperiod**
- Day length
- Affects flowering in many species
- Day-neutral plants flower regardless of photoperiod

**Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density (PPFD)**
- Light intensity measure for plants
- μmol photons/m²/s
- Critical parameter in CEA

**Physical Map**
- Chromosome map based on DNA sequence
- Measured in base pairs (bp, kb, Mb)
- Complements genetic map

**Plant Variety Protection (PVP)**
- IP protection for new plant varieties
- Requirements: DUS (Distinct, Uniform, Stable) + Novelty
- 20-year protection in US

**Ploidy**
- Number of complete chromosome sets
- Haploid (n), diploid (2n), triploid (3n), tetraploid (4n), etc.

**Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)**
- DNA amplification technique
- Basis for many molecular markers
- Essential molecular biology tool

**Polygenic**
- Trait controlled by multiple genes
- Shows continuous variation
- Example: plant height, yield

**Polyploidy**
- More than two complete chromosome sets
- Common in plants
- Can create seedlessness, larger organs

**PPFD (See Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**

**Protoplast**
- Plant cell with cell wall removed
- Used in somatic hybridization
- Can be fused or transformed

---

## Q

**QTL (Quantitative Trait Locus)**
- Chromosomal region affecting quantitative trait
- Identified through marker-trait associations
- Varies in effect size (major vs. minor)

**Quantitative Trait**
- Trait showing continuous variation
- Controlled by multiple genes plus environment
- Examples: yield, size, quality

---

## R

**Realized Heritability**
- Heritability estimated from actual selection response
- h² = R / S
- Empirical estimate

**Recessive**
- Allele only expressed in homozygotes
- Masked by dominant allele in heterozygotes
- Lowercase letter notation (a)

**Reciprocal Cross**
- Two crosses with sexes reversed
- A♀ × B♂ vs. B♀ × A♂
- Tests for maternal effects or cytoplasmic inheritance

**Recombinant**
- Offspring with new allele combinations
- Result of crossing over
- Frequency indicates linkage distance

**Recombinant Inbred Lines (RILs)**
- Homozygous lines from repeated selfing of F2
- Used in QTL mapping
- Permanent populations

**Recurrent Parent (RP)**
- Elite parent in backcross breeding
- Genome is recovered through backcrossing
- Target: >98% RP genome in final line

**Recurrent Selection**
- Cyclical selection and intermating
- Gradual population improvement
- Increases frequency of favorable alleles

---

## S

**Segregation**
- Separation of alleles during gamete formation
- Mendel's First Law
- Results in predictable ratios

**Selection Differential (S)**
- Difference between selected parents' mean and population mean
- Component of breeder's equation
- Measure of selection pressure

**Selection Index**
- Weighted combination of multiple traits
- Optimizes multi-trait selection
- Weights reflect economic importance

**Selection Intensity (i)**
- Standardized selection differential
- Depends on proportion selected
- Higher with more stringent selection

**Self-Incompatibility (SI)**
- Genetic mechanism preventing self-fertilization
- Used in hybrid seed production (some crops)
- Sporophytic or gametophytic types

**Single Nucleotide Polymorphism (SNP)**
- Single base pair difference in DNA
- Most abundant type of variation
- Preferred marker for modern breeding

**Single Seed Descent (SSD)**
- Advancing one seed per plant without selection
- Rapid route to homozygosity
- Selection in later homozygous generations

**Somaclonal Variation**
- Genetic/epigenetic variation from tissue culture
- Can be problem (off-types) or opportunity (novel variation)
- Higher with callus and extended culture

**Somatic Embryogenesis**
- Embryo formation from somatic cells
- Bipolar structure (shoot + root)
- High multiplication potential

**Somatic Hybridization**
- Fusion of protoplasts from different species
- Overcomes sexual incompatibility
- Creates novel combinations

**Specific Combining Ability (SCA)**
- Performance deviation of specific hybrid pair
- Reflects non-additive gene effects
- Complements GCA in hybrid prediction

---

## T

**TILLING (Targeting Induced Local Lesions IN Genomes)**
- Forward genetics approach using mutant populations
- Identify mutations in specific genes
- Non-GMO alternative to transgenics

**Tissue Culture**
- Growing plant cells/tissues/organs on sterile medium
- Applications: micropropagation, transformation, DH production
- Requires sterile technique

**Totipotency**
- Ability of single cell to regenerate whole plant
- Fundamental principle of tissue culture
- Varies among species and cell types

**Transcriptome**
- Complete set of RNA transcripts
- RNA-seq for expression profiling
- Identifies genes active in specific conditions

**Transformation**
- Introduction of foreign DNA into plant genome
- Methods: Agrobacterium, biolistics
- Creates transgenic plants

**Transgene**
- Gene transferred from another organism
- Creates GMO/transgenic plant
- Can be segregated out in some breeding schemes

**Transgressive Segregation**
- Offspring exceeding both parents for a trait
- Result of complementary gene action
- Sought in cross breeding

---

## U

**UPOV (International Union for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants)**
- International treaty on plant variety protection
- 1991 Act is current version
- Harmonizes PVP requirements globally

**Utility Patent**
- Broader IP protection than PVP
- Covers plants, genes, methods
- No research exemption

---

## V

**Variance Components**
- VP = VG + VE + VG×E
- VG = VA + VD + VI
- Essential for heritability estimation

**Variety**
- Group of plants with distinct, uniform, stable characteristics
- Breeding program endpoint
- Protected by PVP or patent

**Vertical Farm**
- CEA system with vertical stacking of growing planes
- Maximizes production per footprint area
- Requires compact plant architecture

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)**
- Difference between air moisture and saturation
- Drives transpiration
- Optimal range: 0.8-1.2 kPa for many CEA crops

---

## W

**Wide Cross**
- Cross between distantly related species or genera
- Often requires embryo rescue
- Source of novel traits (e.g., disease resistance)

---

## Z

**Zygote**
- Cell formed by fusion of gametes
- Diploid (2n)
- Develops into embryo
