## Questions

### Question 1
Plant Variety Protection (PVP) certificates protect:

A) Plant species from extinction
B) Breeder's rights to sexually reproduced varieties
C) Wild plants only
D) Soil microorganisms

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 2
What is the typical PVP certificate duration in the United States?

A) 5 years
B) 10 years
C) 20 years
D) 25 years for trees/vines, 20 years for others

**Correct Answer:** D

---

### Question 3
Utility patents for plants differ from PVP because they:

A) Are weaker protection
B) Cover plant parts, genes, and methods; stronger than PVP
C) Only last 1 year
D) Are not legally recognized

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 4
An Essentially Derived Variety (EDV) is:

A) Completely different from original
B) Predominantly derived from initial variety with minor modifications
C) From a different species
D) Not related to breeding

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 5
The "breeder's exemption" under PVP allows:

A) Free commercial sale of protected varieties
B) Use of protected varieties for further breeding
C) Eliminating all protection
D) Copying varieties exactly for sale

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 6
UPOV (International Union for Protection of New Varieties of Plants) provides:

A) Plant healthcare
B) International framework for plant variety protection
C) Seeds to farmers
D) Greenhouse designs

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 7
To qualify for PVP or patent, a variety must be:

A) Old and common
B) Novel, Distinct, Uniform, and Stable (DUS)
C) From another country only
D) Identical to existing varieties

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 8
Royalty structures for protected varieties typically involve:

A) No compensation
B) Payment per unit of seed sold or per acre planted
C) One-time fee of $1
D) Free unlimited use

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 9
Open-source plant breeding initiatives aim to:

A) Eliminate all breeding
B) Share germplasm freely and prevent restrictive IP
C) Increase patent costs
D) Restrict all access

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 10
Trade secrets in plant breeding protect:

A) Only public information
B) Proprietary techniques, parent lines, and methods kept confidential
C) Nothing
D) Competitor's data

**Correct Answer:** B

---

## Answer Key

1. B - Breeder's rights to sexually reproduced varieties
2. D - 25 years for trees/vines, 20 years for others
3. B - Cover parts, genes, methods; stronger than PVP
4. B - Predominantly derived with minor modifications
5. B - Use of protected varieties for further breeding
6. B - International framework for plant variety protection
7. B - Novel, Distinct, Uniform, and Stable
8. B - Payment per unit sold or per acre planted
9. B - Share germplasm freely and prevent restrictive IP
10. B - Proprietary techniques and methods kept confidential
