## Questions

### Question 1
Marker-Assisted Selection (MAS) is MOST effective for:

A) Complex quantitative traits
B) Single gene or major QTL traits
C) Traits with no genetic basis
D) Cosmetic traits only

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 2
What is "foreground selection" in MAS?

A) Selecting plants in the front of the greenhouse
B) Using markers to select for the target gene/trait
C) Random selection
D) Phenotypic selection only

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 3
"Background selection" in MAS aims to:

A) Eliminate the target gene
B) Recover the recurrent parent genome outside target region
C) Select for unrelated traits
D) Increase crossing over

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 4
Gene pyramiding refers to:

A) Stacking multiple resistance genes in one variety
B) Growing plants in pyramids
C) Reducing gene number
D) Deleting genes

**Correct Answer:** A

---

### Question 5
A marker is 95% accurate for a disease resistance gene. If you screen 100 plants, how many false positives might occur?

A) 0
B) 5
C) 50
D) 95

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** 5% error rate × 100 plants = ~5 errors

---

### Question 6
Marker-Assisted Backcrossing (MABC) reduces backcross generations needed by:

A) Eliminating backcrossing
B) Selecting for target gene AND recurrent parent genome simultaneously
C) Increasing generation time
D) Random selection

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 7
What is the PRIMARY cost consideration for MAS?

A) Marker genotyping costs vs. phenotyping costs
B) Greenhouse rent
C) Labor for data entry
D) Marketing costs

**Correct Answer:** A

---

### Question 8
MAS is particularly valuable for traits that are:

A) Easy and cheap to phenotype
B) Difficult, expensive, or late-expressing to phenotype
C) Visible at germination
D) Not inherited

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 9
"Perfect" or "ideal" markers are:

A) 100 cM from target gene
B) Within or tightly linked (<1 cM) to target gene
C) On different chromosomes
D) Not associated with target

**Correct Answer:** B

---

### Question 10
Integration of MAS into breeding programs requires:

A) Abandoning all phenotypic selection
B) Combining marker and phenotypic data strategically
C) Using only markers for everything
D) Eliminating field trials

**Correct Answer:** B

---

## Answer Key

1. B - Single gene or major QTL traits
2. B - Using markers to select for the target gene/trait
3. B - Recover the recurrent parent genome outside target
4. A - Stacking multiple resistance genes in one variety
5. B - 5
6. B - Selecting for target gene AND recurrent parent genome
7. A - Marker genotyping costs vs. phenotyping costs
8. B - Difficult, expensive, or late-expressing to phenotype
9. B - Within or tightly linked (<1 cM) to target gene
10. B - Combining marker and phenotypic data strategically
