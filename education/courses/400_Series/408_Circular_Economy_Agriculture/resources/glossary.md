# Glossary: Circular Economy in Agriculture

## A

**Acidogenesis** - Second stage of anaerobic digestion where simple organics are converted to volatile fatty acids (VFAs), CO₂, and H₂.

**Activated Carbon** - Highly porous carbon material used for adsorption of organic compounds, odors, and contaminants from water and air.

**Advanced Oxidation Process (AOP)** - Treatment methods that generate hydroxyl radicals (•OH) to oxidize and degrade persistent organic pollutants. Includes UV/H₂O₂, ozonation, and Fenton processes.

**Aerobic Digestion** - Biological decomposition of organic matter in the presence of oxygen. Faster than anaerobic but produces no biogas.

**Anaerobic Digestion (AD)** - Biological process where microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas (methane and CO₂) and digestate.

**ANAMMOX** - Anaerobic ammonium oxidation; a process where ammonia and nitrite are converted directly to nitrogen gas, useful for nitrogen removal.

---

## B

**Biochar** - Carbon-rich material produced by pyrolysis of biomass under oxygen-limited conditions. Used for soil amendment, carbon sequestration, and nutrient retention.

**Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)** - Amount of dissolved oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water. Indicates organic pollution level.

**Biogas** - Mixture of methane (CH₄, 55-65%) and carbon dioxide (CO₂, 30-40%) produced by anaerobic digestion. Can be used for heat, electricity, or upgraded to biomethane.

**Biological Cycle** - In circular economy, the flow of biodegradable materials that can safely return to nature through composting or biological treatment.

**Biomethane** - Purified biogas (>95% methane) suitable for injection into natural gas pipelines or use as vehicle fuel.

**Biorefinery** - Facility that integrates biomass conversion processes to produce multiple products including fuels, chemicals, materials, and energy.

**B Corporation** - Certification for companies meeting high standards of social and environmental performance, accountability, and transparency.

---

## C

**Capital Recovery Factor (CRF)** - Factor used to convert a present value into a stream of equal annual payments, accounting for time value of money.

**Cascading Use** - Principle of using materials first for highest-value applications before successively lower-value uses, maximizing overall value extraction.

**Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)** - Total quantity of oxygen required to oxidize all organic and inorganic matter in water. Higher than BOD.

**Circular Economy** - Economic system designed to eliminate waste and keep resources in use through closed-loop material flows and regenerative design.

**Closed-Loop System** - System where outputs are recycled back as inputs, minimizing external resource requirements and waste generation.

**Combined Heat and Power (CHP)** - Simultaneous generation of electricity and useful heat from a single fuel source. Also called cogeneration.

**Composting** - Aerobic biological decomposition of organic matter into stable humus-like material used as soil amendment.

**Constructed Wetland** - Engineered system of shallow ponds, vegetation, and substrate designed to treat wastewater through natural processes.

**Cradle to Cradle** - Design philosophy where all materials are either safely biodegradable (biological nutrients) or continuously recycled (technical nutrients).

---

## D

**Denitrification** - Biological process converting nitrate (NO₃⁻) to nitrogen gas (N₂) under anaerobic conditions, removing nitrogen from water.

**Digestate** - Nutrient-rich residue remaining after anaerobic digestion. Contains undigested organic matter and plant-available nutrients.

**Discount Rate** - Interest rate used to calculate present value of future cash flows in economic analysis. Typically 6-10% for agricultural projects.

---

## E

**Eco-Industrial Park** - Community of businesses that collaborate to improve environmental and economic performance through resource sharing and waste exchange.

**Electrical Conductivity (EC)** - Measure of dissolved salts in water, expressed in dS/m or mS/cm. Indicates suitability for irrigation.

**Ellen MacArthur Foundation** - Leading organization promoting circular economy transition globally through research, education, and business initiatives.

**Environmental Product Declaration (EPD)** - Standardized document communicating environmental performance of products based on life cycle assessment.

**Eutrophication** - Excessive nutrient enrichment of water bodies leading to algal blooms, oxygen depletion, and ecosystem degradation.

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** - Policy approach where producers are responsible for end-of-life management of their products, incentivizing circular design.

---

## F

**Feedstock** - Raw material input to a process, particularly biomass for energy or biological treatment.

**Fermentation** - Anaerobic metabolic process converting sugars to acids, gases, or alcohol. First stage of biogas production.

**Functional Unit** - Reference measure in life cycle assessment defining what is being studied (e.g., "1,000 kg tomatoes delivered to market").

---

## G

**Gasification** - Thermochemical conversion of biomass to synthesis gas (syngas) at high temperature (700-1,000°C) with limited oxygen.

**Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)** - International framework for sustainability reporting by organizations.

**Green Premium** - Additional price consumers are willing to pay for environmentally sustainable products.

---

## H

**Heavy Metals** - Metallic elements (e.g., cadmium, lead, mercury) that are toxic at low concentrations and accumulate in biological systems.

**Higher Heating Value (HHV)** - Total energy released by complete combustion of fuel, including latent heat of water vapor.

**Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT)** - Average time that liquid remains in a treatment system. Key design parameter for biological reactors.

**Hydrolysis** - First stage of anaerobic digestion where complex organic polymers are broken down into simple monomers.

**Hydroponics** - Method of growing plants without soil, using nutrient solution in water.

---

## I

**Industrial Ecology** - Study of material and energy flows through industrial systems, inspired by natural ecosystems.

**Industrial Symbiosis** - Collaboration among diverse industries where waste or byproducts of one become resources for another.

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** - Discount rate that makes net present value of project equal to zero. Metric for evaluating investment profitability.

**Ion Exchange** - Process using resins to selectively remove specific ions from water, replacing them with less objectionable ions.

---

## K

**Kalundborg** - Danish town hosting world's first and most famous eco-industrial park, with extensive resource sharing among industries.

---

## L

**Leachate** - Liquid that has percolated through material and contains dissolved or suspended substances.

**Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE)** - Average cost per unit of energy over lifetime of a system, accounting for capital, operating costs, and production.

**Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)** - Methodology for evaluating environmental impacts of a product or service throughout its entire life cycle.

**Linear Economy** - Traditional "take-make-dispose" economic model where resources are extracted, used, and discarded.

**Lower Heating Value (LHV)** - Energy released by complete combustion excluding latent heat of water vapor. Practical usable energy.

---

## M

**Mass Balance** - Accounting for all material inputs, outputs, and accumulation in a system based on conservation of mass principle.

**Material Circularity Indicator (MCI)** - Metric (0-1 scale) measuring how restorative material flows are in a product system.

**Material Flow Analysis (MFA)** - Systematic assessment of flows and stocks of materials within a defined system over time.

**Membrane Filtration** - Pressure-driven separation process using semi-permeable membranes to remove particles, microorganisms, or dissolved substances.

**Mesophilic** - Operating temperature range of 30-40°C, optimal for most anaerobic digestion systems.

**Methanogenesis** - Final stage of anaerobic digestion where methane is produced by archaeal microorganisms.

**Microfiltration (MF)** - Membrane process with 0.1-10 μm pore size, removing bacteria and suspended solids.

---

## N

**Nanofiltration (NF)** - Membrane process with 0.001-0.01 μm pore size, removing divalent ions and small organic molecules.

**Net Present Value (NPV)** - Sum of discounted cash flows over project lifetime minus initial investment. Positive NPV indicates viable project.

**Nitrification** - Biological oxidation of ammonia (NH₃) to nitrite (NO₂⁻) and then to nitrate (NO₃⁻) in presence of oxygen.

**Nitrogen Use Efficiency (NUE)** - Percentage of applied nitrogen taken up by crops. Calculated as: (N in harvest / N input) × 100%.

---

## O

**Organic Loading Rate (OLR)** - Mass of organic matter (volatile solids) fed to anaerobic digester per unit volume per day. Typically 1.5-3.0 kg VS/m³/day.

**Ozonation** - Water treatment process using ozone (O₃) for disinfection and oxidation of contaminants.

---

## P

**Pathogen** - Disease-causing microorganism (bacteria, virus, protozoa, helminth).

**Payback Period** - Time required for cumulative savings or revenues from an investment to equal the initial capital cost.

**pH** - Measure of acidity or alkalinity on logarithmic scale from 0 (acidic) to 14 (basic). pH 7 is neutral.

**Phosphorus Use Efficiency (PUE)** - Percentage of applied phosphorus taken up by crops.

**Photocatalysis** - Advanced oxidation process using semiconductor materials (e.g., TiO₂) activated by UV light to generate reactive oxygen species.

**Precision Fermentation** - Use of microorganisms in fermenters to produce specific compounds (proteins, fats, enzymes) with high precision and efficiency.

**Product-as-Service** - Business model where customers pay for functionality rather than owning the product.

**Pyrolysis** - Thermal decomposition of biomass at 300-700°C in absence of oxygen, producing biochar, bio-oil, and syngas.

---

## Q

**Quantitative Microbial Risk Assessment (QMRA)** - Methodology for calculating infection risk from pathogen exposure, used in water reuse safety evaluation.

---

## R

**Regenerative Agriculture** - Farming approach that restores soil health, enhances biodiversity, and sequesters carbon while producing food.

**Renewable Energy Certificate (REC)** - Tradable commodity representing proof that 1 MWh of electricity was generated from renewable source.

**Reverse Logistics** - Process of moving goods from end-user back to manufacturer for return, recycling, or remanufacturing.

**Reverse Osmosis (RO)** - Membrane filtration using high pressure to remove dissolved salts and ions, producing high-purity water.

---

## S

**Sankey Diagram** - Flow diagram where arrow width is proportional to flow quantity, used to visualize material and energy flows.

**SASB** - Sustainability Accounting Standards Board; develops industry-specific standards for reporting material ESG information.

**Struvite** - Magnesium ammonium phosphate (MgNH₄PO₄·6H₂O), crystalline compound precipitated from wastewater for phosphorus recovery.

**Substance Flow Analysis (SFA)** - Material flow analysis focused on tracking specific elements or compounds (e.g., nitrogen, cadmium).

**Syngas** - Synthesis gas composed primarily of carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen (H₂), produced by gasification or steam reforming.

**Systems Thinking** - Approach to problem-solving viewing systems holistically, considering interactions, feedback loops, and emergent properties.

---

## T

**Technical Cycle** - In circular economy, the flow of non-biodegradable materials that must be kept in continuous use through recycling and remanufacturing.

**Technology Readiness Level (TRL)** - Scale (1-9) measuring maturity of technology from basic research (1-3) to commercial deployment (7-9).

**Thermophilic** - Operating temperature range of 50-60°C, used in some anaerobic digestion systems for faster processing and better pathogen kill.

**Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)** - Total amount of inorganic and organic substances dissolved in water, measured in mg/L or ppm.

**Total Solids (TS)** - All matter in sample remaining after drying at 103-105°C, including dissolved and suspended solids.

**Total Suspended Solids (TSS)** - Portion of total solids retained on a filter, indicating particulate matter in water.

**Turbidity** - Measure of water clarity, typically expressed in Nephelometric Turbidity Units (NTU). High turbidity interferes with disinfection.

---

## U

**Ultrafiltration (UF)** - Membrane process with 0.01-0.1 μm pore size, removing viruses, colloids, and macromolecules.

**UV Disinfection** - Use of ultraviolet light (typically 254 nm wavelength) to inactivate microorganisms by damaging DNA/RNA.

---

## V

**Valorization** - Process of converting waste materials into more valuable products through recovery, recycling, or transformation.

**Volatile Fatty Acids (VFAs)** - Short-chain organic acids (acetic, propionic, butyric) produced during acidogenesis phase of anaerobic digestion.

**Volatile Solids (VS)** - Portion of total solids that volatilizes (burns off) when heated to 550°C. Represents organic matter content.

---

## W

**Waste Hierarchy** - Ranking of waste management options from most to least preferred: prevention, minimization, reuse, recycling, energy recovery, disposal.

**Water Footprint** - Total volume of freshwater used to produce goods and services, including direct and indirect water consumption.

**Water Use Efficiency (WUE)** - Ratio of beneficial water use to total water input. In agriculture: crop yield per unit water consumed.

---

## Z

**Zero Waste** - Philosophy and goal of eliminating all waste through prevention, reuse, recycling, and composting. Typically defined as >90% diversion from landfill.

## Units & Conversions

**Volume:**
- 1 m³ = 1,000 L = 264.2 US gallons
- 1 hectare-meter = 10,000 m³

**Mass:**
- 1 tonne = 1,000 kg = 1 metric ton
- 1 kg = 2.205 lb

**Energy:**
- 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ
- 1 MWh = 1,000 kWh = 3,600 MJ
- 1 m³ methane = 35.8 MJ = 9.94 kWh
- 1 therm (natural gas) = 105.5 MJ

**Concentration:**
- 1 ppm = 1 mg/L (for dilute aqueous solutions)
- 1% = 10,000 ppm = 10 g/L

**Pressure:**
- 1 bar = 100 kPa = 14.5 psi
- 1 atm = 1.013 bar

**Electrical Conductivity:**
- 1 dS/m = 1 mS/cm = 1,000 μS/cm
- EC (dS/m) ≈ TDS (mg/L) / 640 (rough approximation)
