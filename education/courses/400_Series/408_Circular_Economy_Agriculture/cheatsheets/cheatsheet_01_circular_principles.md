# Cheatsheet 1: Circular Economy Principles & Framework

## Core Principles

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  THREE PILLARS OF CIRCULAR ECONOMY                  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                      │
│  1. DESIGN OUT WASTE & POLLUTION                    │
│     • Eliminate waste at design stage               │
│     • Prevent pollution through smart choices       │
│     • Rethink products and processes                │
│                                                      │
│  2. KEEP PRODUCTS & MATERIALS IN USE                │
│     • Maximize lifespan and utility                 │
│     • Maintain material quality through cycles      │
│     • Recover and regenerate at end-of-life         │
│                                                      │
│  3. REGENERATE NATURAL SYSTEMS                      │
│     • Return biological nutrients safely to soil    │
│     • Enhance ecosystem services                    │
│     • Build natural capital                         │
│                                                      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Waste Hierarchy (Most to Least Preferred)

1. **PREVENTION** - Design out waste
2. **MINIMIZATION** - Reduce resource inputs
3. **REUSE** - Multiple use cycles
4. **RECYCLING** - Material recovery
5. **ENERGY RECOVERY** - Thermal/biogas
6. **DISPOSAL** - Last resort only

---

## Material Cycles

**BIOLOGICAL CYCLE** (Biodegradable materials):
- Farming → Consumption → Organic Waste → Composting/AD → Soil Building → Farming

**TECHNICAL CYCLE** (Non-biodegradable):
- Manufacturing → Use → Collection → Refurbishment/Recycling → Manufacturing

## Key Metrics Quick Reference

| Metric | Formula | Good Performance |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| **Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE)** | (Nutrient in harvest / Nutrient input) × 100% | >70% |
| **Water Recovery Rate** | (Recycled water / Total water used) × 100% | >90% |
| **Energy Circularity** | (Renewable energy / Total energy) × 100% | >80% |
| **Waste Diversion** | (Waste recovered / Total waste) × 100% | >95% |
| **Material Circularity Indicator (MCI)** | Complex formula (0-1 scale) | >0.6 |

## Cascading Use Principle

```
HIGH VALUE    →    Human Food/Feed         ($2,000-10,000/t)
   ↓               Biochemicals/Materials   ($500-2,000/t)
   ↓               Animal Feed/Fertilizer   ($100-500/t)
   ↓               Energy Recovery          ($20-100/t)
LOW VALUE     →    Soil Amendment           ($5-20/t)
```

Always extract maximum value before moving to lower-value applications.

## Systems Thinking Checklist

- [ ] Define system boundaries clearly
- [ ] Map all inputs and outputs
- [ ] Identify feedback loops (reinforcing & balancing)
- [ ] Recognize time delays and non-linear relationships
- [ ] Look for leverage points (high-impact interventions)

- [ ] Engage stakeholders across the system

## Quick Decision Matrix: Circular vs. Linear

| Aspect | Linear | Circular |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Resources** | Extract & discard | Regenerate & cycle |
| **Value** | Single pass | Multiple cycles |
| **Waste** | Cost/liability | Valuable resource |
| **Energy** | Fossil-dependent | Renewable & recovered |
| **Design** | Disposability | Durability & recovery |
| **Mindset** | Throughput | Stewardship |

## Implementation Quick Wins

1. **Conduct Waste Audit** - Quantify what you're discarding
2. **Start Water Recycling** - Often fastest ROI
3. **Compost Organic Waste** - Simple, immediate impact
4. **Energy Monitoring** - Measure to manage
5. **Supplier Engagement** - Collaborate on packaging returns

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- ❌ "Greenwashing" without real circularity
- ❌ Focusing only on recycling (ignoring reduce/reuse)
- ❌ Overlooking economic viability
- ❌ Neglecting stakeholder engagement
- ❌ Lack of measurement and tracking
- ❌ Trying to do everything at once (start focused)

---

## Key Terminology

**Circular Economy**: Economic system designed to eliminate waste through continuous cycling of resources

**Industrial Symbiosis**: Collaboration where one entity's waste = another's resource

**Closed-Loop System**: Materials circulate indefinitely with minimal loss

**Biological Nutrients**: Organic materials that safely biodegrade

**Technical Nutrients**: Materials designed for continuous technical use

**Cradle to Cradle**: Design framework for 100% recyclable/biodegradable products
