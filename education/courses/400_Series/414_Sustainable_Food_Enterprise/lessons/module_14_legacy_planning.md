ACQUISITION / EXIT

Build permanence into organizational DNA:

**Legal Structures:**
- Benefit corporation or B Corp
- Cooperative ownership
- Charitable trust or foundation ownership
- Perpetual purpose structures

**Governance:**
- Mission guardians on board
- Stakeholder representation
- Supermajority requirements for mission changes
- Independent oversight

**Culture:**
- Values deeply embedded
- Storytelling and history preservation
- Rituals and traditions
- Multi-generational leadership

**Financial:**
- Sustainable business model
- Reserve funds and endowments
- Diversified revenue
- Patient capital structures

Balance mission commitment with fresh perspectives:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────┐

│ • Lived values and integrity       │

┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Relevant expertise (food, social enterprise)│

│ • Change management skills         │
│ • Financial acumen                 │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

FRESH PERSPECTIVE
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • New ideas and energy             │
│ • Different networks               │
│ • Complementary strengths          │
│ • Ability to evolve mission        │
└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Internal vs. External:**

**Internal Successor:**
- Advantages: Knows organization, culture, relationships; continuity
- Disadvantages: May resist change, lack fresh perspective
- Best for: Stable organizations, when strong internal talent exists

**External Successor:**
- Advantages: New ideas, energy, networks; can drive change
- Disadvantages: Learning curve, culture fit risk
- Best for: Organizations needing transformation, pivot, or scaling

**Hybrid:** Internal successor with external advisory board/coach

### Case Study: Greyston Bakery

**Challenge:** Founder Bernie Glassman departed after 20+ years

**Approach:**
- Open hiring model institutionalized (not dependent on founder)
- Board governance with community representation
- External CEO hired with social enterprise experience
- Mission protected in legal structure and culture
- Multiple CEO transitions successfully navigated

**Key Success Factors:**
- Mission embedded in operations (open hiring is the business model)
- Strong board oversight
- Clear values and principles guide decisions
- Regular stakeholder engagement maintains accountability

## 14.3 Exit Strategies for Social Enterprises

### Exit Options and Mission Implications

```
EXIT SCENARIOS
==============

OPTION 1: SALE TO CONVENTIONAL COMPANY
Risk: High (mission often compromised)
Examples: Annie's → General Mills, Ben & Jerry's → Unilever

OPTION 2: SALE TO MISSION-ALIGNED BUYER
Risk: Medium (depends on buyer commitment)
Examples: Nutiva → B Corp buyer, Seventh Generation → Unilever (B Corp)

OPTION 3: EMPLOYEE/MANAGEMENT BUYOUT
Risk: Low (preserves independence and mission)
Examples: King Arthur Baking → ESOP, New Belgium → ESOP

OPTION 4: IPO (Initial Public Offering)
Risk: High (shareholder pressure typically prioritizes returns)
Note: Few social enterprises pursue IPO; goes public-benefit corp helps

OPTION 5: TRANSITION TO NONPROFIT
Risk: Low for mission (tax-exempt, permanent mission)
Examples: Mozilla Foundation, Newman's Own (planned)

OPTION 6: PERPETUAL PRIVATE OWNERSHIP
Risk: Lowest (no exit, mission locked in perpetuity)
Examples: Patagonia → Trust + Nonprofit, IKEA → Stichting INGKA Foundation
```

### Protecting Mission in Sale

If sale is necessary/desired, protect mission through:

**1. Legal Requirements**
- Benefit corporation status (requires mission consideration)
- Sale to benefit corporation buyer
- Mission preservation clauses in purchase agreement

**2. Founder/Board Control**
- Dual-class shares (super-voting for founder)
- Golden shares (veto rights on mission changes)
- Right of first refusal on sale
- Board seats with mission oversight post-sale

**3. Financial Structures**
- Earnouts tied to impact metrics
- Clawbacks if mission compromised
- Ongoing royalties to mission-focused foundation

**4. Stakeholder Agreements**
- Community benefit agreements survive sale
- Supplier contracts honored
- Employee protections

### Case Study: Ben & Jerry's

**Situation:**
- Founders wanted to prevent hostile takeover (2000)
- Sold to Unilever for $326M

**Mission Protections:**
- Independent board retained (separate from Unilever)
- B Corp certification maintained
- Product integrity guarantees (ingredients, sourcing)
- Social mission and activism continued

**Outcome:**
- Mixed reviews: Some mission elements preserved, others questioned
- Significantly greater scale and resources
- Debate continues about trade-offs of acquisition

**Lessons:**
- Even with protections, mission can drift
- Scale and impact vs. independence trade-off
- Legal structures alone insufficient without cultural commitment
- Alternatives (ESOP, perpetual ownership) preferable for pure preservation

## 14.4 Perpetual Purpose Structures

### Steward-Ownership Model

Ownership structure separates economic rights from voting rights, ensuring mission permanence.

```
STEWARD-OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE
===========================

TRADITIONAL OWNERSHIP
Owners have:
• Economic rights (profit, sale proceeds)
• Voting rights (control company)
→ Can sell company, change mission for profit

STEWARD-OWNERSHIP
Separated:
• ECONOMIC RIGHTS: Investors, employees
• VOTING RIGHTS: Stewards (mission guardians)

Stewards:
• Cannot sell company
• Cannot extract excessive value
• Must preserve mission
• Self-perpetuating (select successors)

Economic investors:
• Receive fair returns (limited %)
• No voting control
• Cannot force sale or exit
```

**Implementation:**
- Purpose Trust (holds voting shares)
- Economic shares (non-voting, dividend rights)
- Stewardship board (self-selecting, mission guardians)
- Charter prohibits sale of company

**Examples:**
- Patagonia: Ownership transferred to trust (2%) and nonprofit (98%)
- Bosch (Germany): 94% owned by foundation, family retains voting control
- Zeiss (Germany): Foundation-owned since 1889
- Purpose Network: 100+ companies globally

### Purpose Trust Design

**Trust Structure:**
```
PATAGONIA EXAMPLE (2022)
========================

PATAGONIA PURPOSE TRUST (2% ownership, 100% voting)
• Holds all voting shares
• Yvon Chouinard family are trustees
• Perpetual mission to protect nature
• Cannot sell company
• Selects board of directors

HOLDFAST COLLECTIVE (98% ownership, non-voting)
• 501(c)(4) nonprofit
• Receives 100% of profits (after reinvestment)
• Uses funds for environmental grants
• Family receives no dividends

RESULT:
• Company cannot be sold
• Profits permanently dedicated to mission
• Family receives no ongoing economic benefit
• Mission protected in perpetuity
```

**Creating a Purpose Trust:**
1. Legal counsel experienced in purpose structures
2. Draft trust documents (purpose, trustee selection, governance)
3. Transfer voting shares to trust
4. Establish trustee succession plan
5. Communication with stakeholders
6. Ongoing governance and accountability

## 14.5 Generational Transitions

### Family Business Succession

Many food enterprises are family-owned. Transitioning to next generation while preserving mission requires intentionality.

**Challenges:**
- Next generation may lack interest or capability
- Sibling conflicts
- Value differences between generations
- Tax implications (estate, gift taxes)
- Non-family leadership vs. family control

**Strategies:**

**1. Early and Ongoing Dialogue**
- Discuss succession from early age
- Understand next generation's interests
- Share business and mission education
- No assumptions or pressure

**2. Gradual Involvement**
- Summer work, internships
- Entry-level positions (no special treatment)
- Rotations through different functions
- Increasing responsibility based on merit

**3. Governance and Fairness**
- Family council (separate from board)
- Clear policies (who can work in business, compensation)
- Equitable treatment of family members
- Professional management if family not capable/interested

**4. Ownership Structure Options**
- All children own equally (voting or non-voting)
- Operating child owns/controls
- Family office or trust structure
- Foundation ownership with family as stewards

**5. Mission Preservation**
- Family mission statement
- Trustee roles for mission guardians
- Benefit corporation or B Corp structure
- Professional board with independent directors

### Case Study: Organic Valley

**Structure:** Farmer cooperative (not family, but multi-generational)

**Succession Approach:**
- Cooperative structure ensures farmer-ownership continues
- Next generation of farmers join coop as members
- Democratic governance (continuity through structure, not individual)
- Mission embedded in cooperative principles
- Professional management supports farmer-owners

**Key Insight:** Organizational structures (coop, trust, benefit corp) enable mission continuity beyond any individual or generation

## 14.6 Balancing Legacy and Evolution

### The Founder's Dilemma

Preserving core mission while allowing necessary adaptation:

```
PRESERVE vs. EVOLVE
===================

PRESERVE (Sacred)
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Core purpose and values          │
│ • Stakeholder commitments          │
│ • Ethical standards                │
│ • Quality and integrity            │
└────────────────────────────────────┘

EVOLVE (Adaptive)
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Strategies and tactics           │
│ • Products and services            │
│ • Markets and segments             │
│ • Technology and processes         │
│ • Organizational structure         │
└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Guidance for Next Generation:**
- "Preserve the core, stimulate progress" (Collins & Porras)
- Honor founders while bringing fresh energy
- Engage stakeholders in evolution
- Measure impact continuously (ensure evolution doesn't compromise)
- Document learnings and decisions for future generations

### Living Legacy

Founders can support enduring impact by:

**1. Documenting Institutional Knowledge**
- History and founding story
- Decision-making frameworks and principles
- Key relationships and context
- Lessons learned and failures
- Vision for future

**2. Building Strong Culture**
- Values deeply embedded
- Rituals and traditions
- Storytelling
- Leadership development
- Stakeholder connections

**3. Creating Legal/Governance Structures**
- Mission protection mechanisms
- Stakeholder governance
- Financial sustainability
- Succession plans

**4. Developing Next Generation Leaders**
- Mentorship and coaching
- Gradual transfer of responsibility
- Trust and empowerment
- Support during transition

**5. Letting Go**
- Step back fully (don't micromanage)
- Trust successors
- Offer support when asked
- Celebrate new chapter

## Key Takeaways

1. Longevity threats include founder transitions, acquisitions, mission drift, and organizational failure; design for permanence from beginning
2. Succession planning should begin 3-5 years in advance with careful successor selection balancing mission alignment and fresh perspective
3. Exit strategies range from high-risk (conventional acquisition) to low-risk (employee buyout, nonprofit transition, perpetual ownership)
4. Perpetual purpose structures like steward-ownership and purpose trusts permanently separate voting control from economic rights to protect mission
5. Generational transitions in family businesses require early dialogue, gradual involvement, fair governance, and mission preservation mechanisms
6. Balancing legacy and evolution means preserving core purpose and values while adapting strategies, products, markets, and structure
7. Founders support enduring impact through documentation, culture-building, legal structures, leadership development, and letting go

## Practical Exercise

**Legacy Plan for Your Enterprise**

**Part 1: Longevity Assessment**
- Evaluate current threats to mission longevity
- Assess existing protections (legal, governance, cultural)
- Identify gaps and vulnerabilities
- Prioritize areas for strengthening

**Part 2: Succession Planning**
- Define timeline for current leadership transition
- Create successor profile (characteristics, experience, values)
- Identify internal candidates and development needs
- Design transition process and timeline
- Plan knowledge transfer and relationship introductions

**Part 3: Exit Strategy Selection**
- Evaluate exit options against mission priorities
- Select preferred exit approach with rationale
- Identify mission protection mechanisms for that exit
- Create contingency plans for alternative scenarios

**Part 4: Ownership Structure**
- Research perpetual purpose structures (steward-ownership, purpose trust, foundation)
- Assess feasibility and appropriateness for your enterprise
- Design proposed structure (governance, voting, economic rights)
- Estimate legal and implementation costs
- Create 5-year roadmap toward implementation if desired

**Part 5: Legacy Documentation**
- Write founding story and mission origin
- Document decision-making principles and frameworks
- Record key stakeholder relationships and context
- Articulate vision for 25+ year future
- Create guidance for future leaders (preserve vs. evolve)

**Part 6: Cultural Preservation**
- Identify core values, rituals, and traditions to preserve
- Design culture reinforcement mechanisms
- Create employee ownership or engagement strategies
- Plan stakeholder governance structures
- Develop leadership pipeline and succession depth
