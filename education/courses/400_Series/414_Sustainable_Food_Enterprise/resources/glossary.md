# Glossary of Terms

## Course 414: Sustainable Food Enterprise | EcoFusion Academy

---

## A

**Additionality**
The principle that an intervention creates impact that would not have occurred otherwise. Critical for assessing true social/environmental value.

**Attribution**
The portion of observed outcomes that can be directly attributed to a specific intervention, as opposed to other factors or what would have happened anyway (deadweight).

---

## B

**B Corp (Certified B Corporation)**
A certification granted by B Lab to for-profit companies that meet rigorous standards of social and environmental performance, accountability, and transparency. Requires 80+ points on B Impact Assessment.

**B Impact Assessment (BIA)**
A comprehensive evaluation tool (200 points possible) measuring a company's impact on workers, community, environment, customers, and governance. Used for B Corp certification and continuous improvement.

**Balanced Scorecard**
Performance management framework that includes financial and non-financial metrics across multiple perspectives (typically financial, customer, internal processes, learning/growth; adapted for social enterprises to include stakeholder impact).

**Benefit Corporation**
A legal corporate structure available in most U.S. states that requires companies to create general public benefit, consider stakeholder interests in decisions, and report on social/environmental performance. Different from B Corp certification.

**Beneficiary**
An individual, community, or ecosystem that experiences positive impact from a social enterprise's activities. May or may not be the same as paying customers.

**Business Model Canvas**
A strategic management template for developing new or documenting existing business models, consisting of nine key components. Social Business Model Canvas adds mission and impact dimensions.

---

## C

**Carbon Footprint**
The total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly and indirectly by an organization, expressed as carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e).

**Carbon Sequestration**
The process of capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide, particularly in soil through agricultural practices like cover cropping and reduced tillage.

**Circular Economy**
An economic system aimed at eliminating waste and maximizing resource use through reuse, sharing, repair, refurbishment, remanufacturing, and recycling.

**Community Benefit Agreement (CBA)**
A legally binding contract between community organizations and a developer/business outlining project benefits to be provided to the community (jobs, services, environmental protections, etc.).

**Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI)**
A financial institution that provides credit and financial services to underserved markets and populations, often with flexible terms and technical assistance.

**Contribution Margin**
Revenue per unit minus variable costs per unit. Indicates how much each sale contributes to covering fixed costs and generating profit.

**Cooperative (Co-op)**
A business owned and democratically governed by its members (who may be workers, consumers, or producers), operating for mutual benefit rather than profit maximization.

**Cross-Subsidy Model**
A business model where revenue from one customer segment (typically affluent) subsidizes below-cost offerings to another segment (typically underserved beneficiaries).

---

## D

**Deadweight**
In impact measurement, the amount of outcome that would have happened even without the intervention. Must be subtracted to calculate net impact.

**Direct Trade**
A sourcing model where companies buy directly from producers without intermediaries, emphasizing personal relationships, quality, and transparency. Not third-party certified unlike Fair Trade.

**Double Materiality**
ESG concept that topics are material if they either (1) affect company financial performance or (2) represent significant company impacts on society/environment.

**Drop-Off**
In impact measurement (especially SROI), the rate at which outcomes deteriorate over time after an intervention ends.

---

## E

**Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP)**
A retirement plan that makes employees owners of stock in the company, with tax advantages for the company and wealth-building for employees.

**ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance)**
Framework for evaluating corporate performance on sustainability and ethical issues. Increasingly used by investors to assess risk and opportunity.

**Externality**
A cost or benefit that affects parties who did not choose to incur that cost or benefit. In food systems, often environmental or social costs not reflected in market prices.

---

## F

**Fair Trade**
A trading partnership based on dialogue, transparency, and respect, seeking greater equity in international trade through better prices, working conditions, and terms for marginalized producers and workers.

**Fair Trade Certification**
Third-party verification (e.g., Fair Trade USA, Fairtrade International) that products meet social, environmental, and economic standards including minimum prices and premiums.

**Fiduciary Duty**
A legal obligation to act in the best interest of another party. Traditionally interpreted as maximizing shareholder value; expanded in benefit corporations to include stakeholders.

**Food Desert**
An area with limited access to affordable and nutritious food, particularly lacking grocery stores within reasonable distance.

**Food Hub**
A business or organization that actively manages the aggregation, distribution, and marketing of food products from local and regional producers to wholesale, retail, and institutional customers.

**Food Sovereignty**
The right of peoples to healthy and culturally appropriate food produced through ecologically sound and sustainable methods, and their right to define their own food and agriculture systems.

---

## G

**Greenwashing**
Misleading claims about the environmental or social benefits of a product, service, or company practice. Making impact appear greater than it is.

**GRI (Global Reporting Initiative)**
Most widely used international standards for sustainability reporting, covering economic, environmental, and social impacts.

---

## I

**Impact**
Long-term, ultimate change created by an organization's activities. Distinguished from outputs (what you produce) and outcomes (changes that result).

**Impact Business Model**
In the B Impact Assessment, a category of points available to companies whose core products or services are designed to address specific social or environmental problems.

**Impact Investor**
An investor who seeks to generate measurable social and environmental impact alongside financial returns.

**Impact Measurement and Management (IMM)**
The practice of tracking, assessing, and using data about social and environmental outcomes to improve performance and communicate value.

**Impact Washing**
Similar to greenwashing; exaggerating or misrepresenting social impact to attract impact investors or conscious consumers.

**IRIS+ (Impact Reporting and Investment Standards)**
Catalog of generally accepted metrics maintained by the Global Impact Investing Network (GIIN) for measuring and reporting social and environmental performance.

---

## L

**Living Wage**
The minimum income necessary for a worker to meet basic needs of their family, including food, housing, healthcare, education, transportation, and small savings. Typically higher than legally mandated minimum wage.

---

## M

**Materiality (ESG/Sustainability Context)**
The principle that companies should report on topics that significantly affect stakeholder assessments and decisions, or that represent significant economic, environmental, or social impacts.

**Mission Drift**
The gradual shift of a social enterprise away from its original social or environmental purpose, typically toward financial optimization at the expense of impact.

**Multi-Stakeholder**
An approach that involves multiple types of stakeholders (e.g., workers, suppliers, community, environment) in governance, decision-making, or benefit distribution.

---

## O

**Outcomes**
The short- to medium-term changes that result from an organization's activities and outputs. Precede ultimate long-term impact.

**Outputs**
The direct products, services, or deliverables produced by an organization's activities. Measured in units (meals served, products sold, trainings delivered, etc.).

---

## P

**Patient Capital**
Investment capital that is willing to forgo short-term returns in favor of long-term gains, allowing enterprises time to develop and scale impact.

**Pay Ratio**
The ratio between the highest-paid and lowest-paid worker in an organization. Some social enterprises cap this ratio (e.g., 10:1, 20:1) to reduce inequality.

**Perpetual Purpose Trust**
An ownership structure where voting control of a company is held in trust for the purpose of preserving its mission in perpetuity, while economic rights may be held separately.

**Program-Related Investment (PRI)**
An investment made by a foundation to support charitable activities that involve the potential return of capital within an established time frame. Can accept below-market returns.

---

## R

**Regenerative Agriculture**
Farming and ranching practices that, among other benefits, reverse climate change by rebuilding soil organic matter and restoring degraded soil biodiversity, resulting in carbon sequestration and improved water cycle.

**Regenerative Organic Certified (ROC)**
Certification that combines organic standards with additional requirements for soil health, animal welfare, and farmworker fairness.

**Revenue-Based Financing**
An investment model where the investor receives a percentage of the company's ongoing gross revenues until a predetermined amount has been repaid (typically 1.5-3x the investment). No equity dilution.

**RTO (Recovery Time Objective)**
In business continuity planning, the target duration within which a business process must be restored after a disruption to avoid unacceptable consequences.

---

## S

**SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board)**
Industry-specific standards focused on financially material sustainability topics for investor decision-making.

**Scope 1, 2, 3 Emissions**
- **Scope 1:** Direct emissions from owned or controlled sources
- **Scope 2:** Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, heat, steam
- **Scope 3:** All other indirect emissions in the value chain (suppliers, customers, etc.)

**Social Business Model Canvas**
An adaptation of the Business Model Canvas that integrates mission, impact value propositions, beneficiary segments, impact costs, and impact metrics.

**Social Capital**
The networks of relationships among people who live and work in a particular society, enabling that society to function effectively. Includes trust, norms, and connections.

**Social Enterprise**
An organization that applies commercial strategies to maximize improvements in financial, social, and environmental well-being, rather than maximizing profit for external shareholders.

**Social Entrepreneurship**
The practice of using startup companies and entrepreneurs to develop, fund, and implement solutions to social, cultural, or environmental issues.

**Social Return on Investment (SROI)**
A principles-based method for measuring and accounting for value creation, expressed as a ratio of social/environmental benefits to investment costs (e.g., 5:1 means $5 of value created per $1 invested).

**Stakeholder**
Any individual, group, or organization that affects or is affected by an organization's actions, objectives, and policies. Includes but not limited to shareholders.

**Stakeholder Capitalism**
A system in which corporations are oriented to serve the interests of all stakeholders (customers, suppliers, employees, communities, shareholders, environment) rather than shareholders alone.

**Steward-Ownership**
An ownership structure that separates voting control (held by stewards committed to mission) from economic rights (held by investors or employees). Company cannot be sold for profit.

**Supply Chain**
The network between a company and its suppliers to produce and distribute a specific product to the final buyer, including all steps from raw materials to end customer.

**Sustainability-Linked Loan**
A loan where the interest rate is tied to the borrower's achievement of predefined environmental, social, or governance targets.

---

## T

**TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures)**
Framework for companies to disclose climate-related risks and opportunities to investors, lenders, and insurers.

**Theory of Change (ToC)**
A comprehensive description and illustration of how and why a desired change is expected to happen in a particular context, mapping the causal pathway from inputs through activities, outputs, outcomes, to impact.

**Traceability**
The ability to trace the history, application, or location of an item through the supply chain using documented recorded identification.

**Triple Bottom Line**
A framework that incorporates three dimensions of performance: social, environmental, and financial (people, planet, profit).

---

## U

**Unit Economics**
The direct revenues and costs associated with a particular business model, expressed on a per-unit basis. Fundamental to understanding scalability and profitability.

---

## V

**Values-Based Hiring**
A recruitment approach that evaluates candidates for mission alignment and cultural fit alongside skills and experience.

**Vertical Integration**
The combination in one company of two or more stages of production normally operated by separate companies (e.g., a food company owning farms, processing, distribution, and retail).

---

## W

**Worker Cooperative**
A cooperative owned and democratically governed by its employees, who share in surplus (profit) based on work contributed rather than capital invested.

---

## Z

**Zero-Waste**
A goal and set of principles focused on waste prevention through redesigning resource life cycles so that all products are reused, with no trash sent to landfills or incinerators.
