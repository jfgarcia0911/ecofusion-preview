# Activity: Complete Impact Assessment for a Food Enterprise

## Course 414: Sustainable Food Enterprise | EcoFusion Academy

**Time Required:** 3-4 hours
**Type:** Individual or team exercise
**Deliverable:** Impact assessment report (8-12 pages)

---

## Objective

Conduct a comprehensive impact assessment for a sustainable food enterprise (real or hypothetical), including Theory of Change, metrics selection, data collection plan, and reporting framework.

---

## Instructions

### Part 1: Enterprise Selection and Description (30 minutes)

Select a sustainable food enterprise to assess. This can be:
- Your own venture (existing or planned)
- A real enterprise you research
- A hypothetical enterprise you design

**Write a 1-page description including:**
- **Business name and mission statement**
- **Product/service offering**
- **Target customers and beneficiaries**
- **Business model (how you make money)**
- **Stage** (concept, startup, growth, mature)
- **Annual revenue** (actual or projected)
- **Number of employees**
- **Geographic scope**

**Example:**
```
Name: Harvest Hub Regional Food Cooperative
Mission: Transform our regional food system by connecting regenerative farms
        with institutional buyers while building farmer livelihoods and
        community food security.

Products/Services:
• Aggregation and distribution of locally-produced food
• Technical assistance for regenerative agriculture transition
• Subsidized produce boxes for food-insecure households

Customers: Restaurants, schools, hospitals (wholesale)
Beneficiaries: Small-scale farmers, low-income families, environment

Business Model: Wholesale margins (20-30%), grants for capacity building
               and subsidized programs

Stage: Growth stage
Revenue: $2.5M annually
Employees: 15 FTE
Geography: 100-mile radius, 50 farmer partners
```

---

### Part 2: Theory of Change Development (60 minutes)

Develop a complete Theory of Change mapping your causal pathway from inputs to ultimate impact.

**Step 1: Define Ultimate Impact** (10 years+)
Write 2-3 specific long-term changes you seek to create.

Example:
- Resilient regional food system with thriving farm economies
- Restored ecosystem health on 10,000+ acres
- Equitable food access for all community residents

**Step 2: Identify Intermediate Outcomes** (3-5 years)
What changes must occur to reach ultimate impact?

Example:
- 100 farms adopt regenerative practices
- Average farmer income increases 40%
- Soil organic matter increases 2% on partner farms
- 5,000 low-income households access affordable produce
- Regional food infrastructure strengthened

**Step 3: Define Outputs** (1-2 years)
What products/services create those outcomes?

Example:
- 50 farms receive technical assistance
- $8M in farm revenue facilitated through sales
- 200,000 meals delivered to food-insecure households
- 3 new food hubs established in food deserts

**Step 4: Map Activities**
What do you DO to produce outputs?

Example:
- Recruit and train farmers on regenerative practices
- Aggregate, store, and distribute products
- Market and sell to institutional buyers
- Operate subsidized community food program
- Provide farm business planning support

**Step 5: List Inputs**
What resources are required?

Example:
- Capital: $1M (equipment, working capital, reserves)
- Grants: $500K (technical assistance programs)
- Staff: 15 FTE (operations, farm support, sales)
- Infrastructure: Warehouse, refrigeration, vehicles
- Relationships: Farmer network, buyer relationships

**Step 6: Articulate Assumptions**
What must be true for this logic to work?

Example:
- Farmers willing to transition practices given market incentives
- Buyers will pay premium for local/regenerative
- Regenerative practices will improve soil health
- Logistics can be managed at reasonable cost
- Low-income communities will purchase subsidized produce

**Step 7: Identify External Factors**
What contextual factors affect success?

Example:
- Weather and climate conditions
- Commodity price volatility
- Labor availability
- Policy and regulation changes
- Competing food systems

**Deliverable:** Create visual Theory of Change diagram and 2-3 page written explanation.

---

### Part 3: Metrics Selection (45 minutes)

Develop a Balanced Impact Scorecard with 2-3 metrics per stakeholder group.

**Use this template:**

| Stakeholder | Outcome Goal | Metric 1 | Metric 2 | Metric 3 |
|-------------|--------------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Primary Beneficiary 1** | | | | |
| **Primary Beneficiary 2** | | | | |
| **Environment** | | | | |
| **Employees** | | | | |
| **Investors/Funders** | | | | |

**For each metric, specify:**
- **Definition:** What exactly are you measuring?
- **Unit:** Number, %, $, tons, etc.
- **Calculation:** How is it calculated?
- **Target:** What are you aiming for?
- **Baseline:** Current state (if known)
- **Data source:** Where does data come from?
- **Frequency:** How often measured?
- **IRIS+ ID:** If applicable (search iris.thegiin.org)

**Example:**

**Metric:** Average Farmer Income Change

- **Definition:** Change in annual net farm income for participating farmers compared to baseline
- **Unit:** Dollars ($) and percent (%)
- **Calculation:** (Current year income - Baseline income) / Baseline income
- **Target:** +30% by Year 3
- **Baseline:** $42,000 average (Year 0)
- **Data Source:** Annual farmer survey
- **Frequency:** Annually
- **IRIS+ ID:** PI8302 (Farmer Income)

**Deliverable:** Completed scorecard with 10-15 total metrics across stakeholder groups.

---

### Part 4: Data Collection Plan (45 minutes)

Design how you will collect data for each metric.

**Create a data collection matrix:**

| Metric | Collection Method | Responsible Party | Timeline | Tools/Systems | Quality Assurance |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| | | | | | |

**Collection Methods to Consider:**
- Administrative data (sales records, program databases)
- Surveys (online, phone, in-person)
- Interviews and focus groups
- Direct observation
- Sensor/technology data (soil tests, emissions monitoring)
- Third-party audits or certifications
- Financial statements
- Public data sources

**Example:**

| Metric | Collection Method | Responsible | Timeline | Tools | Quality Assurance |
|--------|------|------|------|------|------|
| Farmer Income Change | Annual survey | Farm Services Manager | Dec each year | Qualtrics survey platform | 80%+ response rate target, validate outliers with follow-up |
| Soil Organic Matter | Lab testing (soil samples) | Agronomist (contract) | Spring (pre-season) | Accredited soil lab | Use consistent lab, same sample protocols |
| Meals Distributed | Program records | Community Programs Coordinator | Real-time tracking | Custom database | Daily reconciliation, monthly audit |
| Carbon Sequestered | Third-party assessment | Sustainability Director | Annual | Regen Ag consultant | Science-based model (COMET-Farm) |

**Data Management:**
- Where will data be stored? (spreadsheet, database, software)
- Who has access?
- How will you ensure data privacy and security?
- Backup procedures?
- How long will data be retained?

**Deliverable:** Complete data collection plan for all metrics.

---

### Part 5: Impact Analysis and Reporting (45 minutes)

Design your impact reporting approach.

**A. Analysis Plan**

For each metric category, describe how you'll analyze data:

**Trend Analysis:** Track over time, identify patterns
Example: "Plot farmer income annually 2023-2027, calculate CAGR, assess progress toward +30% target"

**Cohort Analysis:** Compare groups
Example: "Compare income change for farmers who received intensive technical assistance vs. basic program"

**Correlation Analysis:** Examine relationships
Example: "Analyze correlation between soil health improvement and income increase"

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Impact per dollar
Example: "Calculate cost per ton CO2 sequestered, compare to carbon offset market prices"

**B. Reporting Structure**

Create an outline for your annual impact report:

1. **Executive Summary**
   - Mission and highlights
   - Key metrics summary

2. **Our Theory of Change**
   - Visual diagram
   - Explanation

3. **Impact by Stakeholder**
   - Farmers: [outcomes and metrics]
   - Community/Consumers: [outcomes and metrics]
   - Environment: [outcomes and metrics]
   - Employees: [outcomes and metrics]
   - Stories and testimonials

4. **Financial Summary**
   - Revenue and expenses
   - Sustainability progress
   - Use of funds

5. **Lessons and Looking Ahead**
   - What we learned
   - Adaptations made
   - Next year goals

**C. Reporting Frequency and Audiences**

Define what you'll report, to whom, and when:

| Audience | Content | Frequency | Format |
|----------|---------|-----------|--------|
| Board of Directors | Full metrics, financials | Quarterly | Dashboard + meeting |
| Impact investors | IRIS+ metrics, progress to goals | Quarterly | Email update + annual report |
| Farmers (suppliers) | Aggregate outcomes, pricing, programs | Semi-annually | Newsletter + meeting |
| Community/Customers | Impact stories, key metrics | Annually | Public impact report, website |
| General Public | Overview of mission and impact | Annually | Annual report, social media |

**Deliverable:** Impact reporting plan including analysis approach, report outline, and communication strategy.

---

### Part 6: Reflection and Improvement (15 minutes)

Answer these questions:

1. **Feasibility:** Is your measurement plan realistic given your resources? What would you prioritize if resources were more limited?

2. **Validity:** Do your metrics truly capture the change you're trying to create? What are limitations?

3. **Stakeholder Input:** How would you involve beneficiaries and other stakeholders in defining and reviewing impact metrics?

4. **Learning:** How will you use impact data to improve your enterprise, not just report externally?

5. **Evolution:** How might your impact measurement approach evolve as you grow from startup to mature organization?

## Final Deliverable Format

Compile your work into a single document:

**Impact Assessment Report**

1. **Enterprise Description** (1 page)
2. **Theory of Change** (3-4 pages with visual)
3. **Balanced Impact Scorecard** (2-3 pages)
4. **Data Collection Plan** (2-3 pages)
5. **Impact Reporting Strategy** (1-2 pages)
6. **Reflection** (1 page)

**Total: 10-15 pages**

## Evaluation Criteria

Your submission will be evaluated on:

- **Clarity of Theory of Change** (20 points)
  - Logical causal pathway
  - Clear articulation of assumptions
  - Realistic and specific outcomes

- **Metric Quality** (25 points)
  - SMART+I metrics
  - Balanced across stakeholders
  - Mix of output and outcome metrics
  - Aligned with Theory of Change

- **Data Collection Feasibility** (20 points)
  - Realistic methods given resources
  - Clear responsibilities and timeline
  - Quality assurance approach
  - Integration into operations

- **Reporting Strategy** (20 points)
  - Tailored to audiences
  - Appropriate frequency
  - Analysis plan for learning
  - Storytelling alongside data

- **Reflection and Critical Thinking** (15 points)
  - Recognition of limitations
  - Thoughtful trade-offs
  - Stakeholder-centered approach
  - Commitment to learning and improvement

**Total: 100 points**

## Tips for Success

✓ **Start with the end in mind** - What is the ultimate change you're trying to create?

✓ **Be specific and realistic** - Vague goals lead to unmeasurable outcomes

✓ **Focus on outcomes, not just outputs** - Activities and products matter less than actual change

✓ **Balance rigor with pragmatism** - Perfect measurement is impossible; aim for good enough

✓ **Think about your story** - Numbers + narratives = compelling impact communication

✓ **Design for learning** - Measurement should drive improvement, not just accountability

✓ **Involve stakeholders** - Those experiencing impact should help define success
