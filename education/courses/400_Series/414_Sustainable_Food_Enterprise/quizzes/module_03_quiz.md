**Question 1:** What is a Theory of Change in impact measurement?

A) A change management process
B) A comprehensive description of how and why desired change is expected to happen
C) Financial projections
D) Organizational restructuring plan

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Theory of Change maps the causal pathway from activities → outputs → outcomes → impact, articulating assumptions about how change occurs.*

---

**Question 2:** Which framework provides standardized metrics for impact measurement across social enterprises?

A) GAAP
B) IRIS+ (Impact Reporting & Investment Standards)
C) ISO 9000
D) Six Sigma

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: IRIS+ by the Global Impact Investing Network provides standardized metrics for measuring and reporting social/environmental performance across sectors.*

---

**Question 3:** What does SROI (Social Return on Investment) measure?**

A) Stock market returns
B) The ratio of social value created to resources invested
C) Employee satisfaction
D) Product quality

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: SROI quantifies social/environmental value in monetary terms, comparing value created (benefits) to investment (costs) as a ratio (e.g., 3:1 SROI).*

---

**Question 4:** For a food enterprise focused on farmer livelihoods, which is an appropriate OUTCOME metric?**

A) Number of training sessions held (output)
B) Increase in farmer income or improvement in living standards (outcome)
C) Dollars spent on programs (input)
D) Number of farmers contacted (output)

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Outcomes measure change in stakeholders' lives (income, health, empowerment), while outputs measure activities delivered (trainings, contacts).*

---

**Question 5:** What is the difference between outputs and outcomes?**

A) No difference
B) Outputs are immediate deliverables; outcomes are changes resulting from those deliverables
C) Outcomes are less important
D) Outputs can't be measured

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Outputs = what you deliver (meals served, training sessions). Outcomes = resulting changes (improved nutrition, increased income).*

---

**Question 6:** How frequently should impact data be collected for effective management?**

A) Once at program end
B) Regularly throughout program cycle (monthly/quarterly) to enable course correction
C) Never
D) Only when requested by donors

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Regular data collection enables adaptive management—identifying what's working, making adjustments, and maximizing impact through continuous improvement.*

---

**Question 7:** What is attribution vs. contribution in impact measurement?**

A) The same thing
B) Attribution claims sole causation; contribution acknowledges multiple factors influence outcomes
C) Attribution is easier to prove
D) Contribution doesn't matter

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Attribution (proving you alone caused change) is usually impossible. Contribution (demonstrating you made a meaningful contribution alongside other factors) is more realistic.*

---

**Question 8:** For a food enterprise claiming environmental impact, which metric is most rigorous?**

A) General sustainability claims
B) Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) quantifying emissions, water use, and resource consumption
C) Marketing slogans
D) Customer surveys about perceptions

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: LCA provides science-based quantification of environmental impacts across the value chain, enabling credible claims and comparison to alternatives.*

---

**Question 9:** What is the purpose of a counterfactual in impact evaluation?**

A) To argue against the program
B) To estimate what would have happened without the intervention (baseline for comparison)
C) To eliminate data collection
D) To reduce costs

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Counterfactuals estimate the "but for" scenario—what would have occurred without your program—enabling calculation of net impact.*

---

**Question 10:** What challenge is common in monetizing social value for SROI calculations?**

A) Social benefits are easily priced
B) Difficulty assigning credible financial proxies to intangible benefits (e.g., dignity, empowerment)
C) Too much available data
D) Overly simple calculations

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: SROI requires monetizing benefits like improved health, empowerment, or ecosystem services—challenging because market prices don't exist for these values.*
