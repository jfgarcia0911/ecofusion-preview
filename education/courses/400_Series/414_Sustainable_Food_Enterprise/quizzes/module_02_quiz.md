**Question 1:** In the Business Model Canvas, which component describes HOW the enterprise creates, delivers, and captures value?

A) Customer Segments
B) The entire canvas working together
C) Revenue Streams only
D) Key Activities

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: The entire Business Model Canvas describes value creation (Key Resources, Activities, Partnerships), delivery (Channels, Customer Relationships), and capture (Revenue Streams, Cost Structure).*

---

**Question 2:** What distinguishes a "hybrid value proposition" in social enterprises?

A) Mixing different products
B) Combining social/environmental impact with customer benefits (quality, convenience, price)
C) Serving both B2B and B2C markets
D) Operating in multiple countries

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Hybrid value propositions deliver both mission impact (e.g., fair wages, organic farming) AND customer value (fresh, quality products), aligning purpose with market demand.*

---

**Question 3:** Which revenue model is most appropriate for a community-supported agriculture (CSA) program?

A) Transaction-based sales
B) Subscription model with advance payment for seasonal shares
C) Advertising revenue
D) Licensing fees

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: CSAs use subscription models where customers pay upfront for seasonal shares, providing farmers with working capital and demand certainty.*

---

**Question 4:** What is "cross-subsidization" in social enterprise business models?

A) Government subsidies
B) Using profits from one customer segment or product to subsidize below-cost services to another segment
C) Accepting donations only
D) Tax benefits

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Cross-subsidization allows enterprises to serve low-income populations by using margins from paying customers to offset below-cost offerings.*

---

**Question 5:** For a vertical farm social enterprise targeting food deserts, which customer segment strategy is most sustainable?

A) Only serve low-income customers at break-even prices
B) Multi-segment approach serving restaurants/retailers (higher margin) and community (mission)
C) Ignore financial sustainability
D) Government contracts only

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Multi-segment strategies balance mission (serving food deserts) with financial viability (profitable commercial sales), ensuring long-term sustainability.*

---

**Question 6:** What is the "triple bottom line" framework in social enterprise?

A) Three different product lines
B) Measuring success by People (social), Planet (environmental), and Profit (economic) outcomes
C) Having three founders
D) Operating in three markets

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Triple bottom line expands success metrics beyond profit to include social equity and environmental sustainability—core to mission-driven enterprises.*

---

**Question 7:** Which key partnership is most critical for food enterprises targeting institutional buyers (schools, hospitals)?

A) Individual consumers
B) Distributors with institutional relationships and compliance expertise
C) Competitors
D) Social media influencers

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Institutional sales require specialized distribution, regulatory compliance (FSMA, food safety), and relationship management—making experienced distributors critical partners.*

---

**Question 8:** What is "embedded impact" in business model design?

A) Impact that happens accidentally
B) Social/environmental value creation that is integral to core operations, not an add-on
C) Impact reports
D) Corporate philanthropy

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Embedded impact means mission is inseparable from business operations (e.g., employing disadvantaged workers IS the service delivery model), ensuring alignment.*

---

**Question 9:** For scaling a mission-driven food enterprise, which approach best preserves impact?

A) Rapid expansion without quality control
B) Franchising or partnership models with strong impact standards and monitoring
C) Selling to a conventional corporation
D) Geographic expansion without local partnerships

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Franchising or partnerships with rigorous impact criteria, training, and monitoring allow scale while maintaining mission integrity through replication of proven models.*

---

**Question 10:** What is the primary risk of donor/grant dependency in social enterprise revenue models?

A) Too much money
B) Lack of market discipline, sustainability concerns when funding ends, and mission drift to donor priorities
C) Increased profitability
D) Customer confusion

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Grant dependency creates instability, may misalign activities with mission (pursuing donor priorities), and lacks market validation of value proposition.*
