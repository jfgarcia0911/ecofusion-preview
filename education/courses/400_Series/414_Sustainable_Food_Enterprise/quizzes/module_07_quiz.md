**Question 1:** What is stakeholder capitalism?**

A) Only shareholders matter
B) A system where companies optimize long-term value for all stakeholders (employees, suppliers, communities, environment, shareholders)
C) Government ownership of companies
D) Eliminating shareholders

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Stakeholder capitalism (endorsed by Business Roundtable 2019) prioritizes long-term value creation for all stakeholders, not just short-term shareholder returns.*

---

**Question 2:** What is a Community Benefit Agreement (CBA)?**

A) Free product giveaways
B) Negotiated agreement where company commits to specific community benefits (jobs, training, environmental standards) in exchange for community support
C) Government regulation
D) Marketing campaign

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: CBAs are legally binding agreements negotiated between developers and community coalitions, ensuring concrete benefits like local hiring, living wages, and environmental protections.*

---

**Question 3:** What stakeholder engagement approach is most effective for contentious projects?**

A) Ignoring opposition
B) Early, transparent engagement with diverse voices, addressing concerns through design modifications and benefit-sharing
C) Engaging only supporters
D) Minimal communication

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Effective engagement starts early, includes critics, listens genuinely, demonstrates responsiveness through real changes, and builds trust through transparency.*

---

**Question 4:** What is social capital and why does it matter for food enterprises?**

A) Money invested in stocks
B) Networks of relationships, trust, and reciprocity that enable collective action and community resilience
C) Social media followers
D) Government funding

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Social capital—trusted relationships and networks—enables partnerships, local support, knowledge sharing, and resilience, particularly valuable for community-based food enterprises.*

---

**Question 5:** What is a food policy council and how can enterprises engage?**

A) Government regulatory agency
B) Multi-stakeholder body advising on local food system policy, offering enterprises opportunities to shape supportive policies
C) Marketing board
D) Trade association

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Food policy councils convene diverse stakeholders to recommend policies supporting local food systems—enterprises can contribute expertise and advocate for enabling policies.*

---

**Question 6:** What is the purpose of stakeholder mapping in community engagement?**

A) Creating organizational charts
B) Identifying all affected parties, their interests, influence, and relationships to inform engagement strategies
C) Geographic mapping only
D) Eliminating stakeholders

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Stakeholder mapping reveals who is affected, their concerns, power dynamics, and relationships—enabling tailored engagement and identifying potential allies or opponents.*

---

**Question 7:** What is participatory governance in social enterprises?**

A) Traditional top-down management
B) Including stakeholders (workers, producers, community) in meaningful decision-making through boards, councils, or voting structures
C) Excluding employees from decisions
D) CEO-only decisions

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Participatory governance democratizes power through worker representation on boards, producer cooperatives, community advisory councils, or multi-stakeholder structures.*

---

**Question 8:** For a vertical farm in an urban neighborhood, what community benefit is typically most valued?**

A) Architectural awards
B) Local employment opportunities, job training, and affordable produce access
C) Parking lot size
D) Company profits

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Communities prioritize tangible benefits: jobs for residents, skills training and career pathways, and affordable access to fresh, healthy food.*

---

**Question 9:** What is the difference between stakeholder engagement and stakeholder management?**

A) No difference
B) Engagement implies two-way dialogue and shared influence; management suggests one-way control
C) Management is superior
D) Engagement is obsolete

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: "Management" implies controlling stakeholders; "engagement" recognizes stakeholders as partners with legitimate voice in decisions affecting them.*

---

**Question 10:** What metric can measure community engagement effectiveness?**

A) Number of social media posts
B) Stakeholder satisfaction surveys, community benefit delivery, and quality of ongoing relationships
C) Marketing budget
D) Advertising impressions

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Effectiveness is measured by stakeholder trust and satisfaction, delivery of promised benefits, collaborative problem-solving, and sustained positive relationships.*
