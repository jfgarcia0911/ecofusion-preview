**Question 1:** What does ESG stand for in corporate reporting?**

A) Economic, Sales, Growth
B) Environmental, Social, and Governance
C) Equity, Sustainability, Goals
D) Earnings, Shares, Grants

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: ESG encompasses Environmental (climate, resources), Social (labor, community), and Governance (ethics, board structure) factors material to business performance.*

---

**Question 2:** What is the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) framework?**

A) Financial accounting standard
B) Comprehensive sustainability reporting standard covering economic, environmental, and social impacts
C) Government regulation
D) Marketing certification

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: GRI provides the world's most widely used sustainability reporting standards, enabling transparent disclosure of impacts across all ESG dimensions.*

---

**Question 3:** What is materiality assessment in ESG reporting?**

A) Measuring physical materials
B) Identifying ESG issues that significantly affect business performance and stakeholder decisions
C) Material costs only
D) Building materials selection

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Materiality assessment identifies which ESG topics are most significant to the business and its stakeholders, focusing reporting on what matters most.*

---

**Question 4:** What is the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB) approach?**

A) General sustainability guidance
B) Industry-specific standards identifying financially material sustainability issues for investors
C) Environmental regulations
D) Marketing standards

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: SASB provides industry-specific standards focusing on ESG issues likely to affect enterprise value, designed for investor decision-making.*

---

**Question 5:** What are Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas emissions?**

A) Different gases
B) Scope 1: Direct emissions; Scope 2: Purchased energy; Scope 3: Value chain emissions
C) Temperature scales
D) Regulatory categories

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: GHG Protocol defines Scope 1 (direct operations), Scope 2 (purchased electricity/heat), and Scope 3 (supply chain and product use)—comprehensive carbon accounting.*

---

**Question 6:** For a food enterprise, what is typically the largest source of Scope 3 emissions?**

A) Office electricity
B) Agricultural production and supply chain (upstream emissions)
C) Employee commuting
D) Marketing materials

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Food enterprises' Scope 3 emissions are dominated by agricultural production (fertilizers, land use, livestock) and supply chain—often 80-95% of total footprint.*

---

**Question 7:** What is ESG integration in investment decisions?**

A) Ignoring ESG factors
B) Systematically considering ESG risks and opportunities in valuation and portfolio construction
C) Only financial analysis
D) Charity donations

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: ESG integration incorporates material environmental, social, and governance factors into investment analysis, recognizing they affect risk-adjusted returns.*

---

**Question 8:** What is greenwashing in ESG reporting?**

A) Cleaning buildings
B) Misleading claims about environmental performance or sustainability efforts
C) Accurate reporting
D) Painting buildings green

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Greenwashing occurs when companies exaggerate, make vague claims about, or selectively report environmental benefits while hiding negative impacts.*

---

**Question 9:** What verification method enhances ESG report credibility?**

A) Self-reporting only
B) Third-party assurance by independent auditors
C) No verification
D) Internal-only review

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Independent third-party assurance (limited or reasonable) provides external validation of reported data and claims, building stakeholder trust.*

---

**Question 10:** What stakeholders are primary users of ESG reports?**

A) Only government regulators
B) Investors, lenders, employees, customers, communities, and advocacy groups
C) Competitors only
D) No one reads them

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: ESG reports serve diverse stakeholders: investors assessing risk, employees evaluating employers, customers making purchase decisions, and communities tracking commitments.*
