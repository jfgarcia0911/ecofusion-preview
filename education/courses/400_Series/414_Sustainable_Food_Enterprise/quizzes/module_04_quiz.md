**Question 1:** What is the primary purpose of B Corp Certification?

A) Tax exemption status
B) Third-party verification that a company meets high standards of social/environmental performance, accountability, and transparency
C) Government licensing requirement
D) Marketing gimmick with no standards

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: B Corp Certification (by B Lab) independently verifies companies meet rigorous standards across governance, workers, community, environment, and customers.*

---

**Question 2:** What minimum B Impact Assessment score is required for B Corp Certification?**

A) 50 points
B) 80 points
C) 100 points
D) 150 points

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Companies must score at least 80 out of 200 points on the B Impact Assessment, demonstrating meaningful social/environmental performance.*

---

**Question 3:** What is the key difference between B Corp Certification and Benefit Corporation legal status?**

A) No difference
B) B Corp is a certification (verified performance); Benefit Corp is a legal structure (governance framework)
C) B Corp is only for nonprofits
D) Benefit Corps can't get certified

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: B Corp Certification verifies performance standards; Benefit Corporation is a legal entity type requiring consideration of all stakeholders in decision-making.*

---

**Question 4:** What are the five impact areas assessed in the B Impact Assessment?**

A) Governance, Workers, Community, Environment, Customers
B) Profit, People, Planet, Purpose, Partnerships
C) Strategy, Operations, Finance, Marketing, Sales
D) Quality, Safety, Efficiency, Innovation, Growth

**Answer: A**

*Explanation: The B Impact Assessment evaluates Governance, Workers, Community, Environment, and Customers (with product-specific supplements).*

---

**Question 5:** What legal protection does Benefit Corporation status provide to directors?**

A) No liability for any decisions
B) Legal standing to consider stakeholder interests (not just shareholder profit) in decision-making
C) Tax exemptions
D) Immunity from lawsuits

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Benefit Corporation statutes require directors to consider impact on all stakeholders, providing legal protection when prioritizing mission over short-term profit.*

---

**Question 6:** How often must B Corps recertify?**

A) Never—certification is permanent
B) Every 3 years with annual disclosure
C) Every 10 years
D) Only when requested

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: B Corps must recertify every 3 years to maintain certification, ensuring ongoing commitment and continuous improvement.*

---

**Question 7:** What is required in Benefit Corporation annual reporting?**

A) Nothing additional
B) Public annual benefit report assessing performance against a third-party standard
C) Only financial statements
D) Marketing materials

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Benefit Corps must publish annual reports measuring performance against independent standards (e.g., B Impact Assessment, GRI) and describe stakeholder engagement.*

---

**Question 8:** Which stakeholder consideration is unique to Benefit Corporations compared to traditional corporations?**

A) Shareholders only
B) Legal requirement to balance shareholder interests with workers, community, environment, and long-term value
C) No stakeholder consideration required
D) Only customers matter

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Traditional corporations prioritize shareholder value; Benefit Corps must legally balance all stakeholder interests in material decisions.*

---

**Question 9:** What happens if a Benefit Corporation fails to pursue its stated benefit purpose?**

A) Nothing—it's voluntary
B) Potential enforcement action by shareholders or directors through benefit enforcement proceedings
C) Automatic criminal penalties
D) Immediate dissolution

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Benefit Corps can be held accountable through special "benefit enforcement proceedings" if they materially fail to pursue their stated purpose.*

---

**Question 10:** For a food enterprise, which B Corp impact area typically receives the highest weight?**

A) Governance only
B) Environment and Workers (given agriculture's environmental footprint and labor intensity)
C) Neither matters
D) Only financial performance

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Food/agriculture companies face heightened scrutiny in Environment (sustainability practices) and Workers (fair labor, safety) due to sector impacts.*
