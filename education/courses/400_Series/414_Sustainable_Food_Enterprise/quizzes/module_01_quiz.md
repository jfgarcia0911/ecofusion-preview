# Module 1 Quiz: Foundations of Social Entrepreneurship in Food Systems

## Instructions

**Question 1:** Which of the following BEST defines social entrepreneurship?

A) A nonprofit organization that provides charitable services to communities
B) A for-profit business that donates a percentage of profits to charity
C) An approach that uses business models to address social and environmental problems while generating revenue
D) A government program that supports community development

**Answer: C**

---

**Question 2:** On the social enterprise spectrum (1-7 scale), where would a B Corp certified organic food distributor with strong sustainability commitments typically fall?

A) Position 2 (Philanthropic Support)
B) Position 4 (Balanced Mission & Market)
C) Position 6 (Corporate Social Responsibility)
D) Position 7 (Pure Business)

**Answer: B**

---

**Question 3:** Which food system challenge presents the LARGEST contributor to global greenhouse gas emissions?

A) Food transportation and logistics
B) Agricultural production (crops and livestock)
C) Food processing and manufacturing
D) Retail refrigeration and food waste

**Answer: B** *(Agriculture accounts for 26% of global GHG emissions)*

---

**Question 4:** What is the primary advantage of a B Corporation structure for a mission-driven food enterprise?

A) Lower taxes and simplified regulatory compliance
B) Third-party verification of social/environmental performance and legal protection for mission
C) Guaranteed access to government grants and contracts
D) Exemption from labor laws and environmental regulations

**Answer: B**

---

**Question 5:** In the Patagonia Provisions case study, which of the following was NOT a key element of their business model?

A) Premium pricing that reflects true environmental costs
B) Direct relationships with regenerative farmers and fishermen
C) Franchise model for rapid market expansion
D) Storytelling that connects consumers to production practices

**Answer: C** *(Patagonia Provisions does not use franchising)*

---

**Question 6:** Which type of cooperative structure would Organic Valley (farmer-owned dairy cooperative) be classified as?

A) Consumer cooperative
B) Producer cooperative
C) Worker cooperative
D) Multi-stakeholder cooperative

**Answer: B**

---

**Question 7:** According to the module, what is a common pitfall that social enterprises should avoid?

A) Measuring impact metrics regularly
B) Building relationships with diverse stakeholders
C) Mission drift through gradual prioritization of financial optimization over social goals
D) Seeking both grants and earned revenue

**Answer: C**

---

**Question 8:** Which of the following is a "market failure" that creates opportunity for social enterprise solutions in food systems?

A) Externalities where environmental costs are not reflected in prices
B) Healthy competition among multiple food providers
C) Transparent labeling and consumer information
D) Fair wages for all food system workers

**Answer: A**

---

**Question 9:** The 1% for the Planet commitment, where businesses donate 1% of revenue to environmental causes, originated with which company?

A) Whole Foods Market
B) Patagonia
C) Ben & Jerry's
D) Equal Exchange

**Answer: B**

---

**Question 10:** According to the critical success factor framework, which of the following is MOST essential for mission-driven food enterprises?

A) Having the lowest prices in the market
B) Maximum growth and scale within the first year
C) Clarity of purpose with specific, measurable impact goals
D) Avoiding all financial returns to shareholders

**Answer: C**

---

## Answer Key

1. C - Social entrepreneurship definition
2. B - B Corp position on spectrum
3. B - Agriculture GHG emissions
4. B - B Corp advantages
5. C - Patagonia Provisions model
6. B - Producer cooperative
7. C - Mission drift pitfall
8. A - Market failure (externalities)
9. B - Patagonia founded 1% for Planet
10. C - Clarity of purpose success factor
