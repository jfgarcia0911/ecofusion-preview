**Question 1:** What is the core principle of Fair Trade certification?**

A) Maximum profit for retailers
B) Ensuring producers receive fair prices, decent working conditions, and community development support
C) Lowest possible prices for consumers
D) Fastest delivery

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Fair Trade guarantees minimum prices, fair labor standards, environmental sustainability, and community development premiums to empower marginalized producers.*

---

**Question 2:** What is a Fair Trade premium and how is it used?**

A) Extra profit for middlemen
B) Additional money paid on top of Fair Trade minimum price, invested in community projects democratically chosen by producers
C) Marketing fee
D) Government tax

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Fair Trade premiums provide additional funds beyond minimum price that producer cooperatives democratically allocate to schools, healthcare, infrastructure, or business development.*

---

**Question 3:** What distinguishes Direct Trade from Fair Trade?**

A) No difference
B) Direct Trade involves direct buyer-producer relationships with negotiated pricing, often emphasizing quality and transparency without third-party certification
C) Direct Trade is always cheaper
D) Fair Trade prohibits direct relationships

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Direct Trade builds unmediated relationships between buyers and producers, often paying premiums for quality while providing transparency—without standardized certification.*

---

**Question 4:** What is a living wage and how does it differ from minimum wage?**

A) The same thing
B) Living wage covers basic needs (food, housing, healthcare, education) for a family, typically higher than legal minimum wage
C) Living wage is always lower
D) No practical difference

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Living wages meet actual costs of dignified living for workers and families, while minimum wages often fall short of basic needs.*

---

**Question 5:** For a coffee roaster committed to ethical sourcing, what practice demonstrates strongest producer support?**

A) Purchasing at commodity market price
B) Long-term purchase commitments at fair prices with pre-harvest financing and farmer development programs
C) Spot purchases only at lowest price
D) Eliminating direct farmer contact

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Meaningful support includes stable multi-year contracts, prices covering production costs plus living wage, pre-harvest financing, and capacity building.*

---

**Question 6:** What is the "transparency paradox" in ethical sourcing?**

A) Too much transparency is always harmful
B) Companies claiming transparency often disclose selected positive information while hiding problematic aspects
C) Transparency is impossible
D) Consumers don't care about transparency

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: True transparency requires disclosing challenges and failures alongside successes—many "transparent" companies selectively share only positive information.*

---

**Question 7:** What certification verifies ethical labor practices in food production?**

A) ISO 9000
B) Fair Trade, Rainforest Alliance, or Fair Food Program
C) USDA Organic only
D) No certifications exist

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Fair Trade, Rainforest Alliance, and Fair Food Program certify labor standards including wages, working conditions, freedom of association, and anti-discrimination.*

---

**Question 8:** What is supply chain due diligence for human rights?**

A) Ignoring labor issues
B) Systematic process to identify, prevent, mitigate, and account for human rights impacts in supply chains
C) One-time supplier audit
D) Accepting all supplier self-reports

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Due diligence (per UN Guiding Principles) requires ongoing assessment, remediation of violations, stakeholder engagement, and transparent reporting.*

---

**Question 9:** What practice demonstrates genuine partnership with smallholder farmers?**

A) Purchasing only when prices are lowest
B) Co-investment in farm improvements, knowledge sharing, multi-year contracts, and fair pricing
C) Switching suppliers frequently
D) Dictating all farming practices without input

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: True partnerships involve shared risk/benefit, capability development, long-term commitments, and collaborative decision-making respecting farmer expertise.*

---

**Question 10:** What is the primary limitation of ethical certifications?**

A) They verify nothing
B) Certification assesses compliance at audit time but may miss ongoing issues, and costs can exclude smallest producers
C) They're too cheap
D) All certified products are identical

**Answer: B**

*Explanation: Certifications provide valuable verification but have limitations: snapshot assessment, audit costs excluding very small producers, and potential for gaming the system.*
