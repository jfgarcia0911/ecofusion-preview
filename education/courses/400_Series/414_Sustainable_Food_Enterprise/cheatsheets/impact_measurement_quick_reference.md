## Theory of Change Essentials

```
INPUTS → ACTIVITIES → OUTPUTS → OUTCOMES → IMPACT

INPUTS: Resources you invest
ACTIVITIES: What you do
OUTPUTS: What you produce
OUTCOMES: Changes that result (short-medium term)
IMPACT: Long-term ultimate change
```

**Quick Questions:**
1. What is your ultimate impact goal? (10+ years)
2. What outcomes lead to that impact? (3-5 years)
3. What outputs create those outcomes? (1-2 years)
4. What activities produce outputs?
5. What inputs are required?
6. What assumptions must be true?

## Common Food Enterprise Metrics

### Environmental
- **GHG Emissions:** Scope 1, 2, 3 (tCO2e)
- **Carbon Intensity:** tCO2e per $ revenue or per unit
- **Water:** Withdrawal, consumption, intensity (L/unit)
- **Waste:** Generated (MT), diverted (%), landfill (%)
- **Renewable Energy:** % of total energy
- **Sustainable Sourcing:** % certified (organic, regenerative, etc.)

### Social
- **Farmer/Supplier:** Income change, # trained, fair price premium
- **Employees:** Turnover %, living wage coverage %, safety (LTIR)
- **Customers:** # served, satisfaction (NPS), access improvement
- **Community:** Jobs created, local spending $, engagement events

### Governance
- **Board:** Independence %, diversity %
- **Ethics:** Training %, violations (#)
- **Transparency:** Public reporting (Y/N), third-party verification

## SROI Quick Calculation

```
1. IDENTIFY OUTCOMES
   Example: Increased farmer income

2. QUANTIFY OUTCOMES
   Example: 30 farmers × $10,000 average increase = $300,000

3. ASSIGN FINANCIAL PROXY
   Example: Value = income increase = $300,000

4. ADJUST FOR:
   - Deadweight (would have happened anyway): -20% = -$60,000
   - Attribution (your contribution): 60% = $144,000
   - Drop-off (duration of benefit): Calculate NPV over time

5. CALCULATE RATIO
   Total impact value (all outcomes): $500,000
   Investment: $100,000
   SROI = $500,000 / $100,000 = 5:1
```

## Data Collection Best Practices

### Design Principles
1. **Integrate into operations** - Don't create separate processes
2. **Automate where possible** - Use technology to reduce manual effort
3. **Start simple** - Essential metrics first, add complexity later
4. **Ensure quality** - Train staff, validate data, establish protocols
5. **Respect privacy** - Protect beneficiary and stakeholder information
6. **Close the loop** - Share data back with those who provide it

### Collection Methods by Type
- **Output Metrics:** Admin data (sales, program records) - Continuous
- **Outcome Metrics:** Surveys, interviews, focus groups - Annual/semi-annual
- **Environmental:** Sensors, lab testing, third-party verification - Periodic
- **Financial:** Accounting systems - Monthly/Quarterly

### Data Quality Checks
- ✓ Accuracy - Represents reality correctly
- ✓ Completeness - All required data collected
- ✓ Consistency - Standardized definitions and collection
- ✓ Timeliness - Available when needed
- ✓ Validity - Measures what it should measure

## Impact Reporting Structure

### Annual Impact Report Outline

**1. Executive Summary** (1-2 pages)
- Mission and theory of change
- Key highlights and milestones
- Financial and impact performance summary

**2. Our Approach** (2-3 pages)
- Problem we're addressing
- Our solution and strategy
- Stakeholder engagement

**3. Impact Performance** (5-8 pages)
- Outcomes by stakeholder group
- Metrics with targets and actuals
- Charts, graphs, and visualizations
- Stories and testimonials

**4. Financial Performance** (2-3 pages)
- Revenue and expenses
- Sustainability progress
- Use of funds
- Looking ahead

**5. Lessons Learned** (1-2 pages)
- What worked well
- Challenges faced
- Adaptations made
- Continuous improvement

**6. Future Vision** (1-2 pages)
- Goals for coming year
- Strategic priorities
- How stakeholders can engage

**7. Appendix**
- Detailed metrics tables
- Methodology notes
- Financial statements
- Governance information

## Quick Impact Assessment Questions

**Before Launch:**
1. Who benefits? Who might be harmed?
2. What is the causal mechanism (how does it work)?
3. What evidence supports this approach?
4. How will we know if it's working?
5. What could go wrong?

**During Operations:**
1. Are we reaching intended beneficiaries?
2. Are outcomes materializing as expected?
3. What unintended consequences (positive or negative)?
4. Where can we improve?
5. Are benefits worth the costs?

**For Investors/Funders:**
1. What is the impact thesis?
2. How is impact measured?
3. What evidence of outcomes exists?
4. How does impact per dollar compare to alternatives?
5. What is the plan for increasing impact?

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Measuring only what's easy** - May miss most important outcomes
✓ Focus on material impacts even if harder to measure

❌ **Vanity metrics** - Outputs that sound good but don't show real change
✓ Track outcomes and behavior changes, not just activities

❌ **Cherry-picking data** - Sharing only positive results
✓ Transparent reporting of both successes and challenges

❌ **Attribution confusion** - Claiming 100% credit for complex change
✓ Use contribution analysis, acknowledge context and partners

❌ **One-time measurement** - Snapshot instead of tracking over time
✓ Establish baseline, track regularly, show trends

❌ **Ignoring costs** - Reporting impact without efficiency context
✓ Calculate impact per dollar, compare to alternatives

❌ **Stakeholder exclusion** - Top-down metrics without beneficiary input
✓ Co-design metrics with those experiencing the impact
