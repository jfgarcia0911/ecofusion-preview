# Module 1: CEA Facility Design Fundamentals

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Identify and compare different CEA facility types and configurations
- Apply systematic design methodology to CEA projects
- Conduct stakeholder requirements analysis
- Perform comprehensive site selection and analysis
- Develop conceptual designs that meet project goals

## 1.1 Overview of CEA Facility Types

### Primary Facility Categories

```
CEA FACILITY TAXONOMY
====================

1. GREENHOUSES
   ├── Glass Greenhouses (Venlo, Gothic, etc.)
   ├── Polycarbonate Structures
   ├── Polyfilm Houses (High tunnel, hoop house)
   └── Hybrid Systems (Combined glazing types)

2. VERTICAL FARMS
   ├── Container-based Systems
   ├── Warehouse Conversions
   ├── Purpose-built Vertical Facilities
   └── Modular Vertical Systems

3. PLANT FACTORIES
   ├── Fully Enclosed Systems
   ├── Multi-level Indoor Facilities
   └── Research/Production Hybrids

4. HYBRID FACILITIES
   ├── Greenhouse + Indoor Integration
   ├── Multi-technology Centers
   └── Research + Production Facilities
```

| Container Vertical Farm | Low-Medium | High | None (0%) | Low-Medium | Very High |

## 1.2 Design Process and Methodology

### Six-Phase Design Process

```
PHASE 1: PROGRAMMING & FEASIBILITY
│
├─ Stakeholder Requirements
├─ Market Analysis
├─ Site Analysis
├─ Financial Feasibility
└─ Technology Selection
│
▼
PHASE 2: CONCEPTUAL DESIGN (SCHEMATIC DESIGN)
│
├─ Space Planning
├─ Building Form & Configuration
├─ System Selection
├─ Preliminary Cost Estimate
└─ Design Review & Approval
│
▼
PHASE 3: DESIGN DEVELOPMENT
│
├─ Architectural Development
├─ Structural Engineering
├─ MEP (Mechanical/Electrical/Plumbing) Design
├─ Refined Cost Estimate
└─ Regulatory Review
│
▼
PHASE 4: CONSTRUCTION DOCUMENTATION
│
├─ Detailed Drawings (Architectural, Structural, MEP)
├─ Specifications
├─ Construction Details
├─ Permitting Documents
└─ Bidding Documents
│
▼
PHASE 5: BIDDING & PROCUREMENT
│
├─ Contractor Prequalification
├─ Bid Package Distribution
├─ Bid Review & Analysis
├─ Contract Negotiation
└─ Award & Contract Execution
│
▼
PHASE 6: CONSTRUCTION ADMINISTRATION
│
├─ Construction Oversight
├─ RFI (Request for Information) Management
├─ Change Order Processing
├─ Quality Control
└─ Substantial Completion & Closeout
```

### Design Timeline Estimates

**Small Facility (5,000-20,000 sq ft)**
- Programming: 2-4 weeks
- Conceptual Design: 4-6 weeks
- Design Development: 6-8 weeks
- Construction Documents: 8-12 weeks
- Total Design Duration: 5-7 months

**Medium Facility (20,000-100,000 sq ft)**
- Programming: 4-6 weeks
- Conceptual Design: 6-10 weeks
- Design Development: 10-16 weeks
- Construction Documents: 12-20 weeks
- Total Design Duration: 8-13 months

**Large Facility (100,000+ sq ft)**
- Programming: 6-12 weeks
- Conceptual Design: 10-16 weeks
- Design Development: 16-24 weeks
- Construction Documents: 20-32 weeks
- Total Design Duration: 13-21 months

### Requirements Gathering Framework

**PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS**
- Target crop types and varieties
- Annual production volume (lbs, kg, units)
- Production cycles and timing
- Quality standards and certifications
- Post-harvest handling requirements

**OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS**
- Labor force size and shift structure
- Automation level and technology integration
- Maintenance access and service requirements
- Storage and inventory management
- Shipping and receiving operations

**TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS**
- Climate control specifications
- Lighting requirements (intensity, spectrum, photoperiod)
- Water quality and consumption
- Power requirements and reliability
- Data collection and monitoring systems

**FINANCIAL REQUIREMENTS**
- Total project budget (capital expenditure)
- Operating cost targets
- Revenue projections and payback period
- Financing structure and constraints
- Contingency requirements

## 1.4 Site Selection and Analysis

### Site Selection Criteria

```
SITE EVALUATION SCORECARD
========================

LOCATION FACTORS (Weight: 30%)
├── Market proximity (20 pts)
├── Labor availability (15 pts)
├── Transportation access (10 pts)
├── Distribution network (10 pts)
└── Local agriculture ecosystem (5 pts)

PHYSICAL FACTORS (Weight: 25%)
├── Site size and dimensions (15 pts)
├── Topography and grading (10 pts)
├── Soil conditions (foundation) (10 pts)
├── Drainage (10 pts)
└── Solar access (5 pts)

UTILITY FACTORS (Weight: 25%)
├── Electrical service capacity (15 pts)
├── Water availability and quality (15 pts)
├── Natural gas availability (5 pts)
├── Sewer capacity (5 pts)
└── Telecommunications (5 pts)

REGULATORY FACTORS (Weight: 15%)
├── Zoning compliance (10 pts)
├── Building code jurisdiction (5 pts)
├── Environmental constraints (10 pts)
└── Permitting complexity (5 pts)

ECONOMIC FACTORS (Weight: 5%)
├── Land/lease cost (5 pts)
├── Utility rates (5 pts)
├── Tax incentives (5 pts)
└── Development costs (5 pts)

SCORING: 85-100 = Excellent | 70-84 = Good | 55-69 = Fair | <55 = Poor
```

### Site Analysis Components

**1. GEOTECHNICAL ANALYSIS**
```
Soil Investigation Requirements:
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Test Borings                        │
│ - Minimum 3-4 borings per site      │
│ - Depth: 20-30 ft or to bedrock     │
│ - Analyze soil bearing capacity     │
│ - Identify groundwater level        │
│                                     │
│ Laboratory Testing                  │
│ - Gradation analysis                │
│ - Atterberg limits                  │
│ - Moisture content                  │
│ - Consolidation tests               │
│                                     │
│ Deliverable: Geotechnical Report    │
│ - Soil profile                      │
│ - Bearing capacity recommendations  │
│ - Foundation recommendations        │
│ - Construction considerations       │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**2. TOPOGRAPHIC SURVEY**
```
Required Survey Elements:
- Boundary and property lines
- Existing structures and improvements
- Utilities (overhead and underground)
- Contours (1-2 ft intervals)
- Trees and significant vegetation
- Easements and right-of-ways
- Benchmark elevations
- North arrow and scale
```

**3. UTILITY ASSESSMENT**

| Utility | Assessment Requirements | Key Information Needed |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Electrical** | Service capacity, voltage availability, distance to transformer | Available capacity (kW), primary voltage, upgrade costs |
| **Water** | Supply capacity, pressure, quality testing | Flow rate (GPM), pressure (PSI), water quality report |
| **Sewer** | Capacity, connection requirements | Available capacity, connection fees, treatment requirements |
| **Natural Gas** | Service availability, capacity | Pressure (PSI), capacity (BTU/hr), connection costs |
| **Telecom** | Internet service providers, bandwidth | Available bandwidth, redundancy options, costs |

**4. CLIMATE DATA ANALYSIS**
```
Historical Climate Data Requirements:
────────────────────────────────────
Temperature Data (30-year minimum):
├── Daily max/min temperatures
├── Extreme temperature events
├── Degree days (heating/cooling)
└── Frost dates

Solar Data:
├── Solar insolation (kWh/m²/day)
├── Peak sun hours by month
├── Cloud cover statistics
└── Daylight hours by season

Precipitation:
├── Annual rainfall totals
├── Monthly distribution
├── Snow loads (if applicable)
└── Design storm events

Wind:
├── Prevailing wind direction
├── Average wind speeds
├── Extreme wind events
└── Wind rose data

Data Sources:
- NOAA National Weather Service
- State climatology offices
- Local weather stations
- ASHRAE climate data
```

## 1.6 Design Standards and Best Practices

### Industry Design Standards

**Key Reference Standards:**
- **NGMA** (National Greenhouse Manufacturers Association) - Structural design standards
- **ASABE** (American Society of Agricultural and Biological Engineers) - Agricultural structures
- **ASHRAE** (American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers) - HVAC design
- **IBC** (International Building Code) - Building requirements
- **ASCE 7** - Minimum design loads for buildings

### Design Principles for CEA Facilities

**1. FLEXIBILITY AND ADAPTABILITY**
- Design for multiple crop types and growing systems
- Provide modular infrastructure for easy reconfiguration
- Plan for future expansion
- Use standardized bay dimensions

**2. OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY**
- Minimize travel distances for workers
- Optimize material flow (inputs → production → harvest → shipping)
- Design for equipment access and maintenance
- Plan for efficient harvest and post-harvest operations

**3. ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL**
- Provide uniform climate distribution
- Minimize thermal losses and gains
- Design for proper air circulation
- Plan for optimal humidity management

**4. ENERGY EFFICIENCY**
- Maximize use of natural light (greenhouses)
- Minimize envelope thermal losses
- Design for heat recovery opportunities
- Plan for renewable energy integration

**5. BIOSECURITY AND FOOD SAFETY**
- Design for contamination prevention
- Separate clean/dirty zones
- Provide proper sanitation facilities
- Control access points

- The range of CEA facility types and their characteristics

## Key Takeaways

1. **CEA facilities range from low-tech greenhouses to high-tech vertical farms**, each with distinct capital costs, energy requirements, and operational characteristics.

2. **Systematic design process is essential** - following the six phases from programming through construction administration ensures comprehensive project delivery.

3. **Stakeholder requirements drive design decisions** - understanding production, operational, technical, and financial requirements is critical to project success.

4. **Site selection significantly impacts project viability** - location, physical characteristics, utilities, and regulatory factors must be thoroughly evaluated.

5. **Conceptual design balances multiple objectives** - space efficiency, operational workflows, climate control, and cost must be optimized simultaneously.
