## 5. Processing Technologies

### From Live Insect to Food Ingredient

```
INSECT PROCESSING FLOWSHEET

Step 1: HARVEST
├─ Automated collection (BSF: self-harvesting)
├─ Manual or mechanical (crickets, mealworms)
└─ Optimal timing (peak protein content)
         │
         ▼
Step 2: CLEANING
├─ Wash to remove substrate
├─ Separate dead/defective insects
└─ Initial quality check
         │
         ▼
Step 3: KILLING (Humane + Safety)
├─ Freezing (-18°C, 24-48 hours)
├─ Blanching (boiling water, 1-2 min)
├─ Or combination
└─ Inactivates enzymes, ensures death
         │
         ▼
Step 4: DRYING
├─ Oven drying (60-80°C, 24 hours)
├─ Freeze drying (premium, retains nutrients)
├─ Or microwave-assisted drying
└─ Target: <5% moisture for shelf stability
         │
         ▼
Step 5: DEFATTING (Optional)
├─ Mechanical pressing (oil extraction)
├─ Or solvent extraction (hexane, ethanol)
├─ Separates: Protein meal + Insect oil
└─ Higher protein % meal for some applications
         │
         ▼
Step 6: MILLING
├─ Grinding to powder (flour)
├─ Particle size: 100-500 microns
├─ Homogeneous product
└─ Easier incorporation into foods
         │
         ▼
Step 7: QUALITY CONTROL
├─ Microbial testing
├─ Heavy metal analysis
├─ Protein content verification
├─ Allergen screening
└─ Nutritional profiling
         │
         ▼
OUTPUT PRODUCTS:
├─ Whole dried insects (snacks, garnish)
├─ Insect flour/powder (protein ingredient)
├─ Protein isolate (>80% protein)
├─ Insect oil (omega-3, cooking)
├─ Chitin (bioactive fiber)
└─ Frass (organic fertilizer - separate stream)
```

### Product Forms and Applications

| Product Form | Protein % | Applications | Consumer Acceptance |
|--------------|-----------|--------------|---------------------|

### Allergenicity Concerns

**Cross-Reactivity with Shellfish:**

```
INSECT ALLERGENICITY

Tropomyosin (Major Allergen)
├─ Found in: Crustaceans (shrimp, crab, lobster)
├─ Also in: Insects (crickets, mealworms)
├─ Structure: ~70-80% similar
└─ Result: Cross-reactivity potential

Regulatory Response:
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ "Contains insects. May cause allergic  │
│ reactions in people with shellfish or  │
│ dust mite allergies."                  │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘

Required on labels in EU, recommended in US.

Mitigation Strategies:
├─ Processing to denature tropomyosin (heat, enzymatic)
├─ Selective breeding for low-allergen strains
├─ Alternative species with different allergen profiles
└─ Clear labeling and consumer education

Research Needed:
├─ Dose-response relationships
├─ Long-term exposure studies
├─ Immunotherapy possibilities
└─ Hypoallergenic product development
```

## 9. Case Study: Ynsect (France)

**Background:**
- Founded 2011
- Focus: Mealworm farming at industrial scale
- $600M+ raised (one of largest insect farming raises)
- Vertical farming facility in Amiens, France

**Technology:**
- Automated vertical farming (multi-story racking)
- AI-optimized feeding and climate control
- Robotic harvesting and processing
- Circular economy model (feed = ag byproducts)

**Products:**
- Animal feed (pet food, aquaculture, poultry)
- Protein meal for livestock
- Insect oil
- Organic fertilizer (frass)

**Scale:**
- Current: 10,000+ tons/year capacity
- Target: 100,000 tons/year by 2027
- Largest insect farm globally

**Business Model:**
- B2B focus (ingredients, not consumer brands)
- Partner with major food/feed companies
- Multiple revenue streams

**Challenges Overcome:**
- Automation (reduced labor to <5% of costs)
- Regulatory approvals (EU Novel Food)
- Supply chain development (byproduct sourcing)

**Lessons:**
- Scale is critical for cost competitiveness
- Animal feed market larger/easier than human food
- Automation essential for economics
- Circular economy model adds value

### Strategic Recommendations

**For Insect Farming Startups:**
1. Focus on animal/pet feed initially (easier market)
2. Prioritize automation from day one
3. Secure waste feedstock partnerships
4. Pursue multiple revenue streams (protein + oil + frass)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Insects are among the most resource-efficient protein sources** with FCR of 1.5-2.0 and minimal environmental impact.

2. **Animal feed is the largest and fastest-growing market** for insect protein, with pet food close behind; human food is niche but growing.

3. **Mealworms and crickets lead for human consumption**; black soldier fly dominates animal feed and waste valorization.

4. **Regulatory frameworks are emerging** with EU leading on Novel Food approvals; US has GRAS pathway.

5. **Consumer acceptance is the critical barrier in Western markets**; "invisibility" and gradual normalization strategies required.

6. **Alternative proteins (algae, fungi, bacteria) each have unique advantages** but insects currently most commercially advanced.

7. **Automation and scale are essential** for cost competitiveness; labor must be <10% of costs.
