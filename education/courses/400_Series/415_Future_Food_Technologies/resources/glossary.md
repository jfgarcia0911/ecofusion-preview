# Glossary: Future Food Technologies

**Course 415 - Technical Terminology Reference**

---

## A

**Acellular Product**
Food or ingredient produced through precision fermentation where the final product contains no cells, only the target molecule (e.g., dairy proteins from yeast, but no yeast in final product).

**Adipocyte**
Fat cell. In cultured meat production, adipocytes are cultured to provide fat content for flavor, juiciness, and mouthfeel.

**Aeroponic System**
Growing method where plant roots are suspended in air and misted with nutrient solution. Used in vertical farming and space agriculture.

**AgTech (Agricultural Technology)**
Technology innovations applied to agriculture, including precision farming, robotics, biotechnology, and data analytics.

**Allergenicity**
Potential of a substance to cause an allergic reaction. Important consideration for novel foods (e.g., insect protein cross-reactivity with shellfish allergies).

**Alternative Protein**
Protein sources other than conventional animal agriculture, including plant-based, cellular agriculture, precision fermentation, insect, algae, and fungal proteins.

**Automated Milking System (AMS)**
Robotic systems for dairy farming. Included as comparison to automated food production technologies.

---

## B

**Bioavailability**
The degree to which nutrients from food are absorbed and utilized by the body. Important for evaluating novel protein sources.

**Bioprocess Engineering**
Application of engineering principles to biological production processes, including fermentation, cell culture, and downstream processing.

**Bioreactor**
Vessel in which biological reactions occur under controlled conditions. Used for microbial fermentation, cell culture, and algae production. Sizes range from liters (lab) to hundreds of thousands of liters (commercial).

**Bioregenerative Life Support System**
Closed-loop system using biological organisms (plants, algae, bacteria) to regenerate resources (oxygen, water, food) from waste. Key to long-duration space missions.

**Black Soldier Fly (BSF) - Hermetia illucens**
Insect species widely farmed for protein production, particularly for animal feed. Known for high feed conversion efficiency and ability to consume organic waste.

**Blockchain**
Distributed ledger technology enabling transparent, immutable record-keeping. Applied in food systems for traceability, supply chain management, and smart contracts.

**Brix**
Measure of sugar content in solution, relevant to fruit production, algae cultivation, and quality control.

---

## C

**CAPEX (Capital Expenditure)**
Upfront investment in facilities, equipment, and infrastructure. Major cost component in scaling food technologies.

**Cas9**
Enzyme used in CRISPR gene editing that cuts DNA at specific locations. Part of CRISPR-Cas9 system.

**Casein**
Primary protein in milk (80% of milk protein). Target for precision fermentation replication.

**Cell Line**
Population of cells derived from a single ancestor cell, used for cultured meat production. Cell lines can be primary (limited lifespan) or immortalized (continuous division).

**Cellular Agriculture**
Production of agricultural products (meat, dairy, etc.) from cell cultures rather than whole animals. Includes both cellular products (cultured meat with cells) and acellular products (precision fermentation).

**CELSS (Closed Ecological Life Support System)**
See Bioregenerative Life Support System. NASA terminology for space agriculture systems.

**Chitin**
Structural polysaccharide found in insect exoskeletons and fungal cell walls. Can be extracted as a valuable byproduct with prebiotic fiber properties.

**Chloroplast Transformation**
Genetic modification of chloroplast (plant organelle) genomes rather than nuclear genome. Offers high expression and gene containment (maternal inheritance only).

**CHO Cells (Chinese Hamster Ovary)**
Mammalian cell line widely used in biopharmaceutical production. Can produce complex proteins with mammalian glycosylation.

**Chroma (Chromatography)**
Purification technique separating molecules based on physical/chemical properties. Major cost driver in downstream processing for precision fermentation and cultured meat.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)**
Direct costs of producing a product, including materials, labor, energy, and facility costs. Key metric for evaluating commercial viability.

**Continuous Culture/Processing**
Bioproduction method where inputs are continuously added and products continuously harvested, vs. batch processing. Offers higher productivity but technical challenges.

**CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats)**
Gene editing technology enabling precise modifications to DNA. Used for crop improvement, cell line optimization, and synthetic biology.

**Cultured Meat (Cultivated Meat, Cell-Based Meat)**
Meat produced by culturing animal cells in bioreactors rather than raising and slaughtering animals.

---

## D

**Differentiation**
Process by which stem cells or progenitor cells develop into specialized cell types (e.g., myoblasts differentiating into muscle fibers).

**Digital Twin**
Virtual replica of a physical system (farm, greenhouse, bioreactor) that simulates real-world behavior using real-time data. Used for optimization and scenario testing.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**
Concentration of oxygen in liquid media, critical parameter in bioreactors. Typically measured as % air saturation.

**Downstream Processing (DSP)**
Steps after fermentation/cell culture to purify and formulate the final product. Includes separation, purification, concentration, and formulation.

**Dysphagia**
Difficulty swallowing. Target application for 3D-printed foods with customized textures for elderly or medical patients.

---

## E

**EFSA (European Food Safety Authority)**
EU regulatory body that assesses food safety, including Novel Foods. More stringent approval process than FDA.

**ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)**
Analytical technique for detecting specific proteins. Used in quality control for precision fermentation products.

**Entomophagy**
Practice of eating insects. Common in many cultures globally, emerging in Western markets.

**Eutrophication**
Excessive nutrient pollution (nitrogen, phosphorus) in water bodies causing algae blooms and dead zones. Conventional agriculture is major contributor; novel technologies aim to reduce this impact.

**Extrusion**
Food processing technique forcing material through a die to create shapes. Used in traditional food processing and 3D food printing.

---

## F

**FCR (Feed Conversion Ratio)**
Mass of feed required to produce one unit of body mass. Lower is better. Example: Insects (1.5-2.0), Chicken (2.0), Beef (8-10).

**Fed-Batch Fermentation**
Bioreactor operation where nutrients are added during fermentation to extend production phase and increase yields.

**FBS (Fetal Bovine Serum)**
Blood serum from fetal calves, traditionally used in cell culture media. Major cost driver and ethical concern; serum-free media development is critical.

**Fermentation (Precision)**
See Precision Fermentation. Distinct from traditional fermentation (beer, yogurt) which consumes the whole fermented product.

**FoodTech**
Technology innovations applied specifically to food production, processing, distribution, and consumption.

**Frass**
Insect excrement and shed exoskeletons. Valuable byproduct of insect farming used as organic fertilizer.

---

## G

**Gartner Hype Cycle**
Framework describing technology adoption pattern: Innovation Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity.

**Gene Drive**
Genetic engineering technique that increases inheritance probability above 50%, potentially spreading traits through wild populations. Controversial for pest control applications.

**Gene Editing**
Precise modification of DNA sequences using technologies like CRISPR. Can create changes that could occur naturally, vs. GMOs which often introduce foreign DNA.

**Genetic Use Restriction Technology (GURT)**
Also called "terminator technology." Genetic methods to prevent seed germination, providing containment but controversial.

**Glycosylation**
Addition of sugar molecules to proteins after translation. Critical for protein function, stability, and immunogenicity. Challenge in plant/yeast systems for producing mammalian proteins.

**GMO (Genetically Modified Organism)**
Organism whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering. Includes transgenic organisms with foreign DNA. Regulatory status varies globally.

**GO/NO-GO Decision**
Formal decision point in technology development determining whether to proceed to next phase or terminate/pivot project.

**GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe)**
FDA designation allowing food ingredients to enter market without pre-market approval if scientific consensus on safety exists. Faster pathway than novel food additives.

**Growth Factors**
Proteins that stimulate cell proliferation and differentiation. Examples: FGF, IGF, TGF-β. Major cost driver in cell culture media; target for cost reduction via recombinant production.

---

## H

**HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points)**
Food safety management system identifying and controlling hazards. Required for food production facilities.

**Hayflick Limit**
Number of times normal cells can divide before senescence (typically 40-60 divisions). Challenge for cultured meat requiring many cell doublings; addressed via immortalization.

**Heme (Leghemoglobin)**
Iron-containing molecule produced via precision fermentation by Impossible Foods. Provides "bloody" flavor and color to plant-based burgers.

**Hype Cycle**
See Gartner Hype Cycle.

**Hydrocolloid**
Substance that forms gel when mixed with water. Examples: alginate, agar, pectin. Used in 3D food printing inks and texture modification.

**Hydrolysate**
Protein broken down into smaller peptides and amino acids via enzymatic or chemical hydrolysis. Used for savory flavoring.

**Hydroponics**
Soilless growing method where plant roots grow in nutrient solution. Widely used in vertical farming and space agriculture.

---

## I

**Immortalization**
Process of modifying cells to divide indefinitely, bypassing the Hayflick limit. Methods include telomerase activation or tumor suppressor inactivation. Controversial for food applications.

**Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs)**
Adult cells reprogrammed to embryonic-like state, capable of differentiating into any cell type. Potential for cultured meat but technically complex and expensive.

**In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)**
Using local resources (lunar regolith, Martian atmosphere) rather than transporting from Earth. Critical for space agriculture viability.

**Insect Protein**
Protein derived from farmed insects (crickets, mealworms, black soldier fly). Used for human food and animal feed.

**IoT (Internet of Things)**
Network of physical devices with sensors collecting and exchanging data. Applied in precision agriculture for monitoring and automation.

---

## K

**Karyotype**
Number and appearance of chromosomes in a cell. Monitored in cell lines to ensure genetic stability over passages.

---

## L

**LCA (Lifecycle Assessment)**
Comprehensive analysis of environmental impacts from "cradle to grave." Includes GHG emissions, water use, land use, eutrophication, etc.

**Leghemoglobin**
See Heme. Plant-derived heme protein produced via precision fermentation.

---

## M

**Master Cell Bank (MCB)**
Well-characterized collection of cells stored at early passage, used as source for all future production batches. Critical for consistency and traceability.

**Metabolic Engineering**
Modification of metabolic pathways in organisms to enhance production of desired compounds or introduce new capabilities.

**Microcarrier**
Small spheres (100-300 µm) providing surface for cell attachment in suspension bioreactors. Used in cultured meat and biopharmaceutical production.

**Microgravity**
Condition of very weak gravity, as experienced in orbit. Affects plant growth (gravitropism disrupted) and fluid behavior.

**Molecular Farming (Pharming, Biopharming)**
Use of genetically modified plants to produce recombinant proteins, pharmaceuticals, or industrial enzymes.

**Myoblast**
Muscle precursor cell that proliferates and differentiates into muscle fibers. Key cell type for cultured meat.

**Mycoprotein**
Protein derived from fungal biomass. Example: Quorn (Fusarium venenatum). Produced via fermentation.

**Myotube**
Multinucleated muscle fiber formed by fusion of myoblasts. Contains contractile proteins and exhibits muscle characteristics.

---

## N

**Nanofertilizer**
Fertilizer formulated with nanomaterials for controlled release and enhanced uptake efficiency.

**Nanosensor**
Sensor utilizing nanomaterials for enhanced sensitivity. Applications include soil nutrient detection and pathogen identification.

**Nanotechnology**
Manipulation of matter at atomic/molecular scale (1-100 nanometers). Applications in agriculture include delivery systems, sensors, and packaging.

**Novel Food**
Food or ingredient without significant history of human consumption. Requires regulatory approval (EFSA in EU, FSANZ in Australia/NZ, etc.).

**Nutrigenomics**
Study of relationship between genome and nutrition. Basis for personalized nutrition approaches.

---

## O

**OPEX (Operating Expenditure)**
Ongoing costs to run operations (labor, materials, energy, maintenance). Contrasts with CAPEX (upfront investment).

**Organoleptic**
Relating to sensory properties (taste, smell, texture, appearance). Critical for consumer acceptance of novel foods.

**Orphan Crop**
Locally important crop species that have received limited research investment. Examples: teff, fonio, bambara groundnut. Often climate-resilient.

**Osmolarity**
Concentration of osmotically active particles in solution. Important parameter in cell culture media (typically ~300 mOsm/kg for mammalian cells).

---

## P

**Passage Number**
Number of times cells have been subcultured. Higher passage numbers increase risk of genetic drift. Cultured meat typically requires passages beyond primary cell limits.

**Perchlorate**
Toxic chlorine compound found in Martian soil (0.5-1%). Must be removed for plant growth or human safety.

**Perfusion Culture**
Bioreactor operation with continuous feeding and product removal while retaining cells. Enables very high cell densities.

**PESTEL Analysis**
Framework examining Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal factors affecting technology deployment.

**Photobioreactor**
Closed system for cultivating photosynthetic organisms (algae, cyanobacteria) with controlled light, CO₂, temperature, and nutrients.

**Precision Agriculture**
Farm management strategy using data analytics, sensors, GPS, and variable-rate technology to optimize inputs and yields.

**Precision Fermentation**
Use of genetically engineered microorganisms to produce specific molecules (proteins, fats, vitamins) without those organisms in final product. Also called "acellular agriculture."

**Proliferation**
Cell division and multiplication. First stage in cultured meat production before differentiation.

**Protein Isolate**
Protein product with >90% protein content (dry weight basis). Requires extensive purification.

---

## R

**Recombinant DNA**
DNA formed by combining sequences from different sources. Basis for genetic engineering.

**Regolith**
Loose surface material on Moon or Mars. Must be processed (wash perchlorates, add organics) for use as plant substrate.

**Rheology**
Study of flow and deformation of materials. Critical for formulating 3D food printing inks (must be shear-thinning).

---

## S

**Sabatier Reaction**
Chemical reaction converting CO₂ and H₂ into methane and water. Used in Mars life support systems.

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**
Portion of Total Addressable Market (TAM) that a company can realistically serve. Used in market sizing.

**Satellite Cell**
Muscle stem cell located between muscle fiber and basal lamina. Natural source of muscle regeneration; gold standard cell type for cultured meat.

**Scaffold**
Physical structure providing support for cells to grow into 3D tissue. Materials include microcarriers, hydrogels, plant-based structures. Can be edible or removed.

**Scale-Out**
Increasing capacity by adding more units of same size (e.g., multiple 10,000L bioreactors) rather than scaling up to larger units. Reduces technical risk.

**Scale-Up**
Increasing production capacity by moving to larger equipment (e.g., 1L → 10L → 100L → 10,000L bioreactor). Introduces engineering challenges.

**S-Curve**
Characteristic sigmoid adoption pattern of technologies: slow emergence, rapid growth, plateau at maturity.

**Serum-Free Media**
Cell culture media without animal serum (FBS). Critical for cost reduction and ethical/regulatory viability of cultured meat.

**Shear Stress**
Force parallel to surface, caused by fluid flow or agitation. Can damage cells in bioreactors; must be minimized.

**Single-Cell Protein (SCP)**
Protein derived from microbial biomass (bacteria, yeast, algae). Produced via fermentation, often on unconventional feedstocks (CO₂, H₂, methane).

**Spirulina**
Blue-green microalgae (actually cyanobacteria) with 50-70% protein content. Widely sold as supplement; potential for novel food applications.

**Substrate**
Material on which organisms grow. In fermentation: feedstock (glucose, etc.). In horticulture: growing medium. In enzymatic reactions: molecule acted upon.

**Synthetic Biology**
Design and construction of new biological parts, devices, and systems. Combines engineering principles with biology.

---

## T

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**
Total market demand for a product or service. Used in market sizing and investment analysis.

**Telomerase**
Enzyme that extends telomeres, allowing cells to bypass the Hayflick limit. Overexpression is one immortalization method.

**TRL (Technology Readiness Level)**
NASA-developed scale (1-9) assessing technology maturity from basic research (TRL 1) to proven commercial operation (TRL 9).

**Titer**
Concentration of product in fermentation broth, typically measured in g/L. Higher titers reduce costs.

**Transient Expression**
Temporary gene expression via viral vector infection, without genome integration. Used in molecular farming for rapid production without creating stable GMO.

**Tropomyosin**
Protein found in muscle and invertebrates (including insects and crustaceans). Major allergen causing cross-reactivity between insect protein and shellfish allergies.

---

## U

**Upstream Processing**
Steps before main production (fermentation/cell culture), including media preparation, inoculation, and early-stage culture.

---

## V

**Vertical Farming**
Indoor agriculture with stacked layers, controlled environment (CEA), and artificial lighting. Enables high productivity per land area.

**VLP (Virus-Like Particle)**
Non-infectious particle resembling virus structure. Used in vaccine production, including plant-made vaccines (e.g., Medicago's COVID vaccine).

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)**
Difference between moisture in air and saturation point. Important parameter in controlled environment agriculture affecting transpiration and growth.

---

## W

**Whey**
Liquid byproduct of cheese-making containing ~20% of milk proteins. Major target for precision fermentation replication (beta-lactoglobulin).

**Working Cell Bank (WCB)**
Cells derived from Master Cell Bank, used for routine production. Expanded and stored for day-to-day manufacturing.

---

## X

**Xylose**
Five-carbon sugar added to proteins during glycosylation in plants but not mammals. Plant-specific glycan can cause allergenicity or altered function; removed via glycoengineering.

---

## Z

**Zoonotic Disease**
Disease transmissible from animals to humans. Examples: avian flu, swine flu, COVID-19. Cellular agriculture reduces risk by eliminating live animal farming.
