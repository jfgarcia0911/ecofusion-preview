## Questions

### Question 1
What is cellular agriculture?

A) Traditional livestock farming using cells phones
B) Production of agricultural products from cell cultures instead of whole organisms
C) Planting cells in fields
D) Genetic modification of crops

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cellular agriculture produces meat, dairy, leather, and other products from cell cultures (cellular agriculture) or precision fermentation, without raising and slaughtering animals.

---

### Question 2
What are the two main platforms of cellular agriculture?

A) Hydroponics and aquaponics
B) Cellular agriculture (cell culture) and precision fermentation (microbial production)
C) Organic and conventional
D) Indoor and outdoor

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cellular agriculture encompasses: 1) Cell culture (growing animal cells for meat/leather), and 2) Precision fermentation (engineering microbes to produce proteins).

---

### Question 3
What is the primary challenge limiting commercial scale-up of cultured meat?

A) Consumer acceptance
B) High production costs, particularly growth media (serum), and bioreactor scale limitations
C) Regulatory approval
D) Marketing

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Media costs ($100-400/L for serum-based) and scaling bioreactors to industrial volumes are major technical and economic barriers to cost-competitive cultured meat.

---

### Question 4
What is fetal bovine serum (FBS) and why is it problematic for cultured meat?

A) A plant-based product with no issues
B) Blood serum from cattle fetuses used as growth media; expensive and ethically controversial
C) A synthetic chemical
D) A flavor enhancer

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** FBS, harvested from unborn calves, is expensive ($500+/L), variable, and contradicts animal welfare goals of cultured meat—driving development of serum-free media.

---

### Question 5
What temperature range is typically used for mammalian cell culture in bioreactors?

A) 0-10°C
B) 20-25°C
C) 37°C (human body temperature)
D) 60-80°C

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mammalian cells (cow, pig, chicken) are cultured at 37°C, mimicking animal body temperature for optimal growth and metabolism.

---

### Question 6
Calculate the cost reduction needed: If cultured meat currently costs $50/kg to produce and conventional ground beef costs $5/kg, what cost reduction factor is required?

A) 2×
B) 5×
C) 10×
D) 20×

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Cost reduction = Current cost / Target cost = $50 / $5 = 10× reduction needed for price parity with conventional meat.

---

### Question 7
What is the purpose of scaffolding in cultured meat production?

A) Building support for factories
B) Providing 3D structure for cells to grow into tissue-like architecture
C) Worker safety equipment
D) Packaging material

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Scaffolds (edible biomaterials) provide structural support for cells to organize into 3D tissues with texture approximating whole muscle meat.

---

### Question 8
Which cell type is most commonly used for cultured meat production?

A) Bacterial cells
B) Yeast cells
C) Muscle stem cells (satellite cells) from animals
D) Plant cells

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Satellite cells (muscle stem cells) isolated via biopsy can proliferate extensively and differentiate into muscle fibers, forming cultured meat.

---

### Question 9
What is a stirred-tank bioreactor and why is it used?

A) A storage tank for finished products
B) A vessel with mechanical agitation providing controlled environment (temperature, pH, oxygen) for cell growth at scale
C) A shipping container
D) A packaging machine

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Stirred-tank bioreactors provide precisely controlled conditions for large-scale cell culture with mixing, aeration, and monitoring—standard in bioprocessing.

---

### Question 10
Which country was FIRST to grant regulatory approval for commercial sale of cultivated meat?

A) United States
B) Netherlands
C) Singapore
D) Israel

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Singapore approved GOOD Meat's cultured chicken in December 2020, the first regulatory approval for commercial sale of cultivated meat anywhere in the world.
