## Questions

### Question 1
What is synthetic biology?

A) Making synthetic fabrics
B) Engineering organisms using standardized biological parts to create new functions or redesign existing systems
C) Traditional plant breeding
D) Chemical synthesis only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Synthetic biology applies engineering principles to biology—designing and building biological systems with new capabilities using standardized genetic parts (BioBricks).

---

### Question 2
What is CRISPR-Cas9's primary application in food systems?

A) Food packaging
B) Precise gene editing to enhance crops (yield, nutrition, climate tolerance) or develop new traits
C) Cooking technique
D) Food preservation

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CRISPR enables targeted DNA changes without inserting foreign genes—improving drought tolerance, disease resistance, nutrition, or removing allergens with precision.

---

### Question 3
How do CRISPR-edited crops differ from traditional GMOs in regulation?

A) Identical regulation always
B) Some countries regulate CRISPR edits mimicking natural mutations less stringently than transgenic GMOs
C) CRISPR is always more regulated
D) No regulation exists

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Regulatory approaches vary: some countries (US, Japan) regulate based on product not process—CRISPR crops indistinguishable from conventional breeding face less oversight than transgenic GMOs.

---

### Question 4
What is metabolic engineering?

A) Exercise programs
B) Modifying organism biochemical pathways to enhance production of valuable compounds
C) Building design
D) Food labeling

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Metabolic engineering redesigns cellular metabolism—redirecting resources to overproduce desired molecules (vitamins, flavors, biofuels) or create novel compounds.

---

### Question 5
What breakthrough could synthetic biology achieve in nitrogen fixation?

A) Eliminating nitrogen from food
B) Engineering cereals (wheat, corn) to fix atmospheric nitrogen, reducing fertilizer dependence
C) Increasing fertilizer use
D) Nothing possible

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Researchers aim to transfer nitrogen-fixing capabilities from legumes to cereals via synthetic biology, potentially eliminating fertilizer needs and reducing environmental impact.

---

### Question 6
What is C4 photosynthesis engineering in C3 crops?

A) Changing crop colors
B) Transferring efficient C4 photosynthesis pathway to C3 crops (rice, wheat) to boost yield and heat tolerance
C) Adding vitamin C4
D) Removing photosynthesis

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** C4 plants (corn, sugarcane) are 50% more efficient in hot/dry conditions. Engineering C4 pathway into C3 crops (rice) could increase yields 30-50%.

---

### Question 7
What is the "gene drive" technology and its agricultural application?

A) Cars for transporting genes
B) Genetic modification that spreads through populations; potential to control agricultural pests or invasive species
C) Storage drives for genomic data
D) Transportation systems

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Gene drives propagate genetic changes through populations (overriding Mendelian inheritance), potentially suppressing pest populations—but raises ecological and biosafety concerns.

---

### Question 8
What biosafety concern is associated with gene-edited crops?

A) No concerns exist
B) Potential for unintended effects, gene flow to wild relatives, and ecological impacts
C) All edited crops are dangerous
D) Only benefits, no risks

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** While precise, CRISPR can have off-target effects. Gene flow to wild relatives could spread traits (herbicide resistance), and ecosystem impacts require assessment.

---

### Question 9
What is the current status of CRISPR-edited crops in the marketplace (2025)?

A) Banned globally
B) Several approved and commercialized (e.g., high-oleic soybean, improved tomatoes, mushrooms)
C) None developed yet
D) Only in laboratories

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Multiple CRISPR crops commercialized: Calyxt high-oleic soybean, Pairwise improved greens, Japanese high-GABA tomato—more in development pipelines.

---

### Question 10
How might synthetic biology address climate change in agriculture?

A) Cannot help with climate
B) Developing heat/drought tolerant crops, reducing fertilizer emissions, and creating carbon-capturing plants
C) Only makes climate worse
D) Eliminates weather

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Synthetic biology can engineer climate resilience (heat/drought tolerance), reduce emissions (nitrogen-fixing crops eliminate fertilizers), and enhance carbon sequestration in soils.
