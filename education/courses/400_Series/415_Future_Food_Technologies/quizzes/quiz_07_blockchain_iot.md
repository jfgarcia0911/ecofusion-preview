## Questions

### Question 1
What is blockchain's primary application in food systems?

A) Cryptocurrency trading only
B) Immutable traceability records tracking products from farm to consumer
C) Social media
D) Weather forecasting

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Blockchain creates tamper-proof transaction records enabling transparent traceability, authenticity verification, and trust in supply chains without central authority.

---

### Question 2
What is a smart contract in food supply chains?

A) Paper agreements
B) Self-executing code that automatically enforces terms when conditions are met (e.g., automatic payment upon delivery verification)
C) Legal advice
D) Verbal agreements

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Smart contracts are blockchain-based programs automatically executing transactions when predetermined conditions are verified (e.g., quality specs met, temperature maintained).

---

### Question 3
What does IoT stand for and how does it apply to agriculture?

A) Internet of Things; networks of sensors monitoring crops, soil, weather, equipment in real-time
B) Institute of Technology
C) Iron oxide treatment
D) Input-output tables

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Internet of Things connects sensors, devices, and equipment for real-time data collection on soil moisture, temperature, crop health, enabling precision agriculture.

---

### Question 4
What is edge computing in agricultural IoT?

A) Farming on property edges
B) Processing data locally on sensors/devices rather than sending all data to cloud, enabling faster response
C) Sharp tool edges
D) Cliff-side farming

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Edge computing processes sensor data locally (on-device or nearby gateway), reducing latency, bandwidth, and enabling real-time control when cloud connectivity is limited.

---

### Question 5
How does AI/machine learning enhance precision agriculture?

A) Replacing all farmers
B) Analyzing sensor data to predict yields, detect diseases, optimize irrigation/fertilization, and guide autonomous equipment
C) Eliminating data collection
D) No applications

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AI analyzes massive sensor datasets identifying patterns humans can't detect—predicting issues, optimizing resource use, and enabling autonomous decision-making at scale.

---

### Question 6
What is a digital twin in agriculture?

A) Identical twin farmers
B) Virtual model of a farm/crop system simulating real-world conditions for scenario testing and optimization
C) Backup computer
D) Social media profile

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Digital twins are virtual replicas of physical farms/systems, integrating real-time data to simulate scenarios (weather changes, management decisions) and optimize strategies.

---

### Question 7
What cybersecurity risk is most concerning for connected agricultural systems?

A) No risks exist
B) Ransomware attacks on farm systems, data theft, and sabotage of automated equipment
C) Too much security
D) Only physical theft matters

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Connected farms face cyber threats: ransomware locking climate control, data theft (proprietary genetics), and equipment sabotage—requiring robust cybersecurity.

---

### Question 8
What blockchain challenge limits widespread food system adoption?

A) Too simple to implement
B) Scalability limitations, high energy use (proof-of-work), and interoperability between platforms
C) Perfect technology with no issues
D) Too cheap

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Blockchain faces challenges: transaction throughput limits, energy consumption (Bitcoin-style), cost, and lack of standards preventing cross-platform data sharing.

---

### Question 9
What is data sovereignty in agricultural IoT?

A) Government data control
B) Farmers' ownership and control over data generated by their operations
C) Company ownership of all farm data
D) Data has no owner

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Data sovereignty addresses who owns farm data—farmers assert rights to control, access, and monetize data from their operations rather than tech companies owning it.

---

### Question 10
Which industry is blockchain food traceability MOST developed?

A) Bulk commodity grains
B) High-value supply chains (organic, specialty coffee, seafood) requiring provenance verification
C) Tap water
D) Generic produce

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Blockchain adoption is highest for high-value products where provenance matters (organic certification, single-origin coffee, wild-caught seafood) justifying implementation costs.
