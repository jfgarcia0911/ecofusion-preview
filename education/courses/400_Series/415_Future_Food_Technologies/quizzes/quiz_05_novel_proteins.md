## Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary nutritional advantage of insects as protein sources?

A) Low protein content
B) High-quality protein (all essential amino acids), vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats
C) Only carbohydrates
D) No nutritional value

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Insects provide complete protein (40-75% dry weight), B vitamins, iron, zinc, and omega-3 fatty acids—nutritionally comparable or superior to conventional meat.

---

### Question 2
What is the feed conversion ratio (FCR) of crickets compared to cattle?

A) Crickets: 10:1, Cattle: 2:1 (crickets worse)
B) Both identical at 5:1
C) Crickets: 1.7:1, Cattle: 10:1 (crickets much better)
D) Cannot be compared

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Crickets convert ~1.7 kg feed to 1 kg protein, while cattle require ~10 kg feed per kg protein—making insects far more efficient.

---

### Question 3
Which insect species is most commonly farmed for human food?

A) Houseflies
B) Crickets (Acheta domesticus) and mealworms (Tenebrio molitor)
C) Mosquitoes
D) Ants

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Crickets and mealworms dominate human consumption due to nutritional profile, scalability, consumer acceptance, and established farming protocols.

---

### Question 4
What is the primary market application for farmed insects currently?

A) Whole insects for direct human consumption in Western markets
B) Protein powder, flour, and animal feed (aquaculture, pet food)
C) Pharmaceuticals only
D) Cosmetics

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** While whole insects are consumed in Asia/Africa, Western markets primarily use insect protein as ingredient (flour, powder) and in animal feed due to lower "ick factor."

---

### Question 5
What environmental advantage do insects offer over conventional livestock?

A) Higher emissions
B) 80-90% lower greenhouse gas emissions, land, and water use than cattle
C) Identical impact
D) Higher water use

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Insect farming requires minimal land, water (insects are 65% water content), and produces negligible methane—dramatically lower environmental impact than livestock.

---

### Question 6
What is the "ick factor" and how do companies address it?

A) A disease
B) Consumer disgust response to eating insects; addressed through processing into powders, burgers, or emphasizing sustainability
C) Manufacturing defect
D) Nutritional deficiency

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Western consumers often find whole insects unappealing; companies incorporate insect protein invisibly into familiar products (burgers, bars) or emphasize environmental benefits.

---

### Question 7
What is the regulatory status of insects for food in the EU?

A) Banned completely
B) Novel Food regulation requiring safety approval; several species now approved (mealworms, crickets, locusts)
C) Unregulated
D) Only for animal feed

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** EU Novel Food regulation requires pre-market safety assessment. Several species gained approval: dried yellow mealworm (2021), crickets, locusts for human consumption.

---

### Question 8
Which alternative protein source has the LOWEST environmental impact?

A) Beef
B) Chicken
C) Algae (spirulina, chlorella)
D) Pork

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Algae produces protein with minimal land, fresh water (can use seawater), no methane, and photosynthetic CO₂ capture—the lowest environmental footprint protein source.

---

### Question 9
What is the projected global insect protein market size by 2030?

A) $1 million
B) $50-100 million
C) $1-5 billion
D) $100 billion

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Market analysts project $1-5 billion by 2030 for insect protein (food and feed applications), growing rapidly from current $400-500 million.

---

### Question 10
What is mycoprotein (Quorn)?

A) Mushroom-based protein
B) Protein from continuous fermentation of Fusarium venenatum fungus
C) Synthetic chemical protein
D) Plant-based protein

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mycoprotein is fungal biomass from fermenting Fusarium venenatum, providing complete protein with meat-like texture—commercially successful since 1985 (Quorn brand).
