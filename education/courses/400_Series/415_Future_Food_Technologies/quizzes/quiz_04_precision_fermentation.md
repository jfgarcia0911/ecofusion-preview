## Questions

### Question 1
What is precision fermentation?

A) Traditional beer brewing
B) Using genetically engineered microorganisms to produce specific proteins, fats, or other molecules
C) Fermenting vegetables
D) Composting food waste

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Precision fermentation engineers microbes (yeast, bacteria, fungi) with genes to produce target molecules (e.g., whey protein, heme, collagen) identical to animal-derived versions.

---

### Question 2
Which microorganism is most commonly used as a production platform for precision fermentation?

A) E. coli bacteria
B) Saccharomyces cerevisiae (baker's yeast)
C) Salmonella
D) Mushrooms

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** S. cerevisiae is the workhorse organism—safe (GRAS status), well-understood genetics, scalable fermentation, and produces complex proteins with proper folding.

---

### Question 3
What commercial product demonstrated precision fermentation's viability for food?

A) Soy milk
B) Impossible Foods' heme protein and Perfect Day's dairy proteins
C) Conventional cheese
D) Almond butter

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Impossible Foods' soy leghemoglobin (heme for "meat" flavor) and Perfect Day's whey protein (animal-free dairy) proved commercial and regulatory feasibility.

---

### Question 4
What is the typical production cost for precision fermentation proteins (2025)?

A) Higher than conventional dairy ($10-30/kg vs $5-10/kg)
B) Identical to conventional
C) Free
D) $1000/kg

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Precision fermentation proteins currently cost $10-30/kg, approaching but not yet at parity with conventional dairy proteins ($5-10/kg)—projected parity by 2025-2030.

---

### Question 5
What is downstream processing in precision fermentation?

A) River management
B) Separating, purifying, and formulating the target molecule after fermentation
C) Waste disposal
D) Marketing

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Downstream processing extracts the target protein from biomass, removes impurities, concentrates, and formulates into usable ingredients—often 50% of total cost.

---

### Question 6
What advantage does precision fermentation have over animal agriculture for dairy proteins?

A) Higher cost
B) Identical proteins without animals, with 80-95% lower emissions, land, and water use
C) Worse taste
D) Longer production time

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Precision fermentation produces molecularly identical proteins (casein, whey) with dramatically lower environmental footprint and no animal welfare concerns.

---

### Question 7
What molecule does Impossible Foods produce via precision fermentation for meat flavor?

A) Sugar
B) Soy leghemoglobin (heme protein) providing blood-like flavor
C) Salt
D) Water

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Impossible uses engineered yeast to produce heme (leghemoglobin), an iron-containing molecule that provides meat's savory, slightly metallic "bloody" flavor.

---

### Question 8
What is the regulatory status of precision fermentation products in the US?

A) Banned
B) Most approved as GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) via FDA self-determination or notification
C) Unregulated
D) Requires prescription

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Many precision fermentation ingredients achieve GRAS status through expert panels or FDA notification, streamlining market entry if the protein is identical to conventional versions.

---

### Question 9
Which application of precision fermentation is MOST commercially advanced?

A) Whole cultured meat
B) Specific proteins: dairy proteins, egg proteins, collagen
C) Living organisms
D) Synthetic organs

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Individual protein production (Perfect Day whey, Clara Foods egg whites, Geltor collagen) is most advanced—simpler than whole tissue engineering.

---

### Question 10
What is a key advantage of precision fermentation over cell culture for specific molecules?

A) Lower complexity and established scaling (fermentation is proven industrial process)
B) More expensive
C) Slower
D) Less control

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Fermentation infrastructure exists at industrial scale (pharma, brewing), microbes grow faster than animal cells, and production is more reliable and cost-effective for proteins.
