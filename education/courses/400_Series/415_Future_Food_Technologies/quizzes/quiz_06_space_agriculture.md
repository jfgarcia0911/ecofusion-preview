## Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary challenge of growing plants in microgravity?

A) Too much sunlight
B) Lack of gravity-driven convection for air circulation and water/nutrient delivery
C) Too much water
D) No challenges exist

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Microgravity eliminates convective air/water movement; requires forced ventilation for CO₂ delivery and specialized watering systems to prevent floating water droplets.

---

### Question 2
What growing system has proven most successful on the International Space Station (ISS)?

A) Soil-based farming
B) LED-lit plant growth chambers with controlled environment and porous media/hydroponic systems
C) Outdoor gardens
D) No plants can grow in space

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISS uses Veggie and Advanced Plant Habitat—closed LED chambers with precise environmental control, using capillary-based watering and porous media (arcillite, terra cotta).

---

### Question 3
What is a Closed Ecological Life Support System (CELSS)?

A) Storage container
B) Self-sustaining system recycling air, water, and waste through biological processes (plants, algae, microbes)
C) Air conditioner
D) Food freezer

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CELSS integrates plants, algae, and microbes to regenerate oxygen, purify water, decompose waste, and produce food—minimizing resupply needs for long-duration missions.

---

### Question 4
What percentage closure (recycling efficiency) is targeted for lunar or Mars habitats?

A) 10-20%
B) 50-60%
C) 80-95% closure for water/oxygen, less for food
D) 100% perfect closure

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Practical CELSS targets 80-95% closure for air/water, with some resupply acceptable. 100% closure is theoretically possible but complex and failure-prone.

---

### Question 5
What crop has been successfully grown and consumed by astronauts on the ISS?

A) Wheat
B) Lettuce, radishes, chili peppers, and tomatoes
C) Corn
D) Nothing has been grown

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISS astronauts have grown and eaten multiple crops: red romaine lettuce (2015), radishes (2020), chili peppers (2021), demonstrating space food production feasibility.

---

### Question 6
What is regolith and can it be used for plant growth?

A) A type of spacecraft
B) Lunar or Martian soil; can potentially support plant growth with amendments (nutrients, pH adjustment, microbial inoculation)
C) Pure poison to plants
D) Rocket fuel

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Regolith is loose surface material on Moon/Mars. Studies show plants can grow in lunar/Martian regolith simulants with proper nutrients, pH buffering, and beneficial microbes.

---

### Question 7
How do lessons from space agriculture benefit Earth-based food production?

A) No benefits
B) Advanced CEA technologies (LED efficiency, nutrient recycling, automation) developed for space apply to vertical farming
C) Only useful in space
D) Make Earth farming worse

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Space agriculture drives innovation in resource efficiency, precision control, crop selection, and system monitoring applicable to Earth's vertical farms and sustainable agriculture.

---

### Question 8
What is the estimated timeline for establishing permanent lunar agricultural systems?

A) Already exists
B) 2025-2027
C) 2030-2040 with Artemis program lunar bases
D) Never possible

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's Artemis program targets lunar base establishment 2030+, with agricultural systems (greenhouses, CELSS) following as permanent presence develops.

---

### Question 9
What energy source would most likely power Martian greenhouses?

A) Wood fires
B) Solar panels (abundant sunlight, no atmosphere) with battery storage
C) Wind turbines only
D) No energy available

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars receives sufficient solar energy (43% of Earth's, but no atmospheric losses); solar + batteries would likely power greenhouses until nuclear reactors are established.

### Question 10
Which company is actively developing space agriculture technologies?

A) McDonald's
B) SpaceX, Redwire Space (EAGLEWORKS), NASA partnerships with universities
C) No companies involved
D) Only government programs

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Multiple entities develop space agriculture: NASA internal + university partnerships, private companies like Redwire Space (EAGLEWORKS greenhouse), SpaceX mission support, and startups.
