## Deliverables

### 1. Written Report (3,000-4,000 words)

#### Section 1: Executive Summary (250 words)
- Technology overview
- Key findings
- Strategic recommendation
- Investment thesis (if applicable)

#### Section 2: Technology Overview (500-700 words)
- **What is it?** Clear explanation accessible to non-experts
- **How does it work?** Simplified technical description
- **Current state:** TRL assessment, leading companies/research groups
- **Key innovations:** What makes this technology different/better?

#### Section 3: Market Analysis (600-800 words)
- **Target market:** Who will buy this product/technology?
- **Market size:** TAM, SAM, SOM analysis (Total/Serviceable/Obtainable)
- **Competition:** Incumbent alternatives and emerging competitors
- **Value proposition:** Why would customers adopt this?
- **Pricing strategy:** Premium vs. parity positioning
- **Go-to-market:** Distribution channels, partnerships

#### Section 4: Technical Feasibility (700-900 words)
- **Technology maturity:** Detailed TRL breakdown
- **Key technical challenges:** What must be solved?
- **Solutions in development:** How are challenges being addressed?
- **Scale-up pathway:** Lab → Pilot → Commercial scale
- **Infrastructure requirements:** Facilities, equipment, expertise
- **Timeline:** Realistic pathway to commercial viability

#### Section 5: Regulatory & Safety (400-600 words)
- **Regulatory framework:** FDA, EFSA, others as relevant
- **Approval pathway:** GRAS, Novel Food, specific regulations
- **Safety considerations:** Toxicology, allergenicity, environmental
- **Timeline to approval:** Estimated duration
- **Labeling requirements:** How will product be labeled?

#### Section 6: Sustainability & Impact (400-600 words)
- **Environmental assessment:** GHG, water, land use vs. conventional
- **Social considerations:** Labor, equity, cultural acceptance
- **Ethical concerns:** Animal welfare, GMO, naturalness
- **Lifecycle analysis:** Cradle-to-grave impact

#### Section 7: Economic Analysis (500-700 words)
- **Cost structure:** CAPEX and OPEX breakdown
- **Cost reduction roadmap:** How will costs decline over time?
- **Price parity analysis:** When will it compete with conventional?
- **Funding requirements:** Capital needed for commercialization
- **Revenue model:** How does the company make money?

#### Section 8: Strategic Recommendations (300-500 words)
- **For Startups:** Should they pursue this technology? Key success factors?
- **For Investors:** Invest now, wait, or pass? Risk/return profile?
- **For Incumbents:** Partner, acquire, build in-house, or ignore?
- **For Policymakers:** Support, regulate, or restrict?

#### Section 9: Risk Assessment (300-400 words)
- **Technical risks:** What could fail technically?
- **Market risks:** What if customers don't adopt?
- **Regulatory risks:** What if approvals are delayed/denied?
- **Financial risks:** What if costs don't decline as expected?
- **Mitigation strategies:** How to address each risk?

#### Section 10: Conclusion (150-200 words)
- Summary of key findings
- Final verdict on technology potential
- Recommended next steps

### 2. Visual Presentation (10-12 slides)

Create a slide deck suitable for presenting your findings to:
- Executive team
- Investors
- Board of directors
- Academic conference

#### Required Slides:
1. Title slide with technology name and your name
2. Technology overview (with diagram/image)
3. Market size and opportunity
4. Competitive landscape
5. Technical roadmap (TRL progression)
6. Cost reduction pathway (chart)
7. Regulatory timeline
8. Environmental impact comparison (chart/visual)
9. Risk matrix
10. Investment thesis / Strategic recommendation
11. Timeline to commercialization
12. Conclusion and next steps

## Example Technology Assessment Outline

### Example: Precision Fermentation for Dairy Proteins

**Executive Summary:**
Precision fermentation enables production of dairy proteins (casein, whey) identical to cow-derived proteins using engineered yeast. Leading companies (Perfect Day, Remilk, Imagindairy) have achieved commercial production with GRAS approval. Cost trajectory suggests price parity by 2028-2030. Environmental benefits include 90-95% reduction in GHG, water, and land use. Key challenges: scaling fermentation infrastructure and downstream processing costs. Recommendation: High-potential investment with 5-7 year horizon to mainstream adoption.
