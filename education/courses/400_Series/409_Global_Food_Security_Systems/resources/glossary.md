# Glossary: Global Food Security Systems

**Course 409 - Expert/Professional Level**

This glossary provides definitions for key terms used throughout the course. Terms are organized alphabetically with cross-references where relevant.

---

## A

**Accessibility** - The ability of individuals and populations to obtain food through physical proximity, economic means, and social inclusion without barriers or discrimination.

**Adaptive Capacity** - The ability of systems, institutions, humans, and other organisms to adjust to potential damage, take advantage of opportunities, or respond to consequences from climate change or other stresses.

**Adaptive Management** - A structured, iterative approach to decision-making in the face of uncertainty, with an aim to reduce uncertainty over time through system monitoring and adjustment of strategies based on learning.

**Aeroponics** - A method of growing plants in which roots are suspended in air and misted with nutrient solution, using minimal water. See also: *Controlled Environment Agriculture, Hydroponics*.

**Agrivoltaics** - The co-location of agriculture and solar photovoltaic panels, allowing simultaneous food production and renewable energy generation on the same land area.

**Anemia** - A condition in which the blood has a lower than normal number of red blood cells or hemoglobin, often resulting from iron deficiency. A key indicator of nutritional status, particularly in women of reproductive age.

**Anthropometry** - The measurement of the human body, used in nutrition to assess physical development and nutritional status, particularly in children (height, weight, mid-upper arm circumference).

**Aquaponics** - An integrated food production system combining aquaculture (fish farming) and hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation) in a symbiotic environment. Fish waste provides nutrients for plants; plants filter water for fish. See also: *Controlled Environment Agriculture*.

---

## B

**Beneficiary** - An individual, household, or community that receives benefits from a development program or intervention.

**Biofortification** - The process of increasing the nutritional value of crops through selective breeding, genetic modification, or agronomic practices to address micronutrient deficiencies.

**Blended Finance** - The strategic use of development finance and philanthropic funds to mobilize private capital flows to emerging and frontier markets, combining concessional and commercial capital.

**BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer)** - A form of public-private partnership in which a private entity builds and operates infrastructure for a concession period before transferring ownership to the government. See also: *Public-Private Partnership*.

**Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)** - The ratio of total benefits to total costs of a project or intervention, used in economic analysis to assess value for money. A BCR > 1 indicates benefits exceed costs.

---

## C

**Capacity Building** - The process of developing and strengthening the skills, instincts, abilities, processes, and resources that organizations and communities need to survive, adapt, and thrive.

**CAPEX (Capital Expenditure)** - Funds used to acquire, upgrade, and maintain physical assets such as property, buildings, or equipment. In CEA, includes greenhouse structures, growing systems, and climate control equipment.

**Climate-Smart Agriculture** - An approach to developing agricultural systems that sustainably increase productivity and resilience while reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

**Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA)** - Food production in structures that provide varying levels of environmental control (temperature, humidity, light, water, nutrients) to optimize plant growth. Includes greenhouses, vertical farms, and indoor growing facilities. See also: *Vertical Farming, Hydroponics*.

**Community Supported Agriculture (CSA)** - A food distribution model in which consumers purchase shares of a farm's harvest in advance, sharing risks and rewards with farmers.

**Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)** - A systematic approach to estimating the strengths and weaknesses of alternatives by comparing total expected costs against total expected benefits, including non-market values.

---

## D

**DALY (Disability-Adjusted Life Year)** - A measure of overall disease burden, expressed as the number of years lost due to ill-health, disability, or early death. Used to quantify health impacts.

**Dietary Diversity Score (DDS)** - An indicator measuring the variety of foods or food groups consumed over a specified period, used as a proxy for micronutrient adequacy of diet.

**Disaggregated Data** - Data broken down by relevant categories such as gender, age, income level, ethnicity, or location to enable analysis of differential impacts and inequalities. Essential for equity analysis.

**Do No Harm** - A principle requiring that interventions assess and mitigate potential negative impacts on people and communities, ensuring actions do not inadvertently cause harm or worsen situations.

---

## E

**EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, Amortization)** - A measure of operational profitability, calculated as revenue minus operating expenses (excluding depreciation, interest, and taxes).

**Enabling Environment** - The set of interconnected conditions - including laws, regulations, policies, resource allocations, and social norms - that enable or hinder progress toward objectives.

**Equity** - Fairness and justice in the distribution of benefits, resources, and opportunities. In food security, ensuring vulnerable and marginalized populations are prioritized and benefit proportionally or preferentially.

**Evapotranspiration** - The process by which water is transferred from land to the atmosphere through evaporation from soil and other surfaces and transpiration from plants.

**Exit Strategy** - A plan for concluding an intervention or transferring responsibilities to local actors, ensuring sustainability beyond external support.

---

## F

**FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization)** - United Nations agency leading international efforts to defeat hunger, improve nutrition and food security. Maintains global food security data and provides technical assistance.

**FIES (Food Insecurity Experience Scale)** - An experience-based metric of food insecurity severity, using an 8-item questionnaire to categorize households into food secure, mild, moderate, or severe food insecurity.

**Food Desert** - An area, particularly in urban settings, with limited access to affordable and nutritious food, typically defined by distance from full-service grocery stores and reliance on convenience stores or fast food.

**Food Miles** - The distance food travels from production to consumption, used as an indicator of environmental impact (transportation emissions) and freshness.

**Food Safety** - Assurance that food will not cause harm to consumers when prepared and consumed according to its intended use. Involves hazard identification, prevention, and control.

**Food Security** - A situation that exists when all people, at all times, have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. Based on four pillars: *Availability, Access, Utilization, Stability*.

**Food Sovereignty** - The right of peoples to healthy and culturally appropriate food produced through ecologically sound and sustainable methods, and their right to define their own food and agriculture systems.

---

## G

**Gender-Transformative Approach** - An approach that addresses the root causes of gender inequality, challenges harmful gender norms and power imbalances, and promotes equitable gender relations. Contrasts with gender-blind or gender-accommodating approaches.

**GHI (Global Hunger Index)** - A composite measure of hunger combining undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting, and child mortality. Scored 0-100, with higher scores indicating more severe hunger.

**Globalization** - The process of interaction and integration among people, companies, and governments worldwide, particularly through trade, investment, and technology. Relevant to food systems through international trade and technology transfer.

**Greenhouse** - A structure with walls and roof primarily of transparent material (glass or plastic) in which plants are grown under controlled or semi-controlled conditions. May use natural light only or supplemental artificial lighting. See also: *Controlled Environment Agriculture*.

**Gross Margin** - The difference between revenue and direct costs of production, expressed as percentage of revenue. Indicates core profitability before fixed costs and overhead.

---

## H

**HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points)** - A systematic preventive approach to food safety, identifying physical, chemical, and biological hazards in production processes and defining measures to reduce risks.

**Hidden Hunger** - A form of malnutrition caused by micronutrient deficiencies (vitamins and minerals), which may not manifest as traditional hunger but causes serious health consequences. Affects 2+ billion people globally.

**Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS)** - A measurement tool assessing food insecurity at household level through a 9-item questionnaire about food access behaviors and experiences.

**Hub-and-Spoke Model** - A system with a central facility (hub) providing specialized services and coordination, connected to multiple distributed facilities (spokes) providing frontline services. Used in technical support networks.

**Hydroponics** - A method of growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions in water. Plant roots are supported by inert media (rockwool, perlite, coco coir) or suspended in solution. See also: *Controlled Environment Agriculture, Aeroponics, Aquaponics*.

---

## I

**Impact Investment** - Investments made with the intention to generate positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside financial return. Typically accepts below-market or market-rate returns for mission alignment.

**IPC (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification)** - A standardized scale for classifying severity of acute food insecurity and malnutrition, ranging from Phase 1 (minimal) to Phase 5 (catastrophic/famine).

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return)** - The discount rate that makes the net present value of all cash flows from a project equal to zero, representing the effective annual return on investment.

**Intersectionality** - The interconnected nature of social categorizations such as gender, race, class, and disability, creating overlapping and interdependent systems of discrimination or disadvantage.

---

## K

**Knowledge Transfer** - The process of transferring knowledge, skills, technologies, and methods from those who have them to those who need them. Includes both explicit knowledge (codified) and tacit knowledge (experiential).

---

## L

**LED (Light-Emitting Diode)** - Energy-efficient solid-state lighting used in controlled environment agriculture, with spectrum tunable to plant requirements. Critical technology for vertical farming and supplemental greenhouse lighting.

**Livelihood** - The capabilities, assets (including material and social resources), and activities required for a means of living. A livelihood is sustainable when it can cope with and recover from stresses and shocks.

---

## M

**Malnutrition** - Abnormal nutritional status resulting from inadequate, excessive, or imbalanced intake of nutrients. Includes undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, and overweight/obesity.

**Micronutrients** - Vitamins and minerals required in small quantities but essential for proper growth and development. Deficiencies (hidden hunger) affect billions globally.

**M&E (Monitoring and Evaluation)** - Systematic processes to assess project performance and impact. Monitoring tracks ongoing progress; evaluation assesses overall effectiveness and results.

---

## N

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)** - A hydroponic method in which a thin film of nutrient solution continuously flows over plant roots suspended in channels, providing oxygen and nutrients.

**NGO (Non-Governmental Organization)** - A nonprofit organization operating independently of government, typically focused on humanitarian, development, environmental, or advocacy objectives.

**NPV (Net Present Value)** - The difference between the present value of cash inflows and outflows over time, used to analyze profitability of investments. Positive NPV indicates a good investment.

**Nutrition Transition** - The shift in dietary patterns from traditional foods to more processed foods, refined grains, added sugars, and animal products, associated with economic development and urbanization.

---

## O

**OPEX (Operating Expenditure)** - Ongoing costs for running a business or facility, including labor, energy, materials, maintenance, and administration. Contrasts with capital expenditure (CAPEX).

**Output-Based Aid (OBA)** - A development financing mechanism that pays service providers based on verified delivery of outputs or outcomes rather than inputs, transferring performance risk to providers.

---

## P

**Peri-Urban Agriculture** - Agricultural production in areas surrounding urban centers, typically 10-50 km from city core, balancing land costs with proximity to markets.

**PoU (Prevalence of Undernourishment)** - The percentage of population whose dietary energy consumption is below the minimum requirement for maintaining a normal, active life. Key FAO food security indicator.

**PPP (Public-Private Partnership)** - A cooperative arrangement between government and private sector for financing, designing, implementing, and operating facilities and services traditionally provided by the public sector.

**Precision Agriculture** - Farming management based on observing, measuring, and responding to variability within fields and between plants, often using sensors, data analytics, and automation.

---

## R

**Recirculating System** - A hydroponic or aquaponic system in which water and nutrients are recycled through the system rather than discharged, achieving 90-95% water use efficiency.

**Resilience** - The capacity of systems, communities, or individuals to cope with hazards and disturbances, maintaining essential functions, identity, and structure while adapting and transforming when necessary.

**ROI (Return on Investment)** - A performance measure evaluating efficiency of investment, calculated as (Net Profit / Total Investment) × 100, expressed as percentage.

---

## S

**Safeguards** - Policies and procedures to identify, avoid, minimize, or mitigate adverse environmental and social impacts of projects. Major development institutions require environmental and social safeguards.

**Scenario Planning** - A strategic planning method that develops multiple plausible future scenarios based on critical uncertainties, used to test strategies and build adaptive capacity.

**SDG (Sustainable Development Goals)** - Set of 17 global goals adopted by UN member states in 2015, to be achieved by 2030. SDG 2 specifically addresses zero hunger and sustainable agriculture.

**Smallholder Farmer** - A farmer cultivating a small plot of land (typically <2 hectares), often relying primarily on family labor. Smallholders produce much of the world's food, especially in developing countries.

**Social Enterprise** - An organization that applies commercial strategies to maximize improvements in human and environmental well-being, balancing financial sustainability with social mission.

**Social Return on Investment (SROI)** - A framework for measuring and accounting for social, environmental, and economic value created by an intervention, expressed as ratio of benefits to investment (e.g., 3:1).

**SROI (Social Return on Investment)** - See *Social Return on Investment*.

**Stakeholder** - An individual, group, or organization that has interest or concern in a project or decision, either affecting or being affected by activities.

**Stunting** - Impaired growth and development in children resulting from poor nutrition, repeated infection, and inadequate psychosocial stimulation. Measured as height-for-age below -2 standard deviations from median.

**Sustainability** - Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Encompasses environmental, economic, and social dimensions.

---

## T

**Technology Transfer** - The process of transferring skills, knowledge, technologies, and methods from one organization, context, or country to another, enabling adoption and adaptation.

**Theory of Change** - A comprehensive description of how and why desired change is expected to happen, mapping pathways from inputs through activities, outputs, and outcomes to ultimate impact.

---

## U

**Undernourishment** - Condition of insufficient dietary energy intake to meet continuous basic energy requirements, lasting at least one year. Measured as Prevalence of Undernourishment (PoU).

**Urban Agriculture** - Growing, processing, and distributing food in or around urban areas, including rooftop gardens, community gardens, and controlled environment agriculture.

**Urbanization** - Increasing share of a nation's population living in urban areas, resulting from rural-to-urban migration and natural population growth. Global population projected to be 68% urban by 2050.

---

## V

**Vertical Farming** - Practice of growing crops in vertically stacked layers, often in controlled indoor environments using artificial lighting (typically LEDs). Maximizes production per unit floor area. See also: *Controlled Environment Agriculture*.

**Viability Gap Funding** - Public financial support to make socially or strategically important but commercially unviable projects financially feasible, bridging the "gap" between costs and revenues.

**Vulnerability** - Propensity or predisposition to be adversely affected by hazards. Encompasses susceptibility to harm and lack of capacity to cope and adapt. Food insecurity is a form of vulnerability.

---

## W

**WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene)** - Collective term for water access, sanitation facilities, and hygiene practices. Critical for food utilization and nutrition outcomes.

**Wasting** - Body weight inadequate for height in children, indicating recent and severe weight loss or failure to gain weight. Measured as weight-for-height below -2 standard deviations. A form of acute malnutrition.

**WFP (World Food Programme)** - United Nations agency providing food assistance in emergencies and supporting development programs. Largest humanitarian organization addressing hunger and food security globally.
