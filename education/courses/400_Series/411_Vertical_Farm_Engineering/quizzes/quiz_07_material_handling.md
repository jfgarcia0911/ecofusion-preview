## Questions

### Question 1
What is the primary advantage of Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) in vertical farm operations?

A) Lower initial capital cost than manual carts
B) Reduced labor costs and consistent material flow for repetitive transport tasks
C) Elimination of all human workers
D) No maintenance requirements

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AGVs reduce labor for repetitive transport tasks, provide consistent timing and reliability, and free workers for higher-value activities, despite higher initial costs.

---

### Question 2
For a facility harvesting 500 kg/day with 10 harvests/day, what is the minimum AGV fleet size if each AGV carries 50 kg and completes 2 trips/hour during a 10-hour shift?

A) 1 AGV
B) 2 AGVs
C) 3 AGVs
D) 5 AGVs

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Each harvest = 50 kg. Each AGV: 2 trips/hour × 10 hours × 50 kg = 1,000 kg capacity/day. Need 500 kg ÷ 1,000 kg/AGV = 0.5 AGVs minimum, but with 10 harvests requiring transport timing, need 2 AGVs for operational flexibility.

### Question 3
What is the recommended maximum vertical lift height for manual material handling without mechanical assistance?

A) 30 inches (76 cm)
B) 50 inches (127 cm)
C) 72 inches (183 cm)
D) 96 inches (244 cm)

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Ergonomic guidelines recommend 72 inches (183 cm or ~6 ft) as the maximum height for repetitive manual lifting to prevent worker injury and fatigue.

### Question 4
Which conveyor type is best suited for transporting harvested produce between different elevations in a vertical farm?

A) Flat belt conveyor
B) Incline belt conveyor or spiral conveyor
C) Roller conveyor
D) Pneumatic conveyor

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Incline or spiral conveyors are designed for elevation changes, gently transporting delicate produce between levels while maintaining product quality.

### Question 5
What percentage of a vertical farm's floor space is typically dedicated to material handling aisles and circulation?

A) 5-10%
B) 15-25%
C) 35-45%
D) 50-60%

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Well-designed facilities allocate 15-25% to aisles and circulation, balancing accessibility with maximizing growing area (typically 60-70% of floor space).

### Question 6
For a three-zone facility (propagation, grow, harvest/pack), what material flow pattern minimizes cross-contamination risk?

A) Circular flow with multiple entry points
B) Linear one-way flow from propagation → grow → harvest/pack
C) Bidirectional flow based on convenience
D) Centralized hub with radial spokes

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Linear one-way flow prevents backtracking and cross-contamination between dirty (incoming) and clean (harvested product) zones, essential for food safety.

---

### Question 7
Calculate the required elevator capacity (trips/hour) for a 6-level facility producing 1,000 kg/day harvested over 8 hours, with elevator capacity of 100 kg/trip:

A) 5 trips/hour
B) 10 trips/hour
C) 13 trips/hour
D) 20 trips/hour

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Daily harvest: 1,000 kg ÷ 8 hours = 125 kg/hour. Trips needed: 125 kg/hour ÷ 100 kg/trip = 1.25 trips/hour per level × 6 levels ÷ 2 (efficiency) ≈ 13 trips/hour for all levels.

---

### Question 8
What is the primary waste stream in vertical farms that requires dedicated handling systems?

A) Plastic packaging
B) Root masses and plant waste
C) Chemical containers
D) Office paper

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Root masses, trim waste, and unmarketable plants constitute the largest waste stream (by volume and weight), often requiring composting, anaerobic digestion, or disposal systems.

### Question 9
For automated seeding lines, what production rate (seeds/hour) is typical for commercial systems?

A) 500-1,000 seeds/hour
B) 2,000-5,000 seeds/hour
C) 10,000-20,000 seeds/hour
D) 50,000-100,000 seeds/hour

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Modern automated seeding systems achieve 10,000-20,000 seeds/hour with precision placement, vacuum pickup, and vision systems for quality control.

---

### Question 10
What is the recommended strategy for sizing material handling equipment in vertical farms?

A) Minimum capacity to reduce costs
B) Size for average daily production
C) Size for peak production with 20-30% buffer capacity
D) Maximum possible capacity regardless of need

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sizing for peak production plus a buffer (20-30%) ensures systems can handle production spikes, equipment downtime, and future growth without becoming bottlenecks.
