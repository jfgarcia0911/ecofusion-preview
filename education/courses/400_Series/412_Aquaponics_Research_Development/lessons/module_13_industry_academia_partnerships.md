# Module 13: Industry-Academia Partnerships

## Learning Objectives

- Establish collaborative research models with industry
- Design industry-sponsored research protocols
- Navigate technology licensing and commercialization
- Apply participatory research approaches
- Develop effective knowledge exchange mechanisms

## 13.1 Collaborative Research Models

### Types of Industry-Academia Collaboration

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         COLLABORATION SPECTRUM                         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

LOW ENGAGEMENT                           HIGH ENGAGEMENT
     │                                           │
     ▼                                           ▼
┌─────────┐  ┌─────────┐  ┌─────────┐  ┌────────────┐
│Consult- │  │Contract │  │Sponsored│  │Strategic   │
│ ing     │→ │Research │→ │Research │→ │Partnership │
└─────────┘  └─────────┘  └─────────┘  └────────────┘

1. CONSULTING:
   Duration: Days to weeks
   Payment: Fee-for-service
   IP: Company retains
   Example: "Review our system design and provide
            recommendations"

2. CONTRACT RESEARCH:
   Duration: 3-12 months
   Payment: Project-based
   IP: Negotiated (often company owns)
   Example: "Test effectiveness of our new biofilter
            media in controlled conditions"

3. SPONSORED RESEARCH:
   Duration: 1-3 years
   Payment: Research budget + overhead
   IP: University owns, company has license option
   Example: "Investigate optimal stocking density for
            our target species with 18-month study"

4. STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIP:
   Duration: 3-10 years
   Payment: Major funding + in-kind support
   IP: Shared ownership, joint commercialization
   Example: "Co-develop next generation aquaponics
            technology with $5M over 5 years"
```

## 13.2 Industry-Sponsored Research Protocols

### Research Agreement Terms

```
KEY CONTRACTUAL ELEMENTS:

STATEMENT OF WORK (SOW):
├── Research objectives (specific, measurable)
├── Methodology and approach
├── Deliverables and timeline
├── Roles and responsibilities
└── Success criteria

BUDGET AND PAYMENT:
├── Total project cost
├── Payment schedule (milestone-based typical)
├── Allowable costs
├── F&A (overhead) rate
└── Budget modification process

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS:

BACKGROUND IP:
"All intellectual property owned by either party prior
to this agreement remains with that party."

FOREGROUND IP (Generated during project):

Option 1 - University Ownership:
"University owns all foreground IP. Company receives
first option to negotiate exclusive license with
standard terms: $X upfront, Y% royalties, minimum
annual payments of $Z."

Option 2 - Joint Ownership:
"Foreground IP jointly owned. Both parties may use
for research. Commercialization requires mutual
agreement. Revenue split 60% university / 40% company."

Option 3 - Company Ownership (less common):
"Company owns foreground IP. University retains right
to use for research and teaching purposes."

PUBLICATION RIGHTS:
"University may publish results following:
 1. 60-day review period for company
 2. Removal of confidential information
 3. 6-month delay if patent filing needed
 Company cannot unreasonably withhold approval."

CONFIDENTIALITY:
├── Term: During project + 3 years after
├── Scope: Technical data, business information
├── Exclusions: Public domain, independently developed
└── Return/destruction at project end
```

### Managing Conflicts of Interest

```
POTENTIAL CONFLICTS:

FINANCIAL:
├── Equity in sponsor company
├── Consulting fees from sponsor
├── Royalty payments
└── Stock options

COMMITMENT:
├── Time devoted to sponsored work
├── Student thesis topics (sponsor influence)
├── Publication delays
└── Direction of research program

DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS:

Academic Institution:
├── Annual financial disclosure
├── Prior to proposal submission
├── When conflict arises
└── Update within 30 days of change

Research Subjects (if applicable):
└── Inform participants of sponsor relationship

Students:
└── Alternative thesis options must exist

MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES:

ACCEPTABLE:
├── Consulting <20% time
├── Equity <$10,000 or <5% company
├── Publication rights maintained
└── Independent oversight committee

REQUIRES MANAGEMENT PLAN:
├── Equity >$10,000
├── Board membership
├── Licensing to company with investigator equity
└── Family member employment by sponsor

PROHIBITED:
├── Research decisions controlled by sponsor
├── Student exploitation
├── Suppression of negative results
└── Undisclosed conflicts
```

## 13.3 Technology Licensing and Commercialization

### Licensing Process

```
TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER PATHWAY:

STEP 1: INVENTION DISCLOSURE
Researcher submits disclosure to TTO
├── Description of invention
├── Inventors listed
├── Potential applications
├── Commercial interest (if any)
└── Publications/presentations (pending or past)

STEP 2: EVALUATION
TTO assesses (2-4 weeks)
├── Novelty and prior art search
├── Patent potential
├── Market size estimation
└── Development requirements

DECISION:
├── Proceed with patenting ($10K-$50K investment)
└── Publish without patent (most common)

STEP 3: PROTECTION
If patenting:
├── File provisional application (12-month window)
├── Continue research and development
├── Market technology to potential licensees
└── File utility patent before provisional expires

STEP 4: MARKETING
TTO identifies potential licensees
├── Existing industry partners
├── Relevant companies (cold outreach)
├── Startups (formed around technology)
└── Inventors' networks

STEP 5: NEGOTIATION
License terms discussion
├── Exclusive vs. non-exclusive
├── Field of use limitations
├── Geographic territory
├── Financial terms
└── Diligence requirements

STEP 6: LICENSE EXECUTION
Agreement signed
├── License fee: $10K-$100K (if exclusive)
├── Milestone payments: $50K-$500K total
├── Royalties: 1-10% of net sales
├── Minimum royalties: $5K-$25K annually
└── Patent cost reimbursement

STEP 7: COMMERCIALIZATION
Licensee develops product
├── Further R&D (often collaborative)
├── Regulatory approvals
├── Manufacturing scale-up
└── Market launch (5-10 years typical)
```

### Startup Formation

```
ACADEMIC ENTREPRENEUR PATH:

DECISION FACTORS:

PROCEED WITH STARTUP IF:
├── No existing company suitable
├── Technology requires hands-on development
├── Large market opportunity (>$100M)
├── Team has business interest
└── University supportive

PROS:
├── Control over development
├── Greater potential returns
├── Hands-on impact
└── Entrepreneurial experience

CONS:
├── High risk (90% fail)
├── Time commitment (>20 hrs/week)
├── Conflicts of interest management
├── Funding challenges
└── Leaving academia possible

TYPICAL STRUCTURE:

FOUNDING TEAM:
├── Technical founder (inventor, CTO)
├── Business founder (CEO)
├── Advisors (industry experts)
└── Initial employees (2-5)

CAPITALIZATION:
├── Founder equity: 50-80%
├── University equity: 1-5%
├── Angel investors: 10-30%
└── Advisors: 1-5%

FUNDING STAGES:

FRIENDS & FAMILY: $25K-$100K
└── Proof of concept

ANGEL ROUND: $250K-$1M
└── Product development

SEED ROUND: $1M-$3M
└── Pilot demonstrations

SERIES A: $5M-$15M
└── Commercial launch

COMPANY FORMATION STEPS:

1. Business model development (3-6 months)
2. Incorporate (LLC or C-Corp)
3. Negotiate license from university
4. Secure initial funding
5. Hire key personnel
6. Develop prototype
7. Pilot customers
8. Scale production
9. Series A funding (if successful)
10. Growth and potential exit
```

## 13.4 Participatory Research Approaches

### On-Farm Research Methodology

```
PARTICIPATORY ACTION RESEARCH (PAR):

PRINCIPLES:
├── Farmers/producers as co-researchers
├── Research addresses practical problems
├── Iterative learning and adaptation
├── Local knowledge integration
└── Immediate implementation

PROCESS:

1. PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION (Farmer-led)
   Example: "Our lettuce shows iron deficiency
            symptoms but we don't know optimal
            chelated iron supplementation rate"

2. COLLABORATIVE DESIGN
   Researcher + Farmer together:
   ├── Define treatments (5 iron levels)
   ├── Determine replication (4 systems available)
   ├── Select measurements (visual scores, yield, tissue Fe)
   ├── Set timeline (8-week crop cycle)
   └── Assign responsibilities

3. IMPLEMENTATION
   ├── Farmer: Day-to-day management, visual observations
   ├── Researcher: Sample collection, lab analysis
   └── Joint: Data recording, system monitoring

4. DATA ANALYSIS
   ├── Statistical analysis (researcher-led)
   ├── Economic analysis (joint)
   └── Practical interpretation (farmer-led)

5. DISSEMINATION
   ├── Scientific publication (researcher)
   ├── Field day demonstration (farmer host)
   ├── Extension bulletin (joint authorship)
   └── Grower network sharing (farmer)

BENEFITS:
├── Realistic conditions (not controlled lab)
├── Farmer buy-in and adoption
├── Rapid iteration and refinement
├── Local adaptation
└── Relationship building

CHALLENGES:
├── Less experimental control
├── Confounding variables
├── Time coordination
├── Publication in high-impact journals
└── Replication across diverse farms
```

## 13.5 Knowledge Exchange Mechanisms

### Two-Way Communication

```
INDUSTRY TO ACADEMIA:

PROBLEM-DRIVEN RESEARCH:
Industry identifies production challenges
└→ Academics design research to address

EXAMPLE:
"We're seeing 30% mortality in catfish during transport
to processing. Can you investigate pre-harvest stress
mitigation strategies?"

Academic response:
├── Literature review on transport stress
├── Pilot study testing fasting duration
├── Develop best practices guide
└── Train industry partners

FACILITIES ACCESS:
Commercial farms provide research sites
├── Benefits to industry: Free technical support
├── Benefits to academia: Realistic conditions
└── Agreement: Data sharing, publication rights

TECHNICAL ADVISORY BOARDS:
Industry representatives advise academic programs
├── Curriculum relevance
├── Research priorities
├── Student internship placement
└── Equipment donations

ACADEMIA TO INDUSTRY:

EXTENSION PUBLICATIONS:
├── Fact sheets (2-4 pages)
├── Production guides (20-50 pages)
├── Economic analyses
└── Troubleshooting guides

TECHNICAL REPORTS:
└── Detailed project results before publication

TRAINING PROGRAMS:
├── Short courses (2-5 days)
├── Certificate programs (online, 3-6 months)
├── Graduate internships
└── Postdoc industry placements
```

### Measuring Impact

```
METRICS FOR PARTNERSHIP SUCCESS:

RESEARCH OUTPUTS:
├── Peer-reviewed publications (quantity, impact factor)
├── Conference presentations
├── Patents filed/granted
├── Datasets released
└── Trained personnel (MS, PhD graduated)

TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER:
├── Licenses executed
├── Startup companies formed
├── Products commercialized
├── Revenue generated (licensing, royalties)
└── Jobs created

INDUSTRY OUTCOMES:
├── Practices adopted (# farms, % adopting)
├── Productivity improvements (%, kg/m²)
├── Cost reductions ($, % of production cost)
├── Environmental benefits (water saved, emissions reduced)
└── Economic impact ($value to industry)

EXAMPLE IMPACT STATEMENT:

"Over the 5-year partnership between University X and
Company Y:

- 12 peer-reviewed papers published (avg IF 3.8)
- 2 patents granted, 1 licensed to Company Y
- Novel biofilter technology reduced startup time from
  6 weeks to 3 weeks in commercial trials (n=8 farms)
- Estimated industry savings: $2.3M annually
- 15 farms adopted technology (22% of regional growers)
- 3 PhD and 5 MS students graduated, 6 employed by
  aquaponics companies
- Follow-on grant funding: $1.8M from USDA-NIFA"

QUALITATIVE IMPACTS:
├── Strengthened relationships
├── Enhanced reputation
├── Student career opportunities
├── Industry professionalization
└── Public awareness and support
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Clear Agreements** - Establish detailed contracts addressing IP, publication, and responsibilities
2. **Mutual Benefit** - Ensure both partners gain value from collaboration
3. **Communication** - Maintain regular, transparent dialogue
4. **Conflict Management** - Disclose and manage conflicts of interest proactively
5. **Impact Focus** - Measure and communicate practical outcomes

## Practical Application

Design an industry-academia partnership:
1. Identify a potential industry partner and research need
2. Outline collaboration model (consulting vs. strategic partnership)
3. Draft statement of work with deliverables
4. Develop IP and publication terms
5. Create communication plan and governance structure
6. Define success metrics
