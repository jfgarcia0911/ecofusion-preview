## Questions

### Question 1
What is a nutrient budget in aquaponics research?

A) The financial cost of fertilizers
B) A quantitative accounting of nutrient inputs, outputs, and transformations in the system
C) A shopping list for nutrients
D) The weight of fish feed

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Nutrient budgets track all nutrient flows (inputs from feed, outputs via harvest/water changes, transformations, accumulation) to understand nutrient cycling efficiency and identify losses.

---

### Question 2
In nitrogen budget studies, fish typically retain what percentage of nitrogen from feed?

A) 5-10%
B) 20-30%
C) 50-60%
D) 80-90%

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Fish typically retain only 20-30% of feed nitrogen in body tissue, with 70-80% excreted as ammonia and organic nitrogen, available for plant uptake after nitrification.

---

### Question 3
What isotope tracing technique is commonly used to track nitrogen transformations in aquaponics?

A) Carbon-14 labeling
B) Nitrogen-15 (¹⁵N) stable isotope tracing
C) Phosphorus-32 radioactive tracing
D) Oxygen-18 labeling

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ¹⁵N is a stable (non-radioactive) isotope used to trace nitrogen through feed → fish → ammonia → nitrite → nitrate → plant pathways, revealing transformation rates and efficiency.

---

### Question 4
What is the primary form of phosphorus excreted by fish?

A) Phosphate (PO₄³⁻) in dissolved form
B) Organic phosphorus in feces (solid form)
C) Phosphine gas
D) Phosphorus is not excreted

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Unlike nitrogen (mainly dissolved ammonia), most phosphorus is excreted in feces as organic and inorganic particles, requiring solids mineralization for plant availability.

---

### Question 5
What process converts organic nitrogen in fish waste to plant-available forms?

A) Photosynthesis
B) Mineralization (decomposition) followed by nitrification
C) Evaporation
D) Precipitation

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Organic nitrogen is first mineralized (decomposed to ammonia by heterotrophic bacteria), then nitrified to nitrate by autotrophic bacteria, making it plant-available.

---

### Question 6
In plant nutrient uptake kinetics research, what does the Km value represent?

A) Maximum uptake rate
B) The substrate concentration at which uptake rate is half-maximal
C) Plant height
D) Total nutrient content

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Km (Michaelis constant) is the substrate concentration producing 50% of maximum uptake rate (Vmax), indicating the affinity of uptake systems for specific nutrients.

---

### Question 7
What is the typical nitrogen to phosphorus (N:P) ratio in fish feed?

A) 1:1
B) 5:1 to 8:1
C) 20:1 to 30:1
D) 100:1

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Fish feeds typically have N:P ratios of 5:1 to 8:1, while plants require higher ratios (10:1 to 15:1), often resulting in phosphorus accumulation in aquaponics systems.

---

### Question 8
What analytical method is most accurate for determining total nitrogen in water samples?

A) Visual inspection
B) Taste test
C) Persulfate digestion followed by colorimetric analysis
D) pH measurement

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Persulfate digestion converts all nitrogen forms to nitrate, which is then quantified colorimetrically, providing accurate total nitrogen measurement including organic forms.

---

### Question 9
What does "bioavailability" mean in the context of aquaponics nutrients?

A) The total amount of nutrient present
B) The proportion of a nutrient that plants can absorb and utilize
C) Whether nutrients are organic
D) The cost of nutrients

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Bioavailability describes the fraction of total nutrient that is in a chemical form and physical location accessible for plant uptake and metabolism.

---

### Question 10
In micronutrient research, why is iron often limiting in aquaponics systems?

A) Fish feed contains no iron
B) High pH (>7) causes iron to precipitate as unavailable Fe(OH)₃
C) Plants don't need iron
D) Iron evaporates quickly

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** At pH > 7 (common in aquaponics), ferric iron precipitates as Fe(OH)₃, becoming unavailable to plants. Chelated iron or pH management is required to maintain availability.
