## Questions

### Question 1
In a Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD), what is the primary purpose of blocking?

A) To increase the number of experimental units
B) To account for known sources of variation and improve statistical power
C) To eliminate the need for replication
D) To simplify data analysis

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Blocking groups experimental units by a known source of variation (e.g., greenhouse location, time), allowing researchers to control for these effects and detect treatment differences more effectively.

---

### Question 2
How many treatment combinations exist in a 3 × 4 factorial experiment (3 levels of factor A, 4 levels of factor B)?

A) 7
B) 12
C) 16
D) 24

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Factorial combinations = Levels of A × Levels of B = 3 × 4 = 12 treatment combinations.

---

### Question 3
What is the minimum number of replicates typically recommended for aquaponics experiments to detect moderate effect sizes (Cohen's d = 0.8) with 80% power?

A) 2 replicates
B) 3 replicates
C) 4-6 replicates
D) 10+ replicates

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Power analysis typically indicates 4-6 replicates per treatment are needed to detect moderate effects with standard statistical confidence, balancing statistical power with practical constraints.

---

### Question 4
In a split-plot design for aquaponics research, if fish stocking density is the whole-plot factor and plant species is the subplot factor, which factor has higher experimental precision?

A) Fish stocking density (whole-plot)
B) Plant species (subplot)
C) Both have equal precision
D) Neither factor is precisely estimated

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Subplot factors have higher precision because they are replicated within each whole-plot, receiving more degrees of freedom and smaller error terms in the analysis.

---

### Question 5
What is the purpose of a control treatment in aquaponics experiments?

A) To increase sample size
B) To provide a baseline for comparison and validate experimental effects
C) To meet regulatory requirements
D) To balance the number of treatments

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Controls establish baseline performance, allowing researchers to determine whether treatments produced meaningful changes and ensuring observed effects are real, not artifacts.

---

### Question 6
For a study comparing three fish species in aquaponics systems, what type of experimental design would be most appropriate if each system can only accommodate one species?

A) Completely Randomized Design (CRD)
B) Paired t-test
C) Repeated measures design
D) Crossover design

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** When experimental units (systems) are independently assigned to one treatment (species) with random allocation, a Completely Randomized Design is appropriate.

---

### Question 7
What does "power analysis" determine in experimental design?

A) The electrical requirements of the experiment
B) The sample size needed to detect an effect of a given size
C) The strength of pumps required
D) The statistical software to use

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Statistical power analysis calculates the number of replicates needed to detect a specified effect size with desired confidence (typically 80% power, α = 0.05).

---

### Question 8
In aquaponics research, what is a common reason for using repeated measures designs?

A) Lower cost than independent samples
B) To track changes in the same experimental units over time
C) To avoid statistical analysis
D) Because replication is impossible

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Repeated measures designs measure the same experimental units multiple times (e.g., fish growth weekly), controlling for inter-unit variability and detecting temporal patterns.

---

### Question 9
What is pseudoreplication in aquaponics research?

A) Using artificial controls
B) Treating non-independent samples as independent replicates
C) Replicating only part of an experiment
D) Using simulated data

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pseudoreplication occurs when subsamples from the same experimental unit (e.g., multiple fish from one tank) are treated as independent replicates, inflating degrees of freedom and Type I error rates.

---

### Question 10
For a factorial experiment testing 2 fish densities × 3 plant types with 4 replicates, how many total experimental units are needed?

A) 9
B) 12
C) 18
D) 24

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Total units = Fish densities × Plant types × Replicates = 2 × 3 × 4 = 24 experimental units.
