## Essential Nutrient Requirements

### Macronutrients

**1. Protein and Amino Acids**

| Species Category | Protein Requirement | Digestibility |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Carnivores (salmon, bass) | 45-55% | >85% |
| Omnivores (tilapia, catfish) | 28-36% | >75% |
| Herbivores (carp, grass carp) | 25-30% | >70% |

**Essential Amino Acids (EAA):**
```
All 10 EAAs must be present in correct ratios:

Arginine, Histidine, Isoleucine, Leucine, Lysine
Methionine, Phenylalanine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Valine

Limiting amino acid = growth bottleneck
```

**2. Lipids (Fats)**

**Requirements:**

| Species | Total Lipid | n-3 PUFA | EPA+DHA |
|---------|-------------|----------|---------|
| Salmon | 15-25% | 1.0-2.5% | 0.8-2.0% |
| Tilapia | 5-10% | 0.5-1.0% | 0.3-0.8% |
| Marine fish | 10-20% | 1.5-3.0% | 1.0-2.5% |

**Essential Fatty Acids:**
- Freshwater: Linolenic acid (18:3 n-3)
- Marine: EPA (20:5 n-3) and DHA (22:6 n-3)
- Functions: Energy, membranes, hormones, immunity

**3. Carbohydrates**

```
Utilization varies by species:

High tolerance (tilapia, carp):
- Can use 25-40% carbohydrates
- Glucose metabolism efficient

Low tolerance (salmonids, marine):
- Maximum 15-20% carbohydrates
- Poor glucose metabolism
- Prefer protein/lipid for energy
```

### Micronutrients

**Vitamins:**

| Vitamin | Function | Deficiency Signs | Typical Level |
|---------|----------|------------------|---------------|
| A | Vision, growth | Poor growth, blindness | 4,000-20,000 IU/kg |
| D3 | Calcium metabolism | Bone deformities | 1,000-4,000 IU/kg |
| E | Antioxidant | Muscular dystrophy | 100-400 mg/kg |
| K | Blood clotting | Hemorrhage | 5-20 mg/kg |
| C | Stress, immunity | Scoliosis, poor healing | 100-500 mg/kg |
| B-complex | Metabolism | Various | Per vitamin specs |

**Minerals:**

| Mineral | Function | Requirement |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| Calcium | Bones, osmotic balance | 0.5-2.0% |
| Phosphorus | Bones, energy (ATP) | 0.6-1.2% |
| Magnesium | Enzyme cofactor | 0.04-0.07% |
| Iron | Hemoglobin | 30-170 mg/kg |
| Zinc | Enzymes, growth | 15-50 mg/kg |
| Copper | Hemoglobin formation | 3-10 mg/kg |
| Selenium | Antioxidant | 0.25-0.50 mg/kg |

## Feed Formulation

### Ingredient Selection

**Protein Sources:**

| Ingredient | Protein % | Digestibility | Cost | Notes |
|------------|-----------|---------------|------|-------|
| Fish meal | 60-72% | 85-95% | High | Traditional, declining availability |
| Soybean meal | 44-48% | 75-85% | Medium | Most common plant protein |
| Poultry meal | 60-65% | 80-90% | Medium | Good alternative |
| Blood meal | 80-90% | 75-85% | Medium | High lysine |
| Insect meal | 40-60% | 80-90% | High | Emerging, sustainable |
| Algae meal | 30-60% | 70-80% | Very High | Sustainable, developing |

**Energy Sources:**

| Ingredient | Energy (MJ/kg) | Cost | Digestibility |
|------------|----------------|------|---------------|
| Fish oil | 36-39 | High | >95% |
| Vegetable oils | 36-39 | Medium | 90-95% |
| Wheat | 14-16 | Low | 70-80% |
| Corn | 14-16 | Low | 75-85% |

### Formulation Process

**Step 1: Define Nutrient Targets**

Example - Tilapia Grow-out Feed:
```
Target Specifications:
- Crude protein: 32%
- Crude lipid: 6%
- Crude fiber: <5%
- Ash: <10%
- Energy: 16 MJ/kg
- Lysine: 1.8%
- Methionine: 0.8%
- Phosphorus: 0.8%
```

**Step 2: Select Ingredients**

```
Available ingredients:
1. Fish meal (65% protein)
2. Soybean meal (46% protein)
3. Wheat flour (12% protein)
4. Fish oil
5. Vitamin/mineral premix
6. Binder (alginate or similar)
```

**Step 3: Create Formula**

**Example Tilapia Feed Formula:**

| Ingredient | % | Protein | Lipid | Energy |
|------------|---|---------|-------|--------|
| Fish meal | 15% | 9.75% | 1.5% | 2.4 MJ |
| Soy meal | 40% | 18.4% | 0.4% | 4.8 MJ |
| Wheat flour | 30% | 3.6% | 0.3% | 4.8 MJ |
| Fish oil | 3% | - | 3.0% | 1.2 MJ |
| Premix | 2% | - | - | - |
| Binder | 10% | 0.5% | - | 0.8 MJ |
| **TOTAL** | **100%** | **32.3%** | **5.2%** | **14.0 MJ** |

**Step 4: Nutritional Optimization**

```
Use software or Pearson Square method:
- Balance amino acid profile
- Ensure mineral ratios correct (Ca:P = 1:1 to 2:1)
- Add synthetic amino acids if needed
- Cost optimization while meeting requirements
```

### Process Overview

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         FEED MANUFACTURING PROCESS                 │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                     │
│  1. GRINDING                                       │
│     Reduce ingredients to uniform size             │
│     ↓                                              │
│  2. MIXING                                         │
│     Combine dry ingredients uniformly              │
│     Add liquids (oils, binders)                    │
│     ↓                                              │
│  3. EXTRUSION/PELLETING                           │
│     Heat + Pressure → Pellet formation             │
│     Steam conditioning                             │
│     ↓                                              │
│  4. DRYING                                         │
│     Reduce moisture to 8-10%                       │

│     ↓                                              │

│     ↓                                              │

│     ↓                                              │

│     ↓                                              │

└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Pellet Characteristics

**Water Stability:**
```
Critical for aquatic feeds:
- Minimum: 30 minutes in water
- Preferred: 2-4 hours
- Test: Tumbling can method

Poor stability = nutrient leaching + water pollution
```

**Density:**
- Floating pellets: Density <1.0 g/cm³ (high extrusion temperature)
- Slow-sinking: Density ~1.0-1.1 g/cm³
- Fast-sinking: Density >1.2 g/cm³

**Size Selection:**

| Fish Size | Pellet Diameter |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Larvae | 50-200 μm (powder) |
| Fry (0.1-1g) | 0.5-1.0 mm |
| Fingerlings (1-10g) | 1.0-2.0 mm |
| Juveniles (10-50g) | 2.0-3.0 mm |
| Grow-out (50-500g) | 3.0-6.0 mm |
| Large fish (>500g) | 6.0-12.0 mm |

## Feeding Strategies

### Feeding Rate Determination

**Method 1: Body Weight Percentage**

```
Daily feed = Biomass (kg) × Feeding Rate (%)

Feeding rates by temperature and size:

Tilapia (28°C):
- Fry (1-10g): 8-10% BWD
- Fingerlings (10-50g): 5-7% BWD
- Juveniles (50-200g): 3-5% BWD
- Grow-out (200-500g): 2-3% BWD

Salmonids (15°C):
- Fry: 6-8% BWD
- Fingerlings: 4-5% BWD
- Grow-out: 1-2% BWD
```

**Method 2: Feeding Tables**

```
Adjusted for temperature:

Temperature Factor (TF):
Below optimal: Reduce feeding
Above optimal: Reduce feeding
Optimal range: 100% feeding

Example - Tilapia:
30°C = 100% TF
25°C = 75% TF
20°C = 40% TF
15°C = 10% TF
<15°C = Stop feeding
```

### Feeding Frequency

| Life Stage | Feeds per Day | Rationale |
|------------|---------------|-----------|
| Larvae | 6-12× | Small stomach, constant feeding |
| Fry | 4-6× | High metabolic rate |
| Fingerlings | 3-4× | Moderate metabolism |
| Juveniles | 2-3× | Approaching adult |
| Adults | 1-2× | Lower relative metabolism |

**Automated Feeding:**
- Demand feeders (fish-activated)
- Timer-based feeders
- Computer-controlled systems
- Advantages: Reduced labor, consistent delivery

## Feed Conversion and Growth

### Key Performance Metrics

**1. Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)**

```
FCR = Total Feed Given (kg) / Weight Gain (kg)

Example:
1000 kg feed used
Fish grew from 10,000 kg to 11,500 kg
Weight gain = 1,500 kg

FCR = 1000 / 1500 = 0.67

Lower FCR = Better efficiency
Target FCR by species:
- Tilapia: 1.0-1.4
- Salmon: 0.9-1.2
- Catfish: 1.4-1.8
- Shrimp: 1.5-2.0
```

**2. Specific Growth Rate (SGR)**

```
SGR (%/day) = [(ln W₂ - ln W₁) / Days] × 100

Where:
W₁ = Initial weight
W₂ = Final weight

Example:
Initial: 50g
Final: 200g (after 60 days)

SGR = [(ln 200 - ln 50) / 60] × 100
    = [(5.30 - 3.91) / 60] × 100
    = 2.3%/day
```

**3. Economic Conversion Ratio (ECR)**

```
ECR = Feed Cost / Value of Weight Gain

Example:
Feed: $1.20/kg
Fish price: $4.00/kg
FCR: 1.2

ECR = (1.2 kg feed × $1.20) / (1 kg gain × $4.00)
    = $1.44 / $4.00 = 0.36

Feed cost = 36% of fish value
```

## Alternative and Sustainable Proteins

### Emerging Protein Sources

**1. Insect Meal**

```
Species: Black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens)

Composition:
- Protein: 40-45%
- Lipid: 25-35%
- Excellent amino acid profile
- High in lauric acid

Advantages:
- Grown on organic waste
- Rapid production (14 days)
- Low environmental impact
- Natural fish diet component

Current status:
- Approved in many regions
- Cost decreasing
- Can replace 25-50% fish meal
```

**2. Single-Cell Protein (SCP)**

```
Sources:
- Bacteria (Methylococcus capsulatus)
- Yeast (Candida utilis)
- Microalgae (Spirulina, Chlorella)

Protein content: 50-70%

Benefits:
- Rapid growth
- Controlled production
- Consistent quality
- Can use waste streams (methane, CO₂)
```

**3. Plant Protein Concentrates**

```
Improved processing removes anti-nutritional factors:

Soy protein concentrate (SPC): 65-70% protein
Corn gluten meal: 60-70% protein
Pea protein concentrate: 50-60% protein

Limitations:
- Amino acid imbalances
- Anti-nutritional factors
- Lower palatability
- Digestibility varies
```

## Feed Storage and Quality Control

### Storage Conditions

**Optimal Storage:**

| Parameter | Requirement | Reason |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Temperature | <25°C | Prevents lipid oxidation |
| Humidity | <70% | Prevents mold |
| Light | Dark | Prevents vitamin degradation |
| Pest control | Sealed containers | Rodents, insects |
| FIFO | First in, first out | Use oldest first |
| Max storage | 3-6 months | Nutrient degradation |

### Quality Testing

**Proximate Analysis:**
```
Standard tests:
- Moisture: <10%
- Crude protein: ±2% of target
- Crude lipid: ±1% of target
- Crude fiber: <6%
- Ash: <12%

Test frequency: Every batch
```

**Physical Quality:**
```
Water stability: >2 hours
Pellet durability index (PDI): >85%
Pellet size uniformity: ±10%
Bulk density: Consistent batch to batch
Color and odor: Normal, no rancidity
```

## Troubleshooting Feed Issues

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Poor FCR | Low digestibility, overfeeding | Check feed quality, reduce ration |
| Slow growth | Inadequate nutrition, stress | Analyze feed, check water quality |
| Fat deposition | High energy, low protein | Reformulate, reduce lipid |
| Skeletal deformities | Vitamin C or D deficiency | Supplement vitamins |
| Pale flesh | Low carotenoids | Add astaxanthin |
| Soft pellets | High moisture, poor binding | Improve drying, add binder |
| Feed refusal | Rancidity, poor palatability | Fresh feed, check storage |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Species-specific nutrition** - carnivores vs omnivores differ greatly
2. **Protein quality matters** - amino acid balance, not just percentage
3. **Feed is largest operating cost** - optimize FCR for profitability
4. **Alternative proteins are viable** - insect, SCP, plant concentrates
5. **Storage affects quality** - proper conditions preserve nutrition
6. **FCR is key metric** - measure and improve continuously
7. **Feeding strategy impacts performance** - rate, frequency, size matter
