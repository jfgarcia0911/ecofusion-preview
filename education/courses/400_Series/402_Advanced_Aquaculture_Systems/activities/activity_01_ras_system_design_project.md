# Activity 1: Complete RAS System Design Project

## Course 402: Advanced Aquaculture Systems

**Hands-On Design Exercise | 8-12 Hours | Individual or Team**

---

## Objective

Design a complete recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) for commercial production, applying principles learned throughout the course. This activity simulates real-world system design and prepares students for professional practice.

---

## Project Brief

You have been hired as the lead aquaculture engineer for a new commercial RAS facility. The client has provided the following requirements:

### Client Requirements

```
PROJECT: Coastal Fresh Fish Farm
LOCATION: Coastal region with access to seawater or freshwater
TARGET MARKET: Premium fresh fish for regional distribution
BUDGET: $3-5 million capital investment
TIMELINE: Design phase 3 months, construction 12 months
GOAL: Economically viable, environmentally sustainable operation
```

---

## Part 1: Species Selection (1 hour)

### Task

Select and justify an appropriate species for production.

### Considerations

1. **Market Analysis**
   - What species command premium prices in your region?
   - Current supply and demand
   - Competition analysis
   - Price trends

2. **Technical Feasibility**
   - Species suitable for RAS
   - Temperature requirements (heating/cooling costs)
   - Growth rate and market size timeline
   - Disease resistance
   - Feed availability and cost

3. **Your Selection**

Document your species choice and provide:
- Species name (common and scientific)
- Market price range
- Growth parameters (time to market size)
- Environmental requirements
- Rationale for selection (3-5 key reasons)

## Tips for Success

1. **Start with the end in mind** - Know your target market and price point
2. **Size conservatively** - Include safety factors
3. **Don't forget backups** - Redundancy is essential
4. **Be realistic on economics** - Use actual industry costs
5. **Show your work** - Include all calculations
6. **Think like an engineer** - Practical, buildable designs
7. **Think like a business owner** - Economic viability matters
