## Questions

### Question 1
What is the optimal film depth for NFT systems growing leafy greens?

A) 0.5-1 mm
B) 1-3 mm
C) 3-5 mm
D) 5-10 mm

**Correct Answer:** B) 1-3 mm

**Explanation:** A film depth of 1-3 mm provides adequate nutrient delivery while maintaining sufficient oxygen availability at the root-film interface. Shallower films risk desiccation, while deeper films reduce oxygen transfer.

### Question 2
According to Manning's equation for open channel flow, which factor has the GREATEST impact on flow rate when doubled?

A) Channel slope (S)
B) Cross-sectional area (A)
C) Hydraulic radius (R)
D) Manning's roughness coefficient (n)

**Correct Answer:** B) Cross-sectional area (A)

**Explanation:** In Manning's equation Q = (A × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2)) / n, cross-sectional area (A) has a linear relationship with flow rate, meaning doubling A doubles Q. The other factors have exponential or inverse relationships with smaller effects.

### Question 3
What is the primary advantage of NFT systems over deep water culture (DWC)?

A) Lower capital cost
B) Higher oxygen availability to roots
C) Greater nutrient storage capacity
D) Better temperature stability

**Correct Answer:** B) Higher oxygen availability to roots

**Explanation:** NFT's thin film allows the upper portion of roots to be exposed to air, providing superior oxygen availability compared to DWC where roots are fully submerged. This is NFT's key physiological advantage.

### Question 4
In a 10-meter NFT channel with 1% slope, what is the vertical drop from inlet to outlet?

A) 1 cm
B) 10 cm
C) 100 cm
D) 1000 cm

**Correct Answer:** B) 10 cm

**Explanation:** 1% slope = 1 cm drop per 100 cm (1 meter) of length. For 10 meters: 10 × 1 cm = 10 cm total vertical drop.

### Question 5
Which root zone provides the majority of nutrient uptake in mature NFT systems?

A) Aerial roots (dry zone above film)
B) Transition zone (film contact with root hairs)
C) Submerged zone (fully wetted thick roots)
D) All zones contribute equally

**Correct Answer:** B) Transition zone (film contact with root hairs)

**Explanation:** The transition zone where the thin film contacts abundant root hairs provides the optimal combination of nutrient access and oxygen availability, making it the primary absorption zone (70%+ of uptake).

### Question 6
What is the typical Reynolds number range for NFT systems?

A) <100 (highly laminar)
B) 500-1500 (laminar)
C) 2000-4000 (transitional)
D) >4000 (turbulent)

**Correct Answer:** B) 500-1500 (laminar)

**Explanation:** NFT systems operate in the laminar flow regime (Re < 2000), typically 500-1500. This provides predictable flow behavior but limits oxygen entrainment, requiring supplemental aeration.

---

### Question 7
How does dissolved oxygen saturation change with a temperature increase from 20°C to 30°C?

A) Increases by approximately 10%
B) Remains constant
C) Decreases by approximately 18%
D) Decreases by approximately 50%

**Correct Answer:** C) Decreases by approximately 18%

**Explanation:** DO saturation at 20°C is ~9.1 mg/L, at 30°C is ~7.5 mg/L. The decrease is (9.1-7.5)/9.1 = 17.6%, approximately 18%. This makes temperature control critical for maintaining adequate DO.

### Question 8
What is the maximum recommended channel length for lettuce production in NFT?

A) 3-5 meters
B) 6-8 meters
C) 10-15 meters
D) 20-25 meters

**Correct Answer:** C) 10-15 meters

**Explanation:** Channel length is limited by nutrient depletion, oxygen consumption, and temperature rise. For lettuce, 10-15 meters is maximum effective length, with 6-12 meters being optimal for most commercial operations to maintain <20% nutrient gradient.

### Question 9
According to Michaelis-Menten kinetics, when nutrient concentration equals Km, the uptake rate is:

A) Zero
B) 50% of maximum (Vmax)
C) 75% of maximum
D) 100% of maximum

**Correct Answer:** B) 50% of maximum (Vmax)

**Explanation:** Km is defined as the substrate concentration at which the reaction rate is half of Vmax. This is a fundamental concept in enzyme kinetics and nutrient uptake modeling.

### Question 10
Which historical figure is credited with developing the Nutrient Film Technique in the 1960s?

A) Dr. William Gericke
B) Dr. Allan Cooper
C) Dr. Howard Resh
D) Dr. Merle Jensen

**Correct Answer:** B) Dr. Allan Cooper

**Explanation:** Dr. Allan Cooper at the Glasshouse Crops Research Institute in England developed NFT in the 1960s, addressing limitations of existing hydroponic methods and creating the foundation for modern NFT systems.
