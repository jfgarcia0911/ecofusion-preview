## Questions

### Question 1
What is the optimal EC range for lettuce in NFT systems?

A) 0.5-0.8 dS/m
B) 1.2-1.8 dS/m
C) 2.5-3.5 dS/m
D) 4.0-5.0 dS/m

**Correct Answer:** B) 1.2-1.8 dS/m

**Explanation:** Lettuce performs optimally with EC of 1.2-1.8 dS/m. Lower EC may limit growth, while higher EC can cause tip burn and reduced quality.

---

### Question 2
What is the recommended pH range for most crops in NFT?

A) 4.0-5.0
B) 5.5-6.5
C) 7.0-8.0
D) 8.5-9.5

**Correct Answer:** B) 5.5-6.5

**Explanation:** pH of 5.5-6.5 optimizes nutrient availability for most crops, with slight variations by species. Outside this range, nutrient lockout can occur.

---

### Question 3
How often should nutrient solution be completely replaced in commercial NFT?

A) Daily
B) Weekly
C) Every 2-4 weeks depending on crop and monitoring
D) Annually

**Correct Answer:** C) Every 2-4 weeks depending on crop and monitoring

**Explanation:** Complete solution replacement every 2-4 weeks prevents accumulation of unwanted ions and maintains optimal nutrient ratios, with frequency based on monitoring.

---

### Question 4
What causes nutrient imbalances in recirculating NFT systems?

A) Uniform uptake of all nutrients
B) Differential uptake rates causing some nutrients to accumulate, others deplete
C) Perfect balance maintained automatically
D) Temperature only

**Correct Answer:** B) Differential uptake rates causing some nutrients to accumulate, others deplete

**Explanation:** Plants take up nutrients at different rates based on needs and availability, causing some to deplete (N, K) while others accumulate (Ca, Mg, micronutrients).

---

### Question 5
What is the primary reason for monitoring individual ion concentrations rather than just EC?

A) It's not necessary
B) EC doesn't indicate which specific nutrients are deficient or excessive
C) EC is always wrong
D) Individual ions don't matter

**Correct Answer:** B) EC doesn't indicate which specific nutrients are deficient or excessive

**Explanation:** EC measures total dissolved ions but doesn't distinguish between nutrients. Individual monitoring prevents deficiencies and toxicities masked by overall EC.

---

### Question 6
What is the antagonistic relationship between calcium and potassium?

A) They enhance each other's uptake
B) High K can reduce Ca uptake, potentially causing deficiency symptoms
C) They have no relationship
D) They cannot coexist in solution

**Correct Answer:** B) High K can reduce Ca uptake, potentially causing deficiency symptoms

**Explanation:** High potassium concentrations can competitively inhibit calcium uptake, leading to disorders like tip burn in lettuce despite adequate Ca in solution.

---

### Question 7
What is the benefit of maintaining higher nitrate-to-ammonium ratios in NFT?

A) Lower cost only
B) Reduces pH instability and ammonium toxicity risk
C) No benefit
D) Increases disease

**Correct Answer:** B) Reduces pH instability and ammonium toxicity risk

**Explanation:** Higher NO₃⁻:NH₄⁺ ratios (typically 90:10 to 95:5) maintain pH stability and prevent ammonium toxicity in recirculating systems.

---

### Question 8
What causes EC to increase in NFT systems over time?

A) Nutrient depletion
B) Water uptake exceeding nutrient uptake and accumulation of unused ions
C) Temperature only
D) Disease

**Correct Answer:** B) Water uptake exceeding nutrient uptake and accumulation of unused ions

**Explanation:** Plants typically take up more water than nutrients, concentrating remaining ions. Additionally, differential uptake causes some nutrients to accumulate.

---

### Question 9
What is the purpose of chelated micronutrients in NFT formulations?

A) Increase cost
B) Keep micronutrients soluble and available across pH ranges
C) Add color
D) No purpose

**Correct Answer:** B) Keep micronutrients soluble and available across pH ranges

**Explanation:** Chelates like EDTA or DTPA keep micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu) soluble and plant-available, preventing precipitation especially at higher pH.

---

### Question 10
How does solution temperature affect oxygen solubility?

A) No effect
B) Higher temperature increases oxygen solubility
C) Higher temperature decreases oxygen solubility
D) Only pH affects oxygen

**Correct Answer:** C) Higher temperature decreases oxygen solubility

**Explanation:** As temperature increases, dissolved oxygen saturation decreases significantly (9.1 mg/L at 20°C vs. 7.5 mg/L at 30°C), requiring active management.
