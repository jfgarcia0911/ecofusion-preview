## Questions

### Question 1
What are the three distinct zones in a mature NFT root system?

A) Top, middle, bottom
B) Aerial, transition, and submerged zones
C) Primary, secondary, tertiary
D) Young, mature, old

**Correct Answer:** B) Aerial, transition, and submerged zones

**Explanation:** NFT root systems develop three zones: aerial roots above the film (oxygen uptake), transition zone in contact with the film (primary nutrient uptake), and submerged zone (water uptake).

---

### Question 2
What percentage of nutrient uptake occurs in the transition zone?

A) 20-30%
B) 40-50%
C) 70-80%
D) 90-100%

**Correct Answer:** C) 70-80%

**Explanation:** The transition zone, where root hairs contact the thin film, accounts for approximately 70-80% of total nutrient uptake due to optimal balance of nutrients and oxygen.

---

### Question 3
What is the primary function of aerial roots in NFT systems?

A) Nutrient uptake
B) Oxygen uptake and respiration
C) Water storage
D) Structural support only

**Correct Answer:** B) Oxygen uptake and respiration

**Explanation:** Aerial roots exposed to air provide enhanced oxygen uptake for root respiration, supporting the energy-intensive nutrient absorption process.

---

### Question 4
What causes root mat formation in NFT channels?

A) Disease only
B) Excessive root growth restricting flow and creating preferential pathways
C) Insufficient nutrients
D) Too much light

**Correct Answer:** B) Excessive root growth restricting flow and creating preferential pathways

**Explanation:** Root mats form when dense root growth restricts flow, creating channels within the mat and reducing film uniformity.

---

### Question 5
What is the optimal root zone temperature for most crops in NFT?

A) 10-15°C
B) 18-22°C
C) 25-28°C
D) 30-35°C

**Correct Answer:** B) 18-22°C

**Explanation:** Root zone temperatures of 18-22°C optimize nutrient uptake, oxygen availability, and plant metabolism for most crops.

---

### Question 6
How does root pruning affect NFT system performance?

A) Always improves performance
B) Can improve flow and oxygen availability when done correctly
C) Always damages plants
D) Has no effect

**Correct Answer:** B) Can improve flow and oxygen availability when done correctly

**Explanation:** Strategic root pruning can improve flow distribution and oxygen availability, but must be done carefully to avoid plant stress.

---

### Question 7
What indicates healthy root development in NFT systems?

A) Brown, thick roots only
B) White to light tan roots with abundant fine root hairs
C) Purple roots
D) Completely dry roots

**Correct Answer:** B) White to light tan roots with abundant fine root hairs

**Explanation:** Healthy NFT roots appear white to light tan with abundant fine root hairs indicating active growth and good oxygen availability.

---

### Question 8
What is the relationship between root zone oxygen and nutrient uptake?

A) No relationship
B) Higher oxygen enables active transport and increased uptake
C) Less oxygen increases uptake
D) Oxygen only needed for leaves

**Correct Answer:** B) Higher oxygen enables active transport and increased uptake

**Explanation:** Adequate root zone oxygen is essential for energy (ATP) production needed for active nutrient transport against concentration gradients.

---

### Question 9
What causes brown, slimy root appearance in NFT?

A) Normal aging only
B) Low dissolved oxygen and potential pathogen infection
C) Excessive nutrients
D) Too much light

**Correct Answer:** B) Low dissolved oxygen and potential pathogen infection

**Explanation:** Brown, slimy roots indicate oxygen stress and often accompany pathogen infections like Pythium, thriving in low-oxygen conditions.

---

### Question 10
How frequently should root zones be inspected in commercial NFT operations?

A) Annually
B) Monthly
C) Weekly to daily for early problem detection
D) Never needed

**Correct Answer:** C) Weekly to daily for early problem detection

**Explanation:** Frequent root zone inspection (weekly to daily) allows early detection of problems like mat formation, disease, or oxygen stress before yield impacts.
