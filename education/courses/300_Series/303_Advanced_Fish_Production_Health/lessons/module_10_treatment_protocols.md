## 1. Treatment Calculation Fundamentals

### Volume Calculations

```
SYSTEM VOLUME DETERMINATION

Rectangular Tanks:
  Volume (L) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Depth (m) × 1,000

  Example:
    5m × 2m × 1.5m × 1,000 = 15,000 L = 15 m³

Circular Tanks:
  Volume (L) = π × r² × Depth (m) × 1,000
  Where r = radius (m)

  Example:
    π × (2m)² × 1.5m × 1,000 = 18,850 L

Irregular Ponds:
  Method 1: Average dimensions
  Method 2: Fill and meter water
  Method 3: Dye dilution test

CRITICAL: Always measure actual water depth, not tank depth
```

### Dose Calculations

```
TREATMENT DOSING

Concentration-Based (Bath Treatments):
  Amount (g) = Volume (L) × Concentration (mg/L) ÷ 1,000

  Example (Formalin 15 mg/L in 10,000L):
    10,000 L × 15 mg/L ÷ 1,000 = 150 g
    If using 37% formalin: 150 ÷ 0.37 = 405 mL

Body Weight-Based (Medicated Feed):
  Daily Dose (g) = Biomass (kg) × Dose (mg/kg) ÷ 1,000

  Example (OTC 50 mg/kg, 500 kg biomass):
    500 kg × 50 mg/kg ÷ 1,000 = 25 g OTC per day

Feed Medication:
  Feed Amount (kg) = Biomass (kg) × Feed Rate (%)

  Medicated Feed Preparation:
    Drug (g) = (Biomass × Dose) ÷ Feed Rate ÷ 10

  Example:
    500 kg fish, 50 mg/kg OTC, 2% feed rate
    OTC per kg feed = (500 × 50) ÷ 2 ÷ 10 = 1,250 mg/kg feed
```

## 2. Treatment Methods

### Bath Treatments

```
BATH TREATMENT TYPES

1. PROLONGED IMMERSION:
   Duration: Hours to days
   Concentration: Low
   Use: Mild parasites, continuous exposure

   Example: Salt 1-3 g/L indefinitely

2. SHORT-TERM BATH:
   Duration: 30-60 minutes
   Concentration: Moderate
   Use: Parasites, external bacteria

   Example: Formalin 15-25 mg/L × 1 hour

3. FLUSH/DIP:
   Duration: 5-15 minutes
   Concentration: High
   Use: Pre-stocking treatment, severe parasites

   Example: Salt 30 g/L × 5 minutes

4. FLOW-THROUGH:
   Duration: Continuous
   Concentration: Low
   Use: RAS systems, constant exposure

   Example: Copper 0.15 mg/L continuous
```

### Treatment Delivery Procedure

```
BATH TREATMENT PROTOCOL

PREPARATION:
  □ Calculate system volume accurately
  □ Determine correct dose
  □ Prepare chemical solution
  □ Test water quality (DO, temp, pH)
  □ Ensure backup aeration available
  □ Have emergency equipment ready

DELIVERY:
  □ Dissolve chemical in bucket/container
  □ Dilute to manageable volume
  □ Distribute evenly around tank
  □ Mix thoroughly (but gently)
  □ Time treatment start
  □ Begin monitoring

MONITORING:
  □ Check fish every 10-15 minutes
  □ Watch for distress signs:
    • Gasping
    • Erratic swimming
    • Loss of equilibrium
    • Jumping
  □ Monitor DO levels
  □ Be prepared to terminate treatment

COMPLETION:
  □ Time treatment end
  □ Begin water exchange if needed
  □ Restore normal aeration
  □ Continue monitoring 2-4 hours
  □ Document treatment
```

### Medicated Feed Treatment

```
ORAL MEDICATION PROTOCOL

PREPARATION:
  1. Calculate biomass accurately
  2. Determine daily medication dose
  3. Calculate medication per kg feed
  4. Prepare medicated feed

MIXING METHODS:

  Top-Coating (Simple):
    - Spray oil on feed (10% by weight)
    - Mix medication in oil
    - Coat feed pellets
    - Dry briefly
    - Use within 24 hours

  Incorporation (Better):
    - Grind pellets to powder
    - Mix medication thoroughly
    - Add binder (gelatin, CMC)
    - Re-pelletize or form paste
    - Dry at low temperature
    - Use within 7 days (refrigerated)

  Commercial (Best):
    - Purchase pre-medicated feed
    - Proper stability
    - Accurate dosing
    - Extended shelf life

FEEDING:
  □ Feed only medicated feed during treatment
  □ Ensure all fish eat
  □ Remove uneaten feed after 30 minutes
  □ Continue for full treatment duration
  □ Do not stop early
  □ Maintain water quality

TREATMENT DURATION:
  Antibiotics: 7-14 days (typically 10)
  Antiparasitics: 3-7 days
  Follow label instructions
```

## 3. Common Treatment Protocols

### Bacterial Disease Treatments

```
OXYTETRACYCLINE (OTC)

Indication: Bacterial infections (Aeromonas, Streptococcus, etc.)

Dose: 50-75 mg/kg fish per day

Duration: 10 days

Preparation:
  OTC (g/kg feed) = (Fish weight × 50-75) ÷ Feed rate ÷ 1,000

Example (1,000 kg fish, 2% feed rate, 50 mg/kg dose):
  OTC = (1,000 × 50) ÷ 2 ÷ 1,000 = 2.5 g/kg feed

  Daily feed: 1,000 × 0.02 = 20 kg
  Daily OTC needed: 20 × 2.5 = 50 g

Withdrawal: 21 days (USA)

Monitoring:
  • Appetite (if fish stop eating, pause treatment)
  • Mortality decline by day 3-4
  • Clinical sign improvement


FLORFENICOL

Indication: Bacterial infections (more effective)

Dose: 10-15 mg/kg fish per day

Duration: 10 days

Advantages:
  ✓ More effective than OTC
  ✓ Shorter withdrawal (15 days)
  ✓ Broader spectrum

Disadvantages:
  ✗ More expensive
  ✗ May not be approved for all species


SULFADIMETHOXINE/ORMETOPRIM (ROMET-30)

Indication: Furunculosis, enteric septicemia

Dose: 50 mg/kg day 1, then 25 mg/kg days 2-5

Duration: 5 days

Withdrawal: 3 days (42 degree-days)

Usage: FDA-approved for catfish and salmonids
```

### Parasite Treatment Protocols

```
FORMALIN PROTOCOL (Ich, Trichodina, flukes)

Dose: 15-25 mg/L (ppm)
Duration: 1 hour
Frequency: Every 2-3 days, minimum 3 treatments

Day 1: First treatment
Day 3: Second treatment (targets new generation)
Day 5: Third treatment (mop-up)
Day 7+: Additional if needed

SAFETY:
  ⚠ Increase aeration before treatment
  ⚠ Monitor DO - formalin depletes oxygen
  ⚠ Watch fish closely
  ⚠ Reduce duration if distress
  ⚠ Carcinogenic - use PPE


SALT PROTOCOL (General parasite control)

Prolonged:
  1-3 g/L (ppt) continuous
  Duration: Until 3-5 days after signs disappear
  Use: Ich, Trichodina, stress reduction

Short-term:
  3% (30 g/L) for 5-15 minutes
  Use: Pre-stocking dip, heavy parasitism

Calculation:
  Salt (kg) = Volume (m³) × Concentration (g/L)

Species Tolerance:
  ✓ Most warmwater fish: 3-5 ppt safe
  ✗ Some catfish species: sensitive
  ✗ Some scaleless fish: reduced tolerance


PRAZIQUANTEL (Flukes, tapeworms)

Bath Treatment:
  2-10 mg/L for 3-24 hours
  Usually 5 mg/L for 12 hours
  Single treatment often sufficient

Oral Treatment:
  25-50 mg/kg in feed
  Single dose or 3-day treatment
  Better for internal parasites

Advantages:
  ✓ Very safe
  ✓ Highly effective for flukes
  ✓ Low toxicity

Disadvantages:
  ✗ Expensive
  ✗ May need to prepare own solution


POTASSIUM PERMANGANATE

Dose: 2-4 mg/L
Duration: 30-60 minutes
Use: External parasites, bacteria

CRITICAL PROTOCOL:
  1. Perform DEMAND TEST first:
     - Add 2 mg/L to sample
     - Wait 10 minutes
     - If brown (all consumed), water has high demand
     - Increase dose accordingly

  2. Treatment should remain pink/purple
  3. If turns brown quickly, dose was too low
  4. If fish distress, exchange water immediately

Applications:
  • External parasites
  • Columnaris
  • Saprolegnia (fungus)
  • Egg disinfection
```

## 4. Treatment Complications

### Adverse Reactions

```
TREATMENT DISTRESS SIGNS

MILD:
  • Increased activity
  • Slight color change
  • Increased respiration
  → Monitor closely, may continue

MODERATE:
  • Gasping at surface
  • Erratic swimming
  • Color darkening
  → Reduce treatment duration, increase aeration

SEVERE:
  • Loss of equilibrium
  • Convulsions
  • Jumping from water
  → STOP TREATMENT IMMEDIATELY

EMERGENCY RESPONSE:
  1. Turn off treatment delivery
  2. Begin rapid water exchange (50-100%)
  3. Maximize aeration
  4. Add activated carbon if chemical
  5. Monitor until fish stabilize
  6. Document event
  7. Reassess treatment plan
```

### Treatment Failures

```
WHY TREATMENTS FAIL

WRONG DIAGNOSIS:
  • Treating for bacteria when viral
  • Missing mixed infections
  • Misidentified parasite
  → Reexamine, get laboratory confirmation

INCORRECT DOSAGE:
  • Volume miscalculated
  • Dose too low to be effective
  • Dose too high causing toxicity
  → Verify calculations, measure volume

POOR DELIVERY:
  • Uneven distribution
  • Chemical degraded/expired
  • Fish not eating medicated feed
  → Improve mixing, check product, observe feeding

INCOMPLETE TREATMENT:
  • Stopped too early
  • Missed doses
  • Insufficient duration
  → Complete full course

ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS:
  • Water quality poor
  • Temperature wrong
  • pH affects treatment
  → Optimize conditions first

RESISTANCE:
  • Overuse of same antibiotic
  • Incomplete previous treatments
  → Culture and sensitivity testing, rotate drugs
```

## 5. Emergency Treatments

### Acute Ammonia Spike

```
AMMONIA EMERGENCY PROTOCOL

Detection: NH₃ >0.1 mg/L with fish distress

IMMEDIATE (< 30 minutes):
  1. STOP FEEDING immediately
  2. Begin water exchange (50% minimum)
  3. Increase aeration to maximum
  4. Add salt (1-3 ppt) to reduce toxicity

SHORT-TERM (Hours):
  5. Test and adjust pH (lower pH temporarily)
     - Reduces NH₃ fraction
     - Use carefully (don't crash below 6.0)
  6. Continue water exchange (20-30%/hour)
  7. Add commercial ammonia binder (zeolite, products)
  8. Monitor fish and ammonia levels

MEDIUM-TERM (Days):
  9. Identify and fix root cause:
     - Biofilter failure
     - Overfeeding
     - Dead fish/organic buildup
  10. Gradually resume feeding (50% ration)
  11. Continue testing 2×/day
  12. Restore normal operations slowly
```

### Low Dissolved Oxygen

```
HYPOXIA EMERGENCY

Detection: DO <4 mg/L with fish at surface

IMMEDIATE:
  1. Activate ALL aerators
  2. Add pure O₂ if available
  3. Begin water exchange
  4. Reduce feeding (or stop)
  5. Remove any dead fish

CAUSES TO INVESTIGATE:
  □ Equipment failure (aerator, pump)
  □ Algae die-off
  □ Chemical treatment (formalin)
  □ Temperature spike
  □ Organic overload
  □ Power outage

PREVENTION:
  ✓ Backup aerators
  ✓ Alarm systems
  ✓ Generator backup
  ✓ Regular equipment maintenance
  ✓ DO monitoring
```

## 7. Regulatory Compliance

### FDA-Approved Drugs (USA)

```
LEGAL AQUACULTURE DRUGS

APPROVED FOR FOOD FISH:

Oxytetracycline (Terramycin)
  Species: Most aquaculture
  Withdrawal: 21 days
  Uses: Bacterial diseases

Florfenicol (Aquaflor)
  Species: Salmonids, catfish, tilapia
  Withdrawal: 15 days
  Uses: Bacterial diseases

Sulfadimethoxine/Ormetoprim (Romet-30)
  Species: Salmonids, catfish
  Withdrawal: 3 days (42 degree-days)
  Uses: Specific bacterial diseases

Formalin (Parasite-S, Formalin-F)
  Species: All finfish
  Withdrawal: 0 days (if used correctly)
  Uses: External parasites

35% Hydrogen Peroxide (Perox-Aid)
  Species: Salmonids, catfish
  Withdrawal: 0 days
  Uses: External parasites, low DO


EXTRA-LABEL USE:

AMDUCA allows veterinary extra-label use if:
  ✓ Valid veterinarian-client-patient relationship
  ✓ No approved alternative exists
  ✓ Extended withdrawal time
  ✓ Proper records maintained
  ✓ NOT in medicated feed

Prohibited:
  ✗ Chloramphenicol
  ✗ Fluoroquinolones (enrofloxacin, etc.)
  ✗ Nitrofurans
  ✗ Gentian violet
  ✗ Other FDA-prohibited drugs
```

## 8. Treatment Economics

### Cost-Benefit Analysis

```
TREATMENT DECISION MATRIX

Consider:
  • Cost of treatment
  • Value of fish
  • Likelihood of success
  • Time to market
  • Withdrawal period impact

Example Analysis:

Scenario: 1,000 tilapia, 400g average, Aeromonas infection

Option 1: Treat with Florfenicol
  Cost: $150 (medication)
  Success: 80% (save 800 fish)
  Time: 10 days + 15 day withdrawal
  Value saved: 320 kg × $5/kg = $1,600
  Net: $1,600 - $150 = $1,450 benefit

Option 2: Cull affected, harvest healthy
  Cost: $0 (treatment)
  Loss: 30% mortality = 120 kg
  Immediate harvest: 280 kg × $5/kg = $1,400
  Net: $1,400 (no withdrawal delay)

Option 3: Do nothing
  Risk: 50% mortality = 200 kg lost
  Survivors: 200 kg × $5/kg = $1,000
  Lowest value

DECISION: Option 1 (treat) provides best outcome
```

## 9. Alternative and Emerging Treatments

### Natural and Biological Treatments

```
ALTERNATIVE APPROACHES

PROBIOTICS:
  Mechanism: Competitive exclusion, immune enhancement
  Products: Bacillus spp., Lactobacillus spp.
  Dose: 10⁶-10⁹ CFU/g feed
  Duration: Continuous
  Evidence: Moderate to good

PREBIOTICS:
  Mechanism: Feed beneficial bacteria
  Products: MOS, FOS, beta-glucans
  Dose: 0.1-0.5% of feed
  Evidence: Moderate

PHYTOCHEMICALS:
  Products: Garlic, tea tree oil, oregano
  Mechanism: Antimicrobial, immune stimulation
  Evidence: Variable, mostly anecdotal

BACTERIOPHAGES:
  Mechanism: Virus targets specific bacteria
  Status: Research stage
  Potential: High (antibiotic alternative)

VACCINATION:
  Diseases: Streptococcus, Vibrio, Edwardsiella
  Method: Injection, immersion, oral
  Efficacy: 60-90%
  Cost: Higher upfront, saves treatment costs
  Best approach for prevention
```

## 10. Treatment Best Practices

### Treatment Guidelines

```
TREATMENT PRINCIPLES

1. ACCURATE DIAGNOSIS FIRST
   Don't treat blindly
   Confirm pathogen when possible
   Consider differential diagnoses

2. OPTIMIZE ENVIRONMENT
   Treat water quality problems first
   Support fish health
   Reduce stress

3. USE APPROPRIATE DRUG
   Species-approved when possible
   Effective for pathogen
   Proper dose and duration

4. CALCULATE CORRECTLY
   Measure volume accurately
   Double-check calculations
   Use appropriate concentration

5. MONITOR CLOSELY
   Watch fish during treatment
   Be ready to abort if needed
   Document response

6. COMPLETE FULL COURSE
   Don't stop early
   Even if fish look better
   Prevents resistance

7. FOLLOW WITHDRAWAL
   Comply with regulations
   Ensure food safety
   Document timing

8. PREVENT RECURRENCE
   Address root cause
   Improve management
   Consider vaccination

9. ROTATE DRUGS
   Don't use same antibiotic repeatedly
   Reduces resistance development
   Culture and sensitivity testing

10. DOCUMENT EVERYTHING
    Legal requirement
    Quality assurance
    Pattern recognition
    Continuous improvement
```

## Summary

Effective treatment requires:

1. **Accurate Diagnosis** - Know what you're treating
2. **Correct Calculations** - Measure volume and dose precisely
3. **Proper Delivery** - Ensure even distribution and contact
4. **Close Monitoring** - Watch for efficacy and adverse effects
5. **Complete Protocols** - Full duration and withdrawal compliance
6. **Documentation** - Record all treatments and outcomes

Remember:
- Prevention is better than treatment
- Optimize water quality first
- Use approved drugs when possible
- Monitor fish response closely
- Complete full treatment courses
- Follow withdrawal periods

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Measure accurately** - Volume errors cause treatment failures
2. **Start conservatively** - Can increase dose, hard to reverse overdose
3. **Monitor continuously** - Be ready to abort treatment
4. **Document thoroughly** - Legal and quality requirements
5. **Address root cause** - Treatment alone not enough
6. **Consider economics** - Sometimes culling is better option
