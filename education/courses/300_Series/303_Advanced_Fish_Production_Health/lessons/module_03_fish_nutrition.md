## 1. Nutritional Requirements Overview

### Essential Nutrients

```
FISH NUTRITION PYRAMID

              ┌─────────────┐
              │  Vitamins   │  Micronutrients
              │  Minerals   │  (mg-μg/kg)
              └─────────────┘
         ┌──────────────────────┐
         │   Lipids (Fats)      │  Energy & EFA
         │   10-20% of diet     │  (g-kg/kg)
         └──────────────────────┘
    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
    │      Protein (Amino Acids)      │  Growth & maintenance
    │      30-50% of diet             │  (g/kg)
    └─────────────────────────────────┘
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         Carbohydrates                    │  Energy (limited)
│         10-40% of diet                   │  (g/kg)
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Nutrient Categories

| Category | Function | Deficiency Effects | Excess Effects |
|----------|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Protein** | Growth, repair, enzymes | Stunted growth, weight loss | Pollution, cost |
| **Lipids** | Energy, cell membranes | Poor growth, fatty liver | Fat deposition |
| **Carbohydrates** | Energy (limited) | None specific | Liver glycogen, fat |
| **Vitamins** | Metabolism, immunity | Various syndromes | Toxicity (fat-soluble) |
| **Minerals** | Bones, osmosis, enzymes | Deformities, anemia | Toxicity, antagonism |

### Protein Quality

### Protein-Energy Ratio

**P/E Ratio Calculation:**

```
P/E Ratio = (Protein % × 23.6 kJ/g) ÷ Total Energy (kJ/g)

Optimal Ranges:
  Carnivorous fish: 90-120 mg protein/kJ
  Omnivorous fish: 80-100 mg protein/kJ
  Herbivorous fish: 70-90 mg protein/kJ


Example Calculation:

Feed Composition:
  Protein: 40%
  Fat: 12%
  Carbohydrate: 30%

Energy Content:
  Protein: 40% × 23.6 kJ/g = 9.44 kJ/g
  Fat: 12% × 39.5 kJ/g = 4.74 kJ/g
  Carb: 30% × 17.2 kJ/g = 5.16 kJ/g
  Total: 19.34 kJ/g

P/E Ratio = 9.44 ÷ 19.34 = 0.488 = 48.8%
          = (400 mg protein ÷ 19.34 kJ) = 20.7 mg/kJ

Result: Low for carnivorous fish, need higher P/E
```

## 3. Lipids and Fatty Acids

### Lipid Functions

**Roles in Fish Nutrition:**
1. Energy source (highest caloric density)
2. Essential fatty acids (EFA)
3. Fat-soluble vitamin absorption
4. Membrane structure
5. Hormone precursors

### Essential Fatty Acids

**Omega-3 vs Omega-6:**

```
Freshwater Fish Requirements:
  Linoleic acid (18:2n-6): 0.5-1.5% of diet
  Linolenic acid (18:3n-3): 0.5-1.5% of diet

Marine/Anadromous Fish:
  EPA (20:5n-3): 0.5-1.0% of diet
  DHA (22:6n-3): 0.5-1.0% of diet


Optimal n-3/n-6 Ratio:
  Freshwater: 1:1 to 2:1
  Marine: 5:1 to 10:1
```

### Lipid Levels by Species

| Species | Lipid Range (%) | Optimal Level | Primary Fatty Acid |
|---------|----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Tilapia** | 5-12 | 8-10 | 18:2n-6, 18:3n-3 |
| **Catfish** | 6-14 | 8-12 | 18:2n-6 |
| **Trout** | 15-25 | 18-22 | EPA, DHA |
| **Salmon** | 20-35 | 25-30 | EPA, DHA |
| **Bass** | 10-18 | 12-15 | EPA, DHA |

### Lipid Oxidation

**Preventing Rancidity:**

```
Peroxide Value (PV) Testing:

Fresh oil: <5 meq/kg
Acceptable: <10 meq/kg
Rancid: >20 meq/kg

Protection Methods:
  1. Antioxidants (Vitamin E, BHT, BHA)
  2. Cool, dark storage
  3. Minimize air exposure
  4. Use within 6 months
  5. Natural antioxidants (rosemary extract)
```

## 4. Carbohydrates and Energy

### Carbohydrate Utilization

**Digestibility by Fish Type:**

| Fish Type | Max Carb Level | Digestibility | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| **Carnivorous** | 10-20% | Poor | Limited amylase |
| **Omnivorous** | 25-40% | Moderate | Some adaptation |
| **Herbivorous** | 30-45% | Good | Efficient use |

### Fiber Content

```
Crude Fiber Levels:

Maximum Recommended:
  Carnivores: 3-5%
  Omnivores: 5-8%
  Herbivores: 8-12%

Effects of Excess Fiber:
  - Reduced digestibility
  - Increased FCR
  - Intestinal problems
  - Lower nutrient absorption
```

### Energy Requirements

**Calculating Digestible Energy (DE):**

```
DE = (Gross Energy × Digestibility Coefficient)

Energy from Nutrients:
  Protein: 23.6 kJ/g (5.6 kcal/g)
  Fat: 39.5 kJ/g (9.4 kcal/g)
  Carbohydrate: 17.2 kJ/g (4.1 kcal/g)


Example Feed:
  Protein: 35% × 85% digestibility = 29.75% DP
  Fat: 10% × 90% digestibility = 9% DF
  Carb: 25% × 60% digestibility = 15% DC

  DE = (29.75 × 23.6) + (9 × 39.5) + (15 × 17.2)
     = 702 + 356 + 258
     = 1,316 kJ/100g = 13.16 kJ/g
```

```
Optimal Ca:P Ratio

  Feed contains:
    Calcium: 1.2%
    Phosphorus: 1.0%

  Ca:P = 1.2:1.0 = 1.2:1 ✓ (within range)
```

## 8. Feed Formulation

### Pearson Square Method

**Simple 2-Ingredient Mix:**

```
Problem: Mix fish meal (60% protein) with wheat (12% protein)
         to get 35% protein feed

Pearson Square:

         Fish meal (60%)
              \
               \    35-12 = 23 parts
                \
                35% Target
                /
               /    60-35 = 25 parts
              /
         Wheat (12%)


Total parts: 23 + 25 = 48

Fish meal: (23 ÷ 48) × 100 = 47.9%
Wheat: (25 ÷ 48) × 100 = 52.1%

Verification:
  (60 × 0.479) + (12 × 0.521) = 28.74 + 6.25 = 35% ✓
```

### Least-Cost Formulation

**Linear Programming Approach:**

```
Objective: Minimize cost while meeting nutrient requirements

Constraints:
  1. Protein: ≥35%
  2. Fat: 8-12%
  3. Fiber: ≤5%
  4. Ash: ≤12%
  5. Lysine: ≥2.0%
  6. Methionine: ≥0.8%

Ingredients Available:
  - Fish meal: $1.20/kg, 65% protein
  - Soy meal: $0.50/kg, 48% protein
  - Wheat: $0.30/kg, 12% protein
  - Fish oil: $1.50/kg, 0% protein

Software tools:
  - WinFeed
  - Brill Formulation
  - FeedLive
  - Excel Solver
```

```
  ✓ Crude fiber
  ✓ Ash

```

### Storage Quality

**Feed Degradation Prevention:**

```
Storage Conditions:

Temperature: <25°C (77°F)
  - Above 30°C: Rapid vitamin loss
  - Above 35°C: Fat oxidation accelerates

Humidity: <65% RH
  - Above 70%: Mold growth risk
  - Above 80%: Clumping, spoilage

Moisture Content: <10%
  - 10-12%: Borderline
  - >12%: Mold growth, heat generation


Maximum Storage Times:

Condition          Time Limit
─────────────────────────────
Ideal (<20°C, dry)   6 months
Moderate (20-25°C)   3 months
Warm (25-30°C)       2 months
Hot/humid (>30°C)    1 month
```

## 10. Nutritional Disorders

### Common Deficiencies

**Vitamin C Deficiency (Scurvy):**
```
Signs:
  - Scoliosis (curved spine)
  - Hemorrhaging in fins
  - Poor wound healing
  - Reduced growth

Prevention:
  - 50-200 mg/kg diet
  - Stabilized form (C-phosphate)
  - Fresh feed (<3 months old)
```

**Fatty Liver Disease:**
```
Causes:
  - Excess dietary fat
  - Carbohydrate overload
  - Choline deficiency
  - Lack of exercise

Prevention:
  - Balance fat levels
  - Adequate choline (500+ mg/kg)
  - Limit carbs in carnivores
  - Proper feeding rates
```

**Nutritional Gill Disease:**
```
Causes:
  - Pantothenic acid deficiency
  - Poor water quality

Signs:
  - Gill clubbing
  - Respiratory distress
  - Poor growth

Prevention:
  - 20-50 mg/kg pantothenic acid
  - Quality feed
```

### Feed-Related Health Issues

| Issue | Cause | Solution |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **Obesity** | Overfeeding, high fat | Reduce ration, lower fat |
| **Fatty liver** | Excess carbs/fat | Balance macronutrients |
| **Poor growth** | Inadequate protein/energy | Increase quality |
| **Fin erosion** | Vitamin C deficiency | Supplement vitamins |
| **Anemia** | Iron, B12, folic acid | Mineral/vitamin mix |
| **Soft bones** | Ca, P, Vitamin D | Mineral balance |

## Summary

Fish nutrition requires careful balance of:

1. **Protein** (30-50%) - Growth and maintenance
2. **Lipids** (5-25%) - Energy and essential fatty acids
3. **Carbohydrates** (10-40%) - Energy for omnivores
4. **Vitamins** - Metabolism and health
5. **Minerals** - Structure and physiological functions

Key Principles:
- Species-specific requirements
- Life stage adjustments
- Protein-energy balance
- Essential nutrient provision
- Quality ingredient selection
- Proper feed storage
