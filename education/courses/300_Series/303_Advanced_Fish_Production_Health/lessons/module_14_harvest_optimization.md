## 1. Harvest Planning

### Market Size Determination

```
HARVEST SIZE OPTIMIZATION

Economic Factors:
  • Market price per kg
  • Feed cost
  • FCR at different sizes
  • Growing time
  • Market demand

Break-Even Analysis:
  Total Cost = Fixed Costs + (Variable Cost × Weight)
  Revenue = Price × Weight
  Profit = Revenue - Total Cost


EXAMPLE (Tilapia):

Size  │ Days │ Price  │ Feed  │ Total │ Profit │ $/Day
──────┼──────┼────────┼───────┼───────┼────────┼──────
400g  │ 180  │ $2.00  │ $0.60 │ $0.90 │ $1.10  │$0.0061
500g  │ 210  │ $2.50  │ $0.75 │ $1.05 │ $1.45  │$0.0069 ←BEST
600g  │ 245  │ $3.00  │ $0.92 │ $1.22 │ $1.78  │$0.0073
700g  │ 285  │ $3.50  │ $1.12 │ $1.42 │ $2.08  │$0.0073

Optimal: 600-700g (best profit per day)
```

### Selective Harvest

```
GRADED HARVEST STRATEGY

Benefits:
  ✓ Harvest only market-size fish
  ✓ Allow smaller fish to continue growing
  ✓ Continuous production
  ✓ Optimized tank usage
  ✓ Better price realization

Implementation:
  1. Grade population
  2. Harvest largest class
  3. Return smaller fish to tank
  4. Reduced density improves growth
  5. Repeat every 2-4 weeks


Example Production:
  Month 0: Stock 1,000 fingerlings
  Month 6: Harvest 300 large fish (500g)
  Month 7: Harvest 350 medium fish (500g)
  Month 8: Harvest 320 small fish (final)

  Total: 970 fish (97% survival)
  Average size: 500g
  Extended production but better utilization
```

## 2. Pre-Harvest Management

### Feed Withdrawal

```
PURGING PROTOCOL

Purpose:
  • Empty digestive tract
  • Reduce contamination during processing
  • Improve flesh quality
  • Extend shelf life

Duration by Temperature:

  Temperature │ Withdrawal Time
  ────────────┼────────────────
  15°C        │ 48-72 hours
  20°C        │ 36-48 hours
  25°C        │ 24-36 hours
  30°C        │ 24 hours


Verification:
  - Sample fish and check gut contents
  - Should be empty or minimal
  - Adjust time if needed


Water Quality During Withdrawal:
  ✓ Maintain high DO
  ✓ Continue water exchange
  ✓ Monitor ammonia (may drop)
  ✓ Fish will be less active (normal)
```

### Pre-Harvest Stress Reduction

```
STRESS MINIMIZATION

48 Hours Before Harvest:
  □ Reduce handling
  □ Maintain routine
  □ Optimal water quality
  □ Stop feeding (withdrawal period)
  □ Avoid loud noises
  □ Minimize light changes

Benefits of Low Stress:
  ✓ Better flesh quality
  ✓ Reduced bruising
  ✓ Less gaping (muscle separation)
  ✓ Extended shelf life
  ✓ Reduced blood spots
  ✓ Better fillet yield


Indicators of Pre-Harvest Stress:
  ✗ Fish jumping
  ✗ Aggressive swimming
  ✗ Color changes
  ✗ High mortality
  → Delay harvest if possible
```

## 3. Harvest Methods

### Live Harvest Procedures

```
NETTING AND CROWDING

Equipment:
  - Appropriate-sized nets (mesh size)
  - Crowding panels
  - Harvest tubs/tanks
  - Aeration in holding containers

Procedure:
  1. Lower water level (if possible)
  2. Use crowding panel to concentrate fish
  3. Net carefully (avoid scale loss)
  4. Transfer to harvest container
  5. Minimize air exposure (<30 seconds)
  6. Count or weigh
  7. Transport or process


PUMP HARVEST:

  For large operations:
    - Fish-friendly pumps
    - Vacuum or airlift systems
    - Minimizes handling stress
    - Gentle transfer
    - Direct to processing or transport


SEINE HARVEST (Ponds):

  - Drag net through pond
  - Concentrate in corner or harvest area
  - Dip net transfer
  - Reduce pond water to assist
```

### Humane Slaughter

```
APPROVED METHODS

1. ICE SLURRY (Cold water species):
   - Rapid chilling
   - Insensibility within minutes
   - Common for trout, salmon
   - Simple and effective

2. PERCUSSION (Stunning):
   - Sharp blow to head
   - Immediate unconsciousness
   - Followed by bleeding or icing
   - Small-scale operations

3. ELECTRICAL STUNNING:
   - Controlled current
   - Immediate unconsciousness
   - Requires proper equipment
   - Larger operations

4. CO₂ ANESTHESIA (then slaughter):
   - Immersion in CO₂-saturated water
   - Fish become unconscious
   - Follow with icing or bleeding
   - Welfare approved for some species

5. ANESTHETIC OVERDOSE:
   - Clove oil (50-500 mg/L)
   - Gradual unconsciousness
   - Followed by icing
   - More expensive


NOT ACCEPTABLE:
  ✗ Suffocation in air
  ✗ Ammonia exposure
  ✗ Boiling water
  ✗ Freezing while conscious


VERIFICATION OF DEATH:

  - No opercular movement (>10 min)
  - No response to stimuli
  - No eye movement
  - Cardiac arrest (if monitored)
```

## 4. Post-Harvest Handling

### Chilling and Icing

```
TEMPERATURE MANAGEMENT

Target: Reduce core temperature to 0-4°C rapidly

Ice-to-Fish Ratio:
  1:1 to 2:1 (ice:fish by weight)
  More ice = Faster chilling

Chilling Procedure:
  1. Harvest fish
  2. Place immediately in ice slurry
  3. Layer: Ice-Fish-Ice-Fish
  4. Drain melt water regularly
  5. Replenish ice
  6. Target temperature in <2 hours

Benefits of Rapid Chilling:
  ✓ Slows microbial growth
  ✓ Delays rigor mortis
  ✓ Preserves quality
  ✓ Extends shelf life
  ✓ Maintains texture


RIGOR MORTIS MANAGEMENT:

  Fish entering rigor = Firm, contracted
  Post-rigor = Relaxed, softer

  Best to process:
    - Pre-rigor (immediately)
    - Post-rigor (12-24 hours, species-dependent)

  Avoid processing during rigor:
    - Difficult to fillet
    - Gaping (muscle separation)
    - Poor yield
```

### Quality Control

```
QUALITY ASSESSMENT

FRESHNESS INDICATORS:

  Eyes:
    Fresh: Clear, bright, convex
    Old: Cloudy, sunken, dull

  Gills:
    Fresh: Bright red, no odor
    Old: Brown/gray, slimy, ammonia smell

  Skin:
    Fresh: Bright color, clear mucus
    Old: Dull, yellow/brown mucus

  Flesh:
    Fresh: Firm, elastic (springs back)
    Old: Soft, indentation remains

  Odor:
    Fresh: Sea/fresh smell, mild
    Old: Ammonia, sour, putrid


QUALITY SCORING:

  Grade A (Premium):
    - Excellent appearance
    - Firm flesh
    - Bright gills
    - No damage

  Grade B (Standard):
    - Good appearance
    - Slightly soft
    - Minor blemishes

  Grade C (Processing):
    - Acceptable for value-added
    - Not for fresh market
    - Cosmetic issues


DEFECTS TO MONITOR:

  ✗ Bruising (poor handling)
  ✗ Scale loss (rough netting)
  ✗ Hemorrhaging (stress)
  ✗ Broken fins
  ✗ Gaping (rigor issues)
  ✗ Off-flavors (water quality)
  ✗ Parasites (visible)
  ✗ Deformities
```

## 5. Processing and Value Addition

### Primary Processing

```
PROCESSING STEPS

WHOLE FISH (Dressed):
  1. Kill humanely
  2. Chill rapidly
  3. Remove scales (if needed)
  4. Eviscerate (gut)
  5. Wash thoroughly
  6. Ice or refrigerate
  7. Package

FILLETING:
  1. Scale (if needed)
  2. Remove head
  3. Cut behind gill plate
  4. Fillet along backbone
  5. Remove pin bones (if needed)
  6. Skin (optional)
  7. Trim and wash
  8. Chill

  Yield: 30-45% of whole weight (species-dependent)


VALUE-ADDED PRODUCTS:

  • Smoked fillets
  • Marinated products
  • Breaded/battered portions
  • Fish sausages
  • Fish burgers
  • Dried/salted
  • Canned

  Benefits:
    ✓ Higher price
    ✓ Longer shelf life
    ✓ Market differentiation
    ✓ Use of smaller/damaged fish
```

## 6. Live Transport

### Transport Preparation

```
LIVE HAUL PROTOCOL

Pre-Transport Conditioning:
  □ Feed withdrawal (24-48 hours)
  □ Gradual temperature acclimation
  □ Health inspection
  □ Reduce stress

Transport Density:
  Conservative: 100-150 kg/m³
  Intensive: 200-300 kg/m³ (with pure O₂)

Water Quality Management:
  • Pure O₂ injection (maintain >7 mg/L)
  • Salt addition (3-5 ppt, reduces stress)
  • Temperature control (cool is better)
  • Buffering (maintain pH)

Equipment:
  - Insulated transport tank
  - Oxygen system (tank or generator)
  - Aeration backup
  - DO meter
  - Temperature control


DURING TRANSPORT:

  □ Monitor DO every 30-60 minutes
  □ Maintain oxygen >7 mg/L
  □ Watch fish behavior
  □ Be prepared to stop and aerate
  □ Minimize transport time

  Maximum Transport Times:
    With O₂: 12-24 hours
    Air only: 4-6 hours (low density)


ARRIVAL PROCEDURE:

  1. Test transport water quality
  2. Test receiving water
  3. Temperature acclimation (gradual)
  4. Net transfer or pump
  5. Monitor for 24-48 hours
  6. Expect some stress mortality (<2%)
```

## 7. Harvest Economics

### Cost Analysis

```
HARVEST ECONOMICS

Direct Costs:
  • Labor (harvest crew)
  • Ice
  • Packaging materials
  • Transportation
  • Processing (if applicable)
  • Energy (refrigeration)

Example Calculation (1,000 kg harvest):

  Labor: 4 workers × 4 hours × $15/hr = $240
  Ice: 1,000 kg × $0.15/kg = $150
  Packaging: 200 boxes × $1.50 = $300
  Transport: $200
  Processing: $0.50/kg × 1,000 = $500
  Total: $1,390

  Cost per kg: $1.39


Revenue Options:

  Live Fish: $4.00/kg × 1,000 kg = $4,000
  Whole Dressed: $5.50/kg × 1,000 kg = $5,500
  Fillets: $12/kg × 400 kg (40% yield) = $4,800

  Best margin: Fillets (if market exists)
  Easiest: Live or whole dressed
```

### Market Timing

```
SEASONAL PRICING

Plan harvests for premium periods:

  High Demand Periods:
    • Religious holidays
    • Summer (grilling season)
    • Lent (fish consumption increases)
    • Cultural events

  Price Variations:
    Peak season: 1.5-2× base price
    Off-season: 0.7-0.9× base price

  Strategy:
    - Stock to harvest in peak periods
    - Hold fish (if possible) for better prices
    - Diversify markets
    - Contract sales in advance


STORAGE OPTIONS:

  If delaying harvest:
    ✓ Ensure adequate tank space
    ✓ Maintain water quality
    ✓ Continue feeding (maintenance ration)
    ✓ Monitor for disease
    ✓ Don't exceed density limits

  Alternative: Blast freeze for later sale
    - Frozen product
    - Lower price than fresh
    - Extended storage (months)
```

## 8. Food Safety and Regulations

### HACCP Principles

```
HAZARD ANALYSIS CRITICAL CONTROL POINTS

Critical Control Points in Harvest:

  CCP 1: Harvest Water Quality
    Hazard: Contamination
    Control: Test before harvest
    Limit: Meet standards for food fish
    Monitor: Water quality tests

  CCP 2: Chilling Rate
    Hazard: Bacterial growth
    Control: Ice immediately
    Limit: Reach 4°C within 2 hours
    Monitor: Temperature probes

  CCP 3: Cross-Contamination
    Hazard: Pathogen transfer
    Control: Sanitation protocols
    Limit: Clean equipment
    Monitor: Visual inspection, ATP testing

  CCP 4: Time-Temperature
    Hazard: Spoilage
    Control: Maintain cold chain
    Limit: 0-4°C at all times
    Monitor: Continuous logging


DOCUMENTATION:

  Required Records:
    □ Harvest date and time
    □ Tank/source identification
    □ Quantity harvested
    □ Temperature logs
    □ Processing records
    □ Water quality results
    □ Lot traceability
    □ Quality assessments
```

  • Pet treats
