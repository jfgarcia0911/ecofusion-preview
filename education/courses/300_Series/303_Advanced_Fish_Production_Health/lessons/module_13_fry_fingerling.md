# Module 13: Fry & Fingerling Production

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Course:** 303 - Advanced Fish Production & Health

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Manage larval rearing systems
2. Optimize fry feeding programs
3. Implement grading and size management
4. Minimize mortality during early life stages
5. Transition fingerlings to grow-out

---

## 1. Larval Rearing Systems

### System Design

```
LARVAL REARING TANKS

Tank Size: 100-500L for intensive rearing
Shape: Circular (better flow)
Color: Light (easy observation) or dark (reduces stress)
Bottom: Central drain or flat with siphon

Water Quality:
  DO: >7 mg/L
  Temperature: Optimal (25-28°C warmwater)
  pH: 7.0-8.0
  Ammonia: <0.01 mg/L (larvae very sensitive)
  Nitrite: <0.05 mg/L

Flow Rate: 2-4 turnovers per hour
Gentle aeration: Fine bubbles, not aggressive


STOCKING DENSITY:

  Initial (3-5 dph): 50-100 larvae/L
  Week 1-2: 20-50/L
  Week 2-4: 10-20/L

  Reduce density as fish grow
```

## 2. First Feeding

### Live Feed Production

```
ARTEMIA (Brine Shrimp) HATCHING

Materials:
  - Artemia cysts (high-quality)
  - Conical hatching vessel
  - Air stone
  - Light source
  - Salt (25-35 ppt)

Protocol:
  1. Fill vessel with saltwater
  2. Add cysts (2-5 g/L)
  3. Strong aeration
  4. Light (24h)
  5. Temperature: 25-28°C
  6. Hatch time: 18-24 hours
  7. Harvest nauplii
  8. Rinse before feeding


ROTIFER CULTURE:

  Species: Brachionus plicatilis
  Density: 100-300/mL
  Feed: Microalgae (Nannochloropsis, Chlorella) or yeast
  Harvest: Daily
  Use: Smaller than Artemia, for marine larvae


FEEDING SCHEDULE:

  Days 1-3: Rotifers (if available)
  Days 3-7: Artemia nauplii (small)
  Days 7-14: Artemia + micro-pellets
  Days 14+: Weaning to dry feed
```

### Weaning to Dry Feed

```
WEANING PROTOCOL

Gradual Transition (7-10 days):

  Day 1-3:  90% live feed, 10% micro-pellet
  Day 4-6:  70% live feed, 30% micro-pellet
  Day 7-9:  50% live feed, 50% micro-pellet
  Day 10-12: 25% live feed, 75% micro-pellet
  Day 13+:   100% micro-pellet

Micro-Pellet Size: 100-400 microns initially

Co-feeding Benefits:
  ✓ Better survival
  ✓ Improved growth
  ✓ Easier transition
  ✓ Reduced stress
```

## 3. Fry Management

### Size Grading

```
GRADING PROTOCOL

When to Grade:
  ✓ Size variation >20% CV
  ✓ Visible size differences causing competition
  ✓ Every 2-3 weeks in intensive systems

Grading Methods:

  Bar Graders:
    - Adjustable spacing
    - Size classes by width
    - Gentle on fish

  Mesh Graders:
    - Different mesh sizes
    - Multiple passes
    - Quick sorting

Size Classes (Typically 3):
  Small: <80% mean weight
  Medium: 80-120% mean
  Large: >120% mean


POST-GRADING MANAGEMENT:

  □ Fast grading minimizes stress
  □ Use anesthetic if needed (clove oil)
  □ Grade into separate tanks
  □ Adjust feeding rates per tank
  □ Continue monitoring
```

### Disease Prevention in Fry

```
CRITICAL CONTROL POINTS

WATER QUALITY:
  - More sensitive than adults
  - Lower tolerance for NH₃, NO₂
  - Higher DO requirement
  - Stable temperature critical

NUTRITION:
  - High-protein starter feed (50%+)
  - Frequent feeding (6-8×/day initially)
  - Overfeeding causes mortality
  - Fresh feed essential

STOCKING DENSITY:
  - Avoid overcrowding
  - Regular thinning/grading
  - Monitor growth weekly

BIOSECURITY:
  - Dedicated equipment
  - Fry area isolated
  - Minimize handling
  - Quarantine any transfers


COMMON FRY DISEASES:

  Bacterial (opportunistic):
    - Columnaris
    - Bacterial gill disease
    - Prevention: Water quality, low density

  Parasitic:
    - Trichodina (very common)
    - Ichthyobodo (Costia)
    - Treatment: Salt 1-2 ppt, formalin (reduced dose)

  Nutritional:
    - Swim bladder inflation problems
    - Deformities from vitamin deficiency
    - Solution: Quality feed, proper nutrition
```

## 4. Nursery Phase

### Fingerling Production

```
NURSERY MANAGEMENT (30-90 days post-hatch)

Transition from Fry Systems:
  Size: 1-3 cm
  Move to larger tanks (500-2000L)
  Increase water flow
  Reduce feeding frequency

Feeding Program:
  Feed: Fry/fingerling crumble (45-50% protein)
  Rate: 5-10% BW/day (declining with size)
  Frequency: 4-6 times daily
  Pellet size: Increase with fish size (1-2mm)

Growth Targets:

  Species      │ Age (days) │ Target Size
  ─────────────┼────────────┼─────────────
  Tilapia      │     60     │   20-30g
  Catfish      │     90     │   25-40g
  Trout        │     90     │   15-25g
  Bass         │     75     │   10-20g


DENSITY MANAGEMENT:

  Initial: 1,000-2,000 fingerlings/m³
  Final: 200-500/m³

  Progressive Thinning:
    - Grade every 3-4 weeks
    - Move larger fish to grow-out
    - Reduce density of smaller fish
    - Maintain optimal growth rates
```

## 5. Quality Control

### Fingerling Quality Assessment

```
QUALITY CRITERIA

PHYSICAL:
  □ Uniform size (CV <20%)
  □ No deformities
  □ Good body condition (K >1.0)
  □ Normal pigmentation
  □ Fins intact
  □ Active swimming

HEALTH:
  □ Zero or low parasite load
  □ No disease signs
  □ Good appetite
  □ Appropriate behavior

PERFORMANCE:
  □ Survival >80% (hatch to fingerling)
  □ Growth on target
  □ Feed conversion <1.5


SWIM BLADDER INFLATION:

  Test (for species that require it):
    - Sample 50-100 fingerlings
    - Examine for inflated swim bladder
    - Target: >95% inflation
    - Poor inflation indicates problems

  Causes of Non-Inflation:
    - Poor water surface access
    - Low DO during critical period
    - Nutritional deficiency
    - Bacterial infection
```

---

## Summary

Successful fry and fingerling production requires:
- Optimal water quality (critical for larvae)
- Proper first feeding (live feed often needed)
- Gradual weaning to dry feed
- Regular grading to maintain uniformity
- Disease prevention through biosecurity
- Quality control monitoring

Target: >70% survival from hatch to fingerling
