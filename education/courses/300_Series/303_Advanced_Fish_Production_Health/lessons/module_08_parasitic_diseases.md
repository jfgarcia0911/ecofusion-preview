## 2. Ichthyophthirius (Ich/White Spot Disease)

### Organism Characteristics

**Pathogen:** *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*
- Ciliated protozoan
- Life cycle: 3-7 days (temperature-dependent)
- Cannot survive >48 hours without host
- Most common freshwater parasite

### Life Cycle

```
ICH LIFE CYCLE

Day 0-3: TROPHONT (Feeding Stage)
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │  Embedded in skin    │
    │  Under epithelium    │  Visible white spot
    │  Feeds on cells      │  0.5-1.0 mm
    │  Grows larger        │
    └──────┬───────────────┘
           │ Matures and exits
           v
Day 3-4: TOMONT (Reproductive Stage)
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │  Falls to substrate  │
    │  Encysts             │  Protected
    │  Divides (100-1000×) │  Resistant to treatment
    └──────┬───────────────┘
           │ Releases theronts
           v
Day 5-7: THERONT (Infectious Stage)
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │  Free-swimming       │
    │  Must find host      │  Vulnerable
    │  Within 48 hours     │  to treatment
    │  or dies             │
    └──────┬───────────────┘
           │ Penetrates fish
           v
    CYCLE REPEATS


TEMPERATURE EFFECT ON CYCLE:

Temp (°C)│ Cycle Time │ Implications
─────────┼────────────┼─────────────────────
   10    │  21 days   │ Slow progression
   15    │  10 days   │ Moderate
   20    │   7 days   │ Typical
   25    │   4 days   │ Rapid outbreak
   30    │   3 days   │ Very rapid
```

### Clinical Signs

```
PROGRESSION OF INFECTION

Early (Days 1-3):
  • Flashing (rubbing on surfaces)
  • Increased gill movement
  • Few visible spots
  • Behavioral changes

Peak (Days 4-7):
  • Numerous white spots (0.5-1mm)
  • Spots on fins, body, gills
  • Respiratory distress
  • Lethargy
  • Anorexia
  • Secondary infections

Late (Days 8+):
  • Severe gill damage
  • Osmoregulatory failure
  • High mortality
  • Surviving fish may recover


VISUAL IDENTIFICATION:

    ┌───────────────────────┐
    │ WHITE SPOT DISEASE    │
    │                       │
    │  ●  ●   ●            │  Small white spots
    │    ●      ●  ●       │  Like salt grains
    │  ●    ●     ●        │  0.5-1.0 mm
    │     ●   ●            │  Raised bumps
    │                       │
    │  FINS                 │
    │  ═●═●═●═             │  Spots on fins
    │                       │
    └───────────────────────┘
```

### Diagnosis

```
DIAGNOSTIC METHODS:

1. Clinical Signs:
   ✓ Characteristic white spots
   ✓ Flashing behavior
   ✓ Distribution pattern

2. Wet Mount:
   • Scrape white spot
   • Mount in saline
   • Observe at 100-400× magnification

   Identification:
   - Large (50-1000 μm)
   - Horseshoe-shaped macronucleus (C-shape)
   - Ciliated (moving)
   - Rotating motion

3. Gill Examination:
   • Most damaging site
   • May not have visible external spots
   • Gill wet mount essential
```

### Treatment

**Three-Pronged Approach:**

```
STRATEGY 1: SALT TREATMENT

Prolonged Bath:
  Dose: 1-3 ppt (1-3 g/L)
  Duration: Until 3-5 days after spots disappear
  Mechanism: Osmotic stress on theronts
  Efficacy: 70-90%

  Calculate amount:
    Salt (kg) = Volume (m³) × Concentration (g/L)
    Example: 10 m³ × 2 g/L = 20 kg salt

Advantages:
  ✓ Safe for most fish
  ✓ Inexpensive
  ✓ Readily available
  ✓ No withdrawal

Disadvantages:
  ✗ Not tolerated by all species
  ✗ Moderate efficacy
  ✗ Affects biofilter
  ✗ Requires monitoring


STRATEGY 2: FORMALIN TREATMENT

Dose: 15-25 mg/L (ppm)
Duration: 1 hour
Frequency: Every 2-3 days, minimum 3 treatments
Targets: Free-swimming theronts

Procedure:
  Day 1: First treatment
  Day 3: Second treatment (hits next generation)
  Day 5: Third treatment (hits stragglers)
  Day 7+: Additional if needed

CRITICAL:
  ⚠ Monitor DO - formalin reduces oxygen
  ⚠ Increase aeration during treatment
  ⚠ Remove if fish show distress
  ⚠ Toxic to some species (catfish, loaches)


STRATEGY 3: TEMPERATURE MANIPULATION

Mechanism: Speed up life cycle, treat vulnerable stages

Protocol:
  1. Raise temperature to 28-30°C (if tolerated)
  2. Life cycle completes in 3-4 days
  3. Combine with formalin/salt
  4. Theronts emerge faster
  5. Treatment more effective

Caution:
  • Only for warm-water species
  • Maintain high DO
  • Increases metabolism and stress


STRATEGY 4: COPPER SULFATE (Use with Caution)

Dose: 0.15-0.25 mg/L (ppm)
Duration: Continuous until resolved
Monitoring: Test copper daily, maintain level

Factors:
  • Alkalinity affects toxicity
  • pH dependent
  • Toxic to some species
  • Expensive test required
  • Can damage biofilter

NOT RECOMMENDED for beginners


STRATEGY 5: CHLORAMINE-T

Dose: 3-10 mg/L
Duration: 30-60 minutes
Frequency: Every other day × 3 treatments

Good alternative to formalin
Less oxygen depletion
```

### Prevention

```
ICH PREVENTION PROTOCOL

Quarantine:
  ✓ 30-day minimum for new fish
  ✓ Observe for clinical signs
  ✓ Treat if any parasites detected
  ✓ Separate water systems

Temperature Management:
  ✓ Avoid sudden temperature changes
  ✓ Gradual acclimation (<2°C/day)
  ✓ Stable temperatures reduce outbreaks

Stress Reduction:
  ✓ Optimal water quality
  ✓ Proper stocking density
  ✓ Minimize handling
  ✓ Good nutrition

System Management:
  ✓ UV sterilization (kills theronts)
  ✓ Ozone treatment
  ✓ Drying between crops
  ✓ Disinfection protocols
```

## 3. Trichodina Species

### Organism Characteristics

**Pathogen:** *Trichodina* spp.
- Ciliated protozoan
- Disc-shaped with denticles
- Direct life cycle
- External parasite (skin, gills)

### Identification and Signs

```
TRICHODINA IDENTIFICATION

Microscopy (400×):
    ╔════════════════╗
    ║   ┌────────┐   ║
    ║  ╱ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ╲  ║  Circular shape
    ║ │ Denticles  │ ║  50-80 μm diameter
    ║ │   (teeth)  │ ║  Denticle ring
    ║  ╲ ▼ ▼ ▼ ▼ ╱  ║  Rapid spinning
    ║   └────────┘   ║
    ╚════════════════╝

Clinical Signs:
  • Increased mucus production
  • Cloudy skin appearance
  • Flashing behavior
  • Respiratory distress (if on gills)
  • Usually low-moderate pathogenicity
  • Opportunistic (stress-related)

Heavy Infections:
  • Epithelial damage
  • Secondary bacterial infections
  • Osmoregulatory problems
  • Mortality in severe cases
```

### Treatment

```
TRICHODINA TREATMENT

Salt Bath:
  1-3 ppt continuous
  Very effective

Formalin:
  15-25 mg/L × 1 hour
  Repeat every 2-3 days

Potassium Permanganate:
  2-4 mg/L × 30-60 minutes
  Good efficacy

Hydrogen Peroxide:
  50-100 mg/L × 30-60 minutes
  Emerging treatment

Prevention:
  • Reduce organic matter
  • Improve water flow
  • Decrease stocking density
  • Minimize stress
```

## 4. Ichthyobodo (Costia)

### Organism and Clinical Disease

```
COSTIA DISEASE

Organism: Ichthyobodo necator
  - Small flagellate (10-20 μm)
  - Kidney/pear-shaped
  - Two flagella
  - Cold water favored (<20°C)

Clinical Signs:
  • Excessive mucus ("blue slime")
  • Cloudy, opaque skin
  • Respiratory distress
  • Flashing
  • Lethargic swimming
  • High mortality in fry

Diagnosis:
  Fresh wet mount essential
  - Dead within minutes off host
  - Small size (easy to miss)
  - Vibrating/dancing motion
  - Look at 400× magnification

Treatment:
  Formalin: 15-25 mg/L × 1 hour, repeat 2-3×
  Salt: 3-5 ppt
  Potassium permanganate: 2 mg/L
  Hydrogen peroxide: 50-100 mg/L

Prevention:
  • Optimal water quality
  • Reduce organics
  • Avoid overcrowding
  • Good aeration
```

## 5. Monogenean Flukes

### Dactylogyrus (Gill Flukes)

```
GILL FLUKE DISEASE

Organism: Dactylogyrus spp.
  - Monogenean (direct life cycle)
  - 0.5-1.0 mm
  - Four eye spots
  - Hooks for attachment
  - Eggs hatch in 4-10 days

Location: Gill filaments

Clinical Signs:
  • Respiratory distress
  • Pale or dark gills
  • Increased gill movement
  • Lethargy
  • Anorexia
  • Poor growth

Diagnosis:
  Gill wet mount
  - Visible at 40-100× magnification
  - Four eyespots characteristic
  - Attachment hooks visible
  - Count flukes per gill arch

Treatment:
  Praziquantel: 2-10 mg/L × 3-24 hours
  Formalin: 15-25 mg/L × 1 hour, repeat
  Salt: 1-3% dip × 5-10 minutes
  Potassium permanganate: 2-4 mg/L
```

### Gyrodactylus (Skin Flukes)

```
SKIN FLUKE DISEASE

Organism: Gyrodactylus spp.
  - Live-bearing (no eggs)
  - 0.3-0.8 mm
  - Can see embryo inside adult
  - Direct life cycle
  - Rapid reproduction

Location: Skin, fins

Clinical Signs:
  • Cloudy skin
  • Fin erosion
  • Flashing
  • Red, inflamed areas
  • Scale loss

Diagnosis:
  Skin scraping
  - Smaller than Dactylogyrus
  - Visible embryo inside
  - Attachment hooks

Treatment:
  Same as Dactylogyrus
  Praziquantel most effective
  Must treat 2-3 times (born pregnant)
```

## 6. Crustacean Parasites

### Lernaea (Anchor Worm)

```
ANCHOR WORM INFESTATION

Organism: Lernaea cyprinacea
  - Copepod crustacean
  - Female parasitic (5-15 mm)
  - Anchor-shaped head burrows into tissue
  - Visible to naked eye

Life Cycle: 18-25 days
  Eggs → Nauplii → Copepodid → Adult

Clinical Signs:
  • Visible parasites (thread-like)
  • Inflammation at attachment site
  • Ulcers
  • Secondary bacterial infections
  • Behavioral changes

    ┌────────────────┐
    │   ~~~~~~~~     │  Thread-like
    │   ~~~\/~~~     │  body extending
    │      /\        │  from fish
    │     /  \       │  Anchor embedded
    │  ══╧════╧══   │  in muscle
    └────────────────┘

Treatment:
  Manual Removal:
    - Use forceps
    - Pull straight out
    - Disinfect wound
    - Time consuming

  Chemical:
    - Organophosphates (Dipterex/Dylox)
    - Dose: 0.25-0.50 mg/L
    - Kills free-swimming stages
    - Repeat treatment needed

  Salt:
    - 3% dip × 5-10 minutes
    - Limited efficacy

Prevention:
  • Screen incoming water
  • Quarantine new fish
  • Avoid wild fish contact
  • Good water quality
```

### Argulus (Fish Louse)

```
FISH LOUSE INFESTATION

Organism: Argulus spp.
  - Branchiuran crustacean
  - Disc-shaped, 5-10 mm
  - Two large eyes visible
  - Mobile on fish surface
  - Powerful swimmer

Clinical Signs:
  • Visible parasites
  • Erratic behavior
  • Flashing
  • Jumping
  • Hemorrhagic wounds
  • Secondary infections

Damage:
  • Piercing mouthparts
  • Tissue feeding
  • Toxic saliva
  • Disease transmission

Treatment:
  Organophosphates:
    Dipterex: 0.25-0.50 mg/L
    Repeat every 7 days × 3

  Potassium Permanganate:
    2-3 mg/L × 30-60 minutes

  Manual Removal:
    Pick off visible parasites

Prevention:
  • Screen water intakes
  • UV treatment
  • Avoid contamination
```

## 7. Internal Parasites

### Intestinal Parasites

```
COMMON INTERNAL PARASITES

Cestodes (Tapeworms):
  Location: Intestine, body cavity
  Signs: Poor growth, distended abdomen
  Diagnosis: Necropsy, fecal exam
  Treatment: Praziquantel in feed

Nematodes (Roundworms):
  Species: Camallanus, Capillaria
  Signs: Wasting, anemia
  Diagnosis: Necropsy, visible worms
  Treatment: Levamisole, fenbendazole

Coccidians (Eimeria):
  Location: Intestinal cells
  Signs: Enteritis, poor growth
  Diagnosis: Fecal oocysts, histology
  Treatment: Anticoccidials in feed
```

## 8. Myxozoan Parasites

### Whirling Disease

```
MYXOBOLUS CEREBRALIS

Disease: Whirling Disease (Salmonids)

Life Cycle: Complex
  Fish → Spores → Tubifex worm → TAMs → Fish

Clinical Signs:
  • Whirling swimming (tail-chasing)
  • Skeletal deformities
  • Black tail
  • High mortality in young fish
  • Cartilage damage

Control:
  ✗ No treatment exists
  ✓ Eliminate Tubifex worms (intermediate host)
  ✓ Reduce contact with wild fish
  ✓ Use underground water
  ✓ Disinfection between crops
  ✓ Selective breeding (resistant strains)

Prevention Critical:
  • Source certified disease-free stock
  • Avoid endemic waters
  • Concrete raceways (not earthen)
```

## 9. Parasite Treatment Protocols

### Treatment Selection Guide

```
PARASITE TREATMENT MATRIX

Parasite          │ 1st Choice      │ 2nd Choice     │ 3rd Choice
──────────────────┼─────────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────
Ich               │ Salt 1-3 ppt    │ Formalin       │ Temperature↑
Trichodina        │ Salt 1-3 ppt    │ Formalin       │ KMnO₄
Ichthyobodo       │ Formalin        │ Salt 3-5 ppt   │ H₂O₂
Dactylogyrus      │ Praziquantel    │ Formalin       │ Salt dip
Gyrodactylus      │ Praziquantel    │ Formalin       │ KMnO₄
Lernaea           │ Organophosphate │ Manual removal │ -
Argulus           │ Organophosphate │ KMnO₄          │ Manual
Tapeworms         │ Praziquantel    │ -              │ -
Nematodes         │ Levamisole      │ Fenbendazole   │ -
```

### Treatment Chemicals

**Safety Data:**

```
CHEMICAL TREATMENTS - CRITICAL INFORMATION

FORMALIN (37% Formaldehyde):
  Dose: 15-25 mg/L (ppm) of stock solution
  Duration: 1 hour
  Frequency: Every 2-3 days

  WARNINGS:
    ⚠ Carcinogenic - use PPE
    ⚠ Depletes oxygen - increase aeration
    ⚠ Toxic to some species
    ⚠ pH and alkalinity dependent
    ⚠ Proper disposal required


SALT (NaCl):
  Dose: 1-3 g/L (ppt) continuous
       3% (30 g/L) short dip

  Calculation:
    kg salt = m³ × g/L desired

  Considerations:
    • Affects osmoregulation
    • Impacts biofilter
    • Not all species tolerant
    • Cheap and safe


POTASSIUM PERMANGANATE (KMnO₄):
  Dose: 2-4 mg/L
  Duration: 30-60 minutes

  CRITICAL:
    • Dose depends on organic matter
    • Perform demand test first
    • Purple color indicates active
    • Brown = oxidized (inactive)
    • Remove if fish distress


PRAZIQUANTEL:
  Dose: 2-10 mg/L continuous for 3-24 hours
        OR in feed: 25-50 mg/kg fish

  Uses: Flukes, tapeworms
  Safety: Very safe
  Cost: Expensive


HYDROGEN PEROXIDE:
  Dose: 50-100 mg/L
  Duration: 30-60 minutes

  Notes:
    • Degrades rapidly
    • Oxygen boost
    • Emerging treatment
    • Variable efficacy
```

## 10. Integrated Parasite Management

### Comprehensive Control Strategy

```
IPM - INTEGRATED PARASITE MANAGEMENT

PREVENTION (Priority 1):
  □ Quarantine all new stock (30+ days)
  □ Source from reputable suppliers
  □ Maintain optimal water quality
  □ Reduce stress factors
  □ Proper stocking densities
  □ Good nutrition (immune support)
  □ Regular health monitoring

MONITORING (Priority 2):
  □ Weekly microscopic exams (10 fish)
  □ Record parasite loads
  □ Track environmental data
  □ Observe behavior daily
  □ Seasonal awareness (some parasites seasonal)

THRESHOLD-BASED TREATMENT (Priority 3):
  Light infection: Monitor, improve conditions
  Moderate infection: Consider treatment
  Heavy infection: Treat immediately

TREATMENT (Priority 4):
  □ Identify parasite accurately
  □ Select appropriate chemical
  □ Calculate dose correctly
  □ Monitor during treatment
  □ Repeat as needed
  □ Evaluate efficacy

POST-TREATMENT (Priority 5):
  □ Improve conditions that led to outbreak
  □ Continue monitoring
  □ Support fish recovery (nutrition, water quality)
  □ Clean and disinfect
  □ Prevent recurrence


BIOSECURITY MEASURES:
  ✓ Dedicated equipment per tank
  ✓ Disinfect nets (bleach, dry, UV)
  ✓ Footbaths between systems
  ✓ Hand washing protocols
  ✓ Wildlife exclusion
  ✓ Water treatment (UV, ozone)
  ✓ Drying between production cycles
```

## Summary

Parasitic diseases require:

1. **Accurate Identification** - Microscopy skills essential
2. **Understanding Life Cycles** - Target vulnerable stages
3. **Appropriate Treatment** - Select based on parasite type
4. **Prevention Focus** - Reduce introduction and stress
5. **Integrated Management** - Combine multiple strategies

Major Parasite Groups:
- **Protozoa** - Ich, Trichodina, Costia
- **Monogeneans** - Gill and skin flukes
- **Crustaceans** - Anchor worm, fish louse
- **Internal** - Tapeworms, roundworms
- **Myxozoans** - Whirling disease

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Prevention** is more effective than treatment
2. **Stress** predisposes fish to parasitic infections
3. **Life cycles** determine treatment timing and frequency
4. **Accurate diagnosis** requires microscopy
5. **Multiple treatments** usually needed (repeat infections)
6. **Water quality** is foundation of parasite control
