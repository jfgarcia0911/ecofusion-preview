### Risk Assessment

```
BIOSECURITY RISK MATRIX

Risk = Likelihood × Consequence

Likelihood:           Consequence:
5 = Very likely       5 = Catastrophic (total loss)
4 = Likely           4 = Major (>50% loss)
3 = Possible         3 = Moderate (25-50%)
2 = Unlikely         2 = Minor (10-25%)
1 = Rare             1 = Minimal (<10%)

Risk Score = L × C

Score 20-25: Critical - Immediate action
Score 15-19: High - Priority action
Score 10-14: Medium - Planned action
Score 5-9: Low - Monitor
Score 1-4: Minimal - Accept


EXAMPLE ASSESSMENT:

Threat: Introduction of KHV through new koi purchase

Likelihood: 4 (common in ornamental trade)
Consequence: 5 (can wipe out entire stock)
Risk Score: 20 (CRITICAL)

Mitigation:
  ✓ 60-day quarantine
  ✓ PCR testing before introduction
  ✓ Source from certified suppliers
  → Reduces likelihood to 1
  → New risk score: 5 (acceptable)
```

## 3. Personnel Biosecurity

### Training Program

```
STAFF BIOSECURITY TRAINING

INITIAL TRAINING (Orientation):
  □ Disease risk overview
  □ Facility biosecurity policies
  □ Personal hygiene requirements
  □ Entry/exit procedures
  □ Equipment handling
  □ Disease recognition
  □ Emergency response
  □ Documentation requirements

ONGOING TRAINING:
  □ Monthly refreshers
  □ Disease updates
  □ Protocol changes
  □ Case studies
  □ Compliance review

COMPETENCY ASSESSMENT:
  □ Written test (80% pass)
  □ Practical demonstration
  □ Annual recertification
  □ Corrective action for non-compliance


PERSONNEL RULES:

Prohibited:
  ✗ Visiting other fish farms same day
  ✗ Keeping personal fish at home
  ✗ Wearing farm clothes off-site
  ✗ Bringing equipment from outside
  ✗ Entering without shower (high-security)

Required:
  ✓ Dedicated farm clothing/boots
  ✓ Hand washing before/after fish contact
  ✓ Use of footbaths
  ✓ Report any illness
  ✓ Follow all protocols consistently
```

### Visitor Management

```
VISITOR PROTOCOL

Types of Visitors:
  1. Essential (vets, consultants): Full protocol
  2. Business (buyers, auditors): Modified protocol
  3. Public (tours, education): Restricted areas only

Standard Visitor Procedure:
  □ Advance notification required
  □ Sign visitor log (name, contact, last fish contact)
  □ View biosecurity video (5 min)
  □ Don facility-provided PPE
  □ Use footbaths at each zone
  □ Escort at all times
  □ No photography in sensitive areas
  □ Sign out and debrief

High-Risk Visitors (recent fish farm contact):
  □ Minimum 48-hour waiting period
  □ Shower-in required
  □ Change to facility clothes
  □ Limit areas accessed

Restricted Access:
  ✗ Broodstock facilities
  ✗ Quarantine areas
  ✗ SPF (specific pathogen-free) zones
```

## 4. Equipment Biosecurity

### Disinfection Protocols

```
EQUIPMENT DISINFECTION SOP

PROTOCOL: Net Disinfection

Frequency: After each use (between tanks)

Materials:
  - Bucket with disinfectant
  - Rinse bucket with clean water
  - Drying rack
  - Timer

Procedure:
  1. Remove gross contamination (rinse)
  2. Immerse in disinfectant solution
     (200 ppm chlorine or 1% Virkon)
  3. Soak for minimum 10 minutes
  4. Rinse thoroughly in clean water
  5. Hang to dry in UV light (if available)
  6. Store in clean area

Documentation: Check mark on tank sheet

Dedicated Equipment Strategy:
  ✓ Color-code by production area
  ✓ Never share between quarantine and production
  ✓ Label clearly
  ✓ Minimum 2 sets per area
```

## 5. Facility Design for Biosecurity

### Zone Design

```
BIOSECURITY ZONES

GREEN ZONE (Public):
  - Parking
  - Offices (admin)
  - Meeting rooms
  - No fish contact

YELLOW ZONE (Transition):
  - Shower facilities
  - Locker rooms
  - PPE storage
  - Footbaths
  - Supply receiving

RED ZONE (Production):
  - Fish production areas
  - Hatchery
  - Feed storage
  - Equipment storage


TRAFFIC FLOW PRINCIPLES:

Unidirectional:
  Clean → Dirty
  Young → Old
  Healthy → Sick (if unavoidable)

Separation:
  Physical barriers between zones
  No backtracking
  Dedicated drainage
```

### Infrastructure Elements

```
BIOSECURITY INFRASTRUCTURE

PERIMETER:
  □ Fence (2m high minimum)
  □ Single entry/exit point
  □ Lockable gates
  □ Signage (No Entry, Disease-Free Farm)
  □ Wildlife barriers

ENTRY POINT:
  □ Vehicle tire wash
  □ Personnel entrance
  □ Shower facilities (high-security)
  □ Footbath stations
  □ Hand wash stations
  □ PPE storage

WATER SYSTEMS:
  □ Dedicated source (well, municipal)
  □ Treatment (UV, ozone, filtration)
  □ No shared water with other farms
  □ Effluent management

DRAINAGE:
  □ Separate from incoming water
  □ Treatment before discharge
  □ No standing water
  □ Wildlife exclusion
```

## 6. Disease Surveillance

### Active Monitoring

```
HEALTH SURVEILLANCE PROGRAM

DAILY:
  □ Visual observation (all tanks)
  □ Mortality count and removal
  □ Feeding behavior
  □ Water quality spot checks

WEEKLY:
  □ Detailed fish examination (sample 10-30)
  □ Wet mounts for parasites
  □ Body condition scoring
  □ Mortality analysis

MONTHLY:
  □ Comprehensive water quality
  □ Growth measurements
  □ Health scoring
  □ Parasite load quantification

QUARTERLY:
  □ Laboratory testing (pathogen screening)
  □ Facility inspection
  □ Equipment calibration
  □ Biosecurity audit

ANNUAL:
  □ Full disease certification (if applicable)
  □ Third-party audit
  □ Program review and update
```

### Early Warning System

```
ALERT THRESHOLDS

Level 1 (Monitor):
  • Mortality: 0.2-0.5% per day
  • Off-feed: <10% reduction
  • Action: Increased observation

Level 2 (Investigate):
  • Mortality: 0.5-1.0% per day
  • Off-feed: 10-25% reduction
  • Behavioral changes noted
  • Action: Sample and examine

Level 3 (Respond):
  • Mortality: >1.0% per day
  • Off-feed: >25% reduction
  • Clinical disease signs
  • Action: Diagnose and treat

Level 4 (Emergency):
  • Mortality: >5% per day or sudden deaths
  • Obvious severe disease
  • Action: Emergency protocol, notify authorities
```

## 7. Quarantine and Introduction

### Comprehensive Quarantine Protocol

```
QUARANTINE STANDARD

FACILITY REQUIREMENTS:
  ✓ Separate building or isolated room
  ✓ Independent water supply
  ✓ Separate drainage (no mixing)
  ✓ Dedicated equipment
  ✓ Last area serviced daily

DURATION:
  Minimum: 30 days
  Recommended: 45-60 days
  High-value/broodstock: 90 days

PROTOCOL:

Day 0: Arrival
  □ External examination
  □ Photography
  □ Weight/count
  □ Initial water quality
  □ Acclimation

Days 1-7:
  □ Daily observation
  □ Mortality recording
  □ Feeding behavior

Day 7:
  □ Health examination (10 fish)
  □ Wet mounts (parasites)
  □ Water quality testing

Day 14:
  □ Detailed examination
  □ Bacterial culture if indicated
  □ Parasite count

Day 21:
  □ PCR screening (if viral diseases concern)
  □ Histopathology sample
  □ Growth assessment

Day 30+:
  □ Final health clearance
  □ Laboratory results review
  □ Decision: Release or extend

RELEASE CRITERIA:
  ✓ Zero mortality for 14 days
  ✓ No clinical disease signs
  ✓ Parasite load acceptable
  ✓ All tests negative
  ✓ Good body condition

REJECTION CRITERIA:
  ✗ Reportable disease detected
  ✗ High mortality (>5%)
  ✗ Untreatable condition
  ✗ Poor recovery prognosis
```

## 8. Emergency Response

### Disease Outbreak Response Plan

```
OUTBREAK RESPONSE PROTOCOL

PHASE 1: DETECTION (Hour 0)
  □ Recognize unusual event
  □ Document initial observations
  □ Notify management immediately
  □ Isolate affected unit

PHASE 2: ASSESSMENT (Hours 1-4)
  □ Clinical examination
  □ Sample collection
  □ Water quality verification
  □ Review recent changes
  □ Stop all fish movements

PHASE 3: CONTAINMENT (Hours 4-24)
  □ Enhance biosecurity
  □ Disinfect footbaths
  □ Dedicated equipment for affected area
  □ Limit personnel access
  □ Begin diagnostic workup

PHASE 4: DIAGNOSIS (Days 1-7)
  □ Laboratory testing
  □ Pathogen identification
  □ Extent determination
  □ Risk assessment

PHASE 5: RESPONSE (Days 7+)
  Based on diagnosis:

  Treatable Disease:
    - Implement treatment
    - Monitor response
    - Continue isolation

  Reportable/Untreatable:
    - Notify authorities
    - Depopulation planning
    - Disinfection protocol
    - Restocking timeline

PHASE 6: RECOVERY
  □ Resume normal operations
  □ Review and improve protocols
  □ Staff debriefing
  □ Update biosecurity plan


COMMUNICATION TREE:

Level 1 Alert (Monitor):
  → Farm manager notified

Level 2 Alert (Investigate):
  → Manager + veterinarian/consultant

Level 3 Alert (Respond):
  → Manager + vet + owner

Level 4 Alert (Emergency):
  → All above + regulatory authorities
  → Industry notification
  → Customer communication
```

## Summary

Biosecurity program development requires:

1. **Risk-Based Approach** - Identify and prioritize threats
2. **Written Protocols** - Document all procedures (SOPs)
3. **Staff Training** - Everyone must understand and comply
4. **Infrastructure** - Design facility to support biosecurity
5. **Monitoring** - Regular audits and surveillance
6. **Continuous Improvement** - Update and refine protocols

Key Elements:
- Isolation, Traffic Control, Sanitation
- Quarantine and testing
- Personnel management
- Emergency response
- Documentation and compliance

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Biosecurity is a system**, not single actions
2. **People are the weakest link** - training essential
3. **Consistency is critical** - protocols must be followed always
4. **Documentation proves compliance**
5. **Audits identify gaps** before disease does
6. **Plans must be living documents** - review and update
