```

  • Vibrio anguillarum
  • Vibrio vulnificus

  • Flavobacterium columnare (Columnaris)
  • Flavobacterium psychrophilum (BCWD)

Family Enterobacteriaceae:
  • Edwardsiella tarda

Family Pseudomonadaceae:
  • Pseudomonas fluorescens


GRAM-POSITIVE BACTERIA:

Family Streptococcaceae:
  • Streptococcus iniae
  • Streptococcus agalactiae
  • Lactococcus garvieae

Family Mycobacteriaceae:
  • Mycobacterium marinum
  • Mycobacterium fortuitum
```

## 2. Columnaris Disease

### Causative Agent

**Pathogen:** *Flavobacterium columnare*
- Gram-negative rod
- Motile (gliding)
- Aerobic
- Optimal growth: 25-30°C

### Clinical Signs

```
ACUTE FORM:
  • Sudden mortality (10-50%)
  • Gill necrosis (primary site)
  • Respiratory distress

CHRONIC FORM:
  • White-gray patches on skin, fins, gills
  • "Saddleback" lesion (dorsal area)
  • Fin erosion and fraying
  • Mouth lesions ("mouth rot")
  • Gill discoloration (brown/gray)


Visual Identification:
    ┌──────────────────────┐
    │   HEAD VIEW          │
    │                      │
    │    ╔═══════╗         │  White/gray
    │    ║ MOUTH ║  ←─────────┐patches
    │    ╚═══════╝         │  │
    │                      │  │
    │  ┌─────────────┐     │  │
    │  │   GILL      │←────────┘ Necrotic
    │  └─────────────┘     │    gills
    │                      │
    │   BODY (LATERAL)     │
    │                      │
    │      ░░░░░░░         │  "Saddleback"
    │  ═══════════════     │   lesion
    │                      │
    └──────────────────────┘
```

### Diagnosis

```
DIAGNOSTIC TESTS:

1. Wet Mount:
   - Gill or skin scraping
   - Long, thin, gliding rods
   - "Haystack" arrangement

2. Gram Stain:
   - Gram-negative
   - Long rods (4-10 μm)

3. Culture:
   - Cytophaga agar or Ordal's medium
   - Yellow colonies
   - 25-28°C, 48-72 hours

4. Biochemical:
   - Catalase positive
   - Oxidase positive
   - Chondroitin sulfatase positive
```

### Treatment and Control

**Treatment:**
```
ANTIBACTERIAL OPTIONS:

Copper sulfate (external):
  Dose: 0.5-1.0 mg/L as continuous bath
  Duration: Until signs resolve
  Monitor: Alkalinity, pH
  Caution: Toxic to some species

Potassium permanganate:
  Dose: 2-4 mg/L
  Duration: 30-60 minutes
  Repeat: Every other day, 3 treatments
  Monitor: DO levels

Chloramine-T:
  Dose: 3-10 mg/L
  Duration: 30-60 minutes
  Effective against external bacteria

Medicated Feed (if fish still eating):
  Oxytetracycline: 50-75 mg/kg fish/day × 10 days
  Florfenicol: 10-15 mg/kg fish/day × 10 days
```

**Prevention:**
```
MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES:

Water Quality:
  ✓ Keep temperature <28°C if possible
  ✓ Maintain DO >6 mg/L
  ✓ Reduce organic matter
  ✓ Good water exchange

Stress Reduction:
  ✓ Minimize handling
  ✓ Gradual temperature changes
  ✓ Avoid overcrowding
  ✓ Proper nutrition

Biosecurity:
  ✓ Quarantine new fish
  ✓ Disinfect equipment
  ✓ Footbaths between tanks
  ✓ Remove dead fish promptly
```

## 3. Motile Aeromonad Septicemia (MAS)

### Causative Agent

**Pathogen:** *Aeromonas hydrophila* complex
- Gram-negative rod
- Motile (flagella)
- Facultative anaerobe
- Ubiquitous in aquatic environments

### Clinical Signs

```
ACUTE SEPTICEMIA:
  • Sudden death (minimal external signs)
  • Hemorrhagic septicemia
  • Petechial hemorrhages on skin/fins
  • Exophthalmia (pop-eye)
  • Abdominal distension

CHRONIC FORM:
  • Deep ulcers ("hole-in-side" disease)
  • Scale loss
  • Raised scales (edema)
  • Hemorrhagic fins

INTERNAL FINDINGS:
  • Ascites (fluid in abdomen)
  • Enlarged spleen
  • Hemorrhagic liver
  • Hemorrhagic kidney
  • Friable organs


DISEASE PROGRESSION:

Day 0:     ├─ Stress event (handling, poor water quality)
           │
Day 1-2:   ├─ Lethargy, off feed
           │
Day 3-4:   ├─ External hemorrhages appear
           ├─ Pop-eye develops
           │
Day 5-7:   ├─ Ulcers form
           ├─ Mortality increases
           │
Day 7-14:  ├─ Peak mortality
           └─ Survivors develop chronic ulcers
```

### Diagnosis

```
DIAGNOSTIC PROTOCOL:

1. Clinical Signs:
   - Hemorrhages, ulcers, pop-eye
   - Acute mortality

2. Bacterial Culture:
   - Kidney or lesion swab
   - TSA or BHIA
   - 25-30°C, 24-48 hours
   - Cream to yellow colonies

3. Gram Stain:
   - Gram-negative rods
   - Short, plump morphology

4. Biochemical Tests:
   - Oxidase positive
   - Catalase positive
   - Ferments glucose
   - VP test positive (A. hydrophila)

5. API 20E or VITEK:
   - Commercial ID systems
   - Species confirmation
```

### Treatment and Control

**Treatment:**
```
ACUTE OUTBREAK MANAGEMENT:

1. Immediate Actions:
   □ Stop feeding temporarily
   □ Improve water quality
   □ Increase aeration
   □ Remove dead fish

2. Antimicrobial Therapy:
   (MUST be fish eating)

   Oxytetracycline (OTC):
     50-75 mg/kg fish/day × 10 days
     Mix in feed

   Florfenicol:
     10-15 mg/kg fish/day × 10 days
     More effective but expensive

   Sulfadimethoxine/Ormetoprim:
     50 mg/kg day 1, then 25 mg/kg × 4 days

3. Supportive Care:
   - Salt bath (3-5 ppt) may help
   - Optimize water quality
   - Reduce stocking density

4. Monitor:
   - Daily mortality
   - Feeding response
   - Clinical signs improvement
```

**Prevention:**
```
LONG-TERM CONTROL:

Environmental:
  • Maintain optimal water quality
  • Avoid temperature stress
  • Reduce organic loading
  • Regular water exchanges

Management:
  • Minimize handling stress
  • Proper stocking densities
  • Quarantine new stock
  • Cull chronically infected fish

Vaccination:
  • Autogenous vaccines available
  • Injectable (IP or IM)
  • Vaccinate before stress periods
  • Booster annually

Probiotics:
  • Bacillus spp. in feed
  • Competitive exclusion
  • Immune enhancement
```

## 4. Streptococcosis

### Causative Agents

**Primary Pathogens:**
- *Streptococcus iniae* (warm water)
- *Streptococcus agalactiae* (warm water)
- *Lactococcus garvieae* (cool/warm water)

### Clinical Signs

```
PERACUTE FORM:
  • Sudden death (no external signs)
  • High mortality (30-50%)
  • "Bird's eye" lesions in brain

ACUTE FORM:
  • Erratic swimming (whirling, spinning)
  • Exophthalmia (uni- or bilateral)
  • Darkening
  • Lethargy
  • Anorexia

CHRONIC FORM:
  • Meningoencephalitis (brain inflammation)
  • Skeletal deformities
  • Blindness
  • Weight loss


CHARACTERISTIC SIGNS:

      ╔════════════════╗
      ║  EXOPHTHALMIA  ║
      ║   (Pop-eye)    ║  Unilateral or
      ║      ●  ●      ║  bilateral
      ║                ║
      ║   Spiraling    ║  Neurological
      ║   Swimming  ↻  ║  damage
      ║                ║
      ║  Darkening     ║  Stress response
      ║  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓     ║
      ╚════════════════╝


INTERNAL LESIONS:
  • Enlarged spleen (dark, prominent)
  • Granulomas (white nodules in organs)
  • Ascites (abdominal fluid)
  • Meningitis (inflamed brain covering)
```

### Diagnosis

```
DIAGNOSTIC APPROACH:

1. Clinical Signs:
   - Neurological signs + exophthalmia
   - High mortality
   - Warm water (>25°C)

2. Bacterial Culture:
   - Brain or kidney sample
   - Blood agar (5% sheep)
   - 28-30°C, 48 hours
   - Beta-hemolytic colonies

3. Gram Stain:
   - Gram-positive cocci
   - Chains or pairs

4. Biochemical Tests:
   - Catalase negative
   - Lancefield Group B (S. agalactiae)
   - API Strep

5. PCR:
   - Species confirmation
   - Rapid and accurate
```

### Treatment and Control

**Treatment:**
```
ANTIBIOTIC THERAPY:

Challenges:
  ✗ Often acute - fish die before treatment
  ✗ Neurological damage irreversible
  ✗ Bacteria may be intracellular

Options (if caught early):

Erythromycin:
  50-100 mg/kg fish/day × 21 days
  Long treatment needed

Florfenicol:
  10-15 mg/kg fish/day × 10-15 days
  More effective

Amoxicillin:
  40-80 mg/kg fish/day × 10 days
  Variable efficacy


Bath Treatment (limited efficacy):
  Formalin: 15-25 mg/L × 1 hour
  (Reduces external bacteria only)
```

**Prevention (Most Important):**
```
STREPTOCOCCUS PREVENTION:

Vaccination (HIGHLY RECOMMENDED):
  • Commercial vaccines available
  • Injectable or immersion
  • Vaccinate at 5-10g
  • Protection 6-12 months
  • Efficacy: 70-90%

Management:
  ✓ Avoid temperature spikes >28°C
  ✓ Minimize stress
  ✓ Proper nutrition (immune support)
  ✓ Reduce density
  ✓ Quarantine new stock

Monitoring:
  ✓ Daily observation
  ✓ Early detection critical
  ✓ Cull affected fish promptly
  ✓ Depopulate if severe

ZOONOTIC RISK:
  ⚠ S. iniae can infect humans
  ⚠ Use gloves when handling
  ⚠ Avoid hand wounds
  ⚠ Proper hygiene essential
```

## 5. Edwardsiellosis

### Causative Agents

**Species:**
- *Edwardsiella tarda* (emphysematous putrefactive disease)
- *Edwardsiella ictaluri* (enteric septicemia of catfish - ESC)

### Clinical Signs

```
E. TARDA:
  • Gas-filled lesions (emphysema)
  • Hemorrhagic septicemia
  • Ulcers
  • "Hole-in-head" disease
  • Affects warm water species

E. ICTALURI (ESC):
  • "Hole-in-head" lesions
  • Exophthalmia
  • Petechial hemorrhages
  • Necrotic brain lesions
  • Primarily channel catfish


LESION CHARACTERISTICS:

    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │   HOLE-IN-HEAD      │
    │                     │
    │      ╔═══╗          │  Necrotic
    │      ║ ○ ║  ←───────── crater
    │      ╚═══╝          │
    │                     │
    │   BODY              │
    │   ════════════      │  Petechial
    │   · · · · · ·       │  hemorrhages
    │                     │
    └─────────────────────┘
```

### Diagnosis and Treatment

```
DIAGNOSIS:

Culture:
  • Kidney, brain, or lesion
  • TSA or BHIA
  • 25-30°C, 24-48 hours
  • Small, translucent colonies

Biochemical:
  • Indole positive (key test)
  • H₂S production (E. tarda)
  • API 20E


TREATMENT:

E. tarda:
  Florfenicol: 10-15 mg/kg × 10 days
  Oxytetracycline: 50-75 mg/kg × 10 days

E. ictaluri (ESC):
  Terramycin (OTC): FDA-approved for catfish
  Romet-30: FDA-approved for catfish

Prevention:
  • Vaccination (for E. ictaluri)
  • Water quality management
  • Temperature control (<28°C)
```

## 6. Mycobacteriosis (Fish TB)

### Causative Agents

**Mycobacterium species:**
- *M. marinum* (most common)
- *M. fortuitum*
- *M. chelonae*

### Clinical Signs

```
CHRONIC WASTING DISEASE:

External:
  • Emaciation (prominent head)
  • Skin ulcers
  • Scale loss
  • Exophthalmia
  • Skeletal deformities
  • Slow mortality (months)

Internal:
  • White granulomas (nodules) in organs
  • Enlarged spleen with nodules
  • Liver nodules
  • Kidney granulomas
  • Meningitis


GRANULOMA FORMATION:

    Microscopic:
    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │  ●●●●●●●●●●        │ Epithelioid
    │ ●          ●       │ cells
    │●   Necrotic  ●     │
    │●   Center     ●    │ Acid-fast
    │ ●   (AFB)    ●     │ bacilli
    │  ●          ●      │ inside
    │   ●●●●●●●●●●       │
    └─────────────────────┘
```

### Diagnosis

```
DIAGNOSTIC TESTS:

1. Acid-Fast Stain (Ziehl-Neelsen):
   - Tissue impression smear
   - Stain red = acid-fast bacilli (AFB)
   - Confirmatory test

2. Culture:
   - Special media (Middlebrook, Lowenstein-Jensen)
   - 25-30°C
   - SLOW growth (2-8 weeks)
   - Not practical for farm diagnosis

3. Histopathology:
   - Granulomas with AFB
   - Gold standard
   - Formalin-fixed tissues

4. PCR:
   - Species identification
   - Faster than culture
```

### Management

**Treatment:**
```
REALITY CHECK:

✗ NO practical treatment exists
✗ Antibiotics ineffective (intracellular)
✗ Long-term treatment impractical
✗ Chronic shedding continues

DO NOT WASTE TIME/MONEY ON:
  • Medicated feed (won't work)
  • Prolonged treatment attempts
```

**Control (Depopulation Focus):**
```
MYCOBACTERIOSIS CONTROL:

Option 1: Depopulation (Recommended)
  1. Humanely euthanize all fish
  2. Complete system drainage
  3. Disinfection:
     - Chlorine (200 ppm × 24 hours)
     - Quaternary ammonium compounds
     - Allow to dry completely
  4. Restock with certified disease-free fish

Option 2: Tolerance (Not recommended)
  • Accept chronic low mortality
  • Cull obviously infected fish
  • Do not spread to other systems
  • NEVER use as broodstock

ZOONOTIC WARNING:
  ⚠⚠⚠ M. marinum causes "fish handler's disease"
  ⚠ Skin granulomas in humans
  ⚠ Difficult to treat
  ⚠ Always wear gloves
  ⚠ Cover any wounds
  ⚠ Seek medical attention if infected
```

## 7. Other Bacterial Diseases (Brief Overview)

### Vibriosis

```
Pathogen: Vibrio anguillarum, V. vulnificus
Species: Marine/brackish water fish
Signs: Hemorrhagic septicemia, skin ulcers
Diagnosis: Culture on TCBS (yellow colonies)
Treatment: Oxytetracycline, florfenicol
Prevention: Vaccination available
```

### Pseudomonas

```
Pathogen: Pseudomonas fluorescens
Signs: Hemorrhagic septicemia, ulcers
Diagnosis: Culture (green pigment), oxidase+
Treatment: Antibiotics (variable sensitivity)
Prevention: Water quality, stress reduction
```

### Bacterial Gill Disease (BGD)

```
Pathogen: Flavobacterium branchiophilum
Signs: Respiratory distress, pale gills, hyperplasia
Diagnosis: Wet mount (filamentous bacteria)
Treatment: Chloramine-T, copper sulfate
Prevention: Reduce organics, proper flow
```

## 8. Antibiotic Use and Resistance

### Approved Antibiotics (USA - FDA)

```
FOR FOOD FISH:

Oxytetracycline (OTC):
  Trade names: Terramycin, Oxybiotic
  Species: Most aquaculture species
  Diseases: Bacterial infections
  Withdrawal: 21 days (OTC in feed)

Florfenicol:
  Trade name: Aquaflor
  Species: Catfish, salmonids, tilapia
  Diseases: Bacterial infections
  Withdrawal: 15 days

Sulfadimethoxine/Ormetoprim:
  Trade name: Romet-30
  Species: Catfish, salmonids, trout
  Diseases: Furunculosis, enteric septicemia
  Withdrawal: 3 days (42 degrees-days)

Formalin:
  External parasites and fungi
  Withdrawal: 0 days (if used properly)
```

### Antibiotic Resistance

```
RESISTANCE MECHANISMS:

1. Enzymatic Degradation
   - Beta-lactamases destroy penicillins

2. Efflux Pumps
   - Pump antibiotics out of cell

3. Target Modification
   - Change ribosome structure

4. Reduced Permeability
   - Prevent antibiotic entry


RESISTANCE PREVENTION:

DO:
  ✓ Use antibiotics ONLY when necessary
  ✓ Complete full treatment course
  ✓ Use correct dosage
  ✓ Rotate antibiotics (if multiple treatments)
  ✓ Culture and sensitivity testing
  ✓ Prevent disease (reduce antibiotic need)

DON'T:
  ✗ Use antibiotics prophylactically
  ✗ Underdose or overdose
  ✗ Stop treatment early
  ✗ Use same antibiotic repeatedly
  ✗ Use non-approved antibiotics
  ✗ Ignore withdrawal times
```

## 9. Bacterial Disease Prevention Strategy

### Comprehensive Prevention Plan

```
MULTI-LEVEL PREVENTION

LEVEL 1: BIOSECURITY
  □ Quarantine new stock (30 days)
  □ Source from certified disease-free suppliers
  □ Footbaths at each production unit
  □ Dedicated equipment per tank/system
  □ Visitor protocols
  □ Wild fish exclusion

LEVEL 2: WATER QUALITY
  □ Optimal temperature range
  □ DO >6 mg/L
  □ Ammonia <0.02 mg/L
  □ Nitrite <0.1 mg/L
  □ pH 6.5-8.5
  □ Low organic matter

LEVEL 3: NUTRITION
  □ High-quality feed
  □ Immune-enhancing supplements
  □ Vitamin C (200 mg/kg)
  □ Probiotics/prebiotics
  □ Proper feeding rates

LEVEL 4: STOCKING MANAGEMENT
  □ Appropriate densities
  □ Size grading
  □ Avoid overcrowding
  □ Minimize handling stress
  □ Proper acclimation

LEVEL 5: HEALTH MONITORING
  □ Daily observation
  □ Weekly sampling
  □ Mortality records
  □ Early disease detection
  □ Prompt intervention

LEVEL 6: VACCINATION
  □ Streptococcus vaccine
  □ Edwardsiella vaccine (catfish)
  □ Vibrio vaccine (marine)
  □ Proper vaccine storage/handling
  □ Follow protocols
```

## Summary

Bacterial diseases are major causes of mortality in aquaculture. Success requires:

1. **Early Detection** - Daily monitoring and recognition of clinical signs
2. **Accurate Diagnosis** - Proper sampling and laboratory confirmation
3. **Rapid Response** - Immediate treatment when indicated
4. **Prevention Focus** - Biosecurity and water quality management
5. **Judicious Antibiotic Use** - Only when necessary, full courses
6. **Vaccination** - Where available, highly cost-effective

Major Bacterial Diseases:
- **Columnaris** - External lesions, gill disease
- **Aeromonas** - Hemorrhagic septicemia, ulcers
- **Streptococcus** - Neurological signs, high mortality
- **Edwardsiella** - Hole-in-head, septicemia
- **Mycobacterium** - Chronic wasting (no treatment)

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Water quality** is the foundation of bacterial disease prevention
2. **Stress** is the primary trigger for opportunistic infections
3. **Vaccination** is more effective than treatment
4. **Antibiotic stewardship** prevents resistance
5. **Mycobacteriosis** requires depopulation, not treatment
6. **Zoonotic risks** exist - use proper protection
