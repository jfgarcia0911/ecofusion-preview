## Answer Key (For Instructor)

1. Answer: A - 30% of variation is due to genetics (remaining 70% is environmental)
   High h²: Good selection potential, genetic improvement is effective, traits will respond well to selective breeding, rapid genetic gains possible

2. Hybrid vigor: F1 offspring show superior performance compared to either parent line (heterosis)
   Expected: Faster growth, better feed conversion, increased survival, improved stress tolerance
   Problem: F2 generation loses hybrid vigor, cannot breed true, must maintain pure lines, increased complexity (any 1)

3. Hormone: GnRH (Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone) or LHRHa, HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin)
   Injection sites: (1) Intramuscular (dorsal muscle), (2) Intraperitoneal (abdominal cavity)
   Why used: Synchronized spawning, out-of-season breeding, improved fertilization rates, timing control, non-breeding species

4. Selection criteria (any 4):
   - Growth rate/size
   - Body conformation
   - Disease resistance/health history
   - Fecundity (egg production)
   - Age (2-3 years ideal)
   - Genetic diversity/pedigree
   - No physical deformities
   - Good condition factor

5. Answer: D - All of the above
   Hormone: 17α-methyltestosterone (MT)

6. Answer: C - 50 individuals minimum (some say 50-100)
   Symptoms (any 2):
   - Reduced growth rate
   - Lower fecundity
   - Decreased survival
   - Physical deformities
   - Reduced disease resistance
   - Lower stress tolerance
