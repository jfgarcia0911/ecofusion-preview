## Direct-to-Consumer Channels

### Farmers Markets

**Business Model:**
```
FARMERS MARKET STRUCTURE

Revenue Model:
├─ Direct sales to end consumers
├─ Prices: Retail (often premium)
├─ Payment: Cash, cards, EBT at point of sale
└─ Volume: 100-500 lbs per market day

Costs:
├─ Market fees: $25-100 per day
├─ Staff time: 8-12 hours (setup, sales, breakdown)
├─ Transport: Vehicle, fuel
├─ Packaging: Individual consumer portions
├─ Supplies: Tent, tables, display, signage
└─ Total cost: $150-400 per market day

Profitability:
Average sale: $800-2,000 per market
Costs: $150-400
Net profit: $500-1,600 per market
Margin: 60-75%
```

**Pros:**
- Highest retail prices
- Direct customer feedback
- Brand building
- Customer loyalty
- Cash flow (immediate payment)
- Market research opportunity

**Cons:**
- Weather dependent
- Time intensive (Saturdays)
- Limited scale
- Seasonal (many markets)
- High labor per dollar sold
- Inventory uncertainty

**Best Practices:**
```
FARMERS MARKET SUCCESS FACTORS

Product Selection:
□ Bring variety (12-20 items)
□ Include impulse items (herbs, microgreens)
□ Offer bundles/ready-to-eat options
□ Sample products

Booth Setup:
□ Attractive display with height variation
□ Clear pricing signage
□ Brand visibility (banners, tablecloths)
□ Shade for product and customers
□ Accept multiple payment types

Customer Engagement:
□ Friendly, knowledgeable staff
□ Recipe cards and usage suggestions
□ Email list signup (build direct sales)
□ Social media presence
□ Consistent attendance (build following)

Pricing Strategy:
□ Research competitor pricing
□ Premium for unique varieties
□ Bundle deals for volume
□ "Market special" to move volume
```

**Ideal For:**
- Start-up farms building customer base
- Supplemental income channel
- Brand building and marketing
- High-value specialty products
- Urban/suburban locations

### CSA (Community Supported Agriculture)

**Business Model:**
```
CSA STRUCTURE

Revenue Model:
├─ Upfront payment for season
├─ Members receive weekly shares
├─ Predictable revenue at season start
└─ Volume: 50-200 shares typical

Example CSA:
├─ 100 members @ $500/season (20 weeks)
├─ Gross revenue: $50,000
├─ Weekly distribution: $2,500
└─ Share contents: 6-10 lbs mixed produce

Costs:
├─ Production: $15,000-25,000
├─ Packaging/boxes: $2,000-4,000
├─ Distribution logistics: $1,500-3,000
├─ Marketing/admin: $1,000-2,000
└─ Net profit: $20,000-30,000 (40-60% margin)

Cash Flow Advantage:
- Payment before growing season
- Funds spring production costs
- Reduces financial risk
```

**Delivery Models:**

1. **Pickup Sites**
   - Central locations (community centers, businesses)
   - Members collect shares on set day/time
   - Low delivery cost
   - Requires member commitment

2. **Home Delivery**
   - Farm delivers to doorsteps
   - Premium pricing ($50-100 more per season)
   - Higher delivery cost
   - Convenient for members

3. **Farm Pickup**
   - Members come to farm
   - Zero delivery cost
   - Farm visit builds connection
   - Limits membership to local area

**Pros:**
- Predictable revenue (paid upfront)
- Cash flow early in season
- Loyal customer base
- Reduced marketing ongoing
- Flexible share contents

**Cons:**
- Production risk (share guaranteed regardless)
- Weekly commitment (consistent harvest)
- Member management (communication, complaints)
- Seasonal (not year-round in most climates)
- Member retention challenges

**Best Practices:**
```
CSA SUCCESS STRATEGIES

Member Communication:
□ Weekly newsletters with share contents
□ Recipe ideas and storage tips
□ Farm updates and behind-scenes stories
□ Responsive to questions/concerns

Share Design:
□ Consistent value week-to-week
□ Variety (6-10 different items)
□ Mix of staples and specialty items
□ Choice options when possible
□ Accommodate dietary restrictions

Retention:
□ Exceed expectations in share value
□ Build community (farm visits, events)
□ Flexible pickup options
□ Vacation holds or swaps
□ Early renewal incentives
□ Target retention: >70% year-over-year
```

**Ideal For:**
- Diversified vegetable farms
- Operations seeking cash flow stability
- Farms with community focus
- Growing operations (30+ crop types)

### Online Direct Sales

**Business Model:**
```
E-COMMERCE STRUCTURE

Platform Options:
├─ Own website + e-commerce
│  (Shopify, WooCommerce)
│  Cost: $30-300/month + transaction fees
│
├─ Farm marketplace platforms
│  (Barn2Door, LocalLine, GrazeCart)
│  Cost: $50-200/month + 3-5% transaction
│
└─ Social media sales
   (Instagram, Facebook)
   Cost: $0 + payment processing

Revenue Model:
├─ Online ordering, scheduled delivery/pickup
├─ Prices: Retail
├─ Minimum order: $25-50 typical
└─ Delivery fee: $5-15 or free over threshold

Example Economics:
├─ Average order value: $45
├─ Orders per week: 30
├─ Weekly revenue: $1,350
├─ Platform/payment fees (5%): $68
├─ Delivery cost (avg): $300
├─ Net revenue: $982/week
└─ Margin: ~60-70% (after product costs)
```

**Pros:**
- 24/7 ordering convenience
- Reach beyond physical markets
- Data on customer preferences
- Automated payment processing
- Scalable (less labor than markets)
- Year-round potential

**Cons:**
- Platform and transaction costs
- Delivery logistics required
- Technical setup and maintenance
- Customer acquisition cost
- Competition with grocery delivery
- Minimum order requirements

**Best Practices:**
```
ONLINE SALES OPTIMIZATION

Website/Platform:
□ Mobile-friendly design
□ Clear product photos
□ Detailed descriptions
□ Simple checkout process
□ Multiple payment options
□ Email confirmations

Product Offerings:
□ Pre-built bundles (Family Box, Salad Box)
□ Individual items with minimums
□ Rotating seasonal specials
□ Gift options

Logistics:
□ Defined delivery zones
□ Set delivery days by area
□ Order cutoff times (48hrs before delivery)
□ Pickup options to reduce delivery cost
□ Route optimization

Marketing:
□ Email list building and newsletters
□ Social media promotion
□ Referral discounts
□ Subscription options (recurring orders)
□ SEO for local searches
```

**Ideal For:**
- Tech-savvy farms
- Urban/suburban markets
- Farms seeking to scale
- Year-round operations
- Operations with delivery logistics

## Business-to-Business Channels

### Restaurant Direct Sales

**Business Model:**
```
RESTAURANT CHANNEL STRUCTURE

Relationship Model:
├─ Direct relationship with chef/buyer
├─ Standing orders (weekly/bi-weekly)
├─ Specialty/seasonal items
└─ Premium quality expectations

Typical Account:
├─ Order size: 25-150 lbs per delivery
├─ Frequency: 1-3x per week
├─ Price: Wholesale to retail hybrid (30-50% below retail)
├─ Payment terms: Net 7-30 days
└─ Delivery: Direct to kitchen

Example Portfolio:
├─ 10 restaurant accounts
├─ Average order: $200
├─ Average frequency: 2x per week
├─ Weekly revenue: $4,000
├─ Monthly revenue: $16,000
└─ Margin: 50-60%
```

**Account Tiers:**

```
RESTAURANT ACCOUNT STRATEGY

Tier 1: White Tablecloth / Fine Dining
├─ Order size: $150-500
├─ Frequency: 2-3x week
├─ Price sensitivity: Low
├─ Quality expectations: Highest
├─ Product: Specialty, unique varieties
└─ Margin: 60-70%

Tier 2: Casual Dining / Gastropubs
├─ Order size: $75-200
├─ Frequency: 1-2x week
├─ Price sensitivity: Medium
├─ Quality expectations: High
├─ Product: Mix of staples and specialty
└─ Margin: 50-60%

Tier 3: Cafes / Casual Restaurants
├─ Order size: $30-100
├─ Frequency: 1x week
├─ Price sensitivity: High
├─ Quality expectations: Moderate-High
├─ Product: Staple items, volume
└─ Margin: 40-50%
```

**Pros:**
- Larger, predictable orders
- Premium pricing (above wholesale)
- Steady demand (standing orders)
- Less packaging required
- Chef relationships and feedback
- Menu credits (marketing)

**Cons:**
- Payment terms (not immediate)
- Delivery required (cost and time)
- Quality expectations very high
- Risk of account loss
- Seasonal menu changes
- Early delivery times (pre-service)

**Best Practices:**
```
RESTAURANT SALES SUCCESS

Prospecting:
□ Target farm-to-table / local-focused
□ Visit restaurants, meet chefs
□ Provide samples
□ Share harvest calendar
□ Understand their menu and needs

Account Management:
□ Consistent quality (no surprises)
□ Reliable delivery schedule
□ Communicate shortages in advance
□ Flexibility for special requests
□ Seasonal planning meetings

Operations:
□ Efficient delivery routes
□ Early morning delivery (before service)
□ Proper packaging (chef-ready)
□ Accurate invoicing
□ Clear payment terms and follow-up

Building Value:
□ Unique varieties chefs can't get elsewhere
□ Consistency in supply
□ Menu planning support
□ Social media collaboration
□ Farm tours and relationship building
```

**Ideal For:**
- Farms with specialty/unique products
- Consistent year-round production
- Operations with delivery capability
- Quality-focused growers

### Retail Store Direct

**Business Model:**
```
RETAIL DIRECT STRUCTURE

Partnership Model:
├─ Consignment: Store sells, farm paid for sales
├─ Wholesale: Farm sells to store at wholesale price
└─ Hybrid: Mix of both

Typical Store Account:
├─ Order size: 50-200 lbs per delivery
├─ Frequency: 2-4x per week
├─ Price: Wholesale (50-60% of retail)
├─ Payment terms: Net 7-30 days (wholesale)
│                 Or upon sale (consignment)
└─ Delivery: To store loading dock

Example Store:
├─ Natural grocery, 10,000 sq ft
├─ Order: $300, 2x per week
├─ Weekly sales: $600
├─ Monthly: $2,400
└─ Margin: 45-55% (wholesale)
           60-70% (consignment at retail price)
```

**Consignment vs. Wholesale:**

| Factor | Consignment | Wholesale |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Price** | Retail (farm sets price) | Wholesale (50-60% of retail) |
| **Risk** | Farm (unsold returns) | Store (bought product) |
| **Payment** | After product sells | Net 7-30 days |
| **Merchandising** | Farm often manages | Store manages |
| **Margin** | 60-70% | 45-55% |
| **Best for** | Small stores, farmers markets | Larger stores, volume |

**Pros:**
- Volume potential (higher than markets)
- Less time than farmers markets
- Recurring orders
- Brand visibility
- Year-round potential

**Cons:**
- Lower margins than direct retail
- Payment delays
- Potential for returns/shrink
- Store policies and requirements
- Delivery costs
- Competition with other suppliers

**Best Practices:**
```
RETAIL ACCOUNT MANAGEMENT

Store Selection:
□ Natural/organic grocers
□ Co-ops (local preference)
□ Independent grocers
□ Specialty stores

Setup:
□ Meet with produce manager
□ Provide product samples
□ Discuss pricing and terms
□ Understand delivery requirements
□ Set up as vendor (paperwork, insurance)

Operations:
□ Consistent delivery schedule
□ Stock rotation (check dates)
□ Merchandising support
□ Promotional materials
□ Track sales data
□ Respond to quality issues immediately

Growth:
□ Start with 2-3 SKUs, expand based on performance
□ Seasonal promotions
□ Demo days (sampling)
□ Store newsletter features
□ Monitor sell-through rates
```

**Ideal For:**
- Farms with consistent volume
- Operations seeking to scale
- Quality-focused products
- Certified organic (often preferred)

## Wholesale & Intermediary Channels

### Wholesale Distributors

**Business Model:**
```
DISTRIBUTOR CHANNEL

Structure:
Farm → Distributor → Retail/Restaurant → Consumer

Distributor Role:
├─ Purchases from farm at wholesale
├─ Warehouses and manages inventory
├─ Sells to multiple buyers (stores, restaurants)
├─ Handles logistics and delivery
└─ Markets farm products to buyers

Farm Perspective:
├─ Selling price: Wholesale (40-60% of retail)
├─ Order size: 200-2,000+ lbs
├─ Frequency: 1-2x week (scheduled)
├─ Payment: Net 30-60 days
├─ Delivery: To distributor warehouse
└─ Farm margin: 30-45%

Example:
├─ Weekly delivery: 500 lbs
├─ Wholesale price: $2.50/lb
├─ Weekly revenue: $1,250
├─ Monthly revenue: $5,000
└─ Margin: 35-40% (lower than direct)
```

**Pros:**
- High volume potential (scale)
- Single delivery point (vs. many stops)
- Wider market reach
- Predictable orders
- Distributor handles sales/marketing
- Less time investment than direct sales

**Cons:**
- Lowest margins (40-60% of retail)
- Payment delays (30-60+ days)
- Less customer connection
- Quality standards strict
- Volume requirements
- Dependency risk

**Types of Distributors:**

1. **Broadline Distributors** (Sysco, US Foods)
   - Serve restaurants, institutions
   - Huge scale, competitive pricing
   - Difficult for small farms to access

2. **Specialty Produce Distributors**
   - Focus on high-quality, local, organic
   - Better fit for small-medium farms
   - Examples: Local, regional distributors

3. **Food Hubs**
   - Aggregate from multiple small farms
   - Market collectively
   - Often mission-driven (support local food)
   - Lower volume requirements

**Best Practices:**
```
DISTRIBUTOR RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT

Selection:
□ Research distributors serving your region
□ Understand their customer base
□ Verify they serve your target market
□ Check payment terms and reputation
□ Visit their warehouse/operations

Negotiation:
□ Know your pricing (cost + margin needed)
□ Understand volume capabilities
□ Define quality standards
□ Clarify payment terms
□ Discuss exclusivity (if any)

Operations:
□ Consistent volume and quality
□ Advance notice of shortages
□ Proper packaging and labeling
□ Accurate invoicing
□ Maintain communication

Growth:
□ Meet sales reps and buyers
□ Provide product information/marketing materials
□ Respond quickly to quality issues
□ Expand product line based on demand
□ Build trust over time
```

**Ideal For:**
- Farms seeking to scale quickly
- Operations with consistent volume (500+ lbs/week)
- Farms focused on production (not sales/marketing)
- Established operations with infrastructure

### Food Hubs

**Business Model:**
```
FOOD HUB STRUCTURE

Cooperative Model:
├─ Multiple farms sell through hub
├─ Hub aggregates, markets, distributes
├─ Shared infrastructure (warehouse, vehicles)
├─ Mission: Support local food system
└─ Governance: Often farmer-owned or nonprofit

Farm Participation:
├─ Selling price: Wholesale (50-70% of retail)
├─ Commission/fee: 15-30% of sales
├─ Order size: Flexible, smaller than distributors
├─ Delivery: To hub warehouse
├─ Payment: Net 7-30 days
└─ Margin: 40-55%

Example Food Hub:
├─ 20 member farms
├─ Hub sells to restaurants, stores, institutions
├─ Farm delivers to hub: $800/week
├─ Hub commission: 25%
├─ Farm receives: $600
└─ Hub handles sales, delivery, invoicing
```

**Pros:**
- Lower volume requirements than distributors
- Shared marketing and distribution costs
- Supports local food mission
- Flexible (grow at your pace)
- Community of fellow farmers

**Cons:**
- Commission reduces margins
- Still lower than direct sales
- Dependent on hub success
- May require membership investment
- Coordination challenges

**Ideal For:**
- Small-medium farms
- Farms seeking support and community
- Operations without delivery capability
- Diversified farms (multiple products)

## Multi-Channel Strategy

### Diversified Distribution Model

**Why Diversify Channels:**
```
RISK MITIGATION & OPTIMIZATION

Single Channel Risks:
├─ Market saturation
├─ Seasonal fluctuations
├─ Weather impacts (farmers markets)
├─ Account loss (large restaurant closes)
└─ Price compression

Multi-Channel Benefits:
├─ Income stability (diversified revenue)
├─ Risk reduction (not dependent on one channel)
├─ Market optimization (different prices/margins)
├─ Seasonal flexibility (shift channels)
└─ Growth opportunities (test new markets)
```

**Example Multi-Channel Mix:**

```
BALANCED DISTRIBUTION PORTFOLIO

GreenLeaf Farms - 2,500 lbs/week production

Channel Mix:
├─ Restaurant Direct (40%): 1,000 lbs @ $5.50/lb = $5,500
│  └─ 15 accounts, 2x/week delivery
│
├─ Farmers Markets (25%): 625 lbs @ $7/lb = $4,375
│  └─ 2 markets/week, Sat & Sun
│
├─ Retail Stores (20%): 500 lbs @ $4/lb = $2,000
│  └─ 3 stores, consignment model
│
├─ CSA (10%): 250 lbs @ $6/lb = $1,500
│  └─ 50 members, weekly pickup
│
└─ Wholesale (5%): 125 lbs @ $3/lb = $375
   └─ Distributor for overflow

WEEKLY REVENUE: $13,750
BLENDED MARGIN: 55%
RISK: Diversified across 5 channels
```

### Channel Allocation Strategy

**Decision Criteria:**

```
CHANNEL SELECTION MATRIX

Rank each factor 1-5 for your operation:

                     Margin  Volume  Time   Risk  Total
                     (x2)   (x2)    Invest  Mgmt  Score
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Farmers Market       9      2       2      5     18
Restaurant Direct    8      6       4      4     22
Retail Direct        6      6       4      4     20
CSA                  8      4       4      5     21
Distributor          4      10      5      3     22
Online/Delivery      7      4       3      4     18

Highest Score = Best fit for your operation
Allocate production proportionally to scores
```

**Allocation Principles:**

1. **Diversify but Focus**
   - Aim for 3-5 channels (not too many)
   - Allocate 60-80% to top 2 channels
   - Test new channels at small scale (5-10%)

2. **Match Product to Channel**
   - Premium/unique items: Farmers markets, fine dining
   - Staple greens: Wholesale, retail
   - Mixed variety: CSA, online

3. **Optimize Timing**
   - Harvest to order for restaurants (freshness)
   - Batch harvest for markets (volume)
   - Flexible harvesting for CSA

4. **Balance Labor**
   - High-margin channels (markets) = more labor
   - Volume channels (wholesale) = less labor per pound
   - Balance profit vs. time

## Building Channel Relationships

## Scaling Distribution

**Growth Pathway:**

```
DISTRIBUTION SCALE EVOLUTION

Stage 1: Launch (0-1,000 lbs/week)
├─ Channels: Farmers market + CSA
├─ Focus: Quality, customer feedback
├─ Goal: Prove product-market fit
└─ Timeline: Year 1-2

Stage 2: Diversify (1,000-3,000 lbs/week)
├─ Channels: Add restaurants, retail
├─ Focus: Build direct relationships
├─ Goal: Diversified revenue, stability
└─ Timeline: Year 2-4

Stage 3: Scale (3,000-10,000 lbs/week)
├─ Channels: Add wholesale/distributor
├─ Focus: Efficiency, systems
├─ Goal: Volume growth
└─ Timeline: Year 4-6

Stage 4: Optimize (10,000+ lbs/week)
├─ Channels: Strategic mix, drop low-margin
├─ Focus: Profitability, team management
├─ Goal: Sustainable, profitable scale
└─ Timeline: Year 6+
```

## Summary

Effective distribution strategy:

1. **Match channels to goals**: Margin vs. volume vs. time
2. **Diversify thoughtfully**: 3-5 channels, not too many
3. **Build relationships**: Long-term partnerships create stability
4. **Start direct, add wholesale**: Direct channels build brand and margins
5. **Scale sustainably**: Growth must maintain quality and profitability

**Key Takeaways:**
- No single "best" channel - depends on your operation
- Multi-channel approach balances risk and opportunity
- Relationships are your competitive advantage
- Test small, scale what works

Distribution excellence turns great products into thriving business.
