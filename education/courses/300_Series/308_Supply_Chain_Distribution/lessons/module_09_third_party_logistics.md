By the end of this module, you will be able to:

## Introduction to Third-Party Logistics

### What is 3PL?

**Third-Party Logistics (3PL)** providers offer outsourced logistics and supply chain management services:

```
3PL SERVICE CATEGORIES

TRANSPORTATION (Most Common):
├─ Freight brokerage
├─ Dedicated delivery service
├─ Last-mile delivery
└─ Temperature-controlled transport

WAREHOUSING & DISTRIBUTION:
├─ Cold storage facilities
├─ Cross-docking
├─ Pick and pack services
└─ Inventory management

VALUE-ADDED SERVICES:
├─ Packaging and labeling
├─ Quality inspection
├─ Order fulfillment
└─ Returns management

FULL-SERVICE 3PL:
└─ Complete supply chain management
```

### When to Consider 3PL

**Decision Factors:**

```
3PL EVALUATION MATRIX

CONSIDER 3PL IF:
✓ Expanding beyond current delivery radius
✓ Volume justifies outsourcing (>5,000 lbs/week)
✓ Capital investment in trucks/coolers too high
✓ Seasonal fluctuations (avoid fixed costs)
✓ Focus needed on production vs. distribution
✓ Service quality improves with specialization

KEEP IN-HOUSE IF:
✓ Small volume (<1,000 lbs/week)
✓ Direct customer relationships critical
✓ Delivery routes already optimized
✓ Existing infrastructure sufficient
✓ Margins too tight for outsourcing
✓ Unique requirements (specialty handling)
```

## Types of 3PL for Fresh Produce

### 1. Freight Brokers

**Model:** Connect shippers with carriers

```
FREIGHT BROKER SERVICES

What They Do:
├─ Find carriers for your loads
├─ Negotiate rates
├─ Track shipments
└─ Handle documentation

Pricing:
├─ Percentage of freight cost (15-25%)
├─ Or flat fee per load
└─ You pay broker, they pay carrier

Example:
Your shipment: 1,000 lbs to Denver (200 mi)
Direct carrier cost: $300
Broker fee: 20% = $60
Total cost: $360

Best For:
└─ Occasional long-distance shipping
    Full truckload or LTL shipments
    Farms without carrier relationships
```

**Pros:**
- Access to carrier network
- No need to find/vet carriers
- Volume leverage for pricing

**Cons:**
- Added cost (markup)
- Less control over carrier selection
- Variable service quality

### 2. Dedicated Delivery Services

**Model:** Outsource your local delivery routes

```
DEDICATED DELIVERY 3PL

Service Model:
├─ 3PL picks up from your farm
├─ Delivers to your customers
├─ Follows your schedule and routes
└─ Uses their vehicles and drivers

Pricing Structures:

Option A: Per-Delivery
├─ $8-15 per stop
├─ Minimum order value required
└─ Simple, transparent

Option B: Per-Mile
├─ $1.50-2.50 per mile
├─ Plus $5-10 per stop
└─ Fair for varied routes

Option C: Dedicated Route
├─ $200-400 per route (fixed)
├─ Defined service area
└─ Predictable costs

Example Service:
Your farm: 30 deliveries/week
3PL cost: $10/delivery average
Weekly cost: $300
vs. In-house: $200 (vehicle) + $250 (driver) = $450
SAVINGS: $150/week
```

**Pros:**
- Eliminate vehicle investment
- No driver management
- Scale up/down easily
- Professional delivery

**Cons:**
- Loss of direct customer interaction
- Quality control challenges
- Dependence on 3PL reliability

### 3. Food Hubs (Cooperative 3PL)

**Model:** Aggregation and distribution for local farms

```
FOOD HUB STRUCTURE

How It Works:
┌────────────┐
│  Farm A    │──┐
│  Farm B    │──┼──► Food Hub ──► Customers
│  Farm C    │──┘   (Aggregates,  (Restaurants,
│ (Your farm)│       markets,     Stores,
└────────────┘       distributes) Institutions)

Services:
├─ Pickup from farm
├─ Quality inspection
├─ Storage (cold)
├─ Marketing and sales
├─ Packing/labeling (optional)
├─ Delivery to buyers
└─ Invoicing and payment

Commission:
├─ Typical: 25-35% of sale price
├─ Or: Fixed fee per pound ($0.50-1.00)
└─ Covers all logistics and marketing

Example:
Your product: 500 lbs lettuce
Selling price through hub: $4/lb
Gross revenue: $2,000
Hub commission (30%): $600
Your net: $1,400 ($2.80/lb)

vs. Direct sales: $5/lb but only 300 lbs sold
Direct revenue: $1,500

Food hub advantage: Higher total revenue despite lower per-lb price
```

**Pros:**
- Access to markets you couldn't reach alone
- Shared infrastructure costs
- Marketing support
- Mission-aligned (support local food)

**Cons:**
- Significant commission
- Less control over pricing
- Dependent on hub success
- May compete with other hub farms

### 4. Cold Storage & Distribution Centers

**Model:** Rent space in refrigerated warehouses

```
COLD STORAGE 3PL SERVICES

Standard Services:
├─ Refrigerated storage (rent by pallet or sq ft)
├─ Receiving and put-away
├─ Inventory management
├─ Order picking and packing
├─ Loading for delivery/pickup
└─ Reporting

Pricing:
├─ Storage: $15-30 per pallet per month
├─ Handling: $5-15 per pallet in/out
├─ Picking: $0.50-2.00 per item
├─ Minimum monthly charge: $500-2,000
└─ Setup fee: $500-3,000

Example Use Case:
Peak harvest season: Need overflow storage
Rent 10 pallets × $20/mo = $200/month
vs. Building cooler: $15,000+ capital

Ideal For:
└─ Seasonal storage needs
    Expansion without capital investment
    Distribution to distant markets
```

**Pros:**
- Flexible capacity (scale up/down)
- No capital investment
- Professional facilities
- Strategic locations

**Cons:**
- Ongoing costs
- Less control
- Minimum volume requirements
- Complexity for small operations

## Selecting a 3PL Partner

### Evaluation Criteria

```
3PL PROVIDER ASSESSMENT CHECKLIST

CAPABILITIES:
□ Temperature-controlled transport/storage?
□ Food safety certifications (HACCP, SQF, FSMA)?
□ Service area covers your markets?
□ Capacity for your volume (current + growth)?
□ Technology integration (EDI, API, portals)?

EXPERIENCE:
□ Experience with fresh produce?
□ References from similar farms/businesses?
□ Years in business (stability)?
□ Knowledge of local markets?

FINANCIAL:
□ Pricing transparent and competitive?
□ Flexible contract terms?
□ Insurance coverage adequate?
□ Financial stability (credit check)?

PERFORMANCE:
□ On-time delivery track record?
□ Damage/claims rate?
□ Customer service responsiveness?
□ Technology for tracking/reporting?

ALIGNMENT:
□ Values alignment (sustainability, local food)?
□ Communication style compatible?
□ Willingness to customize services?
□ Long-term partnership potential?
```

### RFP (Request for Proposal) Process

```
3PL RFP TEMPLATE

1. COMPANY OVERVIEW
   └─ Brief description of your farm/business

2. SCOPE OF SERVICES
   ├─ Transportation OR warehousing OR both
   ├─ Service area/routes needed
   ├─ Volume (current and projected)
   └─ Frequency (daily, weekly, etc.)

3. REQUIREMENTS
   ├─ Temperature control specifications
   ├─ Food safety requirements
   ├─ Delivery time windows
   ├─ Technology/reporting needs
   └─ Special handling requirements

4. PRICING REQUEST
   ├─ Per-delivery cost
   ├─ Per-mile cost
   ├─ Storage costs (if applicable)
   ├─ Handling fees
   ├─ Fuel surcharges
   └─ Any other fees

5. QUESTIONS
   ├─ Insurance coverage details
   ├─ Claims process
   ├─ Technology capabilities
   ├─ Scalability options
   └─ Contract terms and flexibility

6. REFERENCES
   └─ Request 3 current clients (preferably fresh produce)

7. TIMELINE
   ├─ Proposal due date
   ├─ Selection date
   └─ Desired start date
```

## Structuring 3PL Agreements

### Key Contract Terms

```
ESSENTIAL CONTRACT ELEMENTS

1. SCOPE OF SERVICES
   - Specific services to be provided
   - Service levels (delivery windows, temperature)
   - Geographic coverage
   - Volume commitments (if any)

2. PRICING
   - Rate structure (per-delivery, per-mile, etc.)
   - Fuel surcharge policy
   - Accessorial charges (extra services)
   - Rate adjustment terms (annual increase?)
   - Payment terms (Net 15, 30, etc.)

3. PERFORMANCE STANDARDS
   - On-time delivery target (>95%)
   - Damage/loss rate (<1%)
   - Temperature compliance (100%)
   - Response time for issues (<2 hours)
   - Reporting requirements (weekly, monthly)

4. LIABILITY & INSURANCE
   - Cargo insurance coverage ($1M+ typical)
   - General liability insurance
   - Claims process and timeline
   - Limitations of liability
   - Force majeure provisions

5. FOOD SAFETY
   - HACCP compliance requirement
   - Temperature monitoring and logging
   - Sanitation standards
   - Traceability documentation
   - Audit rights

6. TERM & TERMINATION
   - Contract length (1 year typical)
   - Auto-renewal provisions
   - Termination notice period (30-60 days)
   - Termination for cause (breach)
   - Early termination fees (if any)

7. TECHNOLOGY & COMMUNICATION
   - Tracking/visibility systems
   - Order placement method
   - Reporting format and frequency
   - Point of contact for issues
   - Escalation procedures
```

### Pricing Models Comparison

```
3PL PRICING MODEL PROS/CONS

PER-DELIVERY MODEL:
Price: $10 per delivery
Pros: Simple, predictable per-order cost
Cons: Expensive for clustered routes
Best For: Variable volume, dispersed customers

PER-MILE + PER-STOP:
Price: $2/mile + $5/stop
Pros: Fair pricing based on distance
Cons: More complex calculation
Best For: Mixed routes (short and long)

DEDICATED ROUTE:
Price: $300 per route (fixed)
Pros: Predictable total cost, incentivizes efficiency
Cons: Pay even if volume low
Best For: Consistent weekly volume

PERCENTAGE OF SALES:
Price: 15% of product value
Pros: Aligns 3PL with your success
Cons: 3PL may prioritize high-value products
Best For: Full-service 3PL partnerships

HYBRID MODEL:
Price: $200 base + $3/delivery over 20 deliveries
Pros: Balances fixed and variable costs
Cons: More complex
Best For: Seasonal fluctuation management
```

## Integrating 3PL into Operations

### Workflow Changes

```
OPERATIONAL INTEGRATION

IN-HOUSE DELIVERY (Before):
1. Harvest product
2. Pack and label
3. Load delivery vehicle
4. Driver delivers route
5. Driver returns, completes logs
6. Process payments

WITH 3PL (After):
1. Harvest product
2. Pack and label
3. Stage for 3PL pickup (organized by route)
4. 3PL picks up (scheduled time)
5. Receive delivery confirmation from 3PL
6. Process customer payments
7. Pay 3PL monthly invoice

Changes Required:
├─ Staging area for 3PL pickup
├─ Clear labeling (customer, address, products)
├─ Pickup schedule coordination
├─ Communication protocol with 3PL
├─ Customer notification of 3PL delivery
└─ Invoice reconciliation process
```

### Communication Protocols

```
3PL COMMUNICATION PLAN

REGULAR COMMUNICATION:
├─ Order submission: 48 hours before pickup
├─ Pickup confirmation: Day before
├─ Delivery tracking: Real-time updates
├─ Weekly performance review call
└─ Monthly business review meeting

EXCEPTION COMMUNICATION:
├─ Delays: Immediate notification
├─ Damages: Within 24 hours
├─ Customer complaints: Same day
├─ Capacity issues: 1 week advance notice
└─ Pricing changes: 30 days notice

POINTS OF CONTACT:
├─ Your operations manager ↔ 3PL operations
├─ Your sales manager ↔ 3PL account manager
├─ Your owner ↔ 3PL senior management
└─ Emergency: 24/7 phone number

TECHNOLOGY INTEGRATION:
├─ Email order confirmations
├─ Online tracking portal
├─ Automated delivery notifications
├─ Monthly performance dashboards
└─ API integration (if available)
```

## Cost-Benefit Analysis

### Calculating 3PL ROI

```
3PL vs. IN-HOUSE COMPARISON

IN-HOUSE DELIVERY COSTS:
Fixed Costs (Annual):
├─ Vehicle: $40,000 (depreciation $8,000/yr)
├─ Insurance: $3,000
├─ Maintenance: $2,000
└─ Total Fixed: $13,000/year

Variable Costs (Annual, 20,000 miles):
├─ Fuel: $3,200
├─ Driver labor: $15,000 (part-time)
├─ Repairs: $1,000
└─ Total Variable: $19,200/year

TOTAL IN-HOUSE: $32,200/year
Deliveries per year: 1,500
Cost per delivery: $21.47

3PL OPTION:
Cost per delivery: $12
Annual deliveries: 1,500
TOTAL 3PL COST: $18,000/year

SAVINGS: $14,200/year (44% reduction)
PLUS: Eliminate vehicle management headaches

BREAKEVEN ANALYSIS:
If 3PL costs >$21.47/delivery, keep in-house
If 3PL costs <$21.47/delivery, outsource makes sense
```

### Hidden Costs & Benefits

```
FULL COST ACCOUNTING

IN-HOUSE HIDDEN COSTS:
├─ Management time (route planning, driver supervision)
├─ Vehicle downtime (repairs, breakdowns)
├─ Capital tied up in vehicles
├─ Liability insurance increases
├─ Compliance complexity (DOT regulations)
└─ Opportunity cost (time not spent on production)

3PL HIDDEN COSTS:
├─ Loss of customer interaction
├─ Reduced flexibility (must plan ahead)
├─ Dependency risk (3PL failure)
├─ Integration effort (systems, processes)
└─ Potential quality variability

3PL HIDDEN BENEFITS:
├─ Scalability (grow without capital investment)
├─ Professional service (better than you might provide)
├─ Technology access (tracking, reporting)
├─ Market expansion (3PL has wider reach)
├─ Risk transfer (3PL carries liability)
└─ Focus on core business (production)
```

## Transitioning to 3PL

### Implementation Plan

```
3PL IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE

WEEKS 1-2: PREPARATION
□ Finalize 3PL contract
□ Designate internal project lead
□ Communicate plan to team
□ Identify operational changes needed
□ Prepare customer communication

WEEKS 3-4: INTEGRATION
□ 3PL orientation/training
□ Integrate technology systems
□ Establish staging area for pickups
□ Create labeling and documentation templates
□ Conduct test deliveries (5-10)

WEEK 5: SOFT LAUNCH
□ Start with 25% of deliveries
□ Daily check-ins with 3PL
□ Monitor performance closely
□ Gather customer feedback
□ Troubleshoot issues quickly

WEEK 6-8: RAMP UP
□ Increase to 50%, then 75% of deliveries
□ Continue monitoring and adjusting
□ Refine processes based on learnings
□ Build confidence in partnership

WEEK 9+: FULL OPERATION
□ 100% of defined deliveries to 3PL
□ Establish regular review meetings
□ Maintain communication protocols
□ Continuous improvement focus

ONGOING:
□ Monthly performance reviews
□ Quarterly strategic planning
□ Annual contract renewal evaluation
```

## Summary

Third-party logistics can transform your distribution:

1. **Evaluate carefully**: Not all farms benefit from 3PL
2. **Choose wisely**: Select partners with produce experience
3. **Structure properly**: Clear agreements protect both parties
4. **Monitor performance**: KPIs ensure accountability
5. **Communicate well**: Regular touchpoints build strong partnerships

**Key Takeaways:**
- 3PL makes sense when outsourcing costs less than in-house
- Focus on core competencies (growing) while 3PL handles logistics
- Food safety and quality standards are non-negotiable
- Start small, scale gradually
- Performance monitoring prevents issues

Strategic 3PL partnerships enable growth without proportional capital investment.
