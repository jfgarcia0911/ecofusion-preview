## Introduction to Post-Harvest Physiology

### Why Physiology Matters

Fresh produce is **alive** after harvest. Living tissues continue metabolic processes that affect:
- **Quality**: Color, texture, flavor, nutrition
- **Shelf life**: How long product remains marketable
- **Safety**: Pathogen growth and contamination risk
- **Value**: Price point and customer satisfaction

Understanding these biological processes enables you to:
- Extend shelf life significantly
- Reduce waste and increase profitability
- Maintain premium quality
- Predict and manage product behavior

## Respiration: The Primary Aging Process

### What is Respiration?

**Respiration** is the breakdown of stored sugars and starches to produce energy for metabolic processes:

```
Aerobic Respiration:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (heat)
(Sugar)   (Oxygen) (Carbon  (Water)  (ATP + Heat)
                    Dioxide)

Result: Loss of:
- Stored sugars (sweetness)
- Weight (moisture)
- Quality (freshness)
- Energy (creates heat)
```

### Respiration Rate Categories

```
RESPIRATION RATE CLASSIFICATION

Very Low    │ 5-10 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
            │ Examples: Onions, garlic, winter squash
            │ Shelf life: Weeks to months
            │
Low         │ 10-20 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
            │ Examples: Peppers, cucumbers, melons
            │ Shelf life: 1-3 weeks
            │
Moderate    │ 20-40 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
            │ Examples: Tomatoes, lettuce, cabbage
            │ Shelf life: 1-2 weeks
            │
High        │ 40-60 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
            │ Examples: Spinach, kale, green beans
            │ Shelf life: 7-14 days
            │
Very High   │ >60 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
            │ Examples: Basil, cilantro, microgreens
            │ Shelf life: 5-10 days
            │
Extremely   │ >100 mg CO₂/kg·hr @ 41°F
High        │ Examples: Asparagus, mushrooms, corn
            │ Shelf life: 3-7 days
```

### Temperature Effect on Respiration

**Q₁₀ Rule**: Respiration rate approximately doubles for every 10°C (18°F) increase

```
TEMPERATURE IMPACT ON LETTUCE SHELF LIFE

Temperature    Respiration   Relative      Expected
               Rate          Quality Loss  Shelf Life
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
32°F (0°C)     25 mg/kg·hr   1x (Baseline) 21 days
41°F (5°C)     32 mg/kg·hr   1.3x          16 days
50°F (10°C)    50 mg/kg·hr   2x            10 days
59°F (15°C)    78 mg/kg·hr   3x            7 days
68°F (20°C)    120 mg/kg·hr  5x            4 days
77°F (25°C)    185 mg/kg·hr  7x            3 days

KEY INSIGHT: Every hour at room temperature costs
approximately 4-6 hours of shelf life at proper refrigeration!
```

### Practical Implications

**Example: Basil Storage**

```
SCENARIO: 100 lbs basil harvested at 8 AM

Option A: Immediate cooling to 50°F
├─ Respiration rate: 85 mg CO₂/kg·hr
├─ Expected shelf life: 7 days
├─ Saleable quantity at day 7: 90 lbs
└─ Revenue @ $16/lb: $1,440

Option B: Left at 70°F for 4 hours, then cooled
├─ High respiration period: 4 hours @ 150 mg/kg·hr
├─ Reduced shelf life: 4-5 days
├─ Saleable quantity at day 5: 75 lbs
└─ Revenue @ $16/lb: $1,200

COST OF DELAY: $240 (17% revenue loss)
```

## Ethylene: The Ripening Hormone

### What is Ethylene?

**Ethylene (C₂H₄)** is a natural plant hormone that triggers ripening and senescence (aging).

```
ETHYLENE CASCADE EFFECT

Ethylene Production → Ripening → More Ethylene → Accelerated Aging

Examples:
- Color change (green to yellow/red)
- Softening (cell wall breakdown)
- Flavor development
- Chlorophyll degradation
- Increased respiration
```

### Ethylene Production Levels

**High Producers** (>100 μL/kg·hr)
- Ripe tomatoes
- Apples
- Pears
- Melons
- Bananas (especially ripe)

**Moderate Producers** (10-100 μL/kg·hr)
- Cucumbers
- Squash
- Mature green tomatoes

**Low Producers** (<10 μL/kg·hr)
- Lettuce
- Spinach
- Kale
- Peppers
- Most herbs

### Ethylene Sensitivity

```
SENSITIVITY CLASSIFICATION

Very Sensitive:
- Lettuce (russet spotting, ribs brown)
- Cucumbers (yellowing, decay)
- Basil (blackening, leaf drop)
- Kale (yellowing)

Moderately Sensitive:
- Peppers (color change)
- Tomatoes (premature ripening)
- Herbs (yellowing, wilting)

Low Sensitivity:
- Onions
- Garlic
- Mature produce already at target ripeness
```

### Incompatible Storage Combinations

| DO NOT Store Together | Why | Solution |
|----------------------|-----|----------|
| Tomatoes + Lettuce | Ethylene causes russet spots | Separate storage rooms |
| Apples + Greens | Rapid yellowing of greens | Physical separation |
| Ripe melons + Herbs | Accelerated senescence | Different zones |
| Cucumbers + Tomatoes | Yellowing, decay | Separate coolers |

### Managing Ethylene

**Prevention Strategies:**

1. **Physical Separation**
```
COOLER LAYOUT FOR ETHYLENE MANAGEMENT

┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                         │
│  ZONE A: High Ethylene Producers        │
│  ┌────────────────────────────────┐     │
│  │ Tomatoes, Melons, Stone Fruit  │     │
│  └────────────────────────────────┘     │
│                                         │
│  ──────────── Barrier ────────────      │
│                                         │
│  ZONE B: Ethylene-Sensitive             │
│  ┌────────────────────────────────┐     │
│  │ Lettuce, Herbs, Leafy Greens   │     │
│  └────────────────────────────────┘     │
│                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

2. **Ventilation**
   - Air circulation dilutes ethylene concentration
   - Fresh air exchange (when temperature allows)
   - Fan systems for air movement

3. **Ethylene Scrubbing**
   - Activated carbon filters
   - Potassium permanganate sachets
   - Commercial ethylene absorbers
   - Cost: $50-200/month for small operation

4. **Temperature Control**
   - Lower temperatures reduce ethylene production
   - Proper cooling slows ripening processes

## Transpiration: Water Loss

### What is Transpiration?

**Transpiration** is the loss of water vapor from plant tissues to the environment.

```
WATER LOSS MECHANISM

Plant Tissue Water → Evaporation → Atmosphere
(High moisture)                    (Lower moisture)

Driving Forces:
- Vapor pressure deficit (VPD)
- Temperature
- Air movement
- Surface area
- Protective coating
```

### Effects of Water Loss

| % Weight Loss | Visual Effects | Market Impact |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| 1-3% | No visible change | Undetectable |
| 3-5% | Slight softening | Quality concern |
| 5-7% | Visible wilting | Reduced price |
| 7-10% | Severe wilting | Unmarketable |
| >10% | Complete limpness | Total loss |

### Water Loss Rates by Crop

```
TRANSPIRATION SUSCEPTIBILITY

Very Susceptible (>10% loss per day at 70°F, 50% RH):
├─ Leafy greens (high surface:volume)
├─ Herbs (minimal protective coating)
├─ Microgreens (thin tissues)
└─ Green onions

Moderately Susceptible (5-10% loss per day):
├─ Lettuce heads
├─ Celery
├─ Broccoli
└─ Cucumbers

Low Susceptibility (<5% loss per day):
├─ Tomatoes (protective skin)
├─ Peppers (thick cuticle)
├─ Winter squash (hard rind)
└─ Onions (protective scales)
```

### Controlling Transpiration

**1. Relative Humidity Management**

```
OPTIMAL HUMIDITY BY CROP TYPE

95-100% RH:
- Leafy greens
- Herbs
- Root vegetables (with tops)

90-95% RH:
- Lettuce heads
- Broccoli
- Celery
- Most vegetables

85-90% RH:
- Tomatoes
- Peppers
- Eggplant

65-70% RH:
- Onions
- Garlic
- Winter squash
```

**2. Temperature Reduction**
- Lower temperature = lower vapor pressure deficit
- Proper cooling reduces transpiration rate
- Maintain consistent temperatures

**3. Packaging**

| Packaging Type | Water Loss Prevention | Best Use |
|----------------|----------------------|----------|
| Perforated bags | Moderate (60% reduction) | Leafy greens, herbs |
| Sealed clamshells | High (90% reduction) | Microgreens, delicate herbs |
| Vented boxes | Low (30% reduction) | Bulk vegetables |
| Hydrocooling + ice | High (95% reduction) | Root vegetables |
| Wax coating | High (80% reduction) | Cucumbers, peppers |

**4. Rapid Cooling**
- Immediate cooling reduces transpiration
- Each hour of delay increases water loss
- Target: Cool within 30 minutes of harvest

**Methods:**

   - Water pans

## Shelf Life Prediction

### Shelf Life Formula

```
Shelf Life = Initial Quality × Storage Conditions × Handling Care
             ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                    Respiration Rate × Time

Factors:
- Initial quality: Harvest maturity, damage, time to cool
- Storage conditions: Temperature, humidity, ethylene
- Handling care: Touchpoints, damage prevention
- Respiration rate: Crop-specific, temperature-dependent
```

### Shelf Life Modeling Example

**Lettuce Head Storage Prediction:**

```
SCENARIO COMPARISON

Scenario A: Optimal Conditions
├─ Harvest: Morning, optimal maturity
├─ Cooling: Within 30 min to 34°F
├─ Storage: 34°F, 98% RH, no ethylene
├─ Handling: Minimal, gentle
└─ PREDICTED SHELF LIFE: 18-21 days

Scenario B: Typical Conditions
├─ Harvest: Midday, good maturity
├─ Cooling: Within 2 hours to 36°F
├─ Storage: 36-38°F, 90% RH, minimal ethylene
├─ Handling: Normal commercial

├─ Cooling: 4-6 hours to 40°F
├─ Storage: 38-42°F, 85% RH, ethylene present
├─ Handling: Multiple touchpoints
└─ PREDICTED SHELF LIFE: 7-10 days

LESSON: Optimal conditions extend shelf life 2-3x!
```

## Harvest Maturity & Quality

### Maturity Indices

**Lettuce:**
- Head firmness: 60-80% compressibility
- Size: 12-16 oz for retail
- Color: Bright, no yellowing
- Leaf texture: Crisp, turgid

**Tomatoes:**
```
TOMATO MATURITY STAGES

Stage 1: Mature Green
├─ Full size, green color
├─ White interior changing to pink
├─ Storage: 55-60°F for ripening
└─ Shelf life: 14-21 days

Stage 2: Breaker
├─ 10-30% color change
├─ Storage: 55-60°F
└─ Shelf life: 10-14 days

Stage 3: Turning
├─ 30-60% color change
├─ Storage: 55°F
└─ Shelf life: 7-10 days

Stage 4: Pink/Light Red
├─ 60-90% color change
├─ Storage: 50-55°F
```

**Herbs:**
- Full leaf development

## Practical Applications

### Cooling Protocol by Crop

**Leafy Greens (Lettuce, Kale, Spinach):**
```
OPTIMAL COOLING PROTOCOL

1. HARVEST (6-8 AM, before heat)
   └─ Target field temperature: <75°F

2. FIELD HANDLING (Immediate)
   ├─ Place in shade structure
   ├─ Mist lightly if wilting
   └─ Transport to facility within 30 min

3. WASHING (Cold water 34-38°F)
   ├─ Removes field heat
   ├─ Cleans surface
   └─ Duration: 2-3 minutes

4. SPINNING/DRYING
   ├─ Remove excess water
   └─ Prevent condensation in storage

5. FORCED-AIR COOLING
   ├─ Target: 34°F within 1 hour
   └─ Monitor core temperature

6. STORAGE
   ├─ 32-36°F
   ├─ 95-100% RH
   └─ Away from ethylene sources

RESULT: 18-21 day shelf life potential
```

**Herbs (Basil, Cilantro, Parsley):**
```
HERB-SPECIFIC PROTOCOL

BASIL (Chilling-sensitive!):
1. Harvest morning, optimal maturity
2. NO WASHING (increases decay risk)
3. Gentle handling into containers
4. Cool to 50-55°F (NOT colder!)
5. Store in perforated clamshells
6. 90-95% RH
7. Shelf life: 5-7 days

CILANTRO & PARSLEY (Cold-hardy):
1. Harvest morning
2. Wash in cold water (34-38°F)
3. Shake dry or light spin
4. Bundle or package
5. Cool to 32-36°F
6. 95-100% RH (hydrate stems)
7. Shelf life: 10-14 days
```

**Fruiting Vegetables (Tomatoes, Cucumbers, Peppers):**
```
FRUITING VEGETABLE PROTOCOL

TOMATOES:
1. Harvest at appropriate maturity stage
2. Handle gently (easily bruised)
3. No washing (can promote decay)
4. Air cool to target temperature:
   - Mature green: 55-60°F
   - Firm ripe: 50-55°F
5. 85-90% RH
6. Separate from ethylene-sensitive crops

CUCUMBERS & PEPPERS:
1. Harvest at full size
2. Gentle washing if needed
3. Dry thoroughly
4. Cool to 45-55°F (depending on crop)
5. 85-95% RH
6. Avoid chilling injury (<45°F for cucumbers)
```

## Common Post-Harvest Problems

### Problem-Solution Matrix

| Problem | Symptoms | Cause | Solution |
|---------|----------|-------|----------|
| **Wilting** | Limp leaves, loss of turgor | Water loss, heat | Rapid cooling, high humidity, proper packaging |
| **Yellowing** | Chlorophyll breakdown | Ethylene, age, high temperature | Ethylene management, lower temperature, faster turnover |
| **Browning** | Oxidation, discoloration | Damage, age, ethylene | Gentle handling, avoid ethylene, maintain cold chain |
| **Softening** | Loss of firmness | Overripening, high temperature | Proper harvest maturity, temperature control |
| **Decay** | Microbial growth | Moisture, damage, contamination | Sanitation, dry storage, avoid condensation |
| **Chilling injury** | Pitting, discoloration | Storage below critical temperature | Maintain minimum temperature for crop |
| **Freezing injury** | Water-soaked appearance, collapse | Storage below 32°F | Monitor low temperature alarm |

### Diagnostic Checklist

**When quality issues arise:**

```
QUALITY INVESTIGATION PROTOCOL

□ 1. IDENTIFY PROBLEM
     What exactly is wrong? (wilting, yellowing, decay, etc.)

□ 2. CHECK TEMPERATURE RECORDS
     - Was cold chain maintained?
     - Any temperature excursions?
     - When did problem likely begin?

□ 3. REVIEW HUMIDITY
     - Was RH appropriate for crop?
     - Evidence of excessive drying?
     - Condensation present?

□ 4. ASSESS ETHYLENE EXPOSURE
     - What else was stored nearby?
     - Any ethylene producers present?

□ 5. EVALUATE HANDLING
     - Any obvious damage?
     - Excessive touchpoints?
     - Packaging appropriate?

□ 6. DETERMINE TIMING
     - Days since harvest?
     - Within expected shelf life?
     - Batch-specific or widespread?

□ 7. IMPLEMENT CORRECTION
     - Immediate: Isolate affected product
     - Short-term: Adjust storage conditions
     - Long-term: Revise protocols

□ 8. DOCUMENT & LEARN
     - Record findings
     - Update procedures
     - Train team
```

## Summary

Post-harvest physiology knowledge enables you to:

1. **Understand biology**: Living tissues require specific care
2. **Predict shelf life**: Based on crop characteristics and conditions
3. **Optimize storage**: Temperature, humidity, and environment
4. **Prevent quality loss**: Manage respiration, ethylene, and water loss
5. **Maximize value**: Longer shelf life and better quality

**Key Principles:**
- Cool rapidly and maintain cold chain
- Match storage conditions to crop requirements
- Minimize respiration through temperature control
- Manage ethylene exposure carefully
- Maintain optimal humidity
- Monitor continuously and respond quickly

Proper post-harvest management can double or triple shelf life, dramatically improving profitability and customer satisfaction.
