### Part 2: Short Answer (15 points)

**26. Five CCPs (3 points):**
- Receiving (contamination prevention)
- Washing (if applicable - sanitizer concentration)
- Cold storage (temperature control)
- Transportation (temperature maintenance)
- Cross-contamination prevention

**27. One-up, one-back (3 points):**
- One-back: Where product came from (supplier, field, harvest date)
- One-up: Where product went (customer, delivery date, quantity)
- Importance: Enables rapid traceback in food safety incidents, FSMA compliance, limits recall scope

**28. Lot code requirements (3 points):**
- Product identification
- Harvest/pack date
- Unique lot/batch number
- Quantity
- Optional: Processing information, location

**29. Illness response (3 points):**
- Document all details immediately
- Conduct traceback (one-back) and traceforward (one-up)
- Isolate suspected product
- Notify health department if required
- Contact all affected customers
- Implement corrective actions
- Document entire process

**30. Food safety culture elements (3 points):**
- Leadership commitment (management priority)
- Employee empowerment (reporting without retaliation)
- Training and education (ongoing, documented)
- Communication (regular meetings, visible reminders)
- Accountability (clear roles, performance tracking)

### Part 3: Calculations & Analysis (15 points)

**31. ROI Calculation (5 points):**
- Time savings: 6 hrs/week × 52 weeks × $20/hr = $6,240/year
- Waste reduction: $4,000/year
- Total annual benefit: $10,240
- Year 1 cost: $3,000 + $1,500 = $4,500
- Year 1 ROI: ($10,240 - $4,500) / $4,500 = 127.6%
- Payback period: $4,500 / $10,240 = 5.3 months

**32. ABC Analysis (5 points):**
- Product A (Lettuce): A Item (80% of revenue)
  - Management: Daily monitoring, tight forecasting, never stock out, premium quality
- Product B (Kale): B Item (16% of revenue)
  - Management: Weekly monitoring, standard forecasting, acceptable occasional stock outs
- Product C (Radishes): C Item (4% of revenue)
  - Management: Minimal monitoring, harvest to order only, stock outs acceptable

**33. E-commerce vs. Farmers Markets (5 points):**

E-commerce Advantages:
- 24/7 sales availability
- Reach beyond local market
- Lower labor per sale (automated)
- Data on customer preferences
- Scalable

E-commerce Disadvantages:
- Platform and payment processing fees
- Delivery logistics required
- Technology learning curve
- Customer acquisition costs
- Competition with large platforms

Farmers Market Advantages:
- Highest prices (retail + premium)
- Direct customer relationships
- Immediate payment
- Brand building face-to-face
- Market research opportunity

Farmers Market Disadvantages:
- Time-intensive (8-12 hrs/market)
- Weather dependent
- Limited scale
- Seasonal (many markets)
- Inventory uncertainty

Grading criteria:
- Root causes identified (3 points)

  - Specific, actionable, address root cause
- Cost/timeline realistic (2 points)
- Financial benefit calculated (2 points)
