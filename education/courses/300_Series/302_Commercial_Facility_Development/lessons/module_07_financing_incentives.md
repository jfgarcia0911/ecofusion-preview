## 2. Small Business Administration (SBA) Loans

### SBA 7(a) Loan Program

```
SBA 7(a) PROGRAM DETAILS

Loan Amount: Up to $5 million
Use: Working capital, equipment, real estate, acquisition
Term:
  - Working capital: Up to 10 years
  - Equipment: Up to 25 years
  - Real estate: Up to 25 years

Interest Rates:
  - Base: Prime + 2.25% to 4.75%
  - Current typical: 10-12%
  - Fixed or variable

Down Payment: 10-20% typical

Fees:
  - Guarantee fee: 2-3.75% of guaranteed portion
  - Closing costs: $5-15K
  - Annual servicing fee: 0.55%

Eligibility:
□ For-profit business
□ Operating in U.S.
□ Owner-operated
□ <$15M net worth
□ <$5M average net income
□ Cannot get funding elsewhere

Documentation Required:
□ Business plan
□ 3-year financial projections
□ Personal financial statements
□ Tax returns (3 years)
□ Legal documents
□ Collateral documentation

Timeline: 60-90 days typical
Approval rate: 60-70% with good application
```

### SBA 504 Loan Program

```
SBA 504 PROGRAM STRUCTURE

Three-Part Financing:
1. Bank loan: 50% (conventional)
2. SBA 504 loan: 40% (low rate, fixed)
3. Owner equity: 10%

Example: $2M project
Bank loan: $1,000,000 @ 7% (variable)
504 loan: $800,000 @ 5.5% (fixed 20-year)
Equity: $200,000 (10%)

Blended rate: ~6.4%
vs. conventional 100% bank: ~7.5%

Loan Amount: Up to $5.5M (certain energy projects: $5.5M)
Term: 20-25 years (real estate/equipment)
Use: Fixed assets only (real estate, equipment, renovation)

Key Benefits:
□ Low down payment (10%)
□ Fixed rate (portion)
□ Long term
□ Frees up working capital

Requirements:
□ Creates or retains jobs (1 per $65-100K)
□ Owner-occupied (51%+ of building)
□ Job creation affidavit
□ Economic development impact

Fees:
- Closing costs: $15-30K
- Packaging fee: $5-8K

Timeline: 90-120 days
Best for: Real estate-heavy projects
```

## 3. USDA Rural Development Programs

### USDA Business & Industry (B&I) Loan

```
USDA B&I GUARANTEED LOAN

Eligibility:
□ Rural area (generally <50,000 population)
□ Defined rural area by USDA
□ Creates/saves rural jobs
□ Improves economic environment

Loan Amount: Up to $25M (90% guaranteed)
Terms: Up to 30 years real estate, 15 years equipment
Interest Rate: Negotiated with lender (typically 6-8%)
Guarantee: Up to 80% (projects >$5M)

Down Payment: 20% minimum

Use:
□ Land, buildings
□ Equipment, machinery
□ Working capital (limited)
□ Refinancing (certain conditions)

Benefits vs. Conventional:
+ Lower down payment (20% vs. 25-30%)
+ Longer terms
+ Guarantee reduces lender risk
+ Competitive rates

Documentation:
Similar to SBA plus rural job creation focus

Timeline: 120-180 days
Best for: Rural CEA projects creating local jobs
```

### USDA Value-Added Producer Grants

```
VAPG PROGRAM

Grant Amount: $75K-250K (50% match required)
Purpose: Planning or working capital for value-added ag

Eligible Activities:
Planning Grants:
□ Feasibility studies
□ Business plans
□ Marketing plans

Working Capital Grants:
□ Marketing expenses
□ Salaries
□ Packaging
□ Distribution

Eligible Applicants:
□ Independent producers
□ Farmer/rancher cooperatives
□ Agricultural producer groups
□ Majority-controlled producer-based ventures

Match Requirement:
50% matching funds required (can be in-kind for planning)

Example:
Total project: $100K
Grant request: $50K
Your match: $50K (cash or in-kind)

Timeline:

Success Tips:
□ Strong business plan

□ Job creation emphasis
□ Local sourcing story
□ Marketing strategy
```

## 4. Equity Financing

### Equity vs. Debt

```
EQUITY FINANCING FUNDAMENTALS

Equity Characteristics:
□ No repayment obligation
□ No interest payments
□ Investors own % of company
□ Investors share in profits/losses
□ Investors may have board seats
□ May have decision-making rights

When to Use Equity:
□ Insufficient cash flow for debt service
□ Want to minimize personal risk
□ Bring in strategic partners
□ Access investor expertise
□ High-growth potential
□ Uncertain early cash flow

Cost of Equity:
Not "free" - investors expect 20-40% IRR
Example: $500K investment
5-year target: $1.2-2.5M return
Often requires 20-40% ownership

Types of Equity:
- Friends & family (informal)
- Angel investors (individuals, $25K-500K)
- Venture capital (institutional, $500K-10M+)
- Private equity (larger, $5M+)
- Crowdfunding (many small investors)
```

### Structuring Equity Deals

```
EQUITY DEAL TERMS

Key Terms to Negotiate:

Valuation:
Pre-money value: Company value before investment
Post-money value: Company value after investment
Example:
  Pre-money: $1M
  Investment: $500K
  Post-money: $1.5M
  Investor ownership: $500K/$1.5M = 33.3%

Liquidation Preference:
1× preference: Investor gets money back first on exit
2× preference: Investor gets 2× investment back first

Board Seats:
How many seats does investor get?
Veto rights on key decisions?

Anti-dilution:
Protection if future rounds at lower valuation

Exit Strategy:
When can investor expect liquidity?
Put rights (can force buyback)?
Drag-along rights (can force sale)?

Dividends:
Will investor receive regular dividends?
Cumulative or non-cumulative?

Information Rights:
Monthly financials?
Annual audits?
Access to management?
```

## 5. Equipment Financing

### Equipment Lease vs. Purchase

```
EQUIPMENT FINANCING OPTIONS

Operating Lease:
Structure: Rent equipment, no ownership
Term: 3-7 years
Payments: Monthly, fully deductible
End of term: Return, extend, or buy (FMV)
Benefits: Lower payments, no obsolescence risk
Best for: Technology that changes rapidly

Capital Lease:
Structure: Lease-to-own
Term: 3-7 years
Payments: Monthly
End of term: Own equipment (minimal buyout)
Benefits: Ownership, depreciation benefits
Best for: Long-life equipment you want to own

Equipment Loan:
Structure: Loan secured by equipment
Down payment: 10-20%
Term: 3-7 years
Benefits: Immediate ownership, depreciation
Best for: Essential equipment, stable technology

Cost Comparison (on $100K equipment):
                    Down Pay   Monthly     Total Cost   Own?
Operating Lease     $0        $1,800      $129,600     No
Capital Lease       $0        $1,950      $140,400     Yes
Equipment Loan      $20,000   $1,650      $138,600     Yes
Cash Purchase       $100,000  $0          $100,000     Yes

Decision Factors:
- Cash availability (lease preserves capital)
- Tax situation (ownership allows depreciation)
- Technology change rate
- Equipment life expectancy
```

## 6. Government Incentives & Programs

### Energy Efficiency Incentives

```
ENERGY INCENTIVE PROGRAMS

Federal:
Investment Tax Credit (ITC): 30% for solar (2023-2032)
Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery (MACRS): Depreciate equipment
Section 179 Deduction: Up to $1.16M immediate expense
Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings Deduction (179D):
  Up to $5/sf for efficient buildings

State/Utility Programs:
Custom Efficiency Rebates:
  HVAC: $50-500/ton
  LED lighting: $20-100/fixture
  Motors/VFDs: $50-200/HP
  Building envelope: $0.50-2/sf

Demand Response:
  Payment for load reduction during peak
  Typical: $50-200/kW/year

On-Bill Financing:
  Utility loans for efficiency upgrades
  Repaid through utility bill
  0-4% interest typical

Example Incentive Stack (20K sf facility):
Solar PV (100 kW): $200K installed
  Federal ITC (30%): -$60,000
  State rebate (10%): -$20,000
  Net cost: $120,000

HVAC upgrades: $75K
  Utility rebate: -$15,000
  Net cost: $60,000

LED lighting: $50K
  Utility rebate: -$10,000
  Net cost: $40,000

Total incentives: $105,000 (32% of $330K total)
```

### Agricultural Incentives

```
AGRICULTURE PROGRAMS

USDA EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program):
Purpose: Conservation practices
Payment: 50-75% of costs (up to $450K/6 years)
Eligible: High tunnels, irrigation, energy efficiency
Application: Annual, through local NRCS office

State Agricultural Grants:
Vary widely by state
Typical: $5-50K for equipment, marketing, research
Competitive application process
Check state dept. of agriculture

Property Tax Exemptions:
Agricultural classification: 50-90% reduction typical
Qualify: Meet minimum acreage/sales thresholds
Renewable Energy exemption: Some states exempt solar value

Sales Tax Exemptions:
Agricultural inputs often exempt
Seeds, nutrients, growing supplies
Varies by state; check regulations

Beginning Farmer Programs:
Loan guarantees
Reduced interest rates
Technical assistance
Land access programs
```

## 8. Financing Timeline & Process

### Financing Workback Schedule

```
FINANCING TIMELINE (Working Backwards from Need Date)

6-9 Months Before:
□ Determine financing need
□ Research options
□ Select best sources
□ Begin business plan

4-6 Months Before:
□ Complete business plan
□ Prepare financial projections
□ Gather documentation
□ Approach lenders/investors

3-4 Months Before:
□ Submit applications
□ Answer questions
□ Provide additional info
□ Site visits

2-3 Months Before:
□ Receive term sheets
□ Negotiate terms
□ Due diligence
□ Appraisals/inspections

1-2 Months Before:
□ Finalize documents
□ Address conditions
□ Legal review
□ Prepare for closing

At Funding Need Date:
□ Closing meeting
□ Sign documents
□ Funds released
□ Begin project

Recommendation: Start 6 months before funding needed
Allows time for issues, multiple sources, best terms
```

## 9. Investor Pitch Preparation

### Pitch Deck Structure

```
INVESTOR PITCH DECK (10-15 slides)

1. Cover: Company name, tagline, contact

2. Problem: What problem are you solving?
   Urban food insecurity, supply chain fragility

3. Solution: How does your CEA operation solve it?
   Local, year-round, pesticide-free production

4. Market Opportunity: How big is the market?
   $XXM local market, growing XX% annually

5. Business Model: How do you make money?
   Revenue streams, pricing, unit economics

6. Competitive Advantage: Why you vs. others?
   Technology, location, team, partnerships

7. Traction: What have you accomplished?
   Sales to date, customers signed, facility built

8. Go-to-Market: How will you acquire customers?
   Direct sales, distribution, marketing plan

9. Financials: Revenue, expenses, profitability
   3-5 year projections, key metrics

10. Team: Who will execute this?
    Founders, key hires, advisors, experience

11. Funding Ask: How much, for what, on what terms?
    $XXK for equipment, working capital, timeline

12. Use of Funds: Detailed breakdown
    Chart showing allocation

13. Exit Strategy: How will investors get paid?
    Acquisition, dividends, buyback, timeline

14. Milestones: What will you achieve?
    12, 24, 36 month goals

15. Contact: Thank you, Q&A, next steps

Deliver in: 10-15 minutes
Practice extensively
Know your numbers cold
Anticipate tough questions
```

## 10. Financing Pitfalls to Avoid

### Common Mistakes

```
FINANCING MISTAKES

1. Under-Capitalizing
   Problem: Not borrowing enough, run out of money
   Solution: 20-30% contingency + 12mo working capital

2. Starting Too Late
   Problem: Need money NOW, no time to shop
   Solution: Start 6-9 months before funding needed

3. Poor Documentation
   Problem: Incomplete applications, delays, denials
   Solution: Complete business plan, financial projections

4. Unrealistic Projections
   Problem: Lenders don't believe numbers
   Solution: Conservative assumptions, comparable benchmarks

5. No Collateral
   Problem: Can't secure loan without assets
   Solution: SBA programs, equipment leases, equity

6. Weak Personal Credit
   Problem: Owner credit impacts business loan
   Solution: Improve credit score before applying

7. No Equity Investment
   Problem: Lenders want "skin in the game"
   Solution: 10-25% equity, shows commitment

8. Shopping Too Hard
   Problem: Multiple credit pulls hurt score
   Solution: Pre-qualify, then formal app to 2-3 lenders

9. Not Reading Terms
   Problem: Agree to unfavorable covenants
   Solution: Legal review, negotiate before signing

10. Giving Up Too Much Equity
    Problem: Lose control, limit upside
    Solution: Understand valuation, negotiate
```

## Summary

Successful financing requires:

1. **Multiple sources** - debt, equity, incentives
2. **Early planning** - 6-9 months before need
3. **Strong documentation** - business plan, financials
4. **Realistic projections** - conservative, defensible
5. **Adequate capitalization** - construction + working capital
6. **Shop carefully** - compare terms, negotiate
7. **Leverage incentives** - free money, use it
8. **Professional advisors** - attorney, accountant review
9. **Maintain flexibility** - backup options if plan A fails
10. **Preserve equity** - debt often cheaper than equity

Financing is the fuel for your project. Get enough, get it early, and get favorable terms. Underfunding is a leading cause of failure.
