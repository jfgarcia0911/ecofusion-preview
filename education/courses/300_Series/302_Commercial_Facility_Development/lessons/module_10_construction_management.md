## 3. Schedule Management

### Critical Path Method (CPM)

```
SCHEDULE TRACKING

Key Elements:
□ Activity list (all tasks)
□ Duration estimates (days per task)
□ Dependencies (what must finish before what)
□ Critical path (longest path to completion)
□ Float/slack (cushion on non-critical tasks)

Monitoring:
- Update schedule weekly
- Track actual vs. planned
- Identify delays immediately
- Analyze impact on completion
- Request recovery plan if behind

Schedule Metrics:
SPI (Schedule Performance Index) = Actual days / Planned days
SPI = 1.0: On schedule
SPI < 1.0: Ahead of schedule
SPI > 1.0: Behind schedule

Example:
Week 12 of 48-week project
Planned: 25% complete
Actual: 20% complete
SPI = 12 / (48 × 0.20) = 12 / 9.6 = 1.25
Conclusion: 25% behind schedule
Action: Request recovery plan

Recovery Options:
- Add crews (GC expense if their delay)
- Extended hours
- Weekend work
- Parallel activities
- Expedite materials
```

## 4. Quality Control

### Inspection Points

```
CRITICAL INSPECTION STAGES

Foundation:
□ Excavation depth and grade
□ Rebar placement and tie
□ Forms square and level
□ Concrete pour and finish
□ Cure time before load

Structure:
□ Anchor bolts location
□ Steel alignment and plumb
□ Welding quality
□ Bolt torque
□ Roof pitch and drainage

Envelope:
□ Insulation installed fully
□ Air barrier continuity
□ Flashing details
□ Roof membrane seams
□ Door/window sealing

Mechanical:
□ Ductwork sealed and insulated
□ Equipment anchored
□ Refrigerant lines insulated
□ Drain piping pitched
□ Vibration isolation

Electrical:
□ Conduit properly secured
□ Wire sizing correct
□ Grounding and bonding
□ Panel labeling
□ GFCI where required

Plumbing:
□ Pipe slope for drainage
□ Pressure test passed
□ Backflow preventer installed
□ Fixture height and alignment
□ Water heater T&P valve

Growing Systems:
□ Racking level and secure
□ Lighting coverage uniform
□ Irrigation pressure uniform
□ Drainage adequate
□ Controls functioning

Quality Issues:
- Identify immediately
- Document with photos
- Notify GC in writing
- Do not approve for payment
- Require correction before proceeding
- Reinspect after correction
```

## 5. Change Order Management

### Change Order Process

```
MANAGING CHANGES

Common Changes:
□ Unforeseen conditions (hidden issues)
□ Owner requests (scope additions)
□ Code requirements (official changes)
□ Design errors/omissions
□ Product unavailability (substitutions)
□ Field coordination (systems conflicts)

Process:
1. Issue identified
2. GC submits change order proposal
   - Description
   - Cost breakdown
   - Schedule impact
   - Justification
3. Owner reviews
   - Verify need
   - Check pricing
   - Get second quote if high
   - Negotiate
4. Owner approves/rejects/counters
5. If approved, issue written CO
6. Update contract sum and schedule
7. Work proceeds

Pricing Review:
□ Labor hours reasonable?
□ Hourly rates match contract?
□ Materials at cost + markup?
□ Markup within agreed % (10-15%)?
□ Overhead/profit reasonable?
□ Any "hidden" costs?

Negotiation Tactics:
- Request itemized breakdown
- Compare to original bid pricing
- Get competitive quote if possible
- Question unnecessary items
- Negotiate markup percentage
- Consider value engineering
- Bundle multiple COs for discount

CO Log:
Track all changes in spreadsheet:
- CO number
- Date
- Description
- Cost
- Schedule impact
- Status
- Cumulative total

Target: Keep COs under 5% of contract
Alert if approaching 3%
```

## 6. Safety Management

### Site Safety Requirements

```
SAFETY PROGRAM ESSENTIALS

GC Responsibilities:
□ Written safety plan
□ Daily toolbox talks
□ Weekly safety inspections
□ Incident reporting
□ OSHA compliance
□ PPE enforcement
□ First aid availability

Owner Oversight:
□ Verify safety plan exists
□ Observe PPE use during visits
□ Note any hazards
□ Report to GC immediately
□ Don't enter unsafe areas
□ Wear required PPE

Required PPE:
□ Hard hat (all site visitors)
□ Safety glasses
□ Work boots (steel toe)
□ High-vis vest
□ Gloves (as appropriate)
□ Fall protection (>6' height)

Common Hazards:
⚠ Fall hazards (open edges, holes)
⚠ Struck-by (equipment, materials)
⚠ Electrical (live wires, tools)
⚠ Caught-in (equipment, trenches)
⚠ Chemical exposure
⚠ Noise

Incident Response:
1. Ensure scene safety
2. Call 911 if serious
3. Provide first aid if trained
4. Notify GC superintendent
5. Document incident
6. OSHA report if required (serious/fatal)
7. Investigate cause
8. Implement corrective actions

Stop Work Authority:
Owner can stop work if:
- Imminent danger observed
- Repeated violations
- Inadequate protection
- Weather hazards (high wind, etc.)
- Until corrected
```

## 7. Submittals & Shop Drawings

### Submittal Review Process

```
SUBMITTAL MANAGEMENT

Types of Submittals:
□ Shop drawings (fabrication details)
□ Product data (cut sheets)
□ Samples (material/color samples)
□ Mix designs (concrete, etc.)
□ Test reports (materials testing)
□ Operating instructions
□ Warranty information

Review Process:
1. GC submits to architect
2. Architect reviews (7-14 days)
   - Checks compliance with specs
   - Marks: Approved, Approved as Noted, Revise, Rejected
3. Returns to GC
4. GC distributes to subs
5. If revisions needed, resubmit

Owner's Role:
- Review major items (growing lights, HVAC, etc.)
- Confirm specs match your requirements
- Verify compatibility with other systems
- Approve or comment
- Track submittals in log

Critical Submittals for CEA:
Priority 1 (Owner must review):
□ Growing lights (PAR, spectrum, efficiency)
□ HVAC equipment (capacity, efficiency)
□ Irrigation equipment (capacity, controls)
□ Water treatment (specifications, performance)
□ Environmental controls (features, integration)

Priority 2 (Architect review, owner check):
□ Electrical panels (sizing, labeling)
□ Plumbing fixtures
□ Racking systems (capacity, finish)
□ Finishes (appearance, cleanability)

Priority 3 (Architect review):
□ Doors and hardware
□ Roofing materials
□ Insulation
□ Paint colors

Submittal Log:
Track in spreadsheet:
- Item description
- Submittal date
- Reviewer
- Review due date
- Status (pending, approved, etc.)
- Resubmit date if needed
- Approval date
```

## 8. Payment Application Review

### Reviewing Pay Apps

```
PAY APPLICATION CHECKLIST

Documents Required:
□ AIA G702: Application and Certification
□ AIA G703: Continuation sheet (line items)
□ Lien waivers (previous payment)
□ Stored materials invoice (if applicable)
□ Change orders executed
□ Updated schedule

Review Process:

1. Verify Work in Place
   Site visit to confirm % complete
   Compare to claimed % complete
   If not visited recently, schedule visit

2. Check Math
   □ Line item % × value = completed value
   □ Sum of completed values
   □ Less previous payments
   □ Less retainage
   □ Plus approved change orders
   □ Equals current payment

3. Verify Lien Waivers
   □ All subs from previous payment
   □ Conditional (upon receipt of payment)
   □ Properly executed
   □ Cover full amount paid

4. Review Stored Materials
   □ Invoice provided
   □ Materials are on-site (verify)
   □ Materials are for this project
   □ Reasonable amount (not excessive)
   □ Properly stored and protected

5. Check Retainage
   □ Correct percentage held (5-10%)
   □ Held on all line items
   □ Held on change orders
   □ Cumulative retainage correct

6. Approve or Question
   If everything checks out: Approve
   If questions: Request clarification
   If overcharged: Negotiate adjustment

7. Process Payment
   Submit to lender (if construction loan)
   Or pay from own funds
   Within contract period (30 days typical)

Red Flags:
🚩 Claimed % seems high vs. actual
🚩 Front-loading (early items > actual value)
🚩 Stored materials excessive
🚩 Missing lien waivers
🚩 Math doesn't reconcile
🚩 Retainage not held
🚩 Change orders not approved
```

## 9. Substantial Completion

### Punchlist Process

```
SUBSTANTIAL COMPLETION WALKTHROUGH

Definition of Substantial Completion:
- Work is essentially complete
- Owner can occupy and use the facility
- Only minor items remain
- Systems are operational

Walkthrough:
□ Schedule with GC, architect, owner
□ Allow 4-6 hours
□ Bring notepad, camera, flashlight
□ Review all spaces
□ Test all systems
□ Check all finishes

Punchlist Categories:
Category 1: Must fix before SC
- Safety issues
- Code violations
- Non-functional systems
- Major defects

Category 2: Fix before final completion
- Minor defects
- Touch-up and cleaning
- Missing items (minor)
- Adjustments needed

Category 3: Warranty items
- Items not yet defective
- To monitor
- May need attention later

Document Format:
Item # | Location | Description | Category | Responsible | Due Date
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
001    | Zone 1   | Light fix #42 out | 1 | Electrician | 5/15
002    | Office   | Touch up paint | 2 | Painter | 5/20

Typical Punchlist Size:
Small project: 50-150 items
Medium project: 150-400 items
Large project: 400+ items

Completion:
- GC fixes items
- Owner reinspects
- Check off completed items
- Final payment upon completion
- Start warranties
```

## Summary

Effective construction management requires:

1. **Active oversight** - weekly site visits, meetings
2. **Schedule monitoring** - track progress, address delays
3. **Quality inspections** - verify work before payment
4. **Change control** - manage changes carefully
5. **Safety vigilance** - ensure safe work environment
6. **Submittal review** - verify product compliance
7. **Payment scrutiny** - pay for work in place only
8. **Thorough punchlist** - document all deficiencies
9. **Complete closeout** - obtain all deliverables
10. **Smooth transition** - prepare for operations

Construction is complex and challenging. Stay engaged, document everything, and address issues immediately for successful project delivery.
