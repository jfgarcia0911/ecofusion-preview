## 3. Equipment Negotiation

### Pricing Strategies

```
NEGOTIATION TACTICS

Package Deals:
Strategy: Bundle multiple items with one supplier
Leverage: Larger order = better pricing
Typical discount: 10-20% vs. individual items
Example:
  Lights list price: $300K
  Racking list price: $80K
  Controls list price: $30K
  Total list: $410K
  Package price: $340K (17% discount)

Early Commitment:
Strategy: Commit before construction starts
Leverage: Supplier can plan production
Typical discount: 5-10%
Risk: Locked in if project delays
Include: Cancellation clause with minimal penalty

Volume Commitment:
Strategy: Commit to future purchases (expansion)
Leverage: Long-term customer value
Typical discount: 10-15% on current order
Example: "We'll buy lights for Phase 2 from you"

Competitive Bidding:
Strategy: Get quotes from 3+ suppliers
Leverage: Competition
Process:
1. RFQ to qualified suppliers
2. Compare quotes
3. Negotiate with top 2
4. Final best and final offer
Typical result: 5-15% better than first quote

Cash Payment:
Strategy: Pay cash vs. payment terms
Leverage: Immediate payment, no credit risk
Typical discount: 2-5%
Consider: Cash flow impact

Terms to Negotiate:
□ Base price (obviously)
□ Volume discounts
□ Payment terms (net 30, 60, 90)
□ Freight (FOB destination vs. origin)
□ Installation support (included or extra)
□ Training (included or extra)
□ Warranty (standard vs. extended)
□ Spare parts pricing (future)
□ Software/firmware updates (included)
□ Return/exchange policy
```

## 6. Delivery Coordination

### Receiving Process

```
DELIVERY MANAGEMENT

Pre-Delivery:
□ Confirm delivery date 1 week prior
□ Ensure site access ready
□ Verify unloading equipment available
□ Coordinate with contractor (if on-site)
□ Arrange storage location
□ Assign receiving personnel

At Delivery:
□ Check BOL (bill of lading) against PO
□ Count packages
□ Inspect for shipping damage
□ Note any damage on BOL before signing
□ Photograph damage if present
□ Unload carefully
□ Move to secure storage

After Delivery:
□ Detailed inspection within 5 days
□ Verify contents against packing list
□ Inspect for concealed damage
□ Test function (if possible)
□ Report discrepancies immediately
□ File warranty cards
□ Update inventory log

Damage Claims:
Visible damage:
1. Note on BOL before signing
2. Photograph
3. Notify supplier same day
4. File claim with carrier

Concealed damage:
1. Discover during unpacking
2. Photograph
3. Notify supplier within 48 hours
4. Stop unpacking (preserve evidence)
5. File claim with carrier

Shortage:
1. Note on BOL if count is short
2. Verify against packing list
3. Notify supplier within 48 hours
4. Request shipment of missing items

Wrong Items:
1. Photograph
2. Notify supplier immediately
3. Arrange return/exchange
4. Don't install wrong items
```

## 8. Performance Verification

### Acceptance Testing

```
EQUIPMENT TESTING PROTOCOL

Growing Lights:
Test: PAR measurement
Tools: Quantum sensor
Method:
1. Measure at multiple points
2. Compare to spec (±10% acceptable)
3. Verify uniformity (<15% variation)
4. Check dimming function
5. Verify photoperiod control

HVAC:
Test: Temperature and humidity control
Method:
1. Run for 24 hours
2. Log temp and RH every 15 min
3. Verify setpoint maintained (±2°F, ±5% RH)
4. Check response to load changes
5. Verify dehumidification capacity

Irrigation:
Test: Flow and pressure uniformity
Method:
1. Measure flow at each zone
2. Verify pressure at emitters
3. Check uniformity (>90%)
4. Test pH and EC injection accuracy
5. Verify dosing ratios (±5%)

Water Treatment:
Test: Output water quality
Method:
1. Test TDS (should be <50 ppm after RO)
2. Test pH
3. Check flow rate vs. spec
4. Verify waste ratio
5. Test under continuous operation

Controls:
Test: Sensor accuracy and response
Method:
1. Compare sensor to calibrated instrument
2. Verify accuracy (±2% typical)
3. Test control response
4. Verify data logging
5. Test alarms

Acceptance Criteria:
✓ Meets or exceeds specifications
✓ No defects or damage
✓ Operates safely
✓ Training completed
✓ Documentation provided

Rejection Criteria:
✗ Does not meet specifications
✗ Defective or damaged
✗ Safety concerns
✗ Poor workmanship
✗ Incomplete installation

If Rejected:
1. Document deficiencies (photos, measurements)
2. Notify supplier in writing
3. Require correction plan
4. Retest after correction
5. Withhold payment until acceptable
```

## 9. Warranty Management

### Warranty Tracking

```
WARRANTY ADMINISTRATION

Warranty Registration:
□ Complete warranty cards
□ Register online (if required)
□ File proof of purchase
□ Note warranty start date
□ Set expiration reminders

Warranty Database:
Equipment | Model | Serial | Warranty Start | Duration | Expiration | Contact
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LED Light | XL-320 | 12345 | 1/15/24 | 5 years | 1/15/29 | 800-555-1234
HVAC Unit | AC-25 | 67890 | 2/1/24 | 10 years | 2/1/34 | 800-555-5678

Warranty Types:
Manufacturer Warranty:
- Standard coverage from manufacturer
- Typically 1-5 years on equipment
- May have parts vs. labor differences
- Requires proper installation and maintenance

Extended Warranty:
- Optional purchase
- Extends coverage beyond standard
- Evaluate cost vs. benefit
- Consider for critical equipment

Installation Warranty:
- From installer (usually 1 year)
- Covers workmanship
- Separate from equipment warranty

Service Contracts:
- Ongoing maintenance and repair
- May include preventive maintenance
- Can extend equipment life
- Consider for complex systems

Warranty Claims:
Process:
1. Identify issue
2. Check if under warranty
3. Review warranty terms (what's covered)
4. Contact warranty provider
5. Obtain RMA number
6. Follow claim procedure
7. Document everything
8. Track resolution

Common Warranty Exclusions:
× Improper installation
× Lack of maintenance
× Normal wear and tear
× Misuse or abuse
× Environmental damage (lightning, flood, etc.)
× Unauthorized modifications
× Expendables (filters, lamps, etc.)

Warranty Maintenance Requirements:
Many warranties require:
□ Professional installation
□ Regular maintenance
□ Use of approved parts
□ Maintenance records
□ Timely reporting of issues

Keep Records:
□ Warranty documents
□ Installation records
□ Maintenance logs
□ Repair history
□ Invoices
□ Correspondence
```

## Summary

Successful equipment procurement requires:

1. **Thorough supplier vetting** - experience, quality, support
2. **Competitive pricing** - multiple quotes, negotiation
3. **Lead time planning** - order early, avoid delays
4. **Clear documentation** - POs, specs, warranties
5. **Delivery coordination** - inspect, document, store
6. **Installation oversight** - verify proper installation
7. **Performance testing** - ensure specs are met
8. **Warranty management** - register, track, maintain
9. **Spare parts planning** - minimize downtime
10. **Supplier relationships** - ongoing support

Equipment represents 30-40% of project budget and directly impacts productivity. Choose wisely, negotiate effectively, and verify performance.
