# Module 6: Project Budgeting

**Duration:** 1.5 hours
**Learning Objectives:**
- Develop comprehensive capital budgets
- Estimate costs accurately for all project phases
- Build appropriate contingencies
- Track costs during development
- Manage change orders and budget variances

## 1. Capital Budget Framework

### Budget Categories

```
COMPREHENSIVE CAPITAL BUDGET STRUCTURE

LAND & REAL ESTATE (25-35% of total)
├─ Land purchase or lease deposit
├─ Building purchase or lease rights payment
├─ Real estate closing costs
├─ Surveys and legal fees
└─ Title insurance

SITE DEVELOPMENT (5-10%)
├─ Site preparation and grading
├─ Demolition (if needed)
├─ Utility connections and extensions
├─ Paving and parking
├─ Landscaping and fencing
└─ Stormwater management

BUILDING SHELL (15-25%)
├─ Foundation and structural
├─ Building envelope (walls, roof)
├─ Doors and windows
├─ Insulation and air sealing
└─ Painting and finishes

MEP SYSTEMS (20-25%)
├─ HVAC equipment and installation
├─ Electrical service and distribution
├─ Plumbing and irrigation rough-in
├─ Fire protection systems
├─ Building controls and automation
└─ Testing and commissioning

GROWING SYSTEMS (15-20%)
├─ Growing lights
├─ Racking, benches, or tanks
├─ Irrigation/fertigation equipment
├─ Environmental sensors
├─ Water treatment systems
└─ System integration and testing

EQUIPMENT & FF&E (5-10%)
├─ Processing equipment
├─ Packaging equipment
├─ Cold storage
├─ Office furniture and equipment
├─ Tools and supplies
└─ Initial inventory

SOFT COSTS (15-20%)
├─ Design and engineering
├─ Permits and fees
├─ Legal and accounting
├─ Project management
├─ Insurance during construction
├─ Financing costs
├─ Testing and commissioning
└─ Contingency
```

## 2. Cost Estimating by Phase

### Conceptual Budgeting (± 30-40% accuracy)

```
EARLY PHASE COST ESTIMATING

Square Foot Method:
Total cost = Growing area × $/sf + fixed costs

Cost Ranges by System Type:
Hydroponic vertical: $200-300/sf
Hydroponic NFT/DWC: $150-250/sf
Aquaponic: $175-275/sf
High-tech greenhouse: $100-200/sf

Example: 10,000 sf hydroponic NFT facility
Growing area: 10,000 sf × $200/sf = $2,000,000
Fixed costs (office, processing): $250,000
Total conceptual budget: $2,250,000

Adjust for:
+ Urban location: +15-25%
+ Complex systems: +10-20%
+ High-end finishes: +10-15%
- Rural location: -10-15%
- Simple systems: -10-15%
- Owner involvement: -5-10%
```

### Preliminary Budget (± 15-25% accuracy)

```
SYSTEM-BASED ESTIMATING

After design team is engaged, estimate major systems:

Building Shell:
New construction: $75-150/sf
Renovation: $40-100/sf
Existing (minimal work): $10-30/sf

HVAC System:
Cooling load: 15-25 tons per 10K sf
Cost: $2,500-4,000 per ton installed
Total: $37,500-100,000 for 10K sf

Electrical:
Service: $15,000-50,000
Distribution: $10-20/sf
Lighting: $15-25/sf growing area
Controls: $5-10/sf
Total: $350,000-550,000 for 10K sf

Growing Systems:
Racking: $8-15/sf
Irrigation: $5-10/sf
Lighting: $30-50/sf
Controls: $3-7/sf
Total: $460,000-820,000 for 10K sf

Sum all systems + soft costs + contingency
```

### Detailed Budget (± 5-10% accuracy)

```
LINE-ITEM BUDGET DEVELOPMENT

After design is 50-75% complete, obtain quotes:

□ GC bids on building work (3 bids minimum)
□ MEP subcontractor quotes (2-3 each)
□ Equipment vendor quotes (2-3 each)
□ Actual design fees
□ Actual permit fees
□ Actual connection fees

Build detailed spreadsheet with:
- Line item descriptions
- Quantity takeoffs
- Unit costs
- Extended costs
- Source (quote, estimate, allowance)
- Confidence level
- Contingency by line item

This becomes your project budget and cost control tool
```

## 4. Contingency Planning

### Contingency Strategy

```
CONTINGENCY ALLOCATION

Design Contingency (5-10% of construction):
Purpose: Cover unknowns during design
When used: Design development phase
Example: Discover need for soil remediation

Construction Contingency (10-15%):
Purpose: Cover field changes, unforeseen conditions
When used: During construction
Example: Hidden structural issues, price increases

Owner Contingency (5-10%):
Purpose: Cover scope changes, betterments
When used: Any time, owner discretion
Example: Decide to upgrade equipment

Escalation Reserve (0-5%):
Purpose: Cover price inflation during long projects
When used: Projects >12 months
Example: Material cost increases

Total Recommended Contingency:
New construction: 20-25%
Renovation: 25-30%
Existing building fit-out: 15-20%

Contingency by Budget Certainty:
Conceptual estimate: 30-40%
Preliminary estimate: 20-30%
Detailed estimate: 15-20%
Contracted/quoted: 10-15%
```

### Contingency Management

```
CONTINGENCY DRAWDOWN PROCESS

1. Maintain Contingency Log
   Date | Item | Amount | Approval | Remaining

2. Require Approval for Use
   <$5K: Project manager
   $5-25K: Owner/sponsor
   >$25K: Board/investors

3. Track Depletion
   Monitor: % of budget vs. % complete
   Alert if: Contingency usage > % complete
   Example: 60% spent, only 40% complete = problem

4. Replenish if Needed
   If contingency depletes early:
   - Reduce scope
   - Value engineer
   - Secure additional funding
   - Delay non-critical elements

5. Release Unused Contingency
   At project completion:
   - Return to operating budget
   - Apply to working capital
   - Reserve for warranty issues (5%)
```

## 5. Financing Cost Considerations

### Construction Financing

```
CONSTRUCTION LOAN COSTS

Typical Terms:
Interest rate: Prime + 2-4% (8-10% currently)
Term: 12-24 months
Draw schedule: Monthly based on % complete
Origination fee: 1-2% of loan amount
Interest: Paid monthly on drawn amount

Example: $2M construction loan
Origination: $40,000 (2%)
Interest: ~$100,000 (assuming 12-month average balance of $1M)
Total financing cost: $140,000 (7% of loan amount)

Ways to Reduce:
- Shorter construction period (less interest)
- More equity/less debt (lower loan amount)
- Shop multiple lenders (better rates)
- Draw promptly (don't lag reimbursements)
```

### Permanent Financing Costs

```
PERMANENT LOAN/MORTGAGE COSTS

Term Loan Costs:
Interest rate: 6-8% (current market)
Term: 15-25 years
Amortization: 20-25 years (may have balloon)
Origination: 1-2%
Appraisal: $3-8K
Environmental Phase I: $3-5K
Legal: $3-8K
Title insurance: 0.5-1% of loan

Example: $1.5M term loan (after $500K down on $2M project)
Origination: $30,000
Appraisal: $5,000
Phase I: $4,000
Legal: $5,000
Title: $10,000
Total closing costs: $54,000 (3.6%)

Include in Project Budget:
Both construction and permanent financing costs should be
budgeted in soft costs category
```

## 6. Value Engineering

### Value Engineering Process

```
VALUE ENGINEERING OPPORTUNITIES

When to Value Engineer:
□ Budget exceeds financing available
□ Bids come in over budget
□ Scope creep has inflated costs
□ Want to optimize ROI
□ Market changes require cost reduction

VE Process:
1. Identify high-cost items
2. Brainstorm alternatives
3. Evaluate impact (cost, performance, schedule)
4. Select best alternatives
5. Redesign as needed
6. Update budget

Common VE Targets in CEA:

Building Shell:
Original: New construction, $1.2M
VE: Existing building renovation, $800K
Savings: $400K
Trade-off: Less ideal layout, shorter ceiling

Growing System:
Original: Top-tier LED fixtures, $400K
VE: Mid-tier LED fixtures, $320K
Savings: $80K
Trade-off: 5% less PAR output, similar efficiency

HVAC:
Original: Premium VRF system, $185K
VE: Quality rooftop units, $135K
Savings: $50K
Trade-off: Slightly less efficient, similar performance

Finishes:
Original: High-end office finishes, $50K
VE: Standard commercial finishes, $30K
Savings: $20K
Trade-off: Appearance only

Total VE Savings: $550K (23% reduction)
```

### What NOT to Value Engineer

```
PROTECT THESE ITEMS

Critical Systems (don't compromise):
□ Building envelope integrity (insulation, air sealing)
□ HVAC capacity (must meet load)
□ Electrical service size (need adequate capacity)
□ Growing light coverage (directly affects yield)
□ Water treatment (crop health)
□ Structural capacity (safety, future needs)
□ Fire protection (code, insurance, safety)

Penny-Wise, Pound-Foolish Cuts:
× Eliminating backup power (risk crop loss)
× Undersizing electrical (limits growth)
× Cheaper environmental controls (poor performance)
× Skimping on insulation (higher operating costs)
× Reducing lighting (lower yields)
× Cutting commissioning (systems won't work right)
× Minimal contingency (will overrun)

Value Engineering vs. Cheap Engineering:
VE: Same performance, lower cost, smarter approach
CE: Lower cost, compromised performance, false economy

Always evaluate: Does this cut compromise productivity or
increase operating costs that outweigh savings?
```

## 7. Cost Control During Construction

### Budget Tracking System

```
BUDGET TRACKING SPREADSHEET

Columns to Include:
1. Budget line item
2. Original budget
3. Approved changes
4. Current budget
5. Committed costs (contracts)
6. Invoiced to date
7. Forecast to complete
8. Variance (over/under)
9. % complete
10. Notes

Update Frequency: Weekly during construction

Example Line Item:
Item: HVAC Equipment
Original budget: $185,000
Approved changes: +$12,000 (added make-up air unit)
Current budget: $197,000
Committed: $195,000 (contract signed)
Invoiced to date: $145,000 (delivered, partially installed)
Forecast to complete: $195,000
Variance: +$2,000 over budget
% complete: 75%
Notes: Installation ahead of schedule
```

### Change Order Management

```
CHANGE ORDER PROCESS

Change Order Types:
1. Owner-requested (scope change)
2. Field conditions (unforeseen)
3. Code/regulatory (required)
4. Design error/omission
5. Value engineering

Change Order Procedure:
Step 1: Identify need for change
Step 2: Contractor provides quote (cost + time)
Step 3: Review and negotiate
Step 4: Approve or reject
Step 5: Issue written change order
Step 6: Update budget and schedule
Step 7: Execute work

Change Order Documentation:
□ Description of change
□ Reason for change
□ Cost impact (itemized)
□ Schedule impact (days)
□ Contract paragraph modified
□ Approval signatures
□ Date issued

Change Order Budget:
Track separately from contingency
Target: <5% of original contract
Alert if: >3% halfway through project
Problem if: >10% at completion

Ways to Minimize Change Orders:
□ Complete design before bidding
□ Thorough site investigation
□ Pre-bid meetings (clarify scope)
□ RFIs answered promptly
□ Quality control (catch issues early)
□ Owner decisions made quickly
```

### Draw Schedule Management

```
CONSTRUCTION LOAN DRAW PROCESS

Monthly Draw Cycle:
Day 1-25:     Work performed
Day 26-27:    Contractor submits pay application
Day 28-29:    Review and approve application
Day 30:       Submit draw request to lender
Day 31-35:    Lender reviews and inspects
Day 36-40:    Funds disbursed
Day 40-45:    Pay contractors

Payment Terms:
Typical: Pay within 30 days of invoice
Retainage: Hold 5-10% until completion
Final payment: After punchlist complete

Pay Application Contents:
□ Schedule of values (line items)
□ Work completed to date (%)
□ Stored materials (delivered, not installed)
□ Previous payments
□ Current payment due
□ Retainage held
□ Lien waivers (for previous payment)
□ Supporting documentation (photos, invoices)

Owner's Review:
□ Verify work is actually complete
□ Quality is acceptable
□ Quantities are accurate
□ Stored materials are on-site
□ Change orders are reflected
□ Math is correct

Lender's Review:
□ Disbursed amount < % complete × loan
□ Proper documentation
□ Lien waivers current
□ Adequate retainage held
□ Inspection confirms progress
```

## 10. Budget Lessons Learned

### Common Budget Pitfalls

```
TOP 10 BUDGETING MISTAKES

1. Insufficient Contingency
   Problem: Budget too tight, no room for issues
   Solution: 20-25% contingency on new construction

2. Incomplete Scope
   Problem: Forgot items (IT, security, spare parts)
   Solution: Detailed checklist, review with operator

3. Escalation Not Considered
   Problem: Prices increase during design/permitting
   Solution: Add 5-10% for projects >12 months

4. Overly Optimistic Estimates
   Problem: Used low end of range for everything
   Solution: Use mid-range estimates, verify with quotes

5. Hidden Costs Missed
   Problem: Impact fees, utility fees, specialized testing
   Solution: Research jurisdiction, ask others

6. Owner-Supplied Items Forgotten
   Problem: Assumed contractor includes everything
   Solution: Clear scope, explicit exclusions list

7. Soft Costs Underestimated
   Problem: Design, PM, financing, insurance add up
   Solution: Budget 15-20% for soft costs

8. Change Orders Not Controlled
   Problem: "While we're at it..." syndrome
   Solution: Strict change order process, budget limit

9. Cash Flow Not Managed
   Problem: Run out of money before completion
   Solution: Monthly cash flow projection, monitor draws

10. No Budget Tracking
    Problem: Don't know you're over until it's too late
    Solution: Weekly budget updates, variance reporting
```

### Budget Success Factors

```
BUDGETING BEST PRACTICES

Pre-Construction:
□ Get multiple quotes (3 minimum)
□ Check references on cost estimates
□ Add adequate contingency
□ Include ALL costs (easy to forget items)
□ Get owner input (operational needs)
□ Build in escalation for long projects
□ Secure financing before starting

During Construction:
□ Track costs weekly
□ Review pay applications carefully
□ Verify work before payment
□ Control change orders strictly
□ Communicate variances immediately
□ Value engineer if needed
□ Maintain contingency reserve

Post-Construction:
□ Document final costs by category
□ Compare to budget (learn for next time)
□ Identify what drove variances
□ Update cost/sf benchmarks
□ Share lessons with team
```

## Summary

Effective project budgeting requires:

1. **Comprehensive budgets** covering all project costs
2. **Appropriate contingency** based on project risk
3. **Detailed estimates** from qualified sources
4. **Value engineering** to optimize costs
5. **Change order control** to minimize overruns
6. **Cash flow management** to ensure adequate funding
7. **Regular tracking** and variance reporting
8. **Clear approval processes** for expenditures
9. **Lessons learned** documentation
10. **Realistic expectations** and honest estimates

A well-managed budget is the foundation of successful project delivery. Under-budgeting leads to project failure; over-budgeting wastes capital. Strive for accuracy and discipline.
