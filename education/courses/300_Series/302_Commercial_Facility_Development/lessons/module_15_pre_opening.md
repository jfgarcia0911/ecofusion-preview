# Module 15: Pre-Opening Operations

**Duration:** 0.5 hours
**Learning Objectives:**
- Finalize operational preparations
- Ramp up production capacity
- Prepare for market launch
- Establish operational rhythms
- Ensure quality and consistency

---

## 1. Production Ramp-Up Strategy

```
PHASED CAPACITY INCREASE

Phase 1: Initial Production (Weeks 1-4)
Capacity: 10-25% of total
Focus: Prove systems, train staff, establish processes
Crops: Single, forgiving variety (lettuce or herbs)
Market: Friends, family, soft launch customers
Goal: Learn and refine without high stakes

Phase 2: Early Expansion (Weeks 5-8)
Capacity: 25-50% of total
Focus: Expand variety, build consistency
Crops: 2-3 varieties, staggered plantings
Market: Early adopter customers, small restaurants
Goal: Establish rhythm, refine quality

Phase 3: Major Scale-Up (Weeks 9-16)
Capacity: 50-75% of total
Focus: Volume production, efficiency
Crops: 4-6 varieties, continuous harvest
Market: Broader customer base, regular deliveries
Goal: Achieve profitability targets

Phase 4: Full Production (Week 17+)
Capacity: 75-100% of total
Focus: Optimization, new varieties, scaling
Crops: Full planned mix
Market: All target customers served
Goal: Maximize productivity and profitability

Why Gradual?
✓ Learn on small batches (mistakes less costly)
✓ Build staff skills progressively
✓ Refine processes before full scale
✓ Avoid overwhelming customers with too much too soon
✓ Manage cash flow (don't overbuy inputs)
✓ Build market demand as supply increases
```

## 2. Crop Scheduling

```
CONTINUOUS HARVEST PLANNING

Staggered Planting Schedule:
Goal: Harvest every day or every few days

Example: Lettuce (28-day crop cycle)
Week 1: Plant Zone 1
Week 2: Plant Zone 2
Week 3: Plant Zone 3
Week 4: Plant Zone 4
Week 5: Harvest Zone 1, replant immediately
Week 6: Harvest Zone 2, replant
[Continue pattern]

Result: Harvest every week

For daily harvests: Plant 1/7 of area daily

Crop Diversity:
Maintain mix for market appeal:
- 40% leafy greens (lettuce, kale, chard)
- 30% herbs (basil, cilantro, parsley)
- 20% specialty greens (arugula, microgreens)
- 10% experimental/seasonal

Planning Tools:
□ Crop schedule spreadsheet
□ Planting calendar
□ Harvest forecast
□ Inventory projection
□ Labor planning

Factors to Consider:
□ Days to harvest (varies by crop and season)
□ Customer demand (weekly orders)
□ Growth rate variations (temperature effects)
□ Market seasonality
□ Labor availability

Flexibility:
□ Plan for variations (±10%)
□ Maintain some open capacity
□ Can accelerate or delay plantings
□ Adjust mix based on demand
```

## 3. Quality Systems

```
QUALITY CONTROL PROGRAM

Crop Quality Standards:
Define grades (e.g., Grade A, B, Cull):

Lettuce Grade A:
□ Vibrant color
□ Tight head formation
□ No tip burn
□ No pest damage
□ 6-12 oz size
□ Fresh appearance

Grade B:
□ Acceptable color
□ Slightly loose
□ Minor tip burn (<10% leaves)
□ No pest damage
□ 4-6 oz or 12-16 oz
□ Fresh

Cull:
□ Discoloration
□ Pest damage
□ Disease
□ Bolting
□ Poor form

Quality Checkpoints:

At Harvest:
□ Visual inspection
□ Size/weight check
□ Damage assessment
□ Sort by grade

At Processing:
□ Trim as needed
□ Re-inspect
□ Wash (if applicable)
□ Package

At Packaging:
□ Weight verification
□ Label accuracy
□ Appearance check
□ Seal integrity

At Delivery:
□ Temperature check (should be 34-38°F)
□ Package condition
□ Customer acceptance

Quality Metrics:
□ % Grade A (target: >90%)
□ % Waste (target: <5%)
□ Customer complaints (target: <1%)
□ Returns (target: <0.5%)
□ Shelf life (target: 10-14 days)

Quality Records:
□ Harvest logs (date, zone, crop, quantity by grade)
□ Processing notes (issues, corrections)
□ Customer feedback
□ Corrective actions
```

## 5. Financial Preparations

```
FINANCIAL SYSTEMS SETUP

Accounting System:
□ Software selected (QuickBooks, Xero, etc.)
□ Chart of accounts established
□ Bank accounts linked
□ Credit card accounts linked
□ Invoicing templates created
□ Financial reports configured

Revenue Tracking:
□ Sales by customer
□ Sales by product
□ Sales by channel
□ Payment tracking
□ Accounts receivable aging

Expense Tracking:
□ Cost of goods sold
  - Seeds/starts
  - Nutrients
  - Growing media
  - Packaging
□ Operating expenses
  - Labor
  - Utilities
  - Rent/mortgage
  - Insurance
  - Marketing
  - Maintenance

Cash Flow Management:
□ Weekly cash flow projection
□ Monitor receivables (get paid promptly)
□ Manage payables (take full terms)
□ Maintain cash reserve (3-6 months expenses)
□ Line of credit (if available)

Financial Metrics:
Weekly:
□ Sales vs. forecast
□ Cash balance
□ Major expenses

Monthly:
□ Income statement
□ Cash flow statement
□ Balance sheet
□ Key ratios (gross margin, etc.)
□ Budget vs. actual variance

Pricing Validation:
□ Track actual COGS
□ Compare to pricing
□ Ensure adequate margin
□ Adjust pricing if needed (2-4× COGS target)

Breakeven Analysis:
Calculate: At what sales level do you cover costs?

Fixed costs (per month): $________
Variable costs (% of sales): _______%
Breakeven sales = Fixed costs / (1 - Variable cost %)

Example:
Fixed: $25,000/month
Variable: 40% of sales
Breakeven = $25,000 / 60% = $41,667/month

Monitor: How many months to breakeven?
```

## 8. Soft Opening

```
SOFT LAUNCH STRATEGY

Purpose:
□ Test systems with lower stakes
□ Build staff confidence
□ Gather feedback
□ Refine processes
□ Create buzz

Soft Opening Plan:

Week 1-2: Friends & Family
□ Invite personal networks
□ Free or discounted products
□ Gather candid feedback
□ Test delivery/fulfillment
□ Refine packaging/presentation

Week 3-4: Select Customers
□ Invite early adopter customers
□ Discounted pricing
□ Request detailed feedback
□ Build relationships
□ Generate testimonials

Week 5-6: Limited Public
□ Farmers market appearance
□ Pop-up events
□ Social media promotion
□ Press outreach (local media)
□ Build email list

Feedback Collection:
□ Product quality survey
□ Packaging feedback
□ Pricing perception
□ Delivery experience
□ Suggestions for improvement

Use Feedback To:
□ Refine products
□ Adjust processes
□ Improve packaging
□ Fine-tune pricing
□ Enhance customer experience

Soft Opening Checklist:
□ Production ramped to 25-50%
□ Staff trained and confident
□ Quality standards established
□ Customer list developed
□ Delivery logistics tested
□ Feedback mechanism in place
□ Adjustments made based on learning

Transition to Grand Opening:
□ Soft opening successful
□ Positive feedback received
□ Issues identified and resolved
□ Team ready for higher volume
□ Marketing materials prepared
□ Media contacts established
□ Grand opening date set
```

## Summary

Successful pre-opening operations require:

1. **Gradual ramp-up** - start small, scale systematically
2. **Crop scheduling** - continuous, staggered harvests
3. **Quality systems** - standards, checkpoints, metrics
4. **Customer preparation** - relationships, logistics, service
5. **Financial systems** - accounting, tracking, cash management
6. **Operational systems** - inventory, scheduling, records
7. **Regulatory compliance** - all licenses, permits, insurance
8. **Soft opening** - test, learn, refine before grand opening

The pre-opening period is critical for establishing good habits and processes. Take the time to get it right.
