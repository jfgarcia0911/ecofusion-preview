## 2. Lease vs. Purchase Analysis

### Financial Comparison Framework

```
LEASE VS PURCHASE DECISION MATRIX

Factor                    Lease           Purchase        Score
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FINANCIAL
Initial capital required  Low (1-2 mo)    High (10-30%)   ____
Monthly payment          Higher           Lower*          ____
Long-term cost           Higher           Lower           ____
Equity building          None             Yes             ____
Tax benefits             Rent deduction   Depreciation    ____
Balance sheet impact     Off-balance      Asset/liability ____

OPERATIONAL
Flexibility              High             Low             ____
Control over property    Limited          Complete        ____
Modification rights      Restricted       Unrestricted    ____
Exit strategy            Easier           Harder          ____
Sublease potential       Maybe            Yes             ____

RISK
Market risk              Lower            Higher          ____
Maintenance responsibility Landlord       Owner           ____
Property value risk      None             Yes             ____
Lease renewal risk       Yes              No              ____

STRATEGIC
Timeline horizon         <7 years         >7 years        ____
Business maturity        Startup/test     Established     ____
Capital availability     Limited          Adequate        ____
Expansion plans          Uncertain        Defined         ____

*If financed; lower if purchased with cash
```

### Financial Examples

**Scenario: 20,000 sq ft warehouse**

**Option A: Lease**
```
Lease Terms: $8.00/sf/year NNN
Base rent:                    $160,000/year
NNN (taxes, insurance, CAM):  $40,000/year
Total annual cost:            $200,000/year
10-year total:               $2,000,000
Equity at end:               $0
```

**Option B: Purchase**
```
Purchase price:              $2,000,000
Down payment (20%):          $400,000
Loan amount:                 $1,600,000
Interest rate:               6.0%
Term:                        20 years

Annual P&I payment:          $137,784
Property tax:                $24,000
Insurance:                   $8,000
Maintenance:                 $16,000
Total annual cost:           $185,784/year
10-year total payments:      $1,857,840
Principal paid down:         $491,000
Remaining equity:            $891,000
Property appreciation (3%):  $686,000
Total equity position:       $1,577,000

Net cost after equity:       $280,840
vs. Lease cost:              $2,000,000
10-year advantage:           $1,719,160
```

**Decision Factors:**
- If capital available and 10+ year horizon: Purchase is advantageous
- If limited capital or <5 year horizon: Lease is appropriate
- If uncertain about location/scale: Lease provides flexibility

## 3. Letter of Intent (LOI)

### LOI Components

```
LETTER OF INTENT STRUCTURE

1. PARTIES
   Buyer/Tenant: ___________________________
   Seller/Landlord: ________________________

2. PROPERTY DESCRIPTION
   Address: _________________________________
   Size: ____________ sq ft
   Legal description: _______________________

3. FINANCIAL TERMS

   For Purchase:
   Purchase price: $_____________
   Earnest money: $_____________
   Down payment: $_____________ (___%)
   Financing contingency: □ Yes  □ No

   For Lease:
   Base rent: $______/sf/year or $______/month
   Lease term: _____ years
   Renewal options: _________________________
   NNN or gross: ____________________________
   Free rent period: _____ months
   Tenant improvements: $________

4. DUE DILIGENCE
   Due diligence period: _____ days
   Inspections included:
   □ Phase I Environmental
   □ Structural
   □ Roof
   □ Mechanical/Electrical/Plumbing
   □ Survey
   □ Zoning verification
   □ Utility capacity verification

5. CONTINGENCIES
   □ Financing approval
   □ Satisfactory inspections
   □ Zoning approval/variance
   □ Partner/board approval
   □ Lease-up of current space
   □ Other: _______________________________

6. TIMELINE
   LOI acceptance: __________ (date)
   Due diligence completion: __________ (date)
   Contract execution: __________ (date)
   Closing/occupancy: __________ (date)

7. BINDING/NON-BINDING
   This LOI is non-binding except for:
   □ Confidentiality
   □ Exclusivity period (_____ days)
   □ Expense reimbursement
```

### LOI Negotiation Strategy

**Key Terms to Negotiate:**

1. **Price/Rent** - Obviously critical
2. **Due diligence period** - Need adequate time (45-60 days typical)
3. **Contingencies** - Protect yourself but don't overdo it
4. **Exclusivity** - Landlord takes property off market during negotiation
5. **Tenant improvements** - Who pays, how much, allowance vs. turnkey
6. **Lease term** - Balance commitment with flexibility
7. **Renewal options** - Lock in future rights

**Negotiation Tips:**

- Be reasonable but protect your interests
- Don't negotiate against yourself (let the other party respond)
- Everything is negotiable until the contract is signed
- Use market data to support your position
- Build relationships, not adversarial dynamics
- Know your walk-away point before negotiating

## 4. Lease Structure & Negotiation

### Lease Types

```
COMMERCIAL LEASE STRUCTURES

Type            Tenant Pays            Best For
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Gross Lease     Base rent only         Simple budgeting
                (LL pays all other)    Small spaces
                                       Office users

Modified Gross  Base rent + portion    Shared buildings
                of operating costs     Medium spaces
                                       Some predictability

NNN             Base + taxes +         Standalone buildings
(Triple Net)    insurance + CAM        Large spaces
                                       Control over costs

Absolute NNN    Base + ALL costs       Single-tenant
                including structure    Long-term
                                       Total control

Percentage      Base + % of sales      Retail/food sales
Rent            (rare in CEA)          Revenue-generating
```

### Key Lease Terms

**Base Rent Escalations:**
```
RENT ESCALATION STRUCTURES

Fixed Increases:
Year 1-3: $8.00/sf
Year 4-6: $8.40/sf (+5%)
Year 7-9: $8.82/sf (+5%)

CPI Adjustments:
Base: $8.00/sf
Annual adjustment: Lesser of CPI or 3%
Provides inflation protection

Fair Market Value:
Reset to market at renewal
Provides flexibility
Less predictability
```

**Free Rent / Rent Abatement:**
- Typical: 1-3 months for build-out
- Use for construction period
- Negotiate based on TI timeline
- Sometimes staggered (50% for 6 months vs. 100% for 3)

**Tenant Improvement Allowance:**
```
TI ALLOWANCE NEGOTIATION

Landlord Offer: $10/sf ($200K for 20K sf)

Options to consider:
1. Take allowance, do own buildout
   Pros: Control, may save money
   Cons: Upfront cost, manage construction

2. Negotiate higher allowance ($15-20/sf)
   Pros: More funding
   Cons: May increase base rent

3. Landlord-provided buildout (turnkey)
   Pros: No upfront cost
   Cons: Less control, may cost more

Typical for CEA: $20-40/sf needed
If allowance is $10/sf, tenant covers $10-30/sf
```

### Critical Lease Clauses

**Use Clause:**
- Specifies permitted uses
- CEA should be explicitly permitted
- Avoid overly restrictive language
- Example: "Agriculture, hydroponic farming, food production, and related uses"

**Assignment & Subletting:**
- Can you transfer lease if you sell business?
- Can you sublease excess space?
- Negotiate for reasonable approval rights

**Maintenance Responsibilities:**
```
MAINTENANCE ALLOCATION (NNN Lease)

Landlord Typically Responsible:
□ Structural repairs (roof, walls, foundation)
□ Parking lot repaving
□ Exterior painting
□ Building systems replacement (HVAC, electrical)

Tenant Typically Responsible:
□ Interior maintenance
□ HVAC filters and routine service
□ Lighting
□ Pest control
□ Janitorial
□ Interior modifications

Negotiate carefully:
• HVAC replacement (expensive!)
• Roof repairs vs. replacement
• Parking lot crack sealing vs. repaving
• Define "structural" clearly
```

**Renewal Options:**
- Include options for 2-3 renewal terms
- Define rent determination method
- Exercise deadline (typically 6-12 months before expiration)
- Example: "Two 5-year options at 95% of fair market value"

**Early Termination:**
- Can you exit early if business fails?
- Usually requires landlord approval
- May require termination fee
- Consider negotiating buyout formula

```
□ Seller provides title insurance: □ Yes  □ No
□ Seller provides survey: □ Yes  □ No
□ Title exceptions: ____________________

Condition of Property:
□ As-is: □ Yes  □ No
□ Repairs to be completed: _______________
□ Credit for repairs: $__________

Closing:
□ Closing date: __________
□ Possession date: __________
□ Prorations: __________
□ Closing costs allocation: __________
```

### Purchase Negotiation Tactics

**Price Negotiation:**

1. **Research comparable sales** (recent sales of similar properties)
2. **Identify property deficiencies** (needed repairs, deferred maintenance)
3. **Make data-driven offer** (justify with comps and repair costs)
4. **Leave room for negotiation** (start below your max)
5. **Use inspection findings** (renegotiate after due diligence)

**Example Negotiation Sequence:**
```
Listed price:              $2,000,000
Your initial offer:        $1,750,000 (12.5% below)
Seller counter:            $1,925,000
Your counter:              $1,825,000
Seller counter:            $1,875,000
You accept or:             $1,850,000 (final)
Inspection finds issues:   Renegotiate -$50,000
Final price:              $1,800,000 (10% below ask)
```

**Seller Concessions to Request:**
- Pay for title insurance
- Pay for survey
- Complete specified repairs
- Credit for needed repairs
- Pay transfer taxes
- Provide seller financing
- Include equipment/fixtures
- Extend due diligence period

## 6. Tenant Improvement Negotiations

### TI Allowance Structure

**Landlord's Perspective:**
- Wants long-term, stable tenant
- Willing to invest to secure lease
- Amortizes TI cost into rent
- Prefers to control construction

**Tenant's Perspective:**
- Needs functional space
- Wants to minimize upfront cost
- Desires control over design
- Seeks maximum allowance

**Typical TI Allowance: $10-30/sf for warehouse space**

### TI Negotiation Strategies

```
TI NEGOTIATION SCENARIOS

Scenario A: Low Allowance, Low Rent
Landlord offers: $10/sf TI allowance
Base rent: $7/sf/year

Your CEA buildout cost: $40/sf
You pay: $30/sf × 20,000 sf = $600,000
Annual rent: $140,000

Scenario B: High Allowance, Higher Rent
Landlord offers: $30/sf TI allowance
Base rent: $9/sf/year

Your CEA buildout cost: $40/sf
You pay: $10/sf × 20,000 sf = $200,000
Annual rent: $180,000

Analysis:
Extra rent: $40,000/year
Capital savings: $400,000
Payback: 10 years

Decision: Depends on capital availability and tenure plans
If staying 10+ years and capital is tight: Choose B
If capital available and tenure uncertain: Choose A
```

### Turnkey vs. Allowance

**Turnkey Buildout:**
- Landlord manages construction
- Tenant approves design
- No upfront tenant cost
- Less tenant control
- Rent reflects cost

**TI Allowance:**
- Tenant manages construction
- Tenant has full control
- Tenant pays amount over allowance
- Faster (potentially)
- More complexity for tenant

**For CEA Projects:**
- Typically better to manage own buildout
- CEA requirements are specialized
- Landlord unlikely to understand needs
- Take allowance and hire own contractors

## 7. Zoning & Land Use Considerations

### Zoning Classifications for CEA

```
ZONING COMPATIBILITY

Zoning Type        CEA Permitted?   Notes
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Agricultural       Yes              Ideal, few restrictions
Industrial         Usually          Check local code
Commercial         Sometimes        May need variance
Mixed Use          Sometimes        Check specific district
Residential        Rarely           Unlikely to be allowed

Common Issues:
• Industrial zoning may prohibit "agriculture"
• Agricultural zoning may prohibit "manufacturing"
• Need to verify CEA is specifically allowed
• May need zoning determination letter
• Variance process can take 3-6 months
```

### Obtaining Zoning Approval

**Step 1: Preliminary Inquiry**
- Call planning department
- Describe your use (CEA food production)
- Ask if permitted in desired zone
- Request informal feedback

**Step 2: Formal Determination**
- Submit zoning determination request
- Provide description of operations
- Include floor plans
- Pay fee ($100-500 typical)

**Step 3: If Not Permitted**
- Research variance process
- Prepare variance application
- Attend planning commission hearing
- Address neighbor concerns
- Allow 2-4 months for approval

**Step 4: Conditions of Approval**
- Operating hours restrictions
- Truck traffic limitations
- Odor/noise controls
- Parking requirements
- Signage restrictions

### Due Diligence Zoning Checklist

```
ZONING VERIFICATION CHECKLIST

□ Obtain zoning map showing property zone
□ Obtain zoning code section for that zone
□ Verify CEA is permitted use (or list of permitted uses)
□ Identify any special requirements (parking, setbacks, etc.)
□ Check for overlay districts (historic, environmental, etc.)
□ Verify no pending zoning changes
□ Obtain written confirmation from planning department
□ Review conditional use permit requirements if applicable
□ Budget for variance if needed ($5-15K + 2-4 months)
□ Include zoning contingency in LOI/contract
```

## 8. Real Estate Structure for Tax Advantages

### Entity Structure Considerations

```
REAL ESTATE OWNERSHIP STRUCTURES

Option 1: Operating Company Owns Real Estate
Structure: Single LLC owns property and operates business
Pros:
  + Simple
  + Lower costs
Cons:
  - Property at risk from business liabilities
  - Less flexible for estate planning
  - Can complicate business sale

Option 2: Separate Real Estate Entity
Structure: RE LLC owns property, Operating LLC leases it
Pros:
  + Protects property from business liabilities
  + Flexible for estate planning
  + Easier to sell business without property
  + Can bring in different investors for each
Cons:
  - More complex
  - Two sets of books/taxes
  - Need formal lease
  - Annual cost (~$1-2K more)

Recommendation for CEA:
Separate entities if purchasing property
Single entity if leasing
```

### Tax Benefits of Real Estate Ownership

```
REAL ESTATE TAX ADVANTAGES

Depreciation:
Building (39 years for commercial):  $40,000/yr on $1.6M building
Land improvements (15 years):        $13,300/yr on $200K improvements
Equipment (5-7 years):               Variable based on systems
Total annual depreciation:           ~$50,000+

Interest Deduction:
Loan amount: $1,600,000 at 6%
Annual interest year 1:              ~$95,000 (deductible)

Property Tax Deduction:
Annual property tax:                 ~$24,000 (deductible)

Cost Segregation Study:
Accelerates depreciation on certain components
Cost: $5-15K
Benefit: $50-200K in first-year deductions
ROI: Often 5-10x in tax savings

1031 Exchange:
Defer capital gains when selling
Reinvest proceeds in new property
Can build portfolio tax-free
Requires qualified intermediary
```

## 9. Common Real Estate Pitfalls

### Top Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Insufficient Due Diligence**
- Skipping inspections to save money
- Not verifying utilities
- Assuming zoning is correct
- Failing to review title carefully

**2. Overpaying for Rent/Purchase**
- Not researching comps
- Accepting first offer
- Emotional decision-making
- Ignoring market conditions

**3. Lease Term Mismatches**
- Too short (lose TI investment)
- Too long (stuck if business changes)
- No renewal options
- Unfavorable rent escalations

**4. Inadequate TI Budget**
- Underestimating buildout costs
- Not accounting for CEA-specific needs
- Forgetting soft costs (design, permits)
- No contingency

**5. Poor Lease Terms**
- Restrictive use clause
- No assignment rights
- Tenant pays for everything
- High CAM charges

**6. Zoning Assumptions**
- Assuming current use can continue
- Not verifying CEA is allowed
- Relying on broker/seller statements
- Missing overlay restrictions

**7. Hidden Costs**
- Not understanding NNN charges
- Underestimating utilities
- Missing special assessments
- Ignoring maintenance costs

**8. Inadequate Exit Strategy**
- Can't sublease or assign
- Early termination prohibited
- Property hard to sell
- No renewal options

### Red Flag Checklist

```
REAL ESTATE WARNING SIGNS

Property Condition:
□ Deferred maintenance evident
□ Roof issues (leaks, age >15 years)
□ Foundation cracks
□ HVAC not functioning
□ Electrical panel undersized or antiquated
□ Plumbing issues evident

Financial:
□ Rent significantly above market
□ Large rent increases scheduled
□ High NNN charges (>$4/sf)
□ Landlord in financial distress
□ Property in foreclosure

Legal/Regulatory:
□ Title issues
□ Liens on property
□ Zoning violations
□ Building code violations
□ Environmental concerns
□ Pending litigation

Location:
□ Neighborhood declining
□ Limited access
□ High crime area
□ Flooding history
□ Environmental hazards nearby

Landlord/Seller:
□ Unresponsive or evasive
□ Won't allow inspections
□ Pressure to close quickly
□ Won't provide documentation
□ Bad reputation
```

## Summary

Successful real estate strategy requires:

1. **Clear understanding** of lease vs. purchase trade-offs
2. **Strong negotiation** of LOI and contract terms
3. **Thorough due diligence** to identify risks
4. **Adequate TI budget** for CEA buildout needs
5. **Zoning verification** before committing
6. **Tax-advantaged structure** for real estate ownership
7. **Reasonable lease terms** balancing commitment and flexibility
8. **Professional advisors** (attorney, accountant, broker)
9. **Adequate timeline** for proper evaluation
10. **Written documentation** of all agreements

Real estate will be one of your largest expenses. Taking time to structure the deal correctly will pay dividends for years to come.
