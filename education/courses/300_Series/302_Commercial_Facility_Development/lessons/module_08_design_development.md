# Module 8: Design Development

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Manage the design process effectively
- Select qualified design professionals
- Review and approve design documents
- Optimize designs for operational efficiency
- Balance capital cost with operating cost

## 1. Design Team Selection

### Key Design Professionals

```
DESIGN TEAM ROSTER

Architect:
Role: Building design, code compliance, permitting
Qualifications: Licensed, CEA or ag experience preferred
Fee: 4-8% of construction cost
When: Early - needed for permits

MEP Engineer:
Role: HVAC, electrical, plumbing design
Qualifications: PE license, understand CEA loads
Fee: 3-6% of construction cost
When: During architectural design

Structural Engineer:
Role: Foundation, framing, loading analysis
Qualifications: PE license, understand equipment loads
Fee: 1-2% of construction cost
When: During architectural design

CEA Consultant:
Role: Growing system design, equipment specs
Qualifications: Operating experience, successful projects
Fee: $10-50K or hourly
When: Very early - informs all other design

Civil Engineer:
Role: Site work, grading, utilities
Qualifications: PE license, local experience
Fee: $5-20K typical
When: If significant site work needed
```

## 2. Design Phases

### Phase Overview

```
DESIGN PROCESS PHASES

PHASE 1: Programming (2-4 weeks)
Deliverables:
□ Space needs analysis
□ Functional requirements
□ Equipment list (preliminary)
□ Site analysis
□ Code review
Fee: Included in design fee or $5-15K separate

PHASE 2: Schematic Design (4-6 weeks)
Deliverables:
□ Conceptual floor plans
□ Building elevations
□ Site plan
□ Major systems approach
□ Preliminary cost estimate
Fee: 15% of design fee
Review carefully: Changes after this are expensive

PHASE 3: Design Development (6-10 weeks)
Deliverables:
□ Detailed floor plans
□ Building sections
□ MEP system layouts
□ Structural approach
□ Specifications outline
□ Refined cost estimate
Fee: 20% of design fee
Review carefully: Last chance for major changes

PHASE 4: Construction Documents (8-12 weeks)
Deliverables:
□ Complete construction drawings
□ Full specifications
□ Details and schedules
□ Code compliance documentation
□ Permit submittals
Fee: 40% of design fee
Thorough review critical: Errors costly to fix in field

PHASE 5: Bidding (2-4 weeks)
Deliverables:
□ Answer RFIs
□ Addenda
□ Bid analysis
Fee: 5% of design fee

PHASE 6: Construction Administration (duration of construction)
Deliverables:
□ Review submittals
□ Answer RFIs
□ Site visits
□ Punch list
Fee: 20% of design fee

TOTAL DESIGN TIME: 6-9 months typical
```

## 4. Optimizing Building Envelope

### Insulation & Air Sealing

```
ENVELOPE PERFORMANCE TARGETS

Climate Zone | Wall R-Value | Roof R-Value | Air Changes/Hr
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Hot (1-2)    | R-13 to R-19| R-30 to R-38 | < 0.25
Mixed (3-4)  | R-19 to R-21| R-38 to R-49 | < 0.20
Cold (5-7)   | R-21 to R-25| R-49 to R-60 | < 0.15

CEA-Specific Considerations:
□ Continuous insulation (no thermal bridging)
□ Vapor barrier on warm side
□ Air barrier (tape all seams)
□ Seal all penetrations
□ Insulated doors
□ Minimal windows (thermal loss)

ROI on Envelope:
Investment: +$2-5/sf for high-performance
Annual savings: $0.50-1.50/sf in energy
Payback: 2-5 years
Plus: More stable environment, better crop quality

Example: 20,000 sf facility
Standard envelope: $60,000
High-performance: $100,000 (+$40,000)
Annual energy savings: $15,000
Payback: 2.7 years
20-year NPV: $150,000+ savings
```

## 5. HVAC System Design

### Right-Sizing HVAC

```
HVAC LOAD CALCULATION

Heat Gain Sources:
Lighting: 3.41 BTU/W (LED watt = heat watt)
Example: 150 kW lights = 511,500 BTU/hr

People: 250-400 BTU/person
Example: 8 people = 2,400 BTU/hr

Equipment: Per manufacturer specs
Example: Pumps, fans = 15,000 BTU/hr

Envelope: Heat transfer through walls/roof
Calculation: U-value × Area × ΔT
Example: 50,000 BTU/hr (summer)

Ventilation: Heating/cooling outside air
CFM × 1.08 × ΔT
Example: 2,000 CFM × 1.08 × 20°F = 43,200 BTU/hr

Total Cooling Load Example (10K sf):
Lighting: 511,500 BTU/hr
People: 2,400
Equipment: 15,000
Envelope: 50,000
Ventilation: 43,200
──────────────────────────
Total: 622,100 BTU/hr ≈ 52 tons

Safety factor: 1.15×
Final size: 60 tons

Heating Load (winter):
Much lower than cooling (lights provide heat)
Mainly envelope loss + ventilation
Typical: 30-40% of cooling load
```

### System Type Selection

```
HVAC SYSTEM COMPARISON

Packaged Rooftop Units:
Pros: Lower cost, easy to replace, simple
Cons: Less efficient, roof penetrations
Best for: <20,000 sf, moderate climate
Cost: $2,500-3,500/ton

Split Systems:
Pros: Flexible placement, efficient
Cons: More components, refrigerant lines
Best for: 5,000-30,000 sf
Cost: $3,000-4,000/ton

VRF (Variable Refrigerant Flow):
Pros: Very efficient, zone control, heat recovery
Cons: Higher cost, specialized service
Best for: >15,000 sf, complex zoning
Cost: $4,000-6,000/ton

Chiller + Air Handlers:
Pros: Centralized, very efficient, scalable
Cons: High cost, complex, space requirements
Best for: >30,000 sf, campus settings
Cost: $3,500-5,000/ton

Geothermal Heat Pump:
Pros: Lowest operating cost, very efficient
Cons: High upfront cost, site dependent
Best for: New construction, adequate land
Cost: $5,000-8,000/ton (including wells)
```

## 6. Electrical System Design

### Panel & Distribution Design

```
ELECTRICAL DISTRIBUTION STRATEGY

Single Growing Zone (< 10K sf):
Main panel → Sub-panel → Branch circuits
Simple, economical

Multiple Zones (10-30K sf):
Main panel → Zone sub-panels → Circuits
Allows independent zone control
Better load management

Large Facility (> 30K sf):
Main switchgear → Multiple panels → Circuits
May include:
- Lighting panels (dedicated)
- HVAC panels (dedicated)
- Receptacle panels
- Equipment panels

Circuit Organization:
Lighting:
□ Group by zone (allows zone-specific DLI)
□ 20A circuits, LED fixtures
□ Separate control wiring

HVAC:
□ Dedicated circuits for each unit
□ Consider soft starters (reduce inrush)
□ May be 208V or 480V

Pumps/Equipment:
□ Sized for motor load + 25%
□ VFDs on appropriate motors

Receptacles:
□ 20A circuits, GFCI in wet areas
□ Adequate quantity (every 25')

Emergency:
□ Generator panel (if backup power)
□ Critical loads only
```

## 7. Plumbing & Irrigation Design

### Water Distribution System

```
WATER SYSTEM DESIGN

Supply Sizing:
Peak demand: Based on irrigation + other uses
Example: 10 GPM irrigation + 5 GPM other = 15 GPM
Pipe size: 1.5" typical for 15 GPM
Larger facilities: 2-3" mains, branch to zones

Pressure Requirements:
Irrigation: 20-40 PSI typical
Fixtures: 40-60 PSI
May need pressure regulator if >80 PSI
May need booster pump if <40 PSI

Zoning Strategy:
Zone 1: Growing area north
Zone 2: Growing area south
Zone 3: Processing/cleaning
Zone 4: Restrooms/break room

Each zone:
□ Isolation valve
□ Pressure gauge
□ Flow meter (optional but recommended)
□ Backflow preventer (irrigation zones)

Drainage Design:
Floor slope: 1/4" per foot minimum to drains
Drain spacing: Every 400-600 sf in wet areas
Drain size: 3-4" typical
Trap primers: Required for floor drains

Materials:
Cold water: PEX, CPVC, copper
Drains: PVC, ABS
Irrigation: PVC, polyethylene
```

## 8. Lighting Layout Design

### Fixture Placement

```
GROW LIGHT LAYOUT OPTIMIZATION

Layout Principles:
□ Uniform coverage (no dark spots)
□ Appropriate spacing (per manufacturer)
□ Account for walkways/aisles
□ Circuit grouping logical
□ Mounting height optimized
□ Future flexibility

Typical Spacing:
LED bars: 3-6" on center
LED panels: Edge-to-edge or slight overlap
HPS (if used): 4-6' on center

Example: 10,000 sf growing area
Fixture: 4' LED bar, 320W, covers 4 sf
Quantity needed: 10,000 sf ÷ 4 sf = 2,500 fixtures
Circuits: 2,500 × 320W = 800 kW ÷ 240V ÷ 80% = 208A load
Number of 20A circuits: 208A ÷ 20A = ~11 circuits

Control Zones:
Minimum: 4 zones (allows crop staging)
Better: 8+ zones (flexibility)
Best: Individual fixture control (maximize flexibility)

Dimming:
0-10V: Simple, economical
DMX: More complex, addressable
Network (IoT): Maximum control, integration
```

## 9. Cost vs. Performance Trade-offs

### Design Optimization

```
CAPITAL VS OPERATING COST ANALYSIS

Decision: Envelope Insulation
Option A: Code minimum (R-19 walls, R-30 roof)
Cost: $50,000
Annual energy: $85,000

Option B: High performance (R-25 walls, R-49 roof)
Cost: $70,000 (+$20,000)
Annual energy: $73,000 (-$12,000/year)
Payback: 1.7 years ✓ DO IT

Decision: HVAC System
Option A: Standard rooftop units (SEER 13)
Cost: $120,000
Annual energy: $42,000

Option B: High-efficiency units (SEER 16)
Cost: $145,000 (+$25,000)
Annual energy: $38,000 (-$4,000/year)
Payback: 6.3 years ✓ MARGINAL

Option C: VRF system (SEER 18+)
Cost: $180,000 (+$60,000)
Annual energy: $35,000 (-$7,000/year)
Payback: 8.6 years ✗ SKIP (for typical project)

Decision: Lighting
Option A: Mid-tier LED (2.7 µmol/J)
Cost: $300,000
Annual energy: $65,000

Option B: Top-tier LED (3.0 µmol/J)
Cost: $360,000 (+$60,000)
Annual energy: $59,000 (-$6,000/year)
Payback: 10 years ✗ SKIP

Decision Framework:
Payback < 3 years: Definitely do it
Payback 3-5 years: Probably do it
Payback 5-7 years: Maybe (depends on capital availability)
Payback > 7 years: Probably skip (unless strategic)
```

## Summary

Effective design development requires:

1. **Qualified design team** with relevant experience
2. **Operator involvement** throughout process
3. **Phased reviews** at schematic, DD, and CD stages
4. **Optimized envelope** balancing cost and performance
5. **Right-sized systems** avoiding over/under capacity
6. **Efficient layouts** supporting operational workflow
7. **Integration coordination** between disciplines
8. **Cost-benefit analysis** on major decisions
9. **Thorough review** of final documents
10. **Flexibility** for future changes and expansion

Good design is the foundation of an efficient, productive facility. Invest the time to get it right - changes are much more expensive after construction begins.
