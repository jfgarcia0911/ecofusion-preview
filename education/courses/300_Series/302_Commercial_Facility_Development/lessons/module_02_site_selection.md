**Duration:** 1.5 hours
**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply comprehensive site evaluation criteria
- Calculate location-specific operating costs
- Perform utility capacity analysis
- Conduct site scoring and comparison
- Identify site-specific risks and opportunities

## 1. Site Selection Importance

### Why Site Selection Matters

The site you choose will impact:
- **Operating costs** (30-50% variation by location)
- **Capital costs** (20-40% variation)
- **Revenue potential** (market access)
- **Permitting difficulty** (months of delay possible)
- **Labor availability** (staffing success)
- **Long-term viability** (5-20+ year commitment)

### Site Selection Impact Analysis

```
SITE DECISION IMPACT OVER 10 YEARS

Factor               Good Site    Poor Site    Difference
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Utility Costs        $2M          $3.5M        +$1.5M
Labor Costs          $4M          $5M          +$1M
Real Estate          $1.5M        $2M          +$500K
Permitting Time      6 months     18 months    +12 months
Market Access        Excellent    Limited      Revenue risk
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total 10-Yr Impact                             $3M+ higher costs
                                               + delayed opening
                                               + market risk
```

**Key Insight:** Spending 3-6 months on thorough site selection can save millions and ensure project success.

```
├─ Electric capacity           5 = >2MW available, 1 = limited

LABOR MARKET           15%

├─ Wage expectations           5 = <$15/hr, 1 = >$25/hr
├─ Technical talent pool       5 = strong, 1 = weak
└─ Workforce stability         5 = stable, 1 = high turnover

CLIMATE                10%
├─ Heating degree days         5 = <2000, 1 = >7000
├─ Cooling degree days         5 = <1000, 1 = >2500
├─ Ambient humidity            5 = 40-60%, 1 = extremes
├─ Natural disasters           5 = low risk, 1 = high risk
└─ Solar resource              5 = excellent, 1 = poor

REGULATORY             10%
├─ Zoning compatibility        5 = permitted, 1 = prohibited
├─ Permitting efficiency       5 = <3 months, 1 = >12 months
├─ Building codes              5 = standard, 1 = restrictive
├─ Environmental regs          5 = standard, 1 = strict
└─ Agricultural support        5 = strong, 1 = none

INFRASTRUCTURE          5%
├─ Road access                 5 = highway, 1 = poor
├─ Internet availability       5 = fiber, 1 = limited
├─ Services (fire, police)     5 = excellent, 1 = limited
├─ Supplier proximity          5 = <50 miles, 1 = >200 miles
└─ Professional services       5 = available, 1 = limited

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL SCORE          100%      Maximum: 5.0

Interpretation:
4.5-5.0: Excellent site - proceed with confidence
4.0-4.4: Good site - manageable challenges
3.5-3.9: Fair site - significant compromises
3.0-3.4: Poor site - major concerns
<3.0: Unsuitable - keep looking
```

### Using the Scoring Matrix

1. **Gather data** for each criterion
2. **Score each factor** objectively (1-5)
3. **Calculate weighted scores** (score × weight)
4. **Total the weighted scores** for overall site rating
5. **Compare multiple sites** using consistent criteria
6. **Identify improvement opportunities** for lower-scoring areas

## 3. Utility Analysis

### Electrical Service Evaluation

**Critical Questions:**
- What is the available service size?
- What is the utility's electric rate structure?
- Are demand charges significant?
- Are there time-of-use rates?
- What incentives are available?
- What is the interconnection timeline?

**Capacity Requirements by Facility Size:**

### Water Service Evaluation

**Required Capacity:**
- Hydroponic: 10-20 gallons per sq ft per year
- Aquaponic: 20-40 gallons per sq ft per year
- Includes makeup water for evapotranspiration, blowdown, cleaning

**Water Quality Requirements:**
- TDS: <200 ppm preferred
- pH: 6.0-8.0
- Chlorine/chloramine: Must be removed
- Hardness: <150 ppm preferred
- Contaminants: No heavy metals

**Water Cost Analysis:**

```
WATER SERVICE EVALUATION

System Type      Annual Use     @ $3/1000gal  @ $8/1000gal
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10K sf hydro     100-200 Kgal   $300-$600     $800-$1,600
10K sf aqua      200-400 Kgal   $600-$1,200   $1,600-$3,200
20K sf hydro     200-400 Kgal   $600-$1,200   $1,600-$3,200
20K sf aqua      400-800 Kgal   $1,200-$2,400 $3,200-$6,400

Note: High-quality municipal water preferred
Well water may require extensive treatment
Consider water reclamation systems for high-cost areas
```

### Natural Gas Service (if applicable)

**Applications:**
- Supplemental heating in cold climates
- CO2 generation (combustion)
- Backup power generation

**Availability Impact:**
- With gas: 30-50% heating cost savings vs. electric
- Without gas: All-electric design required
- Consider propane as alternative in rural areas

### Lease vs. Purchase Decision

```
LEASE VS PURCHASE ANALYSIS

Factor                  Lease              Purchase
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Upfront Capital         Lower (deposit)    Higher (down payment)
Monthly Cost            Higher             Lower (if financed)
Flexibility             Higher             Lower
Long-term Cost          Higher total       Lower total
Equity Building         No                 Yes
Renovation Control      Limited            Complete
Exit Strategy           Easier             Harder
Tax Treatment           Deduct rent        Depreciation + interest

Decision Framework:
LEASE IF:                          PURCHASE IF:
• Testing market                   • Established operation
• Limited capital                  • Adequate capital
• <5 year horizon                  • 10+ year horizon
• Want flexibility                 • Want control
• Uncertain scaling                • Planning expansion

Example: 20,000 sf facility
Lease: $8/sf/yr = $160K/year
Purchase: $100/sf = $2M (or $15K/mo at 6% over 20 years)

Total Cost Over 10 Years:
Lease: $1,600,000
Purchase: $1,800,000 (payments) - $2,000,000 (equity) = -$200K (net)
```

### Jurisdiction Comparison

Ask these questions about each potential jurisdiction:

1. **How does the jurisdiction classify CEA?**
   - Agricultural (beneficial)
   - Industrial/manufacturing (neutral)
   - Special use (may require hearings)

2. **What is the typical permit timeline?**
   - Historical data for similar projects
   - Current department backlog

3. **Are there pre-application meetings?**
   - Can expedite process
   - Identify issues early

4. **What fees apply?**
   - Building permit fees
   - Impact fees
   - Utility connection fees

5. **Are there agricultural incentives?**
   - Tax breaks
   - Fee waivers

## 9. Site Comparison Process

### Step-by-Step Site Evaluation

**Step 1: Initial Screening**
- Eliminate sites that fail critical requirements
- Size, zoning, utilities, price range

**Step 2: Data Collection**
- Gather complete information for remaining sites
- Utility rates, labor costs, building details

**Step 3: Scoring**
- Apply evaluation matrix consistently
- Document assumptions and data sources

**Step 4: Financial Modeling**
- Pro forma for each site
- Include site-specific costs and revenues

**Step 5: Risk Assessment**
- Identify unique risks for each site
- Estimate mitigation costs

**Step 6: Team Review**
- Present findings to stakeholders
- Discuss trade-offs and preferences

**Step 7: Due Diligence**
- Deep dive on top 1-2 sites
- Professional inspections and studies

**Step 8: Final Decision**
- Select site with best overall profile
- Negotiate terms

### Red Flags to Watch For

**Deal Breakers:**
- Contaminated soil (Phase I Environmental issues)
- Inadequate electrical service (not upgradeable)
- Prohibited zoning (cannot be changed)
- Structural deficiencies (cost prohibitive)
- Flood zone (cannot mitigate)
- Unsuitable water quality (cannot treat economically)

**Serious Concerns (may be addressable):**
- Poor roof condition (budget $5-15/sf replacement)
- Inadequate HVAC (budget for new system)
- Limited electrical (budget $50-150K upgrade)
- Zoning requires variance (adds 3-6 months)
- Moderate environmental issues (may need remediation)

**Manageable Issues:**
- Cosmetic repairs
- Minor plumbing upgrades
- Office improvements
- Parking expansion
- Landscaping

## Summary

Effective site selection requires:

1. **Comprehensive evaluation** using weighted criteria
2. **Utility analysis** focusing on long-term operating costs
3. **Building suitability** assessment for CEA requirements
4. **Market validation** confirming customer access
5. **Climate consideration** understanding energy impacts
6. **Labor market** evaluation for staffing success
7. **Regulatory review** to avoid permitting delays
8. **Thorough due diligence** before commitment
9. **Financial modeling** for each site option
10. **Risk assessment** identifying and mitigating concerns

The site you select will impact every aspect of your operation for years to come. Invest the time and resources to choose wisely.
