# Module 9: Contractor Selection

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Evaluate and select qualified contractors
- Structure competitive bidding processes
- Negotiate favorable contract terms
- Understand contract types and risk allocation
- Avoid contractor-related problems

---

## 1. Contractor Types & Roles

### General Contractor (GC)

```
GENERAL CONTRACTOR OVERVIEW

Responsibilities:
□ Overall project management
□ Subcontractor coordination
□ Schedule management
□ Quality control
□ Safety management
□ Permitting coordination
□ Material procurement (some items)
□ Cleanup and protection

Compensation:
Fee structure: Cost + fee or lump sum
Typical markup: 10-20% on subcontractor work
Typical fee: 8-15% on GC-performed work

When to Use:
✓ Conventional building work
✓ Want single point of responsibility
✓ Need coordination expertise
✓ Limited owner construction experience

When to Skip:
Consider CM or owner-builder if:
- Very experienced owner
- Highly specialized work
- Very simple project
- Want to reduce markup
```

### Construction Manager (CM)

```
CONSTRUCTION MANAGER APPROACH

CM as Agent:
Role: Owner's representative, manage subs
Fee: Fixed fee or % (typically 3-6%)
Contracts: Owner contracts directly with subs
Risk: Owner bears cost overrun risk
Benefit: Transparency, lower markup

CM at-Risk (CMAR):
Role: Like GC, guarantees maximum price
Fee: Typically 5-10%
Contracts: CM contracts with subs
Risk: CM bears cost overrun risk (up to GMP)
Benefit: Cost certainty, early involvement

Best for:
- Large, complex projects
- Early cost input needed
- Owner wants transparency
- Phased construction
```

## 3. Bidding Process

### Competitive Bid Process

```
BID PROCESS TIMELINE

Week 1: Pre-Bid
□ Finalize construction documents
□ Prepare bid package
□ Identify potential bidders (5-8)
□ Send invitation to bid

Week 2: Bid Period Starts
□ Issue bid documents
□ Hold pre-bid meeting
□ Bidders visit site
□ Answer questions (RFIs)

Week 3: Bid Period
□ Continue answering RFIs
□ Issue addenda if needed
□ Bidders develop pricing

Week 4: Bid Due & Award
□ Receive bids
□ Review and analyze
□ Check references
□ Interview top 2-3
□ Select contractor
□ Negotiate final terms
□ Execute contract

Total Time: 4-6 weeks
```

```
COMPREHENSIVE BID PACKAGE

Instructions to Bidders:

□ Bid submission format
□ Pre-bid meeting date/time
□ Site visit arrangements
□ Question procedure
□ Award timeline

Project Information:

□ Site address
□ Owner information
□ Architect/engineer contact

Scope of Work:
□ Construction drawings (full set)
□ Specifications
□ Site survey
□ Geotechnical report (if available)
□ Existing conditions photos

Contract Documents:
□ Sample contract (AIA A101 or similar)
□ General conditions
□ Supplementary conditions
□ Insurance requirements
□ Bonding requirements

Bid Forms:
□ Bid form (price breakdown)
□ Bid bond form
□ Subcontractor list form
□ Qualification statement
□ References form

Project Requirements:
□ Schedule (target dates)
□ Quality standards
□ Safety requirements

□ Warranty requirements
```

## 4. Contract Types

### Contract Structure Options

```
CONTRACT TYPE COMPARISON

Lump Sum (Fixed Price):
Description: Single price for defined scope
Best for: Well-defined scope, complete drawings
Risk: Contractor bears cost overrun risk
Owner risk: Lowest (price certain)
Contractor markup: 15-25% (accounts for risk)
Example: $1,200,000 for complete building

Cost Plus Fixed Fee:
Description: Reimburse costs + fixed fee
Best for: Undefined scope, design-build, early start
Risk: Owner bears cost overrun risk
Owner risk: Highest (final cost uncertain)
Contractor markup: 5-10% fee on costs
Example: Actual costs + $75,000 fee
Requires: Open book accounting, cost controls

Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP):
Description: Cost plus with ceiling
Best for: Early involvement, some uncertainty
Risk: Contractor bears overrun above GMP
Owner risk: Medium (capped price)
Contractor markup: 8-15%
Example: Costs + 10% fee, not to exceed $1,350,000
May include: Savings sharing if under GMP

Unit Price:
Description: Price per unit of work
Best for: Quantities uncertain (site work, etc.)
Risk: Shared (quantity risk to owner, price risk to GC)
Owner risk: Medium (total depends on quantities)
Example: $45/CY excavation, $8/SF concrete

Time & Materials (T&M):
Description: Hourly labor + materials at cost
Best for: Small projects, service work, changes
Risk: Owner bears all risk
Owner risk: Highest (no price certainty)
Markup: 20-50% on labor, 10-20% on materials
Example: $75/hr + materials + 15%
Use: Only for small scopes or emergency work

Recommendation for CEA:
New construction: Lump sum (firm scope)
Renovation: GMP (uncertainty)
Site work: Unit price (variable quantities)
Growing systems: Lump sum (defined)
```

## 5. Contract Terms

### Key Contract Provisions

```
CRITICAL CONTRACT CLAUSES

Price & Payment:
□ Contract amount: $________
□ Payment terms: Net ___ days
□ Retainage: ___% (typical 5-10%)
□ Payment schedule: Monthly based on % complete
□ Final payment: After punchlist, lien waivers
□ Interest on late payment: ___% (if any)

Schedule:
□ Start date: ________
□ Substantial completion: ________ (__ days)
□ Final completion: ________
□ Liquidated damages: $_____/day (if applicable)
□ Weather days: ___ allowed
□ Schedule updates: Monthly CPM

Scope:
□ Drawings: Dated ________, __ sheets
□ Specifications: Dated ________, __ pages
□ Alternates included: ________
□ Exclusions: ________ (list clearly)
□ Allowances: ________ (for undefined items)

Changes:
□ Change order process: Written only
□ Time for pricing changes: ___ days
□ Markup on changes: ___% (negotiate 10-15% max)
□ No work without written authorization

Warranties:
□ Workmanship: 1 year typical
□ Systems: Per manufacturer (pass through)
□ Roof: ___ years
□ Structural: ___ years
□ Warranty start: Date of substantial completion

Insurance & Bonding:
□ General liability: $1M/$2M minimum
□ Workers comp: Statutory
□ Builder's risk: All-risk, replacement cost
□ Performance bond: 100% of contract (if required)
□ Payment bond: 100% of contract (if required)

Dispute Resolution:
□ Notice of claims: Within ___ days
□ Mediation: Required before litigation
□ Arbitration: □ Required □ Optional
□ Attorney's fees: Prevailing party recovers
□ Venue: ________ County, State

Termination:
□ For cause: Defined reasons (breach, etc.)
□ For convenience: Owner's right (with payment)
□ Bankruptcy: Auto termination
□ Notice period: ___ days
```

## 6. Negotiating Contract Terms

### Negotiation Strategy

```
CONTRACT NEGOTIATION PRIORITIES

Must-Have Terms (Non-negotiable):
✓ Adequate insurance coverage
✓ Clear scope definition
✓ Firm completion date
✓ Quality standards
✓ Warranty provisions
✓ Lien waiver requirements
✓ Change order process

Negotiate Hard:
⚡ Price (within reason)
⚡ Payment terms (retain leverage)
⚡ Liquidated damages (if schedule critical)
⚡ Change order markup (cap at 15%)
⚡ Retainage percentage
⚡ Warranty period (seek 2 years)

Give When Appropriate:
• Payment terms (30 days vs 45 days)
• Schedule (if feasible)
• Working hours (vs. premium rates)
• Allowances (let them include)

Red Flags - Don't Agree:
× Pay-if-paid (should be pay-when-paid)
× No retainage (need leverage for completion)
× Unlimited change order markup
× Waive consequential damages (both ways)
× Arbitration mandatory (prefer mediation first)
× Short warranty (less than 1 year)
× No liquidated damages (if schedule critical)
× Broad exclusions (shifting risk to owner)

Negotiation Tactics:
1. Know your priorities (what matters most)
2. Understand contractor's position
3. Look for win-win solutions
4. Don't negotiate against yourself
5. Get everything in writing
6. Use "if...then" proposals
7. Be willing to walk away
8. Remember: Relationship matters
```

## 7. Contractor Red Flags

### Warning Signs

```
CONTRACTOR WARNING SIGNS

Financial Red Flags:
🚩 Requests large upfront payment (>10%)
🚩 Can't provide bonding
🚩 Won't provide financial statements
🚩 Frequent complaints about cash flow
🚩 Pays subs late (ask subs!)
🚩 Liens filed on other projects

Experience Red Flags:
🚩 No similar projects
🚩 Won't provide references
🚩 References are vague or old
🚩 Not licensed where required
🚩 No insurance or inadequate coverage

Communication Red Flags:
🚩 Slow to respond to questions
🚩 Avoids putting things in writing
🚩 Promises things that seem unrealistic
🚩 Pressure tactics ("sign now or...")
🚩 Dismissive of your concerns

Operational Red Flags:
🚩 No safety program
🚩 High EMR (>1.2)
🚩 Doesn't visit site before bidding
🚩 Bid much lower than others (>15%)
🚩 Bid much higher than others (>20%)
🚩 Vague in describing approach
🚩 No quality control program

If You See Multiple Red Flags:
→ Don't hire this contractor
→ Even if lowest bid
→ Risk of poor performance, delays, litigation
→ Not worth the savings
```

## 8. Subcontractor Management

### Subcontractor Requirements

```
SUBCONTRACTOR OVERSIGHT

Require GC to Provide:
□ Subcontractor list (before contract)
□ Qualification summaries
□ Proof of insurance (all subs)
□ License verification (where required)
□ Safety records

Key Subcontractors (may want to approve):
□ MEP contractors (major scope)
□ Structural (critical)
□ Growing system installer (specialized)
□ Fire protection (code compliance)

Subcontractor Approval Rights:
Option 1: Pre-approved list in bid
  GC lists specific subs in bid
  Owner approves before award
  Changes require owner approval

Option 2: Post-award approval
  GC proposes subs after award
  Owner has X days to approve
  Owner can reject with cause

Option 3: No approval rights
  GC chooses all subs
  Owner has no say
  Fastest but risky

Recommendation: Pre-approved list for major trades
```

## 10. Common Contractor Issues

### Problem Prevention & Resolution

```
AVOIDING CONTRACTOR PROBLEMS

Prevention Strategies:

Clear Documentation:
✓ Complete drawings before bidding
✓ Detailed specifications
✓ Site conditions clearly disclosed
✓ Scope clearly defined
✓ Exclusions listed explicitly

Proper Selection:
✓ Thorough vetting
✓ Check references (actually call them)
✓ Verify insurance and bonding
✓ Review past projects (visit sites)
✓ Interview key personnel

Effective Contract:
✓ Use standard contract (AIA, etc.)
✓ Don't delete important provisions
✓ Negotiate before signing
✓ Include all required items
✓ Both parties review with attorney

Active Management:
✓ Weekly site visits
✓ Review pay apps carefully
✓ Inspect work before payment
✓ Document everything
✓ Address issues immediately
✓ Maintain professional relationship

Common Problems & Solutions:

Problem: Slow progress
Solutions:
- Review schedule weekly
- Identify delays early
- Request recovery schedule
- Consider liquidated damages
- Add crews if needed (GC expense)

Problem: Quality issues
Solutions:
- Reject non-conforming work immediately
- Document with photos
- Require correction at GC expense
- Increase inspection frequency
- Hold retainage until corrected

Problem: Excessive change orders
Solutions:
- Review each carefully
- Verify scope is truly changed
- Get multiple quotes if possible
- Negotiate pricing
- Consider value engineering
- Document all decisions

Problem: Safety violations
Solutions:
- Stop work immediately if serious
- Require corrective action
- Increase safety monitoring
- Consider OSHA report if severe
- Terminate if repeated violations

Problem: Payment disputes
Solutions:
- Review pay app line-by-line
- Only pay for completed work
- Maintain retainage
- Require lien waivers
- Document percentage complete
- Mediate if needed

When to Terminate:
- Abandonment of project
- Repeated safety violations
- Bankruptcy
- Loss of license/insurance
- Material breach of contract
- Failure to pay subs (liens filed)

Last resort - expensive and disruptive
Try to resolve issues first
```

## Summary

Successful contractor selection and management requires:

1. **Thorough vetting** - experience, references, financials
2. **Competitive bidding** - 3-5 qualified bidders
3. **Careful analysis** - not just lowest price
4. **Appropriate contract** - matches project risk
5. **Clear terms** - scope, schedule, payment, quality
6. **Effective negotiation** - fair to both parties
7. **Subcontractor oversight** - approve key trades
8. **Active management** - weekly engagement
9. **Documentation** - everything in writing
10. **Problem resolution** - address issues immediately

The right contractor, with a fair contract and active management, is essential to successful project delivery.
