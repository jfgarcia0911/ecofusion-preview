# Glossary: Advanced Biological Systems
## Course 311

---

## A

**Aerobic**: Requiring or occurring in the presence of oxygen.

**Ammonia-Oxidizing Bacteria (AOB)**: Bacteria that convert ammonia to nitrite as the first step of nitrification, primarily Nitrosomonas and Nitrosospira species.

**Anaerobic**: Occurring in the absence of oxygen; some processes like denitrification occur in anaerobic zones.

**Antagonist**: An organism that inhibits or kills other organisms, often used for biological disease control.

**Autotrophic**: Organisms that produce their own food from inorganic compounds; nitrifying bacteria are autotrophs using ammonia for energy.

## B

**Bacillus**: A genus of beneficial bacteria commonly used for disease suppression and plant growth promotion.

**Bioaugmentation**: The addition of beneficial microorganisms to enhance biological processes in a system.

**Biofilm**: A structured community of microorganisms attached to a surface and encased in a self-produced matrix of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS).

**Biofilter**: A device containing media that supports microbial communities for biological water treatment, particularly nitrification.

**Biofloc**: Aggregates of bacteria, algae, protozoa, and other organisms that can serve as supplemental protein for fish.

## C

**CFU (Colony Forming Units)**: A measure of viable bacterial cells, typically expressed as CFU per milliliter or gram.

**Colonization**: The establishment of microbial populations on surfaces or in the root zone.

**Competitive Exclusion**: The principle where beneficial microbes occupy niches, preventing pathogen establishment.

**Cycling**: The process of establishing nitrifying bacteria populations in a new aquaponic system before adding fish.

## D

**Denitrification**: The biological conversion of nitrate to nitrogen gas by anaerobic bacteria, removing nitrogen from the system.

**Decomposer**: Organisms that break down dead organic matter, releasing nutrients for plant uptake.

## E

**EPS (Extracellular Polymeric Substances)**: The matrix of polysaccharides, proteins, and other molecules that bacteria secrete to form biofilms.

**Endophyte**: Microorganisms that live within plant tissues without causing disease, often providing benefits.

## G

**Generation Time**: The time required for a bacterial population to double; varies from minutes (heterotrophs) to hours (nitrifiers).

## H

**Heterotroph**: Organisms that obtain energy by consuming organic compounds; heterotrophic bacteria compete with nitrifiers for oxygen and space.

## I

**Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR)**: Plant defense responses triggered by beneficial microbes that protect against pathogens.

**Inoculation**: The introduction of beneficial microorganisms to a system or growing medium.

## M

**MBBR (Moving Bed Biofilm Reactor)**: A biofilter type using suspended plastic media that provides surface area for biofilm growth.

**Mineralization**: The breakdown of organic matter by microorganisms, releasing inorganic nutrients.

**Mycorrhizae**: Symbiotic fungi that associate with plant roots, enhancing nutrient and water uptake.

## N

**Nitrite-Oxidizing Bacteria (NOB)**: Bacteria that convert nitrite to nitrate as the second step of nitrification, primarily Nitrobacter and Nitrospira species.

**Nitrification**: The two-step biological process converting ammonia to nitrite to nitrate.

**Nitrogen Cycle**: The biogeochemical cycle describing nitrogen transformations in ecosystems.

## P

**PGPR (Plant Growth-Promoting Rhizobacteria)**: Beneficial bacteria that colonize plant roots and enhance growth through various mechanisms.

**Probiotics**: Beneficial microorganisms administered to fish to improve gut health and immunity.

**Pythium**: A genus of water molds that cause root rot; controlled through biological antagonists like Trichoderma.

## R

**Rhizosphere**: The zone of soil or growing media directly influenced by root secretions and associated microorganisms.

## S

**Siderophore**: Iron-chelating compounds produced by bacteria that can make iron available to plants while limiting pathogen growth.

**Specific Surface Area (SSA)**: The total surface area of biofilter media per unit volume, measured in m²/m³.

**Substrate**: The material or surface on which microorganisms grow; in biofilters, this is the media.

## T

**TAN (Total Ammonia Nitrogen)**: The sum of ionized (NH4+) and un-ionized (NH3) ammonia in water.

**Trichoderma**: A genus of beneficial fungi used for biological disease control and enhanced nutrient availability.

## V

**Vermicompost**: Compost produced through the action of earthworms, rich in beneficial microorganisms.
