# Module 6: Composting and Vermicomposting

## Overview
Composting and vermicomposting produce nutrient-rich, microbially-active amendments that can enhance aquaponic systems. This module covers the biology of decomposition, practical composting methods, and safe integration of compost products into aquaponics.

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand aerobic decomposition biology
- Produce high-quality compost and vermicompost
- Extract and apply compost safely in aquaponics
- Recognize risks and mitigation strategies
- Integrate waste streams for circular systems

---

## Composting Fundamentals

### What is Composting?

**Definition:** Controlled aerobic microbial decomposition of organic matter into a stable, humus-like material.

**Key Word: AEROBIC** - Oxygen is essential for proper composting.

### Key Microbial Groups

**Bacteria (most abundant initially):**
- Decompose simple compounds
- Generate most heat
- Rapid reproduction
- Dominate early stages

**Fungi (important later):**
- Decompose cellulose, lignin
- Hyphal networks
- Create soil structure
- Dominate maturation

**Actinobacteria:**
- Streptomyces species
- Produce antibiotics
- Characteristic earthy smell (geosmin)
- Late-stage decomposition

**Protozoa:**
- Graze on bacteria
- Mineralize nutrients
- Moderate bacterial populations

## Critical Composting Parameters

### 1. Carbon to Nitrogen Ratio (C:N)

**Optimal: 25-30:1**

```
Too High C:N (>40:1):                Too Low C:N (<20:1):
• Slow decomposition                 • Ammonia loss (smell!)
• Nitrogen limited                   • Heat excess
• May need N supplement              • Potential toxicity
• Examples: Sawdust, straw alone     • Examples: Fresh manure alone

Balanced (25-30:1):
• Rapid, complete decomposition
• Minimal nutrient loss
• Good heat generation
• Examples: Mixed brown + green materials
```

**Common Material C:N Ratios:**

| Material | C:N Ratio | Category |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Sawdust | 400:1 | Very high C |
| Wood chips | 700:1 | Very high C |
| Straw | 80:1 | High C |
| Dry leaves | 50:1 | High C |
| Corn stalks | 60:1 | High C |
| Food scraps | 15:1 | Low C, high N |
| Grass clippings | 20:1 | Low C, high N |
| Vegetable waste | 12:1 | Low C, high N |
| Coffee grounds | 20:1 | Low C, high N |
| Poultry manure | 10:1 | Very low C, high N |
| Fish waste (solids) | 5:1 | Very low C, high N |

**Balancing Strategy:**
```
Mix high C (browns) + high N (greens) = optimal C:N

Example:
3 parts dry leaves (C:N 50:1)
+
1 part food scraps (C:N 15:1)
=
Approximately 30:1 (ideal!)
```

---

### 2. Moisture Content

**Optimal: 50-60%**

```
Moisture Test (Squeeze Test):
1. Grab handful of compost material
2. Squeeze firmly

Results:
• Water drips out → Too wet (>65%)
• Stays in a ball, few drops, glistens → Perfect (50-60%)
• Crumbles immediately → Too dry (<40%)
```

**Moisture Problems:**

**Too Wet:**
- Anaerobic conditions (O2 excluded)
- Foul smell
- Slow decomposition
- Nutrient leaching
- **Fix:** Add dry browns, turn more frequently

**Too Dry:**
- Microbial activity stops
- No decomposition
- Dusty
- **Fix:** Add water gradually, turn to distribute

---

### 3. Aeration (Oxygen)

**Requirement:** >5% O2 concentration (about 10% of atmosphere)

**Aeration Methods:**

| Method | Frequency | Effort | Effectiveness |
|--------|-----------|--------|---------------|
| **Turning** | Every 3-7 days | High | Excellent |
| **Passive aeration (pipes)** | Setup once | Low | Moderate |
| **Forced air (blower)** | Continuous | Low (energy) | Excellent |
| **Tumbler rotation** | Every 2-3 days | Moderate | Good |

**Signs of Poor Aeration:**
- Foul smell (H2S, ammonia)
- Low temperature
- Slimy texture
- Fruit flies, pests

---

### 4. Particle Size

**Optimal: 1-2 inches (2.5-5 cm)**

**Too Large (>3 inches):**
- Low surface area
- Slow decomposition
- Large air spaces (good) but less microbial contact

**Too Small (<0.5 inches):**
- Compacts easily
- Poor aeration
- Anaerobic zones

**Solution:** Mix particle sizes (bulking agents like wood chips + fine greens)

---

### 5. Temperature

**Pathogen Kill Requirements:**
- 55°C (131°F) for 3 days: Kills most pathogens and weed seeds
- 60°C (140°F) for 1 day: More thorough
- 65°C (149°F): Maximum, above this kills beneficial thermophiles

**Temperature Management:**
- Monitor with compost thermometer (must reach into center)
- Turn when temperature peaks (usually 3-7 days)
- Maintain thermophilic phase for at least 3 turnings
- Allow cooling and maturation

## Hot Composting Method

### Berkeley Method (Fast Composting)

**Timeline:** 18 days to finished compost!

```
Day 1:   Build pile (minimum 1 cubic yard/meter)
         C:N 25-30:1, moisture 50-60%

Day 2-3: Temperature rises to 40-50°C

Day 4:   TURN #1 - Mix outer material to center
         Add water if needed
         Temp should spike again

Day 7:   TURN #2 - Temp should be 55-65°C

Day 10:  TURN #3

Day 14:  TURN #4 - Temp declining

Day 18:  Finished compost!
         (may need additional maturation)
```

**Requirements:**
- Large volume (critical mass for heat retention)
- Optimal C:N ratio
- Frequent turning (4x in 18 days)
- Good aeration
- Proper moisture

## Vermicomposting (Worm Composting)

### Why Worms?

**Advantages over thermophilic composting:**
- No turning required
- Lower temperature (15-25°C)
- Excellent for continuous feeding (kitchen scraps)
- Worm castings are superior to regular compost (more plant-available nutrients)
- Produces "worm tea" (liquid fertilizer)
- Can be done indoors (small scale)

**Disadvantages:**
- Slower than hot composting
- No pathogen/weed seed kill
- Worms are sensitive (pH, temperature, ammonia)
- Requires careful feeding management

---

### Vermicomposting Biology

**Eisenia fetida (Red Wiggler Worm):**
- Temperature: 15-25°C (59-77°F) optimal
- pH: 6.0-7.0
- Moisture: 70-80% (wetter than regular compost)
- Feed: Consumes 50-100% of body weight per day
- Reproduction: Fast (doubles every 60-90 days under ideal conditions)
- Avoids light (stays in bedding)

**Worm Digestive System:**
```
Food → Gizzard (grinding) → Intestine (microbial digestion) → Castings
                                 │
                                 └─ Microbes break down organics
                                    Worms absorb nutrients
                                    Castings enriched with:
                                    • Beneficial microbes
                                    • Plant hormones
                                    • Humic acids
                                    • Available nutrients
```

**Microbes in Vermicompost:**
- Bacteria: 10^9 cells/g (extremely high!)
- Fungi: Lower than regular compost (worms prefer bacteria)
- Actinobacteria: Present, beneficial
- Protozoa: Moderate levels

---

**Horizontal Migration System:**
- Worms migrate horizontally to new food
- Harvest finished side, refill with bedding
- Continuous system

---

### Vermicomposting Management

**Feeding:**
- Start slow: 0.5 lb food per lb of worms per week
- Increase as worms multiply
- Bury food under bedding (reduces fruit flies)
- Avoid: citrus (too acidic), meat/dairy (odor, pests), oily foods

**Bedding Materials:**
- Shredded cardboard (excellent, high carbon)
- Shredded newspaper (OK, avoid glossy)
- Coconut coir (excellent moisture retention)
- Aged compost (good starter)
- Avoid: Fresh manure (too hot/ammoniated)

**Moisture:**
- Keep at 70-80% (like wrung-out sponge)
- Drainage essential (no waterlogging)
- Mist if too dry

**Temperature:**
- Ideal: 18-24°C (64-75°F)
- <10°C: Worms inactive
- >30°C: Worms stressed, may die
- Indoor bins easier to temperature control

**pH Management:**
- Monitor pH weekly (target 6.5-7.0)
- Add crushed eggshells or lime if too acidic
- Avoid overfeeding (causes acidic conditions)

---

### Harvesting Vermicompost

**Method 1: Light Method**
- Dump bin contents in pile under bright light
- Worms burrow down (light avoidance)
- Scrape off finished castings from top
- Repeat in layers
- Return worms to bin

**Method 2: Flow-Through (No Harvesting Needed)**
- Continuously harvest from bottom
- Worms stay on top
- Easiest method

**Method 3: Migration Method**
- Push finished compost to one side
- Add fresh bedding and food to other side
- Wait 2-3 weeks (worms migrate to food)
- Harvest worm-free finished side

## Compost Tea

### What is Compost Tea?

**Definition:** Aqueous extract of compost, designed to transfer beneficial microorganisms to plants.

**Two Types:**

**1. Aerated Compost Tea (ACT):**
- Actively brewed with aeration
- Microbial populations multiply during brewing
- Higher microbial counts
- MUST be aerobic (DO >6 mg/L always)
- Use within 4-8 hours

**2. Non-Aerated Compost Tea:**
- Simple water extraction
- Lower microbial counts
- Easier but riskier (can go anaerobic)
- Use immediately

### Compost Tea in Aquaponics: RISKS

**Major Concerns:**

**1. Pathogen Introduction:**
- E. coli, Salmonella if compost source contaminated
- Especially risky with manure-based composts
- **Mitigation:** Use only plant-based compost, no manure

**2. Oxygen Depletion:**
- If tea goes anaerobic, massive O2 consumption
- Can crash fish tank
- **Mitigation:** Apply only to plant zones, NOT fish tanks

**3. Organic Overload:**
- BOD (biological oxygen demand) spike
- Heterotrophic bloom
- **Mitigation:** Dilute heavily, apply in moderation

**4. pH Swing:**
- Organic acids can drop pH
- **Mitigation:** Monitor pH after application

**5. Ammonia Spike:**
- Mineralization of organic N
- **Mitigation:** Monitor ammonia, apply sparingly

---

### Safe Compost Tea Application in Aquaponics

**Best Practices:**

```
SAFE:
✓ Apply to media bed surface (not water column)
✓ Use plant-based compost only (no manure)
✓ Dilute 1:10 or higher
✓ Apply in small amounts
✓ Monitor water quality after application
✓ Apply when fish load is low
✓ Use only well-aged, finished compost

UNSAFE:
✗ Apply directly to fish tank
✗ Use manure-based compost tea
✗ Apply undiluted
✗ Large volume applications
✗ Use if tea smells bad (anaerobic!)
✗ Apply to DWC or NFT (harder to control)
✗ Use in systems with disease history
```

**Recommended Approach:**
- Foliar spray for plants (bypasses water entirely)
- Or use dry compost/vermicompost directly in media (safer)
- Reserve compost tea for soil-based systems

## Integrating Compost into Aquaponics

### Direct Application

**Vermicompost in Media Beds:**
```
Application Rate: 5-10% by volume mixed into media

Protocol:
1. Harvest finished vermicompost
2. Mix into media around plant bases
3. Or incorporate into new planting holes
4. Slow-release nutrients + microbial inoculation

Benefits:
• Microbial diversity
• Slow nutrient release
• Improved media structure
• Reduced compaction

Risks: Minimal if using aged, finished vermicompost
```

---

### Compost Extract (Not Tea)

**Cold Water Extract:**
```
1. Place vermicompost in mesh bag
2. Submerge in dechlorinated water (1:5 ratio)
3. Let sit 4-24 hours (NO aeration)
4. Remove bag
5. Apply liquid to root zones

Less microbial growth than ACT
Lower risk
Mainly nutrient extraction
```

---

### Worm Leachate ("Worm Tea")

**Caution:** Not the same as compost tea!

**Leachate:** Liquid that drains from worm bins

**Problems:**
- May contain excess salts
- Can be anaerobic
- Variable composition
- Potential pathogens

**If Using:**
- Dilute heavily (1:10)
- Only apply to plants, never fish
- Smell test (should be earthy, not foul)
- Aerate before use

**Better:** True aerated worm casting tea (brewed from castings)

## Compost Quality Assessment

### Visual Indicators

| Parameter | Good Compost | Poor Compost |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Color** | Dark brown to black | Light brown, or patches of white/green |
| **Texture** | Crumbly, friable | Slimy, compacted, or woody |
| **Odor** | Earthy (like forest) | Ammonia, sulfur, or sour |
| **Particle size** | Small, uniform | Large undecomposed chunks |
| **Temperature** | Ambient (finished) | Hot (not finished) or cold and wet (anaerobic) |
| **Moisture** | Damp but not wet | Soggy or bone dry |

---

### Maturity Tests

**1. Smell Test:** Should smell earthy, not ammonia or rotten

**2. Temperature Test:** Ambient (finished) vs still hot (active)

**3. Germination Test:**
```
Protocol:
1. Fill pot with compost
2. Plant fast-germinating seeds (radish, lettuce)
3. Fill control pot with potting mix
4. Water both, place in light
5. Observe for 7-10 days

Mature compost: Normal germination and growth
Immature compost: Poor germination, yellow seedlings (N immobilization or phytotoxins)
```

**4. Bag Test:**
```
1. Place sample in sealed plastic bag
2. Leave 24-48 hours
3. Open and smell

Finished: Earthy smell
```

## Summary

Composting and vermicomposting can enhance aquaponic systems when applied safely:

**Key Takeaways:**
1. Composting is aerobic microbial decomposition (never let it go anaerobic!)
2. Key parameters: C:N 25-30:1, moisture 50-60%, O2 >5%, temp 55-65°C for pathogen kill
3. Hot composting: 3-12 weeks depending on management
4. Vermicomposting: Slower but produces superior product (worm castings)
5. Red wigglers (Eisenia fetida) ideal for vermicomposting
6. Compost tea in aquaponics: HIGH RISK, use cautiously or avoid
7. Direct vermicompost application to media beds: SAFE and beneficial
8. Integrate fish and plant waste into compost systems for zero waste
9. Mature, finished compost only (test for maturity)
10. Plant-based compost safer than manure-based for aquaponics
