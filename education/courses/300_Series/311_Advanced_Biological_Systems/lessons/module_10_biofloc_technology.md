# Module 10: Biofloc Technology (BFT)

## Overview
Biofloc Technology represents an alternative approach to water quality management using microbial flocs as both biological filter and supplemental feed. While traditional aquaponics and BFT differ fundamentally, understanding BFT principles can inform hybrid approaches and intensive fish production strategies.

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand biofloc formation and composition
- Recognize C:N ratio management in BFT systems
- Evaluate BFT advantages and challenges
- Compare BFT with traditional aquaponics
- Apply biofloc principles selectively in aquaponic contexts

---

## What is Biofloc Technology?

### Definition

**Biofloc Technology (BFT):** An intensive aquaculture system where water exchange is minimized and waste nutrients are converted into microbial biomass (flocs) that remain in the water as suspended aggregates, serving both water treatment and supplemental feed functions.

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║         TRADITIONAL AQUAPONICS vs BIOFLOC TECHNOLOGY                      ║
╠═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                           ║
║  TRADITIONAL AQUAPONICS:                                                  ║
║  ──────────────────────────                                               ║
║  Fish → Waste (NH3) → Biofilter (attached biofilm) → NO3 → Plants        ║
║                                                                           ║
║  • Biofilm on media surfaces                                              ║
║  • Plants remove nitrate                                                  ║
║  • Water recirculation with minimal loss                                  ║
║  • Balanced fish:plant ratio critical                                     ║
║                                                                           ║
║  ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════        ║
║                                                                           ║
║  BIOFLOC TECHNOLOGY:                                                      ║
║  ─────────────────────                                                    ║
║  Fish → Waste (NH3) → Flocs (suspended biofilm) → Microbial protein      ║
║                          ↓                                ↓               ║
║                    Ammonia assimilation          Fish eat flocs           ║
║                    by heterotrophs               (supplemental feed)      ║
║                                                                           ║
║  • Suspended microbial aggregates                                         ║
║  • Minimal or NO water exchange                                           ║
║  • NO plants (typically)                                                  ║
║  • Flocs serve as biofilter AND feed                                      ║
║  • Very high stocking densities possible                                  ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Biofloc Composition and Structure

### What is a Biofloc?

```
Biofloc Aggregate (micro-scale):

    ╭─────────────────────────╮
    │  ● Bacteria              │
    │  ◐ Algae                 │
    │  ▲ Protozoa              │
    │  ◊ Detritus              │
    │  ░ EPS Matrix            │
    │                          │
    │   ●◐●                    │
    │  ░ ● ▲ ░                 │
    │ ░● ◊ ● ● ░               │
    │  ░ ● ●◐░                 │
    │   ░░ ●░                  │
    ╰─────────────────────────╯

Size: 50-1000+ μm
Density: Settles slowly (target: 10-15 mL/L)
```

### Typical Composition

| Component | % of Biofloc (dry weight) |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Bacteria** | 30-40% |
| **Algae** (if sunlight exposure) | 10-30% |
| **Protozoa** | 5-15% |
| **Detritus** (dead cells, feces, uneaten feed) | 20-40% |
| **EPS (extracellular polymeric substances)** | Variable, permeates floc |

**Nutritional Value:**
- Protein: 25-45% (dry basis)
- Lipids: 1-10%
- Vitamins and minerals
- Digestible by many fish species (tilapia, shrimp especially)

## Biofloc Formation and Management

### C:N Ratio Control (CRITICAL)

**Principle:** Add carbon source to stimulate heterotrophic bacteria, which assimilate ammonia into biomass.

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║              C:N RATIO MANAGEMENT IN BFT                                  ║
╠═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                           ║
║  WITHOUT CARBON ADDITION (C:N ~8:1 from feed):                            ║
║  ────────────────────────────────────────────────                         ║
║  NH4+ → Autotrophic nitrifiers → NO2- → NO3-                              ║
║       (slow, 8-24 hr doubling time)                                       ║
║                                                                           ║
║  Result: Ammonia accumulation, requires water exchange                    ║
║                                                                           ║
║  ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════        ║
║                                                                           ║
║  WITH CARBON ADDITION (C:N ~12-20:1):                                     ║
║  ───────────────────────────────────────────                              ║
║  NH4+ + Carbon → Heterotrophic bacteria → Microbial biomass               ║
║                  (fast, 20 min doubling time!)                            ║
║                                                                           ║
║  Result: Rapid ammonia assimilation, minimal accumulation                 ║
║                                                                           ║
║  Heterotrophs OUTCOMPETE nitrifiers due to:                               ║
║  • Faster growth rate                                                     ║
║  • Lower oxygen requirement per N assimilated                             ║
║  • Carbon availability                                                    ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### Carbon Source Selection

| Carbon Source | C:N Ratio | Application Rate | Pros | Cons |
|--------------|----------|------------------|------|------|
| **Molasses** | ~400:1 | 1-2 kg per kg feed | Cheap, available, fast | Can lower pH, messy |
| **Dextrose/Glucose** | ~400:1 | 1-2 kg per kg feed | Clean, fast uptake | Expensive |
| **Glycerol** | ~700:1 | Adjust accordingly | Readily available | Varies in purity |
| **Starch/Flour** | ~300:1 | 1-2 kg per kg feed | Cheap, slow release | Can cloud water |
| **Tapioca/Cassava** | ~300:1 | 1-2 kg per kg feed | Slow release, cheap | Varies in quality |

### C:N Ratio Calculation and Carbon Addition

```
Step 1: Estimate Nitrogen Input
N (kg/day) = Feed (kg/day) × Protein % × 0.16 × 0.25
             (Assuming 25% of N is excreted as ammonia)

Example:
10 kg feed/day, 32% protein
N = 10 × 0.32 × 0.16 × 0.25 = 0.128 kg N/day

Step 2: Calculate Carbon Need for Target C:N
Target C:N = 12:1 (conservative) to 20:1 (aggressive)

Carbon needed = N × Target C:N
For 12:1: 0.128 × 12 = 1.54 kg C/day

Step 3: Calculate Carbon Source Amount
If using molasses (40% carbon):
Molasses = 1.54 kg C / 0.40 = 3.85 kg molasses/day

Step 4: Application
• Dissolve in water
• Add slowly throughout day (or split 2-3x)
• Monitor ammonia (should stay <1 mg/L)
```

## Critical BFT Parameters

### 1. Mixing/Aeration

**Purpose:**
- Keep flocs suspended (prevent settling and anaerobic zones)
- Provide dissolved oxygen (high demand!)
- Ensure homogeneous distribution

**Requirements:**
- Continuous vigorous aeration
- Air stones, paddlewheels, or blowers
- DO maintained >5 mg/L minimum (>6 mg/L preferred)

**Energy Cost:** HIGH (major operating expense)

---

### 2. Biofloc Density (Volume)

**Measurement:** Imhoff cone or graduated cylinder

```
Protocol:
1. Collect 1-liter sample
2. Allow to settle for 30 minutes
3. Read settled volume (mL/L)

Target: 10-15 mL/L (1-1.5%)
Too low (<5 mL/L): Insufficient biofloc, poor ammonia control
Too high (>20 mL/L): Excessive solids, gill clogging risk, DO demand
```

**Management:**
- If too high: Remove excess (harvesting device or partial water exchange)
- If too low: Add carbon, increase feeding, be patient (flocs build over time)

---

### 3. Alkalinity

**Challenge:** Heterotrophic processes and any residual nitrification consume alkalinity

**Management:**
- Target: 150-200 mg/L as CaCO3
- Add buffer as needed (calcium carbonate, sodium bicarbonate)
- Monitor pH (target 7.0-8.5)

---

### 4. Nutrient Balance

**Unlike aquaponics:**
- Phosphorus accumulates (no plant uptake)
- Trace minerals accumulate
- Occasional water exchange may be needed (10-20% monthly) to purge

## BFT System Operation

### Startup Protocol

```
Weeks 1-2: Biofloc Development
├─ Stock fish at low density (20-50% of target)
├─ Begin feeding lightly
├─ Add carbon source daily (C:N 12:1)
├─ Maintain vigorous aeration
├─ Monitor ammonia daily
├─ Expect ammonia spike (Week 1-2)
└─ Flocs begin forming (brown water appearance)

Weeks 3-4: Floc Maturation
├─ Ammonia declines as flocs establish
├─ Nitrite may spike briefly (some nitrification)
├─ Biofloc volume increases (monitor)
├─ Gradually increase feeding
└─ Can increase stocking density

Weeks 5-6: Stable Operation
├─ Ammonia <1 mg/L
├─ Biofloc volume 10-15 mL/L
├─ Full feeding rate
├─ Full stocking density
└─ Routine monitoring and carbon addition
```

---

### Routine Monitoring

| Parameter | Frequency | Target |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | Continuous or 3x/day | >5 mg/L |
| **Ammonia** | Daily | <1 mg/L |
| **Nitrite** | 2-3x/week | <1 mg/L |
| **Biofloc volume** | 2-3x/week | 10-15 mL/L |
| **pH** | Daily | 7.0-8.5 |
| **Alkalinity** | Weekly | 150-200 mg/L |
| **Temperature** | Daily | Species-specific |

## Advantages of BFT

1. **Minimal Water Use:**
   - Little to no daily exchange (biosecurity)
   - Water conservation (critical in arid regions)

2. **High Stocking Densities:**
   - 50-300 kg/m³ possible (vs 20-50 kg/m³ in RAS)
   - Intensive production in small footprint

3. **Supplemental Feed:**
   - Fish consume biofloc (reduce feed costs 10-30%)
   - Improved fish nutrition (vitamins, minerals, probiotics)

4. **Improved Disease Resistance:**
   - Probiotic effect of beneficial bacteria
   - Competitive exclusion of pathogens

5. **Nutrient Efficiency:**
   - Rapid nutrient cycling (heterotrophs fast)
   - Reduced ammonia spikes

6. **Biosecurity:**
   - Minimal water exchange = less pathogen introduction risk

---

## Challenges of BFT

1. **Energy Intensive:**
   - Continuous vigorous aeration (high electricity cost)
   - Mixing to keep flocs suspended

2. **Management Complexity:**
   - Daily carbon addition calculations
   - Multiple parameter monitoring
   - Fine-tuning required

3. **Solid Accumulation:**
   - Flocs must be managed (can't build indefinitely)
   - Periodic harvesting needed

4. **Not Suitable for All Species:**
   - Best: Tilapia, shrimp, some catfish
   - Poor: Trout (don't consume biofloc well, require high DO)

5. **Nitrate Accumulation (if nitrification occurs):**
   - Toxic at high levels (>200-300 mg/L for some species)
   - May require water exchange eventually

6. **Inconsistent Results:**
   - High variability in biofloc composition
   - Requires experience to master

7. **No Plant Production:**
   - Traditional BFT is fish-only
   - Defeats purpose of aquaponics!

## BFT and Aquaponics: Compatibility?

### Hybrid Approaches

**1. Biofloc-Assisted Aquaponics:**
```
Concept: Use some carbon addition to boost heterotrophs, but maintain plants

Partial C:N manipulation:
• Target C:N ~10:1 (lower than pure BFT)
• Some heterotrophic ammonia assimilation
• Some autotrophic nitrification for plants
• Reduced biofloc density (5-10 mL/L)

Potential benefits:
• Faster ammonia processing
• Supplemental fish feed (modest)
• Improved system stability

Challenges:
• Balancing carbon addition (not too much)
• Ensuring adequate nitrate for plants
• Increased solids management
```

**2. BFT Fish Rearing + Aquaponic Finishing:**
```
Concept: Raise fish intensively in BFT, transfer to aquaponics for finishing

Protocol:
1. Raise fingerlings to juvenile size in BFT (8-12 weeks)
   • High density, fast growth
   • Minimal water use
2. Transfer to aquaponic system for grow-out
   • Cleaner environment (less solids)
   • Nitrate production for plants
   • Lower density, lower stress

Benefits:
• BFT nursery: intensive, efficient
• Aquaponics finishing: plant production, marketable product

Operational:
• Two separate systems
• Careful acclimation during transfer
```

**3. Biofloc Side Stream:**
```
Concept: Maintain small BFT reactor, harvest flocs as fish feed supplement

Setup:
• Small tank (5-10% of fish tank volume)
• Intensive aeration, carbon addition
• Produce biofloc
• Harvest and feed to fish in main aquaponic system

Benefits:
• Protein supplementation (reduce feed cost)
• Probiotics for fish gut health
• Main system remains traditional aquaponics

Complexity: Moderate (two systems to manage)
```

## BFT Research Findings

**Tilapia:**
- Excellent performance in BFT
- Consume biofloc readily
- Growth rates comparable to traditional systems
- Improved immune parameters

**Shrimp:**
- Widely used commercially
- 20-30% reduction in feed costs
- Higher survival in some studies
- Major application in Asia, Latin America

**Catfish:**
- Moderate success
- Less avid biofloc consumers than tilapia
- Still benefit from water quality improvement

**Trout/Salmon:**
- Generally unsuitable
- High DO requirements (difficult to maintain with high biofloc)
- Don't consume biofloc effectively

## Practical Recommendations

### When to Consider BFT

**Strong Candidates:**
- Regions with severe water scarcity
- Biosecurity-critical operations (breeding stock)
- Intensive fish production (no plants desired)
- Species that consume biofloc well (tilapia, shrimp)
- High-value markets (offset energy costs)

**Poor Candidates:**
- Plant production is primary goal (aquaponics better)
- Limited energy budget (aeration cost prohibitive)
- Cold-water species (trout)
- Simplicity desired (BFT is complex)

---

### Incorporating BFT Principles into Aquaponics

**Selective Application:**

1. **Modest Carbon Addition (Trial Basis):**
   - Start conservative (C:N ~8:1, just above natural)
   - Monitor nitrate levels (ensure plants still get adequate NO3)
   - Observe fish health, plant growth
   - Adjust based on results

2. **Enhanced Microbial Activity:**
   - Focus on diverse microbial communities
   - Use probiotic-rich bioflocs as fish feed (if producing separately)
   - Recognize heterotrophs' role alongside nitrifiers

3. **Solids Management:**
   - Learn from BFT suspended solids management
   - Balance between removal (clarity) and retention (microbial food/fish feed)

**Caution:** Don't compromise plant production for BFT benefits. Aquaponics = fish + plants. BFT = fish + flocs.

## Summary

Biofloc Technology offers an alternative paradigm to traditional biofiltration, with both opportunities and challenges for aquaponic practitioners:

**Key Takeaways:**
1. BFT uses suspended heterotrophic flocs for ammonia assimilation and supplemental feed
2. Carbon addition (C:N 12-20:1) is critical to shift microbial community
3. Very high stocking densities and minimal water exchange possible
4. Energy-intensive (continuous vigorous aeration required)
5. Best suited for tilapia, shrimp; unsuitable for trout
6. Traditional BFT incompatible with plant production (no nitrate generation)
7. Hybrid approaches possible but complex (biofloc-assisted aquaponics, split systems)
8. Understanding BFT principles valuable for comprehending microbial dynamics
9. Selective incorporation of BFT ideas (modest carbon addition, probiotic flocs) may benefit some aquaponic systems
10. If plants are priority, traditional aquaponics superior to BFT
