# Module 3: Biofilm Science

## Overview
Biofilms are structured microbial communities encased in self-produced polymers attached to surfaces. Understanding biofilm formation, structure, and management is crucial for optimizing biofilter performance and preventing problems in aquaponic systems.

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand biofilm formation stages and structure
- Recognize the roles of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS)
- Manage biofilm thickness for optimal performance
- Prevent and address biofilm-related problems
- Apply biofilm knowledge to system design

---

## What is a Biofilm?

### Definition and Characteristics

**Biofilm:** A structured community of microorganisms embedded in a self-produced matrix of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS), attached to a surface.

**Key Characteristics:**
- 90-97% water content
- 3-10% microbial cells
- 50-90% EPS matrix
- Highly organized architecture
- 100-1000x more resistant to antimicrobials than planktonic cells
- Protected microenvironment

## Biofilm Formation Stages

### Stage 1: Conditioning (Minutes to Hours)

**Process:**
- Clean surface exposed to water
- Organic molecules adsorb to surface
- Proteins, polysaccharides, humic acids accumulate
- Surface properties change (charge, hydrophobicity)

**Key Events:**
- No living organisms yet
- Purely physical/chemical process
- Creates favorable surface for bacterial attachment
- Occurs within seconds to hours

**Management Implications:**
- Cannot prevent conditioning (it's instantaneous)
- Media pre-treatment doesn't prevent it
- Surface chemistry matters (rough > smooth for attachment)

---

### Stage 2: Reversible Attachment (Hours to Days)

```
Bacterial Approach and Initial Attachment:

Free-swimming bacterium
       ○
      ╱│╲    Flagella propel toward surface
     ╱ │ ╲   (chemotaxis to nutrients)
        │
        ▼
    ~~~~~○~~~~~  Weak attachment
    ~~~~~~~~~~~  (van der Waals forces)
    ~~~~~~~~~~~  Can still swim away
    ═══════════  Conditioned surface
```

**Characteristics:**
- Bacteria land on surface via swimming or drift
- Weak bonding (electrostatic, van der Waals forces)
- Cells can still detach and swim away
- "Trial period" - testing if surface is suitable
- 1-24 hours typically

**Factors Favoring Attachment:**
- Rough surfaces (more anchoring points)
- Low flow areas (easier to stick)
- Nutrient availability
- Optimal temperature and pH
- Right bacterial species (some attach better)

---

### Stage 3: Irreversible Attachment (Days)

```
Permanent Adhesion:

   ○  Cell produces adhesins
  ╱│╲ (pili, fimbriae, adhesive proteins)
   │  ↓
  ═○═══  Firmly anchored
  ═════  EPS production begins
  ═════  Cannot easily detach
```

**Characteristics:**
- Bacteria produce adhesion structures (pili, fimbriae)
- Covalent bonding to surface
- Begin producing EPS (slime)
- Phenotype changes (different genes expressed)
- Point of no return

**Timeline:** 1-3 days after initial attachment

---

### Stage 4: Microcolony Formation (Days to Weeks)

```
Colony Growth:

    ○           ○  ○          ○● ○●          ○●●●○
    ↓            ↓  ↓           ↓ ↓            ╱ ╲
    ○  →      ○  ○  ○  →   ○● ○● ○●  →   ○●●●●●●●○
   ═══        ═══════       ═══════       ═══════════

  Single    Division     Microcolony    Mature colony
   cell     begins       (2-5 days)     (1-2 weeks)

● = cells embedded in EPS
```

**Characteristics:**
- Bacterial cell division on surface
- EPS production increases dramatically
- Microcolonies of 10-1000 cells form
- Spatial organization begins
- Water channels develop
- 3-14 days

**EPS Functions:**
- Structural scaffold (holds cells in place)
- Protection from predators (protozoa can't penetrate)
- Protection from antimicrobials and UV
- Water retention (prevents desiccation)
- Nutrient concentration (absorbs and holds nutrients)
- Horizontal gene transfer facilitation

- Maturation: 4-8 weeks in aquaponics

**Functional Zones:**
1. **Surface (aerobic):** Active nitrification, heterotrophs
2. **Middle:** Transition zone, moderate activity
3. **Base (anaerobic):** Denitrification, dead cells, storage

**Why Dispersal Happens:**
- Nutrient depletion in biofilm core
- Accumulation of waste products
- Quorum sensing signals ("too crowded")
- Physical shear from water flow
- Predation pressure

**Dispersal Benefits:**
- Colonize new surfaces
- Prevent excessive biofilm thickness
- Remove dead cells
- Maintain active surface layer
- System-wide distribution of bacteria

**Management Consideration:** Some dispersal is healthy and natural. Excessive dispersal (cloudy water) indicates stress or overloading.

## Extracellular Polymeric Substances (EPS)

### Composition

**Typical EPS composition:**
- 40-95% Polysaccharides (complex sugars)
- 1-60% Proteins
- 1-40% Nucleic acids (DNA/RNA)
- <1-10% Lipids
- Humic substances
- Water (up to 97% of total biofilm mass)

## Biofilm Thickness Management

### Optimal Thickness for Nitrification

**Too Thin (<100 μm):**
- Insufficient biomass
- Low overall nitrification capacity
- Vulnerable to disturbances
- Easily removed by predation or flow

**Optimal (200-400 μm):**
- High surface area to volume ratio
- Active outer layers well-oxygenated
- Efficient nutrient diffusion
- Stable but not excessive

**Too Thick (>1000 μm):**
- Oxygen-limited deep layers (anaerobic)
- Nutrient diffusion limited
- Accumulation of dead cells
- Risk of sloughing events
- Potential for sulfide production (H2S)
- Reduced flow through media

### Controlling Biofilm Thickness

**Passive Methods (Design):**

1. **Flow Rate:**
   - Higher flow = thinner biofilm (shear)
   - Lower flow = thicker biofilm
   - Optimal: 2-4 gpm per sq ft media surface
   - Turbulent flow > laminar for control

2. **Media Selection:**
   - Smooth media = thinner, easier detachment
   - Rough media = thicker, more stable
   - Choose based on application

3. **Predation:**
   - Protozoa graze on biofilm surface
   - Keeps biofilm "trimmed"
   - Natural control mechanism
   - Reduced in systems with UV or ozone

**Active Methods (Management):**

1. **Mechanical Cleaning:**
   - Backwashing (if designed for it)
   - Gentle media rinsing (not recommended for clay media)
   - Media tumbling in MBBR (self-cleaning)
   - Frequency: Only when clogging observed

2. **Hydraulic Cleaning:**
   - Increase flow temporarily
   - Flush media beds
   - Intermittent operation (flood/drain)

3. **Biological Control:**
   - Maintain protozoan populations
   - Avoid sterilization (UV, ozone) if possible
   - Diverse microbial community self-regulates

## Biofilm in Different Filter Types

### Media Bed Biofilters

**Characteristics:**
- Static media (gravel, LECA, clay)
- Thick biofilm (400-1000 μm)
- Stable, mature communities
- Low disturbance

**Management:**
- Minimal cleaning (never scrub!)
- Monitor flow rates (clogging indicator)
- Gentle backwash only if designed for it
- Protect during system changes

**Advantages:**
- Very stable nitrification
- High diversity
- Simple operation

**Disadvantages:**
- Can clog with solids
- Slow to establish
- Hard to clean if needed

---

### Moving Bed Bioreactors (MBBR)

```
MBBR Biofilm Control:

   ○  ○  ○     Carriers collide
    ╲ │ ╱      Biofilm abraded to optimal thickness
     ╲│╱       Self-regulating
      ○
   ═══════

Optimal biofilm: 100-300 μm (thin)
Continuously renewed
High activity
```

**Characteristics:**
- Mobile carriers (K1, K3, etc.)
- Thin, highly active biofilm (100-300 μm)
- Self-cleaning through abrasion
- Continuous mixing

**Management:**
- No cleaning needed
- Monitor carrier movement (ensure mixing)
- Observe biofilm color (brown = healthy)
- Replace carriers if damaged

**Advantages:**
- Self-regulating thickness
- No clogging
- Easy to monitor
- High nitrification rates

**Disadvantages:**
- Energy for mixing
- Noisy
- Media cost

---

### Trickling Filters

**Characteristics:**
- Exposed to air (wet/dry cycles)
- Thin biofilm (200-400 μm)
- Natural sloughing from drying
- High oxygen exposure

**Management:**
- Ensure even water distribution
- Prevent complete drying
- Monitor for biofilm buildup
- Clean distribution system

**Advantages:**
- Self-limiting thickness (drying)
- Excellent oxygenation
- Natural biofilm control

**Disadvantages:**
- Evaporative cooling
- Can dry out if flow stops
- Seasonal management (cold weather)

## Biofilm-Related Problems

### Problem 1: Excessive Biofilm (Clogging)

**Symptoms:**
- Reduced flow through media
- Water backs up or overflows
- Increased head loss (pumps work harder)
- Anaerobic odors (H2S smell)
- Dead zones in filter

**Causes:**
- Overfeeding (excess organics)
- Insufficient flow rate
- Lack of mechanical pre-filtration
- Accumulated solids
- No predation (sterile system)

**Solutions:**
- Reduce feeding rate
- Increase flow (if possible)
- Add mechanical filter upstream
- Backwash (if designed) or gentle rinse
- Remove solids from system
- Increase protozoan activity (inoculate from pond/stream)

---

### Problem 2: Biofilm Sloughing Events

**Symptoms:**
- Sudden cloudy water (white/brown)
- Chunks of biofilm visible
- Temporary ammonia spike
- Turbidity increase

**Causes:**
- Sudden environmental change (temp, pH, flow)
- Toxic shock (chemicals, chlorine)
- Excessive biofilm thickness
- Starvation (low feeding after high feeding)
- Power outage (oxygen depletion)

**Solutions:**
- Identify and remove cause
- Increase aeration
- Reduce feeding
- Monitor water chemistry closely
- Be patient (biofilm will regrow in 1-2 weeks)
- Mechanical filtration to remove sloughed material

---

### Problem 3: Insufficient Biofilm

**Symptoms:**
- Persistent ammonia despite adequate surface area
- Clear media (no brown/tan film visible)
- New system or recent disturbance

**Causes:**
- System too new (allow 4-8 weeks)
- Toxic exposure (chlorine, antibiotics)
- Insufficient nutrients (underfeeding)
- Extreme pH or temperature
- UV or ozone exposure

**Solutions:**
- Patience if new system
- Seed with established media
- Verify water is dechlorinated
- Check and optimize pH, temp, DO
- Feed lightly but consistently
- Protect from UV/ozone

---

### Problem 4: Anaerobic Zones (H2S Production)

**Symptoms:**
- Rotten egg smell (hydrogen sulfide)
- Black deposits on media
- Gray or black water
- Fish respiratory distress

**Causes:**
- Excessive biofilm thickness
- Low dissolved oxygen
- Stagnant zones (poor circulation)
- Accumulated organic matter

**Solutions:**
- Increase aeration dramatically
- Improve water circulation
- Clean/backwash filters
- Reduce feeding
- Check for dead zones in plumbing
- Remove accumulated sediment

## Advanced Topics

### Quorum Sensing in Biofilms

**What is quorum sensing?**
- Bacterial "communication" via chemical signals
- Autoinducers (signaling molecules) released by cells
- Concentration-dependent (high density = high signal)
- Coordinates group behaviors

**Quorum-Sensing Regulated Behaviors:**
- Biofilm formation (EPS production)
- Dispersal events
- Virulence factor production
- Bioluminescence
- Antibiotic production

**Implications for Aquaponics:**
- Biofilm self-regulates its structure
- Dispersal is coordinated (not random)
- Pathogen biofilms may be more virulent
- Potential future management tool (quorum quenching)

---

### Biofilm Heterogeneity

**Spatial Heterogeneity:**
- Different species in different zones
- Chemical gradients (O2, pH, nutrients)
- Distinct microenvironments within millimeters

**Example in Nitrifying Biofilm:**
```
Surface:     Nitrobacter (NOB), heterotrophs
             ↓ O2 = 4-6 mg/L, pH 7.5

Mid-layer:   Nitrosomonas (AOB), facultative anaerobes
             ↓ O2 = 1-3 mg/L, pH 7.2

Base:        Denitrifiers, sulfate reducers
             ↓ O2 < 0.5 mg/L, pH 7.0
```

**Management Implication:** A single biofilm supports multiple beneficial processes simultaneously (nitrification, denitrification, decomposition).

---

### eDNA in Biofilms

**Extracellular DNA (eDNA):**
- Released from lysed cells
- Structural component of EPS
- Facilitates horizontal gene transfer
- Nutrient source (P, N)

**Functions:**
- Strengthens biofilm structure
- Gene exchange between species
- Chelates cations (protective)

**Relevance:** Biofilms are genetic exchange "hotspots" - beneficial genes (antibiotic resistance, degradation pathways) spread rapidly.

## Biofilm Management Best Practices

### Do's:

- **Do** allow 4-8 weeks for mature biofilm establishment
- **Do** maintain stable environmental conditions (temp, pH, flow)
- **Do** provide consistent nutrient input (regular feeding)
- **Do** optimize DO, pH, temperature for nitrifiers
- **Do** use mechanical pre-filtration (reduce organics to biofilter)
- **Do** monitor flow rates as clogging indicator
- **Do** protect biofilters during system changes
- **Do** expect and allow natural dispersal (slight turbidity)

### Don'ts:

- **Don't** clean biofilter media unnecessarily
- **Don't** use chlorinated water
- **Don't** expose biofilters to UV or ozone
- **Don't** allow complete drying (except trickling filters)
- **Don't** make sudden environmental changes
- **Don't** use antibiotics in main system
- **Don't** scrub media (destroys biofilm)
- **Don't** expect instant biofilm establishment

## Monitoring Biofilm Health

### Visual Indicators:

| Observation | Meaning | Action |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| **Thin brown/tan film** | Healthy, active | None needed |
| **Thick brown film** | Mature, possibly excessive | Monitor flow rates |
| **Black deposits** | Anaerobic (sulfide) | Increase DO, reduce organics |
| **White/cream color** | High heterotroph activity | Reduce organics |
| **Green tint** | Algae growing | Reduce light |
| **No visible biofilm** | Insufficient or new | Patience, seed, optimize conditions |
| **Slimy, detaching** | Sloughing event | Identify cause, increase aeration |

---

### Functional Indicators:

- **Ammonia removal:** <0.5 mg/L (effective biofilm)
- **Nitrite removal:** <0.5 mg/L (mature NOB population)
- **Stable pH:** Buffered by biofilm microenvironments
- **Clear water:** Protozoa controlling planktonic bacteria
- **No odor:** Aerobic biofilm dominance

## Summary

Biofilms are the functional units of biological filtration in aquaponics:

**Key Points:**
1. Biofilms form in 6 stages: conditioning, reversible attachment, irreversible attachment, microcolony formation, maturation, dispersal
2. EPS matrix is the majority of biofilm mass and provides structure, protection, and nutrient concentration
3. Optimal biofilm thickness: 200-400 μm for maximum nitrification
4. Different filter designs produce different biofilm characteristics
5. Biofilm management is about optimization, not elimination
6. Protect biofilms from disturbances and sudden changes
7. Mature, diverse biofilms are resilient and efficient
