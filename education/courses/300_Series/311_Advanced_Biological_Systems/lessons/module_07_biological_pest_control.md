# Module 7: Biological Pest Control

## Overview
Biological pest control uses living organisms to suppress pest populations, reducing reliance on chemical pesticides. This module covers beneficial insects, natural enemies, and integrated pest management strategies for aquaponic greenhouses.

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify major beneficial insects and their target pests
- Implement biological control programs effectively
- Understand predator-prey dynamics and establishment
- Integrate biological control with cultural and physical methods
- Monitor and evaluate biological control success

---

## Principles of Biological Pest Control

### What is Biological Control?

**Definition:** The use of living organisms (natural enemies) to reduce pest populations to economically acceptable levels.

**Three Main Types:**

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║              TYPES OF BIOLOGICAL CONTROL                                  ║
╠═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                           ║
║  1. CLASSICAL BIOLOGICAL CONTROL (Importation)                            ║
║     • Import natural enemy from pest's native range                       ║
║     • Establish permanent population                                      ║
║     • One-time release (self-sustaining)                                  ║
║     • Example: Vedalia beetle for cottony cushion scale                   ║
║     • Rarely used in aquaponics (better for outdoor/invasive species)     ║
║                                                                           ║
║  2. AUGMENTATIVE BIOLOGICAL CONTROL (Most relevant)                       ║
║     • Periodic releases of commercially produced beneficials              ║
║     • Supplement existing populations                                     ║
║     • May or may not establish permanently                                ║
║     • Example: Weekly releases of parasitic wasps                         ║
║     • PRIMARY METHOD for greenhouse aquaponics                            ║
║                                                                           ║
║  3. CONSERVATION BIOLOGICAL CONTROL                                       ║
║     • Protect and enhance existing natural enemies                        ║
║     • Provide habitat, food, shelter                                      ║
║     • Avoid harmful pesticides                                            ║
║     • Example: Flowering plants for parasitoid nectar                     ║
║     • COMPLEMENTARY strategy in aquaponics                                ║
║                                                                           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

## Major Beneficial Insects

### 1. Ladybugs (Lady Beetles) - Coccinellidae

**Target Pests:** Aphids (primary), scale, mealybugs, mites

**Common Species:**
- *Hippodamia convergens* (Convergent lady beetle) - Most commercially available
- *Cryptolaemus montrouzieri* (Mealybug destroyer)
- *Stethorus punctillum* (Spider mite destroyer)

**Life Cycle:**
```
Egg (yellow, oval, clusters) → Larva (alligator-like, 2-3 weeks) →
Pupa (attached to leaf) → Adult (red/orange with spots)

Complete cycle: 4-6 weeks
Adult lifespan: 2-3 months
Prey consumption: 50-60 aphids/day (adult), 400+ aphids (larva lifetime)
```

**Release Strategy:**
```
Rate: 1,500-5,000 per 1,000 sq ft (depending on infestation)
Timing: Evening (reduces flight)
Method: Place near aphid colonies
Frequency: One-time release (they tend to disperse)

CHALLENGE: Adults fly away after eating local aphids
SOLUTION: Use larvae (less mobile) or create banker plants
```

---

### 2. Green Lacewings - Chrysopidae

**Target Pests:** Aphids, thrips, whiteflies, mealybugs, small caterpillars, spider mites

**Species:** *Chrysoperla** rufilabris,* *C. carnea*

**Life Cycle:**
```
Egg (on stalks) → Larva ("aphid lion", 2-3 weeks) →
Cocoon (7-10 days) → Adult (feeds on nectar/honeydew)

Complete cycle: 4-6 weeks
Larval prey consumption: 200-600 aphids over 2-3 weeks
```

**Why Excellent:**
- Larvae are voracious predators
- Wide prey range
- Adults stay if pollen/nectar available
- More likely to establish than ladybugs

**Release Strategy:**
```
Rate: 1,000-5,000 eggs/larvae per 1,000 sq ft
Timing: Evening, any temperature >60°F
Method: Distribute eggs in plant canopy
Frequency: 2-3 releases, 1-2 weeks apart (preventive)
          Weekly releases (curative)
```

---

### 3. Parasitic Wasps (Several Families)

**For Aphids:**
- *Aphidius colemani* - Small aphids (green peach aphid)
- *Aphidius ervi* - Large aphids (potato aphid)
- *Aphelinus abdominalis* - Multiple aphid species

**For Whiteflies:**
- *Encarsia formosa* - Greenhouse whitefly (most effective)
- *Eretmocerus eremicus* - Silverleaf whitefly, sweetpotato whitefly

**For Thrips:**
- *Amblyseius cucumeris* (predatory mite, not wasp, but similar use)
- *Orius insidiosus* (minute pirate bug - predator)

**Parasitoid Biology:**
```
Adult wasp finds host insect → Lays egg inside/on host →
Larva develops inside host (host dies) → Mummy forms (parasitized aphid/whitefly) →
Adult wasp emerges from mummy

Host dies: Larva consumes host from inside
Mummy: Dead, parasitized pest (brown/black for aphids, black scale for whiteflies)
```

**Release Strategy (Aphidius example):**
```
Rate: 1-5 wasps per square foot per week
Timing: Early morning (wasps less active, easier to distribute)
Method: Release bottles/cards throughout canopy
Frequency: WEEKLY releases essential (short-lived adults)
Establishment: Can establish if prey present continuously

Monitor: Count mummies (parasitized aphids) to assess success
```

---

### 4. Predatory Mites - Phytoseiidae

**Target Pests:** Spider mites (primary), thrips

**Common Species:**
- *Phytoseiulus persimilis* - Two-spotted spider mite specialist
- *Neoseiulus californicus* - Generalist, multiple mite species
- *Amblyseius swirskii* - Whiteflies and thrips
- *Amblyseius cucumeris* - Thrips

**Biology:**
```
Egg → Larva → Protonymph → Deutonymph → Adult
Development: 5-7 days at 25°C
Lifespan: 4-6 weeks
Prey consumption: 5-20 spider mites/day

Movement: Crawls (does not fly), moves between plants via contact
```

**Release Strategy:**
```
Rate: 10-50 per plant (curative), 2-5 per plant (preventive)
Method: Sprinkle on infested leaves, direct contact needed
Frequency: 2-3 releases at 2-week intervals
Humidity: Requires >60% RH (dry conditions limit effectiveness)

EXCELLENT for long-term spider mite control
```

---

### 5. Rove Beetles - Staphylinidae

**Target Pests:** Fungus gnat larvae, shore fly larvae, thrips pupae, springtails

**Species:** *Dalotia coriaria* (formerly *Atheta coriaria*)

**Biology:**
```
Adult and larva both predatory
Habitat: Media surface, root zone
Life cycle: 3 weeks
Active hunters in growing media
```

**Release Strategy:**
```
Rate: 500-1,000 per 1,000 sq ft
Method: Distribute on media surface
Timing: Preventive (before fungus gnats establish)
Can establish permanent populations in greenhouses
```

---

### 6. Beneficial Nematodes - Steinernematidae, Heterorhabditidae

**Target Pests:** Fungus gnat larvae, thrips pupae (in soil), beetle larvae

**Species:**
- *Steinernema feltiae* - Fungus gnats, thrips
- *Heterorhabditis bacteriophora* - Beetle larvae

**Biology:**
```
Infective juvenile (IJ) stage → Enters host insect →
Releases symbiotic bacteria → Host dies (24-48 hrs) →
Nematodes reproduce inside host → New IJs emerge

Host death: Bacterial septicemia (rapid)
Host turns red/brown from bacterial infection
```

**Application:**
```
Rate: 50-250 million per 1,000 sq ft (varies by pest pressure)
Method: Drench media with nematode suspension
Conditions: Moist media essential, apply evening (UV-sensitive)
Temperature: Soil temp >50°F, <85°F optimal
Viability: Use immediately (short shelf life)
Reapplication: Every 2-4 weeks if pests persist
```

## Monitoring and Scouting

### Sticky Traps

**Yellow Sticky Traps:**
- Attract: Whiteflies, aphids, fungus gnats, leafminers
- Placement: Just above plant canopy, 1 per 100-200 sq ft
- Check: Weekly, count insects

**Blue Sticky Traps:**
- Attract: Thrips (preferentially)
- Use in combination with yellow

**Sticky Trap Monitoring:**
```
Count insects weekly → Plot trends → Determine action thresholds

Example Threshold:
Aphids: >5 per trap per week = Release parasitoids
Whiteflies: >1 per trap = Release Encarsia
Fungus gnats: >10 per trap = Apply nematodes
```

---

### Visual Inspection

**Weekly Scout Routine:**
1. Inspect 10-20% of plants (different plants each week)
2. Check undersides of leaves (most pests hide there)
3. Look for: Insects, eggs, damage, mummies (parasitized pests)
4. Record findings

**Signs of Pests:**
- Sticky honeydew (aphids, whiteflies)
- Stippling (spider mites)
- Distorted growth (aphids, thrips)
- Webbing (spider mites)
- Whiteflycast skins (whitefly)
- Silver trails (thrips feeding)

## Establishing Beneficial Populations

### Banker Plant Systems

**Concept:** Maintain a continuous population of prey (non-pest aphids) on non-crop plants to support beneficials.

```
Banker Plant System Example:

Barley or wheat plants → Infested with grain aphids (Rhopalosiphum padi)
                         (Don't attack vegetables)
                              ↓
                    Aphidius colemani released
                              ↓
                    Parasitoids reproduce on grain aphids
                              ↓
                    Continuous supply of wasps
                              ↓
                    Spread to crop when pests appear

Benefits:
• Continuous beneficial population
• No need for weekly releases
• Cost-effective long-term
• Rapid response to pest outbreaks

Maintenance:
• Replace banker plants every 6-8 weeks
• Monitor grain aphid levels (too many = problem)
• 1 banker plant per 50-100 sq ft
```

---

### Open Rearing Systems

**Concept:** Allow beneficials to reproduce freely in greenhouse

**Requirements:**
- Year-round prey availability (low pest levels tolerable)
- No broad-spectrum pesticides
- Suitable microclimate
- Supplemental nectar sources

**Examples:**
- Aphid parasitoids (will establish if aphids always present at low levels)
- Predatory mites (establish in strawberries, cucumbers)
- Lacewings (if pollen/nectar plants included)

## Biological Control Program Design

### Step 1: Identify Pests

**Common Aquaponic Greenhouse Pests:**
- Aphids (green peach, potato aphid)
- Whiteflies (greenhouse, sweet potato)
- Spider mites (two-spotted)
- Thrips (western flower, onion)
- Fungus gnats
- Caterpillars (occasional)

### Step 2: Select Beneficials

**Match beneficial to pest:**

| Pest | Primary Beneficial | Secondary Beneficial |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Aphids** | Aphidius parasitoids | Lacewings, ladybugs |
| **Whiteflies** | Encarsia parasitoids | Delphastus beetles |
| **Spider mites** | Phytoseiulus predatory mites | Neoseiulus mites |
| **Thrips** | Amblyseius cucumeris mites | Orius minute pirate bugs |
| **Fungus gnats** | Beneficial nematodes | Rove beetles (Dalotia) |

### Step 3: Determine Release Rates

**Preventive Program (Low/No Pests):**
- Lower rates
- Biweekly to monthly releases
- Goal: Establishment before pests arrive

**Curative Program (Active Infestation):**
- Higher rates
- Weekly releases
- Goal: Knock down populations quickly

**Example: Aphidius for Aphids**
```
Preventive: 0.5-1 wasp/sq ft every 2 weeks
Curative:   2-5 wasps/sq ft weekly until control achieved
```

### Step 4: Create Release Schedule

**Sample Schedule (1,000 sq ft greenhouse):**

| Week | Aphidius (aphids) | Encarsia (whitefly) | Phytoseiulus (mites) | Nematodes (gnats) |
|------|------------------|--------------------|--------------------|------------------|
| 1 | 1,000 | 500 | 50/plant | - |
| 2 | - | - | - | 250M |
| 3 | 1,000 | 500 | - | - |
| 4 | - | - | - | - |
| 5 | 1,000 | 500 | - | 250M |
| 6 | - | - | Reapply if needed | - |

*Adjust based on monitoring results*

### Step 5: Monitor and Adjust

**Success Indicators:**
- Decreasing pest counts on traps
- Presence of mummies (parasitized pests)
- Reduced plant damage
- Presence of beneficial insects during scouting

**If Control Failing:**
- Increase release rates
- Release more frequently
- Add complementary beneficials
- Check environmental conditions
- Consider cultural controls

## Environmental Optimization for Beneficials

### Temperature

| Beneficial | Optimal Temp Range | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|-------|
| **Aphidius** | 64-77°F (18-25°C) | Reduced activity <60°F |
| **Encarsia** | 68-80°F (20-27°C) | Requires warmth |
| **Phytoseiulus** | 70-80°F (21-27°C) | Slower at cool temps |
| **Lacewings** | 65-85°F (18-30°C) | Wide tolerance |
| **Nematodes** | 60-85°F soil (15-30°C) | Media temperature critical |

**Management:** Maintain consistent greenhouse temps within ranges

---

### Humidity

**Most beneficials prefer moderate to high humidity:**
- Predatory mites: >60% RH required
- Lacewings: 50-70% RH ideal
- Parasitoids: 50-80% RH

**Too Low (<40% RH):**
- Predatory mites ineffective
- Pest mites thrive
- Shorter beneficial lifespans

**Too High (>90% RH):**
- Fungal disease risk (plants)
- Generally tolerated by beneficials

**Aquaponics Advantage:** Naturally higher humidity than soil greenhouses!

---

### Light

- Most beneficials unaffected by light levels
- Some parasitoids more active in bright light
- Avoid UV sanitation lights near beneficial release areas

---

## Compatibility with Pesticides

### Harmful to Beneficials

**AVOID in systems with biological control:**
- All synthetic insecticides (pyrethroids, organophosphates, neonicotinoids)
- Broad-spectrum organic pesticides (pyrethrin, rotenone)
- Many fungicides
- Insecticidal soaps (kills on contact)
- Horticultural oils (smothers)

### Safer Options (If Needed)

**Use with caution, still impacts beneficials:**
- Bt (*Bacillus thuringiensis*) - Caterpillars only, some beneficial larva harm
- Spinosad - Moderately selective, best applied in evening after beneficials settle
- Neem oil - Repellent effect, some contact toxicity

**Best Practice:** SPOT TREAT infested plants only, avoid broadcast applications

## Conservation Biological Control

### Providing Nectar and Pollen

**Why:**
- Many beneficial adults feed on nectar/pollen
- Increases lifespan and fecundity
- Keeps beneficials in greenhouse

**Suitable Plants:**
- Alyssum (sweet alyssum) - Excellent for parasitoids
- Buckwheat - Attracts many beneficials
- Dill, fennel, cilantro (allowed to flower)
- Yarrow
- Marigold (non-pest repelling varieties)

**Integration:**
- Interplant among crops
- Dedicate small areas to insectary plants
- 5-10% of greenhouse area

---

### Shelter and Overwintering Sites

- Leave some plant debris in corners (ground beetles, spiders)
- Provide water sources (shallow dishes with stones)
- Create refuges (bundles of hollow stems)

---

## Trouble shooting Biological Control Failures

### Problem: Beneficials Don't Establish

**Possible Causes:**
- Pesticide exposure (check for residues)
- Extreme temperatures
- Low humidity
- Insufficient prey (nothing for them to eat)
- Poor quality beneficial insects (check supplier)

**Solutions:**
- Verify environmental conditions
- Avoid ALL pesticides 2 weeks before and after releases
- Ensure adequate pest presence (beneficials need food!)
- Switch suppliers if repeated failures

---

### Problem: Pests Increase Despite Releases

**Possible Causes:**
- Release rates too low
- Wrong beneficial for the pest
- Beneficials arriving too late (large pest population)
- Environmental conditions favor pests over beneficials

**Solutions:**
- Increase release rates 2-5x
- Add complementary beneficials
- Remove heavily infested plants
- Optimize environment for beneficials

---

## Economic Considerations

**Costs:**
- Initial investment moderate to high
- Ongoing releases: $50-500/month (1,000 sq ft, depends on program)
- Labor for monitoring and releases

**Benefits:**
- Reduced pesticide costs
- No pesticide application labor
- Safer for workers and consumers
- Premium pricing potential (organic, pesticide-free)
- Long-term sustainability

**Break-Even:**
- Usually within 1-2 seasons for commercial operations
- Hobby growers: More about values than economics

## Summary

Biological pest control is highly compatible with aquaponic systems:

**Key Takeaways:**
1. Use augmentative biological control (periodic releases) as primary strategy
2. Match beneficials to specific pests (no one-size-fits-all)
3. Monitor weekly with traps and visual scouting
4. Preventive releases more cost-effective than curative
5. Banker plants and conservation support long-term establishment
6. Environmental conditions critical (temp, humidity) for success
7. NEVER use broad-spectrum pesticides in biocontrol programs
8. Combine biological control with cultural methods (IPM pyramid)
9. Patience required - biocontrol slower than pesticides but sustainable
10. Aquaponics naturally suited to biocontrol (high humidity, enclosed environment)
