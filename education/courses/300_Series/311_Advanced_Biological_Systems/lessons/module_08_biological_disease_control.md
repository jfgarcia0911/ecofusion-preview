# Module 8: Biological Disease Control

## Overview
Plant diseases caused by fungi, bacteria, and oomycetes can devastate aquaponic crops. Biological control using beneficial microorganisms offers a sustainable alternative to fungicides, particularly important in integrated aquaponic systems where chemical use must be minimized.

**Duration:** 1 hour
**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify common plant pathogens in aquaponics
- Understand mechanisms of biological disease control
- Apply biocontrol agents effectively
- Implement disease-suppressive management practices
- Integrate biological control with cultural disease management

---

## Common Plant Diseases in Aquaponics

### Water-Borne Diseases (Most Problematic)

**1. Pythium spp. (Water Mold/Root Rot)**
- **Type:** Oomycete (not true fungus)
- **Symptoms:** Brown, mushy roots; wilting; stunted growth; damping-off in seedlings
- **Conditions:** Low oxygen, warm water (>24°C), waterlogged conditions
- **Spread:** Swimming zoospores through water

**2. Phytophthora spp. (Root and Crown Rot)**
- **Type:** Oomycete
- **Symptoms:** Similar to Pythium, plus crown rot, blackening
- **Conditions:** Similar to Pythium
- **Spread:** Water-borne zoospores

**3. Fusarium spp. (Wilt)**
- **Type:** True fungus
- **Symptoms:** Wilting, yellowing (one-sided often), vascular browning
- **Conditions:** Warm conditions, stressed plants
- **Spread:** Media, water, infected transplants

### Foliar Diseases

**4. Powdery Mildew** (*Oidium*, *Erysiphe*, *Sphaerotheca*)
- **Symptoms:** White powdery coating on leaves
- **Conditions:** High humidity, moderate temps, poor air circulation

**5. Gray Mold (Botrytis)**
- **Symptoms:** Gray fuzzy growth on dead/dying tissue, spreads to healthy tissue
- **Conditions:** Cool, humid, poor circulation

**6. Bacterial Leaf Spots** (*Pseudomonas*, *Xanthomonas*)
- **Symptoms:** Water-soaked spots, yellowing halos
- **Conditions:** High humidity, overhead watering, wounds

## Mechanisms of Biological Disease Control

```

╠═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                           ║
║  1. COMPETITION                                                           ║
║     Biocontrol agent outcompetes pathogen for:                            ║
║     • Nutrients                                                           ║
║     • Space (colonization sites)                                          ║
║     • Oxygen                                                              ║
║     Example: Trichoderma rapidly colonizes root zone, excludes Pythium    ║
║                                                                           ║
║  2. ANTIBIOSIS (Antibiotic Production)                                    ║
║     Biocontrol agent produces compounds toxic to pathogens:               ║
║     • Antibiotics                                                         ║

║     • Volatile compounds                                                  ║

║                                                                           ║

║     Biocontrol fungus attacks and consumes pathogen:                      ║
║     • Coiling around pathogen hyphae                                      ║

║     • Direct penetration and digestion                                    ║
║     Example: Trichoderma attacks Fusarium, Pythium, Rhizoctonia           ║
║                                                                           ║
║  4. INDUCED SYSTEMIC RESISTANCE (ISR)                                     ║
║     Biocontrol agent triggers plant's own defenses:                       ║

║     • Primed immune response                                              ║
║     Example: PGPR trigger jasmonic acid pathway, broad disease resistance ║
║                                                                           ║
║  5. PROMOTION OF PLANT VIGOR                                              ║
║     Healthier plants = better disease resistance:                         ║
║     • Enhanced nutrient uptake                                            ║
║     • Root growth stimulation                                             ║
║     • Stress tolerance                                                    ║
║     Example: Mycorrhizae improve P uptake, overall plant health           ║
║                                                                           ║
```

## Biocontrol Agents

### 1. Trichoderma spp. (Fungi)

**Species:**
- *Trichoderma harzianum*
- *Trichoderma viride*
- *Trichoderma atroviride*

**Mechanisms:**
- Mycoparasitism (attacks other fungi directly)
- Competition for nutrients and space
- Antibiotic production
- ISR induction
- Root growth promotion

**Target Pathogens:**
- Pythium, Phytophthora
- Fusarium, Rhizoctonia
- Botrytis (foliar)
- Sclerotinia

**Application:**
```
Transplant Treatment:
• Dip seedling roots in Trichoderma suspension (10^6-10^7 spores/mL)
• 15-30 minutes contact time
• Transplant immediately

Media Amendment:
• Mix Trichoderma into media before planting
• Or apply to planting hole

Root Drench:
• Apply to established plants monthly (preventive)
• Drench around root zone
• Ensure media moisture for spore germination

Effective at: 15-30°C, pH 4-8 (wide tolerance)
```

**Commercial Products:**
- RootShield (T. harzianum)
- T-22 (T. harzianum strain KRL-AG2)
- Trianum (T. harzianum)

---

### 2. Bacillus spp. (Bacteria)

**Species:**
- *Bacillus subtilis*
- *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens*
- *Bacillus pumilus*
- *Bacillus licheniformis*

**Mechanisms:**
- Antibiotic production (lipopeptides: surfactin, iturin, fengycin, bacillomycin)
- ISR induction
- Competition
- Biofilm formation on roots (protective barrier)

**Target Pathogens:**
- Pythium, Phytophthora (moderate efficacy)
- Fusarium
- Bacterial leaf spots
- Powdery mildew (some strains)
- Gray mold (Botrytis)

**Application:**
```
Seed Treatment:
• Soak seeds in Bacillus suspension before germination

Transplant Dip:
• Similar to Trichoderma

Root Drench:
• Weekly to monthly applications (10^7-10^9 CFU/mL)
• Preventive and curative

Foliar Spray:
• Dilute suspension, apply to leaves
• Early morning or evening
• For powdery mildew, bacterial spots

Effective at: 10-40°C (very wide range), pH 5-8
```

**Commercial Products:**
- Serenade (B. subtilis QST 713)
- Cease (B. subtilis)
- Companion (B. subtilis GB03)

**Advantage:** Spore-forming (very stable, long shelf life)

---

### 3. Pseudomonas spp. (Bacteria)

**Species:**
- *Pseudomonas fluorescens*
- *Pseudomonas putida*
- *Pseudomonas chlororaphis*

**Mechanisms:**
- Siderophore production (iron competition, starves pathogens)
- Antibiotic production (phenazines, pyrrolnitrin, pyoluteorin)
- ISR induction
- Biofilm formation

**Target Pathogens:**
- Pythium, Fusarium
- Bacterial pathogens (competition)
- Gray mold

**Application:**
```
Similar to Bacillus, but:
• Non-spore-forming (less stable)
• Requires refrigeration (many products)
• Higher viability needed (10^8-10^9 CFU/mL)
```

**Commercial Products:**
- Bio-Save (P. syringae - for post-harvest, not growing crops)
- SoilGard (P. fluorescens)

---

### 4. Streptomyces spp. (Actinobacteria)

**Species:**
- *Streptomyces griseoviridis*
- *Streptomyces lydicus*

**Mechanisms:**
- Diverse antibiotic production
- Competition
- Mycoparasitism (some strains)

**Target Pathogens:**
- Fusarium, Rhizoctonia
- Pythium (moderate)
- Alternaria, Botrytis

**Application:**
```
Media Amendment (best):
• Mix into media at planting
• Colonizes and persists well

Root Drench:
• Less common but possible

Effective at: 15-30°C, neutral pH
```

**Commercial Products:**
- Mycostop (S. griseoviridis)
- Actinovate (S. lydicus)

---

### 5. Non-Pathogenic Fusarium (Biological Vaccine)

**Concept:** Inoculate with non-pathogenic Fusarium strains to outcompete and induce resistance to pathogenic strains.

**Example:** *Fusarium oxysporum* (non-pathogenic strain Fo47)

**Application:** Research stage, limited commercial availability

---

### 6. Beneficial Oomycetes

**Example:** *Pythium oligandrum*

**Mechanism:**
- Mycoparasitism (attacks other Pythium species)
- ISR induction

**Target:** Pythium spp., Phytophthora

**Application:** Available in some regions (Polyversum)

## Cultural Disease Management

### 1. Environmental Control

**Dissolved Oxygen:**
- **Target:** >5 mg/L in root zones
- **Benefit:** Inhibits Pythium and Phytophthora (anaerobic preference)
- **Method:** Increase aeration, improve circulation

**Temperature:**
- **Root Zone:** Keep <24°C (75°F) if possible
- **Benefit:** Slows Pythium growth (optimal for pathogen 25-30°C)
- **Method:** Insulate tanks, shade, evaporative cooling

**Humidity:**
- **Foliar:** 50-70% (not >85% continuously)
- **Benefit:** Reduces powdery mildew, Botrytis spores
- **Method:** Ventilation, dehumidification

**Air Circulation:**
- **Target:** Gentle, continuous airflow
- **Benefit:** Dries leaf surfaces, reduces fungal spore settlement
- **Method:** Fans (not direct blast on plants)

---

### 2. Sanitation

**Strict Hygiene Protocol:**
```
✓ Remove dead/diseased plant material IMMEDIATELY
✓ Disinfect tools between plants (70% alcohol, 10% bleach solution)
✓ Clean hands between plants when scouting
✓ Quarantine new transplants (2-week observation period)
✓ Remove plant debris from growing areas
✓ Sterilize pots/trays between uses
✓ Use pathogen-free media and water sources
✓ Dispose of diseased plants (do NOT compost in system)
```

---

### 3. Plant Spacing

- **Adequate spacing:** Improves air circulation
- **Reduces:** Humidity between plants, pathogen spread
- **Aquaponics Challenge:** High-density planting is common (trade-off)
- **Solution:** Prioritize airflow even at slight density reduction

---

### 4. Resistant Varieties

**When Available, Use:**
- Fusarium-resistant tomatoes (many modern hybrids)
- Pythium-resistant lettuces (select breeding)
- Powdery mildew-resistant cucumbers

**Research:** New varieties continuously developed

## Disease-Specific Biocontrol Strategies

### Pythium/Phytophthora Root Rot

**Biocontrol Approach:**
```
Prevention (Best):
1. Trichoderma application at transplant (root dip + media drench)
2. Bacillus subtilis root drench weekly for first 4 weeks
3. Maintain DO >5 mg/L always
4. Keep water temperature <24°C

Early Symptoms Detected:
1. Isolate affected plants if possible
2. Increase Trichoderma application (drench 2x per week)
3. Apply hydrogen peroxide to affected roots (30 sec dip, 3% solution)
4. Drastically increase DO (>8 mg/L)
5. Consider removing severely affected plants

Established Infection:
• Remove affected plants
• Apply Trichoderma to surrounding plants (preventive)
• Review environmental conditions
• May need temporary H2O2 water treatment (be cautious with biofilter!)
```

---

### Fusarium Wilt

**Biocontrol Approach:**
```
Prevention:
1. Use pathogen-free transplants (critical!)
2. Trichoderma at transplant
3. Monthly Bacillus drench
4. Avoid plant stress (optimal nutrition, temperature)

Symptoms Appear:
1. REMOVE infected plant immediately (vascular disease spreads)
2. Do NOT compost in system
3. Disinfect area where plant was growing
4. Apply biocontrol to neighboring plants
5. No cure once vascular system infected

Long-term:
• Use resistant varieties (tomatoes especially)
• Rotate crop types if possible
• Media sterilization between crops (if severe recurring problem)
```

---

### Powdery Mildew

**Biocontrol Approach:**
```
Prevention:
1. Lower humidity (<70%)
2. Improve air circulation
3. Avoid leaf wetness
4. Bacillus foliar spray weekly (preventive)

Early Infection:
1. Bacillus or Trichoderma foliar spray (2-3x per week)
2. Increase air movement around plants
3. Prune heavily infected leaves
4. Milk spray (10% milk in water, 1x per week) - proven effective!
5. Sulfur dusts (if biocontrol insufficient and allowable)

Advanced Infection:
• May need conventional fungicides (if valuable crop)
• Evaluate if disease resistance too low in variety
```

---

### Gray Mold (Botrytis)

**Biocontrol Approach:**
```
Prevention:
1. Remove all dead/dying plant material immediately
2. Avoid overhead watering
3. Lower humidity (<70%)
4. Air circulation critical
5. Bacillus foliar spray weekly

Infection Present:
1. Remove all infected tissue (be generous, beyond visible infection)
2. Bacillus or Trichoderma spray remaining tissue
3. Dramatically improve air circulation
4. Lower humidity (<60% if possible)
5. Avoid plant stress (Botrytis is opportunistic)
```

## Combining Biocontrol Agents

### Compatible Combinations

**Trichoderma + Bacillus:**
- Complementary mechanisms
- Can apply together or sequentially
- Broader spectrum of disease control

**Multiple Bacillus Strains:**
- Different strains produce different antibiotics
- Synergistic effects possible
- Commercial products often multi-strain

**PGPR + Disease Biocontrol:**
- Azospirillum (growth) + Bacillus (disease control)
- Complementary benefits

### Incompatible Combinations (Avoid)

**Trichoderma + Fungicides:**
- Most fungicides kill Trichoderma
- Wait 2 weeks after fungicide before applying Trichoderma

**Any Biocontrol + Broad-Spectrum Antimicrobials:**
- Copper fungicides: Kill bacteria (Bacillus, Pseudomonas)
- Hydrogen peroxide: Kills all microbes
- Chlorine: Kills everything

## Monitoring Disease and Biocontrol Effectiveness

### Disease Scouting Routine

**Weekly:**
1. Inspect 10-20% of plants (rotate which plants)
2. Check roots (if accessible): Color, firmness, smell
3. Check leaves: Spots, powdery coating, wilting
4. Check stems: Cankers, discoloration
5. Record findings and locations

**Disease Incidence Calculation:**
```
Disease Incidence = (Number of infected plants / Total plants inspected) × 100%

Track over time to evaluate biocontrol program effectiveness
```

---

### Biocontrol Success Indicators

**Positive Signs:**
- Decreasing disease incidence over time
- No new infections appearing
- Colonization of roots by biocontrol agents (Trichoderma visible with magnification)
- Healthy, vigorous plant growth

**Negative Signs:**
- Increasing disease despite biocontrol
- Biocontrol agents not establishing (environmental issue?)
- Continued plant losses

**If Biocontrol Failing:**
1. Re-evaluate environmental conditions (DO, temp, humidity)
2. Increase application frequency/concentration
3. Try different biocontrol species/strain
4. Combine multiple biocontrol agents
5. Improve cultural practices (sanitation, spacing)
6. As last resort: Consider fungicides (avoid if possible in aquaponics)

## Biocontrol in Different Aquaponic Systems

### Media Beds

**Advantages:**
- Solid substrate for microbial colonization
- Biocontrol agents establish well
- Can amend media with agents

**Application:**
- Drench applications effective
- Media incorporation at planting

---

### DWC (Deep Water Culture)

**Challenges:**
- No solid substrate
- Biocontrol must colonize roots in water
- Free-floating microbes wash away

**Strategies:**
- Frequent applications (weekly)
- Focus on root dip at transplant
- Maintain optimal DO (limits Pythium)

---

### NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)

**Challenges:**
- Similar to DWC
- Thin water film limits biocontrol establishment

**Strategies:**
- Root dip critical
- Biweekly applications
- Consider media in channels (allows biocontrol colonization)

## Summary

Biological disease control is essential in aquaponic systems where chemical fungicide use is problematic:

**Key Takeaways:**
1. Prevention vastly superior to cure (focus on environmental optimization)
2. Trichoderma and Bacillus are workhorses of biocontrol in aquaponics
3. Apply biocontrol agents preventively at transplant (most effective timing)
4. Combine biocontrol with cultural practices (sanitation, air flow, DO)
5. Multiple mechanisms of action provide broader disease suppression
6. Environmental control (especially DO and temperature) is critical for root diseases
7. Pythium/Phytophthora are primary concerns in aquaponic systems
8. Biocontrol slower than fungicides but sustainable and safe
9. Monitor disease incidence to evaluate biocontrol effectiveness
10. No single solution - integrated approach essential
