# Module 15: Material Selection

## Introduction

Material selection impacts system durability, safety, cost, and regulatory compliance. Aquaponic environments are corrosive—constant moisture, biological activity, and nutrient salts accelerate degradation. This module covers material properties, compatibility testing, and specification for long-term reliability.

**Duration:** 1 hour

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Evaluate material properties for aquaponic applications
2. Assess corrosion resistance and chemical compatibility
3. Apply food safety standards (NSF, FDA)
4. Select appropriate plastics, metals, and coatings
5. Specify gaskets, seals, and adhesives
6. Plan for material lifespan and replacement cycles

---

## 1. Material Properties

### 1.1 Key Selection Criteria

| Property | Importance | Test Method |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Corrosion resistance | Critical | ASTM B117 (salt spray) |
| Chemical resistance | Critical | ASTM D543 (immersion) |
| UV stability | High (outdoor) | ASTM G154 (weathering) |
| Food safety | Critical | NSF-61, FDA CFR 177 |
| Mechanical strength | High | ASTM D638 (tensile) |
| Thermal stability | Medium | ASTM D648 (heat deflection) |
| Cost | High | Supplier quotes |

### 1.2 Water Chemistry Considerations

```
Typical Aquaponic Water:
pH: 6.5-7.5
TDS: 300-800 ppm
Nitrate: 50-200 ppm
Temperature: 60-80°F
Dissolved oxygen: 5-8 ppm

Corrosive factors:
- Nitric acid formation (from nitrification)
- Chlorides (if using salt)
- Biofilm formation
- Oxygen + moisture (rust)
```

### 2.2 Plastic Compatibility

```
Compatibility with Common Chemicals:

Material | Nitric Acid | Sulfuric Acid | NaOH | H₂O₂ | Ozone
---------|-------------|---------------|------|-------|-------
HDPE     | Good        | Excellent     | Good | Fair  | Poor
PVC      | Fair        | Good          | Good | Fair  | Poor
PP       | Good        | Excellent     | Excellent | Good | Fair
PTFE     | Excellent   | Excellent     | Excellent | Excellent | Good

Good = No degradation after 1 year
Fair = <5% degradation after 1 year
Poor = >10% degradation or failure
```

## 3. Metals for Aquaponics

### 3.1 Stainless Steel

**Grade Selection:**
```
304 Stainless: General purpose, low chloride
- Composition: 18% Cr, 8% Ni
- Corrosion: Good in freshwater
- Cost: $$

316 Stainless: Marine grade, high chloride resistance
- Composition: 18% Cr, 10% Ni, 2% Mo
- Corrosion: Excellent in all aquaponic conditions
- Cost: $$$

Recommendation: Use 316 for:
- Pump shafts
- Fasteners in wet areas
- Food contact surfaces
- Saltwater systems

Use 304 for:
- Structural members (dry areas)
- Equipment frames
- Wire mesh (with coating)
```

### 3.2 Galvanized Steel

```
Hot-Dip Galvanized (HDG):
Zinc coating: 1.8-3.0 oz/ft²
Lifespan: 10-25 years in aquaponic environments

Concerns:
- New galvanizing: Zinc leaching (toxic to fish)
- Solution: Age coating 6 months or seal with epoxy
- pH <6.5: Accelerated zinc dissolution

Applications (with precautions):
- Greenhouse frames (not in contact with water)
- Outdoor structures
- Electrical conduit
```

### 3.3 Aluminum

```
Alloy 6061-T6:
- Excellent corrosion resistance
- Lightweight (1/3 weight of steel)
- Non-toxic to fish
- Good for structures and frameworks

Concerns:
- Lower strength than steel (use larger sections)
- Galvanic corrosion with dissimilar metals
- Avoid direct contact with copper, brass

Protection:
- Anodizing (increases corrosion resistance)
- Powder coating (color + protection)
- Isolate from dissimilar metals with plastic washers
```

## 4. Coatings and Sealants

### 4.1 Protective Coatings

| Coating Type | Substrate | Thickness | Lifespan | Cost |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|----------|------|
| Epoxy (2-part) | Steel, concrete | 10-20 mils | 10-20 years | $$$ |
| Polyurethane | Steel, wood | 5-10 mils | 5-10 years | $$ |
| Powder coat | Aluminum, steel | 2-4 mils | 15-25 years | $$ |
| Plastisol | Steel | 20-40 mils | 20-30 years | $$$ |

**Epoxy Pool Paint (Common Choice):**
```
Application: Interior tank coating, concrete sealing
Preparation: Critical for adhesion
1. Clean surface (pressure wash)
2. Etch concrete (muriatic acid)
3. Neutralize and rinse
4. Dry completely (24-48 hours)
5. Prime if necessary
6. Apply 2-3 coats (24 hr between coats)
7. Cure 7-10 days before water contact

Coverage: 200-300 ft²/gallon
Cost: $50-80/gallon
```

### 4.2 Sealants and Adhesives

**Silicone:**
```
100% Silicone (NOT silicone II with mildewcide):
- NSF-51 rated (food equipment)
- Flexible, waterproof
- UV resistant
- Temperature range: -60°F to 400°F

Applications:
- Tank seams
- Bulkhead installations
- Pipe penetrations

Cure time: 24 hours (full cure 7 days)
```

**PVC Cement:**
```
Types:
- Regular: General purpose, medium-bodied
- Heavy-duty: Thick, gap filling
- Rain-R-Shine: Sets underwater

Application method:
1. Cut pipe square
2. Deburr edges
3. Dry fit (mark alignment)
4. Apply primer to both surfaces
5. Apply cement to both surfaces
6. Insert and twist 1/4 turn
7. Hold 30 seconds
8. Wait 2 hours before pressure test
9. Wait 24 hours for full cure

Health & Safety:
- Use in ventilated area
- Wear gloves
- Avoid skin contact
- Keep away from heat sources
```

## 5. Gaskets and Seals

### 5.1 Gasket Materials

| Material | Temp Range | Chemical Resistance | Applications | Cost |
|----------|------------|---------------------|--------------|------|
| **EPDM** | -40°F to 250°F | Excellent (water, ozone) | General purpose | $ |
| **Viton (FKM)** | -20°F to 400°F | Excellent (acids, oils) | Chemical exposure | $$$ |
| **Silicone** | -100°F to 450°F | Good (food safe) | Food contact | $$ |
| **Neoprene** | -40°F to 250°F | Good (water, ozone) | General purpose | $ |

**Selection Guide:**
```
Bulkhead fittings: EPDM or Silicone
Pump seals: Viton (if available)
Pipe flanges: EPDM
Food contact: Silicone (NSF-51)
High temp: Viton or Silicone
```

### 5.2 O-Ring Sizing

```
O-Ring Groove Design:
Groove width = O-ring diameter × 1.5
Groove depth = O-ring diameter × 0.7

Example: 2mm O-ring cross-section
Groove width = 2 × 1.5 = 3mm
Groove depth = 2 × 0.7 = 1.4mm

Compression: 15-25% of cross-section
Too much = accelerated wear
Too little = leakage
```

## 6. Food Safety Compliance

### 6.1 NSF Standards

**NSF-61: Drinking Water Components**
```
Applies to:
- Pipes and fittings
- Tanks and liners
- Valves
- Gaskets and seals

Testing requirements:
- Heavy metal leaching
- VOC emissions
- Taste and odor

Look for NSF-61 marking on products
```

**NSF-51: Food Equipment Materials**
```
Applies to:
- Direct food contact surfaces
- Growing containers
- Processing equipment

Requirements:
- Non-toxic
- Corrosion resistant
- Cleanable
- Durable
```

### 6.2 FDA Regulations

```
FDA CFR Title 21:
Part 177: Indirect Food Additives (plastics)
Part 178: Indirect Food Additives (coatings)

FDA-compliant plastics:
- HDPE
- PVC (properly formulated)
- Polypropylene (PP)
- PET (polyethylene terephthalate)

Verify with material data sheets (MDS)
```

## Summary

Material selection is critical for long-term system reliability:

1. Prioritize corrosion resistance and food safety
2. Use food-grade plastics (HDPE, PVC, PP)
3. Select appropriate metals (316 SS preferred)
4. Apply proper coatings and sealants
5. Choose compatible gaskets and seals
6. Ensure NSF/FDA compliance
7. Plan for material lifespan and replacement

Investing in quality materials prevents costly failures and ensures food safety.
