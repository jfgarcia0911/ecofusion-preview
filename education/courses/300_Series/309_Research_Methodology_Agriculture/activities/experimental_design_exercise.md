## Part 2: Critique Flawed Designs (20 minutes)

### Flawed Design #1

**Scenario:** A student wants to compare 4 fish feed brands on tilapia growth.

**Their Design:**
- 1 large tank (2000L)
- 100 tilapia divided equally: 25 fish per feed brand
- Feeds assigned to quadrants of the tank (NE = Feed A, SE = Feed B, SW = Feed C, NW = Feed D)
- Fish weighed individually at start and after 8 weeks
- Plans to use one-way ANOVA to compare 4 groups (n=25 per group)

**Questions:**

1. **What is the major design flaw?** (Hint: Think about experimental units)

2. **Is this pseudoreplication? Why or why not?**

3. **What is confounded with treatment?**

4. **How should this design be corrected?**

---

### Flawed Design #2

**Scenario:** Comparing three LED light spectrums on basil growth.

**Their Design:**
- Spectrum A tested in Spring (March-April)
- Spectrum B tested in Summer (June-July)
- Spectrum C tested in Fall (September-October)
- Same greenhouse, same system
- 30 plants per spectrum
- Statistical test: One-way ANOVA comparing the three spectrums

**Questions:**

1. **What is confounded with treatment?**

2. **Why is this a problem?**

3. **How could this design be improved?**

4. **Is the sample size adequate? Show calculation.**

### Flawed Design #3

**Scenario:** Testing if automated pH control improves plant growth.

**Their Design:**
- Two greenhouses (A and B)
- Greenhouse A: Install automated pH control
- Greenhouse B: Manual pH control (current method)
- 100 plants in each greenhouse
- Measure yield after 6 weeks
- Statistical test: t-test comparing two greenhouses (n=100 per group)

**Questions:**

1. **What is the sample size (n) for this study?**
- [ ] n=2 (two greenhouses)

- [ ] n=200 (total plants)

2. **Is this pseudoreplication? Explain.**

## Answer Key / Example Solutions

### Flawed Design #1 - Pseudoreplication

**Answers:**

1. **Major flaw:** Pseudoreplication - treating 100 fish as independent units when they all share one tank

2. **Pseudoreplication:** YES. The experimental unit is the tank, not individual fish. There is only n=1 per treatment (one quadrant), not n=25.

3. **Confounded:** Tank location (quadrant position) is confounded with feed type. Differences could be due to feed OR position effects (water flow, light, etc.)

4. **Correction:** Use 4 separate tanks per feed brand (4 feeds × 4 tanks = 16 tanks total) with 6-7 fish per tank. Randomize feed assignment to tanks. Then n=4 per treatment.

---

### Flawed Design #2 - Temporal Confounding

**Answers:**

1. **Confounded:** Season (and associated factors: temperature, day length, solar radiation)

2. **Problem:** Cannot separate spectrum effect from seasonal effects. Any differences could be due to spectrum OR season.

3. **Improvement:** Test all three spectrums simultaneously in one season. Randomize spectrum assignment to growing spaces within the greenhouse.

4. **Sample size:** If expecting medium effect (d=0.5) with 3 groups, need approximately 52 per group (from ANOVA table). Current n=30 is underpowered.

---

### Flawed Design #3 - Greenhouse-Level Pseudoreplication

**Answers:**

1. **Sample size:** n=2 (two greenhouses, not 100 plants per treatment)

2. **Pseudoreplication:** YES. The experimental unit is the greenhouse (where treatment is applied), not individual plants. Plants within a greenhouse are not independent.

3. **Confounded:** Any greenhouse-specific factors (location, age, equipment, microclimate, management) are confounded with treatment.

4. **Redesign:** Use multiple smaller systems (e.g., 10 systems, randomly assign 5 to automated and 5 to manual). Or alternate automated/manual control within the same greenhouse across time periods with crossover design.
