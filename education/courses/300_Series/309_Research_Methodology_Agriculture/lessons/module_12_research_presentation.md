## Oral Presentation Structure

### Standard Format (10-15 minutes)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              PRESENTATION TIME ALLOCATION                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                     │
│  INTRODUCTION (10-15%)                1-2 minutes                   │
│  ├─ Hook/attention grabber                                         │
│  ├─ Background                                                     │
│  └─ Research question/objective                                    │
│                                                                     │
│  METHODS (15-20%)                     2-3 minutes                   │
│  ├─ Experimental design                                            │
│  ├─ Key procedures (not every detail)                             │
│  └─ Analysis approach                                              │
│                                                                     │
│  RESULTS (40-50%)                     5-7 minutes                   │
│  ├─ Main findings (2-4 key points)                                 │
│  ├─ Supporting data                                                │
│  └─ Visual presentation (graphs, tables)                           │
│                                                                     │
│  DISCUSSION/CONCLUSIONS (20-25%)      2-3 minutes                   │
│  ├─ Interpretation                                                 │
│  ├─ Implications                                                   │
│  ├─ Limitations                                                    │
│  └─ Future directions                                              │
│                                                                     │
│  QUESTIONS                            Variable                      │
│                                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Opening Slide

**Title Slide Should Include:**
- Presentation title
- Your name
- Affiliation
- Date
- Acknowledgments/funding (optional)

**Opening Statement Examples:**

**Hook:** "What if you could grow fish and vegetables using 90% less water than traditional farming?"

**Question:** "How do we optimize nutrient delivery in aquaponic systems?"

**Surprising fact:** "By 2050, we'll need 70% more food with 40% less water—aquaponics offers a solution."

### Introduction Slides

**Slide 1: Problem/Context**
- Why is this important?
- Current challenges
- 1-2 key statistics or facts

**Slide 2: Knowledge Gap**
- What's unknown?
- Limitations of previous work
- Visual: flowchart or timeline

**Slide 3: Research Objective**
- Clear statement of aim
- Hypothesis (if applicable)
- Preview of approach

### Methods Slides

**Focus on:**
- Experimental design diagram
- Key treatments or groups
- Sample sizes
- Main measurements

**Skip:**
- Detailed protocols
- Routine procedures
- Statistical formulas

**Visual:** System diagram, experimental layout, or timeline

### Results Slides

**One Finding Per Slide:**
- Clear title stating finding
- Supporting graph or table
- Minimal text (bullet points)

**Example Slide Title:**
"Medium Stocking Density Maximized Lettuce Yield"

**Graph showing means with error bars**
**Single bullet:** "20 kg/m³ produced 30% more yield than 10 kg/m³ (p<0.001)"

### Discussion/Conclusion Slides

**Interpretation:**
- Connect findings to research question
- Compare to literature
- Explain mechanisms

**Implications:**
- Practical applications
- Who benefits? How?

**Limitations:**
- 1-2 key limitations
- Be honest but brief

**Future Directions:**
- Next logical steps
- Unanswered questions

### Final Slide

**Not "Questions?"**

**Instead:**
- **Summary:** Key takeaways
- **Recommendations:** Actionable advice
- **Acknowledgments:** Funding, collaborators, assistance
- **Contact:** Email, website, QR code

**Keep visible during Q&A for reference**

## Slide Design Principles

### The 6×6 Rule

**Maximum:**
- 6 bullet points per slide
- 6 words per bullet point

**Better:** Fewer words, more visuals

### Font Guidelines

**Size:**
- Title: 36-44 pt
- Body text: 24-32 pt
- Captions: 18-20 pt

**Font:**
- Sans serif (Arial, Calibri, Helvetica)
- Consistent throughout
- Avoid decorative fonts

**Emphasis:**
- Bold for emphasis
- Avoid ALL CAPS
- Limit italics
- Underline only for hyperlinks

### Color Schemes

**Principles:**
- High contrast (text vs. background)
- Consistent palette (3-5 colors)
- Color-blind friendly
- Professional appearance

**Good Combinations:**
- Dark text on light background (best for lit rooms)
- Light text on dark background (better for dark rooms)

**Avoid:**
- Red/green combinations (color-blind issue)
- Neon colors
- Low contrast (gray on gray, yellow on white)

### Layout

**Consistency:**
- Same template throughout
- Align elements
- Balanced composition

**White Space:**
- Don't crowd slides
- Margins around edges
- Space between elements

**Visual Hierarchy:**
- Most important → largest/boldest
- Secondary info → smaller/lighter

### Graphs and Images

**Graphs:**
- Large enough to read from back of room
- Clear axis labels
- Remove gridlines if unnecessary
- Use color meaningfully

**Images:**
- High resolution (not pixelated)
- Relevant to content
- Proper attribution if not your own

**Tables:**
- Use sparingly (hard to read on slides)
- Maximum 3×5 (3 columns, 5 rows)
- Round numbers appropriately
- Highlight key values

## Research Poster Design

### Standard Size

**Most Common:**
- 48" × 36" (landscape)
- 36" × 48" (portrait)

**Check conference guidelines!**

### Poster Layout

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  TITLE (Large, clear - readable from 6 feet)                   │
│  Authors, Affiliations, Contact Info                           │
├─────────────┬─────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│             │             │                                    │
│ INTRODUCTION│   METHODS   │         RESULTS                    │
│             │             │                                    │
│ • Background│ • Design    │  [Graph 1]    [Graph 2]            │
│ • Objective │ • Procedure │                                    │
│ • Hypothesis│ • Analysis  │  [Table]      [Graph 3]            │
│             │             │                                    │
├─────────────┴─────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│                           │                                    │
│      DISCUSSION           │       CONCLUSIONS                  │
│                           │                                    │
│ • Interpretation          │  • Key findings (3-4 bullets)      │
│ • Comparison              │  • Practical implications          │
│ • Limitations             │  • Future work                     │
│                           │                                    │
├───────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┤
│  REFERENCES               ACKNOWLEDGMENTS          [QR Code]   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Design Principles

**Readability from Distance:**
- Title: 85-100 pt (readable from 20 feet)
- Section headers: 48-60 pt
- Body text: 24-32 pt (readable from 4 feet)
- Captions: 18-20 pt

**Visual Flow:**
- Left to right, top to bottom
- Or columnar (top to bottom in each column)
- Use arrows if flow isn't obvious
- Number sections if needed

**Color and Graphics:**
- Light background (less ink, easier to read)
- 40% text, 40% graphics, 20% white space
- 2-4 high-impact figures
- Avoid walls of text

**Practical Tips:**
- Print preview at 25% to see from distance
- Laminate or mount on foam board
- Bring push pins/Velcro
- Prepare 1-minute verbal summary

### Poster Content

**Introduction:**
- 1 paragraph (150-200 words)
- Problem, gap, objective

**Methods:**
- Bullets or short paragraphs
- Diagram of experimental design
- Key details only

**Results:**
- Primarily visual (graphs, images)
- Short captions
- 2-4 main findings

**Discussion:**
- Concise interpretation
- Connection to literature
- 1-2 paragraphs

**Conclusions:**
- 3-5 bullet points
- Take-home messages
- Practical applications

**References:**
- 5-10 key citations
- Small font (16-18 pt)
- Full citations or numbered system

## Presentation Delivery

### Before the Presentation

**Preparation:**
- Practice out loud (minimum 3 times)
- Time yourself
- Anticipate questions
- Prepare backup slides (for potential questions)
- Test equipment
- Arrive early

**Technical Check:**
- Laptop compatibility
- Remote clicker
- Video/animation playback
- Backup: USB drive, cloud, email to self

### During the Presentation

**Body Language:**
- Stand up straight
- Face audience (not screen)
- Make eye contact
- Use natural gestures
- Move purposefully (don't pace)
- Smile

**Voice:**
- Speak loudly and clearly
- Vary pace and tone
- Pause for emphasis
- Avoid filler words (um, uh, like)
- Breathe

**Slide Navigation:**
- Use remote pointer
- Don't read slides verbatim
- Elaborate on bullet points
- Refer to visuals: "As you can see in this graph..."
- Know when to advance

**Time Management:**
- Pace yourself (not too fast/slow)
- Check time discreetly
- Have a slide you can skip if running long
- End on time (better to finish early than run over)

### Handling Questions

**Listen Carefully:**
- Let person finish question
- Repeat question if audience can't hear
- Ask for clarification if needed

**Answer Strategies:**

**If You Know:**
- Answer directly and concisely
- Refer to relevant slide if helpful
- Connect to your data

**If You Don't Know:**
- "That's a great question. I don't have those data."
- "Interesting point. I'd need to look into that."
- "We didn't measure that in this study, but it would be worth investigating."
- Offer to discuss after or exchange contact info

**Difficult Questions:**
- Stay calm and professional
- Don't get defensive
- Acknowledge valid criticisms
- "You raise an important limitation..."

**Redirect:**
- If question is off-topic: "That's beyond the scope of this study, but happy to discuss afterward."
- If someone challenges methodology: "That was our rationale, but I'd be interested to hear your thoughts after."

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| **Too much text on slides** | Use bullet points, images, graphs |
| **Reading slides verbatim** | Elaborate, don't read |
| **Monotone delivery** | Vary voice, show enthusiasm |
| **Turning back to audience** | Face forward, use remote |
| **Running over time** | Practice, prioritize key points |
| **Apologizing for mistakes** | Move on confidently |
| **Saying "um" repeatedly** | Pause silently instead |
| **Rushing through results** | Results are the core—give them time |

## Adapting to Different Audiences

### Technical Audience (Researchers)

**Characteristics:**
- Familiar with methods
- Want details
- Critical evaluation

**Approach:**
- More methodology
- Statistical details
- Nuanced discussion
- Technical terminology OK

### General Audience (Public, Growers)

**Characteristics:**
- Varied backgrounds
- Practical interest
- Less statistical knowledge

**Approach:**
- Less methodology
- Simple statistics
- Emphasize implications
- Avoid jargon
- Use analogies

### Funder/Stakeholder Audience

**Characteristics:**
- ROI focused
- Practical outcomes
- Broader impact

**Approach:**
- Emphasize applications
- Economic benefits
- Societal impact
- Future potential

## Virtual Presentations

### Platform Preparation

**Technology:**
- Test platform beforehand
- Check audio/video
- Have phone number backup
- Close unnecessary applications
- Stable internet connection
- Mute notifications

**Environment:**
- Quiet space
- Good lighting (face lit, not backlit)
- Neutral background or virtual background
- Camera at eye level

### Engagement Strategies

**Without Visual Feedback:**
- Ask questions periodically
- Use polls if platform allows
- Encourage chat participation
- Check for questions frequently
- Record if permitted (for those who can't attend)

**Maintain Energy:**
- Even more important remotely
- Look at camera (not screen)
- Use animations sparingly
- Incorporate interaction

## Elevator Pitch

### 30-Second Summary

**Purpose:** Concisely explain research to anyone

**Structure:**
1. **Problem** (5-10 seconds)
2. **Your solution** (10-15 seconds)
3. **Impact** (5-10 seconds)

**Example:**

"Aquaponic growers struggle to balance fish stocking rates—too low and plants lack nutrients, too high and water quality suffers. We tested three stocking densities and found that 20 kilograms per cubic meter optimizes plant growth while maintaining healthy conditions for fish. This gives growers evidence-based guidelines to maximize production and profitability."

**Practice until natural!**

## Practical Exercise

### Create a Lightning Talk

**Assignment:**
Design a 3-minute presentation with 5 slides on your research project or a hypothetical study:

1. Title slide
2. Problem/objective
3. Methods (visual)
4. Key result (graph)
5. Conclusion/implications

**Constraints:**
- Maximum 5 slides
- No more than 3 bullets per slide
- At least one visual (graph, diagram, image)
- Practice and time yourself

## Key Takeaways

1. **Structure:** Introduction (problem/objective), Methods (brief), Results (focus here), Discussion/Conclusions
2. **Slides:** Minimal text, maximum clarity, one idea per slide
3. **Design:** High contrast, large fonts, consistent template, meaningful visuals
4. **Delivery:** Practice, maintain eye contact, speak clearly, manage time
5. **Posters:** Readable from distance, visual emphasis, clear organization
6. **Questions:** Listen, stay professional, admit unknowns honestly
7. **Audience:** Adapt depth and language to audience background
