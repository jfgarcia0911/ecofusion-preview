## Developing Research Questions

### From Observation to Hypothesis

**Step-by-Step Process:**

```
OBSERVATION
"Lettuce in north section grows slower than south section"
    ↓
QUESTION
"Why does growth differ by location?"
    ↓
BACKGROUND RESEARCH
Light levels: North = 300 μmol/m²/s, South = 450 μmol/m²/s
Literature: Optimal light for lettuce = 400-600 μmol/m²/s
    ↓
SPECIFIC QUESTION
"Does light intensity affect lettuce growth rate in aquaponic systems?"
    ↓
HYPOTHESIS
"Increasing light intensity from 300 to 450 μmol/m²/s will increase
lettuce growth rate by at least 20%"
    ↓
TESTABLE PREDICTION
"Lettuce grown under 450 μmol/m²/s will have higher fresh weight,
more leaves, and greater height than lettuce grown under 300 μmol/m²/s
after 4 weeks of growth"
```

### Characteristics of Good Research Questions

**FINER Criteria:**

- **F**easible: Adequate subjects, time, resources, expertise
- **I**nteresting: To investigator, community, field
- **N**ovel: Confirms, refutes, or extends previous work
- **E**thical: Approved by review board
- **R**elevant: Addresses important problem

### Example: Bad vs. Good Research Questions

**Too Broad:**
"How does nutrition affect plants?"

**Too Narrow:**
"Does 0.5 mg/L increase in nitrate affect leaf #7 width on 35-day-old Buttercrunch lettuce in 2 m² DWC systems?"

**Just Right:**
"How does nitrate concentration (10, 20, 40, 80 mg/L) affect lettuce yield and quality in deep water culture aquaponic systems?"

## Experimental Design Planning

### Design Selection Flowchart

```
How many factors?
│
├─ One factor
│  │
│  └─ Known sources of variation?
│     │
│     ├─ No → Completely Randomized Design (CRD)
│     │
│     ├─ One source → Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD)
│     │
│     └─ Two sources → Latin Square Design
│
└─ Two or more factors
   │
   └─ Study interactions?
      │
      ├─ Yes → Factorial Design
      │
      └─ Some factors hard to randomize → Split-Plot Design
```

### Sample Size Justification

**Include in Proposal:**

```
Sample size was determined by power analysis using G*Power software
(Faul et al., 2007). To detect a moderate effect size (Cohen's d = 0.5)
with 80% power at α = 0.05 (two-tailed), we require n = 64 per group.
Accounting for potential 10% attrition, we will recruit n = 71 per
treatment group (213 total plants across 3 treatments).

Power analysis assumptions:
- Effect size: d = 0.5 (based on pilot data showing 15g difference
  with SD = 30g)
- Power: 0.80 (standard)
- Significance level: α = 0.05
- Test: One-way ANOVA (3 groups)
```

### Preliminary/Pilot Data

**Why Include:**
- Demonstrates feasibility
- Justifies sample size calculations
- Shows variables can be measured
- Indicates promising results

**What to Include:**
- Small sample results (n=5-10)
- Variability estimates (SD, CV)
- Photos of system/organisms
- Proof of concept

**Example:**

```
"Preliminary data from 5 plants per treatment over 2 weeks showed
mean fresh weight of 45 ± 12g (low light) vs. 62 ± 15g (high light),
suggesting a detectable effect exists. These data were used to
estimate variability for power analysis."
```

## Methods Section for Proposals

### Level of Detail

**More detail than published papers:**
- Exact procedures
- Equipment specifications
- Replication justification
- Contingency plans

### Key Components

**1. Experimental Design**

```
This study will employ a 3×2 factorial randomized complete block design
with three nutrient levels (low, medium, high) and two light spectrums
(red, blue), with 4 replicate blocks (n=24 experimental units total).
Blocking by greenhouse section will account for spatial gradients in
temperature and natural light. Treatments will be randomly assigned
within each block using a random number generator.
```

**2. Study System/Organisms**

```
Six identical aquaponic systems (1000L fish tank, 2m² grow bed) will
be constructed at [Location]. Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
fingerlings (50±5g initial weight) will be obtained from [Supplier].
Buttercrunch lettuce (Lactuca sativa) seeds will be germinated in
rockwool cubes for 14 days before transplanting into systems at 20cm
spacing (10 plants per grow bed).
```

**3. Treatments**

```
Nutrient levels will be manipulated by varying fish feeding rates:
- Low: 1% body weight/day
- Medium: 2% body weight/day
- High: 3% body weight/day

Light spectrum treatments:
- Red: 660nm peak, 200 μmol/m²/s
- Blue: 450nm peak, 200 μmol/m²/s

All other parameters will be held constant: temperature (23±2°C),
photoperiod (16:8 LD), pH (6.5-7.0), and DO (>5 mg/L).
```

**4. Measurements**

```
Plant measurements (weekly):
- Height: base to apical meristem, ±0.5cm (digital caliper)
- Leaf count: fully expanded leaves >2cm
- SPAD: chlorophyll content (SPAD-502Plus meter)

Harvest measurements (week 6):
- Fresh weight: total above-ground biomass, ±0.1g (Scout Pro balance)
- Dry weight: 70°C for 48h until constant weight

Water quality (3×/week):
- pH, DO, EC, temperature: YSI ProDSS multiparameter
- TAN, NO₂-N, NO₃-N: Hach DR900 colorimeter

Fish measurements (biweekly):
- Individual weight: random sample of 10 fish per tank, ±1g
- Length: total length, ±1mm
- Condition: visual health assessment
```

**5. Statistical Analysis**

```
Data will be analyzed using R version 4.3.1. Two-way ANOVA will
evaluate main effects of nutrient level, light spectrum, and their
interaction on plant growth variables. Assumptions (normality,
homogeneity of variance) will be tested using Shapiro-Wilk and
Levene's tests. If assumptions are violated, data will be transformed
(log or square-root) or non-parametric alternatives will be used.
Post-hoc comparisons will employ Tukey's HSD test. Significance
will be set at α = 0.05. Results will be presented as mean ± SE.
```

**6. Data Management**

```
All data will be recorded on paper datasheets (see Appendix A) and
entered into Excel templates within 24 hours. Weekly backups will be
performed to university server and external hard drive. Raw data files
will be retained and never overwritten. All data processing steps will
be documented in R scripts for reproducibility.
```

## Proposal Review Criteria

### What Reviewers Look For

**Scientific Merit (40%):**
- Importance of research question
- Novelty and innovation
- Sound methodology
- Appropriate analysis plan

**Feasibility (30%):**
- Realistic timeline
- Adequate resources
- Investigator expertise
- Reasonable scope

**Impact (20%):**
- Contribution to knowledge
- Practical applications
- Broader benefits

**Budget (10%):**
- Reasonable costs
- Well-justified
- Cost-effective

### Common Reasons for Rejection

1. **Unclear hypothesis or objectives**
2. **Flawed experimental design**
3. **Insufficient sample size**
4. **Unrealistic timeline or budget**
5. **Lack of preliminary data**
6. **Poor writing (hard to understand)**
7. **Overambitious scope**
8. **Missing critical details**

## Executing a Pilot Study

### Purpose of Pilot Studies

**Goals:**
- Test feasibility
- Identify problems
- Refine protocols
- Estimate variability
- Train personnel
- Generate preliminary data

**NOT to:**
- Test hypotheses (yet)
- Produce publishable results (usually)

### Pilot Study Design

**Keep it simple:**
- Small sample size (n=5-10)
- Shorter duration
- Fewer variables
- Focus on procedures

**Example:**

```
PILOT: Testing pH Management Protocol

Objective: Verify that pH can be maintained at 6.5±0.3 using
proposed control system

Systems: 3 aquaponic systems (30 plants each)
Duration: 2 weeks
Measurements: pH (3×/day), tilapia health (daily), plant appearance
Budget: $500 (existing systems, supplies only)

Success criteria:
✓ pH remains 6.5±0.3 for >90% of measurements
✓ <5% fish mortality
✓ No visible plant stress
✓ Protocol can be completed in <30 min/day
```

### Learning from Pilots

**Document everything:**
- What worked?
- What didn't?
- Protocol changes needed?
- Unforeseen challenges?
- Equipment issues?
- Time required?

**Use to revise proposal:**
- Update methods based on lessons learned
- Refine timeline
- Adjust budget
- Include pilot results as preliminary data

## Presentation and Defense

### Final Presentation

**Format:** 15-minute presentation + 5 minutes Q&A

**Slides:**
1. Title slide
2. Background and significance (2 slides)
3. Research question/hypotheses (1 slide)
4. Experimental design diagram (1 slide)
5. Methods overview (1-2 slides)
6. Expected results (1-2 slides)
7. Timeline and budget summary (1 slide)
8. Significance and impact (1 slide)
9. Acknowledgments/questions (1 slide)

**Total:** 10-12 slides

### Defending Your Proposal

**Common Questions:**

**Scientific:**
- "Why is this important?"
- "How does this differ from [previous study]?"
- "What if your hypothesis is wrong?"
- "Have you considered [alternative explanation]?"

**Methodological:**
- "Why did you choose this design?"
- "Is your sample size adequate?"
- "How will you control for [confounding variable]?"
- "What if [equipment] doesn't work?"

**Practical:**
- "Is this feasible in the timeframe?"
- "Can you do this with the proposed budget?"
- "What expertise do you have?"
- "What's your backup plan?"

**Answer Strategies:**
- Listen fully before answering
- Acknowledge good points
- Explain your rationale
- Admit if you don't know
- Offer to investigate further
