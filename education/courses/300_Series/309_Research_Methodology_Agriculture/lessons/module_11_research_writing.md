# Module 11: Research Writing

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
1. Structure scientific papers according to IMRaD format
2. Write clear and concise methods sections
3. Present results effectively with tables and figures
4. Compose compelling discussions and conclusions
5. Follow citation and referencing conventions
6. Avoid common writing errors

## Scientific Paper Structure (IMRaD)

### The IMRaD Format

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      IMRaD STRUCTURE                                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                     │
│  INTRODUCTION    "What did you study and why?"                      │
│  ├─ Background context                                             │
│  ├─ Literature review                                              │
│  ├─ Knowledge gap                                                  │
│  ├─ Research objective                                             │
│  └─ Hypothesis (optional)                                          │
│                                                                     │
│  METHODS         "How did you do it?"                               │
│  ├─ Experimental design                                            │
│  ├─ Materials and procedures                                       │
│  ├─ Data collection                                                │
│  └─ Statistical analysis                                           │
│                                                                     │
│  RESULTS         "What did you find?"                               │
│  ├─ Present findings objectively                                   │
│  ├─ Tables and figures                                             │
│  ├─ Statistical results                                            │
│  └─ No interpretation yet                                          │
│                                                                     │
│  AND                                                                │
│                                                                     │
│  DISCUSSION      "What does it mean?"                               │
│  ├─ Interpret results                                              │
│  ├─ Compare to literature                                          │
│  ├─ Explain mechanisms                                             │
│  ├─ Address limitations                                            │
│  ├─ Implications                                                   │
│  └─ Future research                                                │
│                                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Additional Components

**Before Main Text:**
- Title
- Authors and affiliations
- Abstract
- Keywords

**After Main Text:**
- Conclusions (sometimes separate section)
- Acknowledgments
- References
- Supplementary materials

## Writing the Title

### Characteristics of Good Titles

**Effective titles are:**
- Specific and informative
- Concise (usually <15 words)
- Include key variables
- Avoid abbreviations
- No "A study of..." or "An investigation into..."

### Examples

**Poor:**
"A Study of Aquaponics"

**Better:**
"Effects of Fish Stocking Density on Water Quality and Plant Growth in Aquaponic Systems"

**Best:**
"Tilapia Stocking Density Affects Nitrate Concentrations and Lettuce Yield in Coupled Aquaponic Systems"

### Title Formats

**Declarative (states main finding):**
"High Stocking Density Improves Nutrient Availability for Plants in Aquaponic Systems"

**Descriptive (describes study):**
"Effects of Three Stocking Densities on Water Chemistry and Crop Production in Commercial-Scale Aquaponics"

**Question Format (less common):**
"Does Fish Stocking Density Affect Plant Productivity in Aquaponic Systems?"

## Abstract

### Purpose

Self-contained summary enabling readers to decide if full paper is relevant

### Structure (150-250 words)

```
BACKGROUND (1-2 sentences)
Context and rationale

OBJECTIVE (1 sentence)
Specific research question or hypothesis

METHODS (2-3 sentences)
Design, subjects, measurements, analysis

RESULTS (3-4 sentences)
Key findings with statistics

CONCLUSIONS (1-2 sentences)
Main implications
```

### Example Abstract

```
Optimal fish stocking density in aquaponic systems remains undefined,
despite its importance for nutrient provision to plants. This study
investigated the effects of three tilapia stocking densities (10, 20,
and 30 kg/m³) on water quality parameters and lettuce (Lactuca sativa)
yield in commercial-scale coupled aquaponic systems. Nine replicate
systems were randomly assigned to density treatments and monitored for
60 days. Water samples were analyzed weekly for pH, dissolved oxygen,
ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate. Lettuce fresh weight was measured at
harvest. Nitrate concentrations increased with stocking density (18.5,
42.3, and 68.7 mg/L for low, medium, and high density, respectively;
p<0.001). Lettuce yield was highest at medium density (145 g/plant)
compared to low (112 g/plant) and high (138 g/plant) densities
(p=0.003). Dissolved oxygen decreased with increasing density (6.8,
5.2, and 4.1 mg/L; p<0.001), approaching critical levels for fish
health at high density. Results suggest that medium stocking density
(20 kg/m³) optimizes plant nutrient availability while maintaining
adequate water quality for fish welfare in coupled aquaponic systems.
```

**Keywords:** aquaponics, tilapia, stocking density, water quality, lettuce, nutrient management

## Introduction

### Funnel Structure

```
        BROAD
        ┌────────────────────┐
        │  General context   │
        └────────┬───────────┘
                 │
        ┌────────▼───────────┐
        │  Relevant literature│
        └────────┬───────────┘
                 │
        ┌────────▼───────────┐
        │  Knowledge gap     │
        └────────┬───────────┘
                 │
        ┌────────▼───────────┐
        │  Study objective   │
        └────────────────────┘
        SPECIFIC
```

### Components

**Paragraph 1: General Context**
- Broad importance of topic
- Why readers should care
- Set the stage

**Example:**
"Aquaponic systems integrate fish production with hydroponic crop cultivation, offering potential for sustainable food production with reduced water and land requirements. As global demand for protein and vegetables increases, aquaponics has gained attention as a climate-resilient agricultural technology."

**Paragraphs 2-3: Literature Review**
- What is known
- Previous research findings
- Build case for study

**Example:**
"Previous research has demonstrated that aquaponic systems can achieve vegetable yields comparable to hydroponic systems while simultaneously producing fish (Rakocy et al., 2006). However, nutrient availability for plants depends on fish feeding rates and stocking density (Goddek et al., 2015). While studies have examined individual components..."

**Paragraph 4: Knowledge Gap**
- What is unknown
- Limitations of previous work
- Transition to your study

**Example:**
"Despite growing interest, few studies have systematically compared stocking densities in commercial-scale systems, and optimal densities remain undefined. Most previous work used laboratory-scale systems, limiting generalizability to commercial production."

**Final Paragraph: Objective and Hypothesis**
- Specific research question
- Hypothesis (if applicable)
- Brief mention of approach

**Example:**
"The objective of this study was to determine the optimal tilapia stocking density for coupled aquaponic lettuce production by evaluating effects of three densities on water quality and plant yield. We hypothesized that intermediate density would maximize plant productivity while maintaining acceptable water quality parameters."

## Methods

### Purpose

Enable replication by other researchers

### Organizational Approaches

**Option 1: Chronological**
- Follow order experiments were conducted
- Good for simple, linear studies

**Option 2: By Category**
- Experimental design
- Study organisms
- System setup
- Measurements
- Statistical analysis

### Level of Detail

**Include:**
- Specific equipment models and manufacturers
- Concentrations, temperatures, times
- Sample sizes and replication
- Statistical tests and software
- Randomization and blocking
- Any deviations from standard protocols

**Exclude:**
- Routine procedures (e.g., "Fish were fed daily")
- Excessive background (belongs in Introduction)
- Results (belong in Results)

### Subsections

**Experimental Design**

```
This study employed a completely randomized design with three
treatments (stocking densities: 10, 20, 30 kg/m³) and three
replicate systems per treatment (n=9 total). Tilapia juveniles
were randomly assigned to systems using a random number generator.
```

**Systems and Organisms**

```
Nine identical aquaponic systems (2 m³ fish tank, 4 m² grow bed)
were constructed in a climate-controlled greenhouse (22-25°C,
60-70% RH). Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) juveniles
(initial weight 50±5 g) were obtained from... Buttercrunch
lettuce (Lactuca sativa) seedlings (21 days old) were
transplanted at...
```

**Measurements**

```
Water quality parameters were measured weekly using a YSI ProDSS
multiparameter meter (YSI Inc., Yellow Springs, OH). pH,
dissolved oxygen (DO), and electrical conductivity (EC) were
measured in situ. Water samples (50 mL) were collected from
each system and analyzed for total ammonia nitrogen (TAN),
nitrite-nitrogen, and nitrate-nitrogen using a Hach DR900
colorimeter following manufacturer protocols.
```

**Statistical Analysis**

```
Data were analyzed using R version 4.3.1 (R Core Team, 2023).
One-way ANOVA was used to compare treatments, following
verification of assumptions (normality: Shapiro-Wilk test;
homogeneity of variance: Levene's test). Post-hoc comparisons
were conducted using Tukey's HSD test. Significance was set
at α = 0.05. Results are presented as mean ± standard error.
```

### Common Verbs

Use **past tense** for what you did:
- measured, collected, analyzed, assigned, calculated

Use **present tense** for established facts:
- pH is measured, ANOVA is used, tilapia are omnivorous

## Results

### Principles

1. **Present findings objectively**
   - No interpretation (save for Discussion)
   - "Temperature decreased" not "Temperature unfortunately decreased"

2. **Organize logically**
   - By research question
   - By dependent variable
   - Chronologically

3. **Use past tense**
   - "Yield increased with density"
   - "There was a significant effect"

4. **Integrate text, tables, and figures**
   - Don't repeat all table data in text
   - Highlight key findings in text
   - Direct readers to tables/figures

### Text Guidelines

**Report statistics completely:**

```
INCOMPLETE:
"Treatment significantly affected yield (p<0.05)."

COMPLETE:
"Treatment significantly affected yield (F₂,₂₁=12.4, p<0.001, η²=0.54)."
```

**Highlight patterns:**

```
POOR:
"Treatment A yielded 120 g, Treatment B yielded 145 g, and
Treatment C yielded 138 g. The difference was significant
(p=0.003)."

BETTER:
"Yield differed significantly among treatments (F₂,₂₁=8.7, p=0.003),
with Treatment B producing 21% more than Treatment A (145 vs. 120 g,
p=0.002), while Treatment C was intermediate (138 g)."
```

### Statistical Reporting

**t-test:**
"Plant height was significantly greater in Treatment B than Treatment A (t₁₈=3.45, p=0.003)."

**ANOVA with post-hoc:**
"Stocking density significantly affected nitrate concentration (F₂,₆=45.2, p<0.001). Post-hoc analysis (Tukey's HSD) revealed that all three densities differed significantly from each other (p<0.05), with concentrations increasing with density."

**Regression:**
"Days of growth significantly predicted yield (F₁,₂₈=67.3, p<0.001, R²=0.71). The regression equation was: Yield (g) = -15.3 + 4.2×Days."

**Correlation:**
"Temperature and growth rate were strongly positively correlated (r=0.78, n=15, p<0.001)."

## Tables and Figures

### When to Use Each

**Table:**
- Exact numerical values important
- Many variables to compare
- Reference for statistical values

**Figure:**
- Show patterns, trends, relationships
- Visual comparison more important
- Engage readers

**Neither:**
- Very simple results (1-2 values)
- Include in text instead

### Table Design

**Good Table:**

**Table 1. Water quality parameters (mean ± SE) in aquaponic systems at three tilapia stocking densities**

| Parameter | Low (10 kg/m³) | Medium (20 kg/m³) | High (30 kg/m³) | p-value |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| pH | 7.2 ± 0.1ᵃ | 7.1 ± 0.1ᵃ | 6.9 ± 0.1ᵇ | 0.021 |
| DO (mg/L) | 6.8 ± 0.2ᵃ | 5.2 ± 0.3ᵇ | 4.1 ± 0.2ᶜ | <0.001 |
| TAN (mg/L) | 0.3 ± 0.1ᵃ | 0.5 ± 0.1ᵃᵇ | 0.7 ± 0.1ᵇ | 0.018 |
| NO₃-N (mg/L) | 18.5 ± 2.1ᵃ | 42.3 ± 3.2ᵇ | 68.7 ± 4.5ᶜ | <0.001 |

*Different superscript letters indicate significant differences (Tukey's HSD, α=0.05). TAN=total ammonia nitrogen; DO=dissolved oxygen.*

**Table Checklist:**
- [ ] Title is descriptive and standalone
- [ ] Units specified
- [ ] Sample sizes noted
- [ ] Statistical differences indicated
- [ ] Footnotes explain abbreviations and statistics
- [ ] Aligned decimals
- [ ] Horizontal lines only (no vertical lines)

### Figure Design

**Effective Figures:**
- Clear axis labels with units
- Legible font size (≥10 pt)
- High contrast (black/white or color-blind friendly)
- Error bars defined in caption
- Minimal "chart junk"
- Self-explanatory caption

**Figure Types:**

**Bar Chart:** Compare means across categories
**Box Plot:** Show distribution and outliers
**Scatter Plot:** Show relationship between variables
**Line Graph:** Show change over time
**Histogram:** Show frequency distribution

**Example Caption:**

"**Figure 1. Effect of stocking density on lettuce fresh weight.** Bars represent mean ± SE (n=3 systems with 10 plants each). Different letters indicate significant differences (Tukey's HSD, p<0.05). Medium density (20 kg/m³) produced highest yield."

## Discussion

### Structure

**Paragraph 1: Summary of Main Findings**
- Briefly restate key results
- Direct answer to research question

**Middle Paragraphs: Interpretation**
- Compare to literature (support or contrast)
- Explain mechanisms
- Address unexpected findings
- Discuss each major result

**Paragraph on Limitations**
- Acknowledge study constraints
- Potential sources of bias
- Generalizability

**Final Paragraph: Implications and Future Directions**
- Practical applications
- Contribution to field
- Suggested next studies

### Writing Tips

**Interpret, don't just repeat results:**

```
POOR (just repeating):
"We found that Treatment B produced higher yields than Treatment A."

BETTER (interpreting):
"The superior yields in Treatment B likely resulted from increased
nitrate availability (42 vs. 18 mg/L), which is within the optimal
range for lettuce growth (Resh, 2012). These findings suggest that
medium stocking density provides adequate nutrient inputs without
compromising water quality."
```

**Compare to literature:**

```
"Our findings align with those of Smith et al. (2020), who also
reported optimal lettuce growth at 20 kg/m³ density. However,
unlike their laboratory-scale study, we observed lower dissolved
oxygen levels, likely due to our larger tank volumes and higher
organic matter accumulation in commercial systems."
```

**Address limitations honestly:**

```
"Several limitations should be noted. First, our 60-day study
period may not capture seasonal variations. Second, results
are specific to Nile tilapia and buttercrunch lettuce; other
species combinations may respond differently. Third, our
greenhouse climate control may limit generalizability to outdoor
systems."
```

**Suggest future research:**

```
"Future studies should examine long-term system stability beyond
60 days, test additional crop species, and conduct economic
analyses to determine profitability at different stocking
densities."
```

### Conclusion Section

**Keep concise (one paragraph):**
- Restate main findings
- Practical takeaway
- Don't introduce new information

**Example:**

"This study demonstrates that medium stocking density (20 kg/m³) optimizes lettuce production in coupled aquaponic systems by balancing nutrient availability with water quality maintenance. These findings provide evidence-based guidance for commercial aquaponic operators seeking to maximize crop yields while ensuring fish welfare."

## Citation and Referencing

### In-Text Citations (APA Style)

**One author:**
Smith (2022) found... or (Smith, 2022)

**Two authors:**
Smith and Jones (2022) or (Smith & Jones, 2022)

**Three or more authors:**
Smith et al. (2022) or (Smith et al., 2022)

**Multiple citations:**
(Chen, 2021; Jones, 2022; Smith, 2023)

### Reference List (APA Style)

**Journal Article:**
```
Smith, J. A., & Jones, B. C. (2022). Optimal stocking density
    for tilapia in aquaponic systems. Aquaculture Research,
    45(3), 234-245. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx
```

**Book:**
```
Rakocy, J. E. (2012). Aquaponics: Integrating fish and
    plant culture. Wiley-Blackwell.
```

**Chapter in Edited Book:**
```
Goddek, S., & Vermeulen, T. (2018). Comparison of Lactuca
    sativa growth performance in conventional and RAS-based
    hydroponic systems. In S. Goddek et al. (Eds.),
    Aquaponics food production systems (pp. 147-165).
    Springer.
```

**Website:**
```
FAO. (2020). The state of world fisheries and aquaculture.
    Food and Agriculture Organization. Retrieved from
    https://www.fao.org/publications/sofia/2020
```

## Common Writing Errors

### 1. Passive Voice Overuse

**Weak:** "The fish were fed by the technician daily."
**Better:** "Technicians fed fish daily."

**Note:** Passive is acceptable when actor is unknown or unimportant:
"Samples were analyzed in triplicate."

### 2. Vague Language

**Vague:** "A lot of fish died."
**Precise:** "Mortality was 15% (7 of 47 fish)."

**Vague:** "Temperature increased slightly."
**Precise:** "Temperature increased by 2.5°C."

### 3. Redundancy

**Redundant:** "past experience," "12 noon," "completely full"
**Better:** "experience," "noon," "full"

**Redundant:** "In order to measure the pH..."
**Better:** "To measure the pH..."

### 4. Acronym Abuse

**First use:** Define acronyms
"dissolved oxygen (DO)"

**Then:** Use acronym consistently
"DO levels decreased..."

**Don't:** Define in abstract and again in body (abstract is separate)

### 5. Tense Confusion

**Past:** What you did (Methods, Results)
"We measured pH daily."

**Present:** General truths, what paper shows
"Figure 1 shows that yield increases..."
"Tilapia are omnivorous."

### 6. First Person Debate

**Traditional:** Avoid "we" and "I"
**Modern:** "We" is increasingly accepted

**Acceptable:** "We hypothesized..." "We measured..."
**Avoid:** "I think..." "We believe..." (state objectively)

## Practical Exercise

### Critique This Abstract

```
This study was about aquaponics. We wanted to see if different
amounts of fish would change how plants grow. We used 3 fish
amounts and grew lettuce. Some tanks had lots of fish and some
had less. We measured the plants at the end. The results showed
that the medium amount was best. High amounts had problems with
oxygen. We think that 20 kg is a good amount to use.
```

**Identify problems and rewrite.**

## Key Takeaways

1. **IMRaD structure:** Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion
2. **Methods:** Provide enough detail for replication
3. **Results:** Objective presentation with complete statistics
4. **Discussion:** Interpret findings, compare to literature, acknowledge limitations
5. **Tables and figures:** Use appropriately, make self-explanatory
6. **Citations:** Follow style guide consistently (usually APA for agriculture)
7. **Clarity:** Be precise, concise, and avoid jargon
