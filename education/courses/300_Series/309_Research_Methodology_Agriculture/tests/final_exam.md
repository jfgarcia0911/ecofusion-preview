**8. What is the main difference between a literature review summary and synthesis?** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
A summary restates what each individual source says separately, while synthesis integrates findings across multiple sources to identify patterns, themes, contradictions, and gaps. Synthesis creates new understanding by connecting ideas from different sources rather than just listing them.

**9. Why is it important to include both positive and negative results in a literature review?** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
Including negative/null results provides a balanced view, prevents confirmation bias, reveals the true state of evidence, identifies conflicting findings that need explanation, and prevents overestimation of effect sizes that can result from publication bias.

**14. Calculate the required sample size per group to detect a medium effect size (d=0.5) with 80% power at α=0.05. Use the simplified formula: n = 16 × (σ²/δ²). Assume σ = δ for medium effect.** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
```
For d = 0.5, this means δ/σ = 0.5, so δ = 0.5σ
n = 16 × (σ²/(0.5σ)²)
n = 16 × (σ²/0.25σ²)
n = 16 × 4
n = 64 per group
```

**16. Explain why randomization is important in experimental design and how it differs from blocking.** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Randomization** assigns treatments to experimental units by chance, eliminating selection bias and distributing unknown confounding variables equally across groups. It's required for valid statistical inference.

**Blocking** groups similar experimental units before randomization to account for known sources of variation. Randomization occurs within blocks.

**Difference:** Randomization addresses unknown variability; blocking addresses known variability. Randomization is required; blocking is optional but increases precision.

---

**17. A researcher wants to study 4 light spectrums AND 3 nutrient levels. What type of experimental design should they use, and how many treatment combinations will there be? What additional information can this design provide compared to separate experiments?** (5 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Design:** Factorial design (4×3)

**Treatment combinations:** 4 × 3 = 12

**Additional information:** A factorial design can test for **interactions** between light and nutrients—whether the effect of light depends on nutrient level (or vice versa). Separate experiments can only evaluate main effects and cannot detect interactions. Factorial designs are also more efficient, requiring fewer total experimental units than conducting separate studies.

**24. Explain the difference between accuracy and precision in measurement, and provide an example of a measurement that is precise but not accurate.** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Accuracy** = closeness to true value (low systematic error)
**Precision** = repeatability of measurements (low random error)

**Example:** A pH meter consistently reads 7.5 when tested in a pH 7.0 buffer solution.
- **Precise:** Yes (consistent readings)
- **Accurate:** No (systematically biased +0.5 pH units)
- **Cause:** Meter needs calibration

**30. Why must post-hoc tests (like Tukey's HSD) be used after a significant ANOVA instead of multiple t-tests?** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
Multiple t-tests inflate the family-wise Type I error rate (risk of false positive). With k groups, there are k(k-1)/2 possible pairwise comparisons. Each test at α=0.05 compounds the error. Post-hoc tests like Tukey's HSD control the overall error rate while making all pairwise comparisons, maintaining the true α=0.05 for the entire family of tests.

**31. A correlation of r = -0.65 (p = 0.002) is found between water temperature and dissolved oxygen. Interpret this result and explain why correlation doesn't prove causation.** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Interpretation:** There is a strong negative correlation—as temperature increases, dissolved oxygen decreases. This relationship is statistically significant (p = 0.002), unlikely due to chance.

**Correlation ≠ Causation because:**
- Temperature could cause DO decrease (physics of gas solubility)
- Both could be affected by a third variable (e.g., time of day, algae growth)
- Could be coincidental
Correlation shows association, not causal direction. Controlled experiments are needed to establish causation.

**32. What is statistical power, and what are three ways to increase it?** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Statistical power** is the probability of detecting a true effect when it exists (1 - β), or the ability to avoid Type II error.

**Three ways to increase power:**
1. **Increase sample size** (most common)
2. **Increase effect size** (use stronger treatments)
3. **Reduce variability** (better controls, standardization)
4. **Increase α** (less conservative, usually not recommended)
5. **Use more powerful test** (parametric vs. non-parametric)

*(Any 3 correct = full credit)*

**Example Answer:**
**Classification:** Very large effect size (d > 0.8)

**Practical meaning:** The difference between groups is 1.2 standard deviations apart. This is a substantial, meaningful difference that would likely be important in practice, not just statistically. The treatment has a strong effect.

**34. A 95% CI for the difference in means is [-2, 15]. Can you conclude there is a significant difference at α=0.05? Why or why not?** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**No, cannot conclude significant difference.**

**Reason:** The 95% CI includes 0 (ranges from -2 to +15), meaning zero difference is plausible. When a CI for a difference includes zero, the p-value will be ≥ 0.05, indicating no significant difference. We cannot rule out the possibility of no difference between groups.

## SECTION 7: INTERPRETATION & COMMUNICATION (12 points)

**35. Distinguish between statistical significance and practical significance with an agricultural example.** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Statistical significance** (p<0.05) indicates a result is unlikely due to chance, but doesn't indicate the magnitude or importance of the effect.

**Practical significance** refers to whether the effect is large enough to matter in real-world application, considering costs, benefits, and implementation.

**Example:** A study of 1,000 plants finds a new fertilizer increases yield by 3 grams (p=0.001). This is statistically significant but may not be practically significant if the fertilizer costs $100 more per season and the 3-gram increase generates only $5 in additional revenue.

**36. What are TWO potential sources of bias in research, and how can each be minimized?** (4 pts)

**Example Answer:**

**1. Selection bias**
- **Cause:** Non-random assignment of treatments
- **Minimize:** Use randomization procedures, random number generators

**2. Measurement bias**
- **Cause:** Observer knows treatment when measuring outcomes
- **Minimize:** Use blinding (observer doesn't know which treatment), standardized protocols

*(Other acceptable answers: publication bias, funding bias, attrition bias, etc., with appropriate controls)*

**37. A paper reports "Although not statistically significant (p=0.08), there was a trend toward higher yield." Critique this statement.** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**Problems with this statement:**
- "Trend" is subjective and has no formal statistical definition
- Results are either significant at chosen α or not
- p=0.08 does not meet conventional α=0.05 threshold
- Could indicate underpowered study rather than real trend

**Better reporting:** "No significant difference was detected (p=0.08). This may reflect insufficient power (power=0.45); a larger sample size may be needed to detect an effect if one exists."

**40. A study funded by a commercial aquaponics equipment company finds their product superior to competitors. Does this automatically invalidate the results? Why or why not? What should you look for?** (3 pts)

**Example Answer:**
**No, funding source alone doesn't invalidate results**, but it warrants careful scrutiny.

**Look for:**
- Was the study design rigorous (randomized, blinded, adequate controls)?
- Were conflicts of interest disclosed transparently?
- Was the comparison fair (equal conditions for all products)?
- Were limitations acknowledged honestly?
- Can results be verified by independent researchers?
- Is the magnitude of effect realistic or too good to be true?

Industry funding introduces potential bias but doesn't necessarily mean results are wrong. Evaluate the methods and look for independent replication.

**45. You design an experiment but a reviewer questions whether your sample size is adequate. What information would you provide to justify your sample size?** (2 pts)

**Example Answer:**
Provide a **power analysis** showing:

- Adjustment for expected attrition/dropout
- Final sample size determination

**46. Integration Question: Design a simple research study to answer this question: "Does water temperature (20°C vs. 25°C) affect tilapia growth rate?" In your answer, include:**
