# Research Methodology Glossary

**Course 309: Research Methodology for Agriculture**

---

## A

**Accuracy** - Closeness of a measurement to the true value; low systematic error.

**Alpha (α)** - Significance level; probability of Type I error (typically 0.05).

**Alternative Hypothesis (H₁ or Hₐ)** - Statement that there IS an effect or difference; opposite of null hypothesis.

**ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)** - Statistical test comparing means of three or more groups.

**Applied Research** - Research focused on solving specific practical problems.

**Assumption** - Condition that must be met for a statistical test to be valid (e.g., normality, equal variance).

**Attrition** - Loss of subjects/units during a study; dropout rate.

**Attrition Bias** - Systematic differences between those who complete a study and those who don't.

---

## B

**Basic Research** - Research aimed at increasing fundamental knowledge without immediate practical application.

**Beta (β)** - Probability of Type II error; failing to detect a real effect.

**Bias** - Systematic error that leads to incorrect conclusions; can be selection bias, measurement bias, etc.

**Blinding** - Keeping researchers and/or subjects unaware of treatment assignment to prevent bias.

**Blocking** - Grouping similar experimental units before randomization to account for known sources of variation.

**Boolean Operators** - Logical connectors (AND, OR, NOT) used to combine search terms in literature databases.

**Bonferroni Correction** - Adjustment to significance level when conducting multiple comparisons to control family-wise error rate.

---

## C

**Case-Control Study** - Observational study comparing subjects with an outcome (cases) to those without (controls).

**Categorical Variable** - Variable with discrete categories (e.g., species, color, treatment group).

**Causation** - One variable directly causes changes in another; requires more than correlation.

**Chi-Square Test** - Statistical test for categorical data; tests independence or goodness of fit.

**Cluster Sampling** - Probability sampling where groups (clusters) are randomly selected, then all units within selected clusters are sampled.

**Coefficient of Variation (CV)** - Standard deviation divided by mean, expressed as percentage; relative variability.

**Cohort Study** - Observational study following a group over time to observe outcomes.

**Completely Randomized Design (CRD)** - Experimental design where treatments are randomly assigned to homogeneous units without blocking.

**Confidence Interval (CI)** - Range of plausible values for a population parameter; typically 95% CI.

**Confirmatory Research** - Research testing a priori hypotheses (as opposed to exploratory).

**Confounding Variable** - Extraneous variable that correlates with both independent and dependent variables, potentially biasing results.

**Continuous Variable** - Variable that can take any value within a range (e.g., weight, temperature, pH).

**Control Group** - Group receiving no treatment or standard treatment; provides baseline for comparison.

**Controlled Variable** - Variable held constant across all experimental groups.

**Correlation** - Statistical relationship between two variables; does not imply causation.

**CRAAP Test** - Framework for evaluating sources: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.

**Critical Value** - Threshold value of a test statistic for determining significance.

---

## D

**Data** - Observations or measurements collected during research.

**Degrees of Freedom (df)** - Number of independent values that can vary in analysis; affects distribution of test statistics.

**Dependent Variable** - Outcome variable; what is measured in response to independent variable manipulation.

**Descriptive Statistics** - Numerical and graphical summaries of data (mean, median, SD, etc.).

**Detection Bias** - Systematic differences in outcome assessment between groups.

**Discrete Variable** - Variable with distinct, separate values (e.g., count data).

**Discussion** - Section of research paper interpreting results, comparing to literature, and addressing implications.

**Double-Blind** - Neither subjects nor researchers know treatment assignment.

---

## E

**Effect Size** - Magnitude of difference or strength of relationship; independent of sample size (e.g., Cohen's d, eta-squared).

**Empirical** - Based on observation or experimentation rather than theory alone.

**Error Bars** - Graphical representation of variability or uncertainty; can show SD, SE, or CI.

**Eta-Squared (η²)** - Effect size measure for ANOVA; proportion of variance explained by treatment.

**Experimental Design** - Plan for conducting an experiment, including treatment assignment, replication, and controls.

**Experimental Unit** - Smallest unit to which treatment is independently applied.

**Exploratory Research** - Research examining data without a priori hypotheses to generate ideas.

**External Validity** - Extent to which results can be generalized beyond the specific study.

---

## F

**F-statistic** - Test statistic for ANOVA and regression; ratio of between-group to within-group variance.

**Factorial Design** - Experimental design studying two or more factors simultaneously to examine main effects and interactions.

**Family-Wise Error Rate** - Probability of making at least one Type I error in a family of tests.

**Fisher's Exact Test** - Alternative to chi-square for categorical data with small expected frequencies.

**Fixed Effect** - Effect of a factor with specific, limited levels of interest (e.g., three particular nutrient formulas).

---

## G

**Generalizability** - See External Validity.

**Goodness of Fit** - How well data fit an expected distribution or model.

**G*Power** - Free software for conducting power analyses.

---

## H

**HARKing** - Hypothesizing After Results are Known; presenting post-hoc explanations as a priori hypotheses.

**Heterogeneity** - Variability or diversity; opposite of homogeneity.

**Histogram** - Graphical display of frequency distribution for continuous data.

**Homogeneity of Variance** - Assumption that groups have similar variances; tested with Levene's test.

**Homoscedasticity** - Constant variance of residuals across levels of predictor variable (regression assumption).

**Hypothesis** - Testable prediction about relationship between variables.

---

## I

**Impact Factor** - Measure of journal influence based on citation frequency.

**IMRaD** - Standard scientific paper structure: Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion.

**Independent Samples** - Observations from different subjects; not paired or matched.

**Independent Variable** - Variable manipulated by researcher; predictor or treatment variable.

**Inferential Statistics** - Using sample data to make inferences about populations.

**Interaction** - When the effect of one factor depends on the level of another factor.

**Internal Validity** - Extent to which study measures what it claims to measure.

**Interval Scale** - Measurement scale with equal intervals but no true zero (e.g., temperature in °C).

**Intervention** - Treatment or manipulation applied in experimental study.

---

## K

**Kruskal-Wallis Test** - Non-parametric alternative to one-way ANOVA for comparing three or more groups.

**Kurtosis** - Measure of "tailedness" of a distribution.

---

## L

**Latin Square Design** - Experimental design controlling for two sources of variation simultaneously.

**Levene's Test** - Statistical test for homogeneity of variance.

**Likert Scale** - Ordinal scale commonly used in surveys (e.g., strongly disagree to strongly agree).

**Linear Regression** - Statistical method for modeling relationship between continuous predictor(s) and outcome.

**Literature Review** - Comprehensive summary and synthesis of published research on a topic.

---

## M

**Main Effect** - Effect of one factor averaged across levels of other factors in factorial design.

**Mann-Whitney U Test** - Non-parametric alternative to independent t-test.

**Mean** - Average; sum of values divided by number of values.

**Measurement Bias** - Systematic error in data collection.

**Median** - Middle value when data are sorted; 50th percentile.

**Meta-Analysis** - Statistical synthesis of results from multiple studies.

**Metadata** - Data about data; information describing a dataset.

**Mixed Model** - Statistical model with both fixed and random effects.

**Mode** - Most frequently occurring value.

**Multiple Comparisons** - Making many statistical comparisons, which inflates Type I error rate.

**Multivariate** - Involving multiple outcome variables.

---

## N

**Nominal Scale** - Categorical measurement with no inherent order (e.g., species, color).

**Non-Parametric** - Statistical methods not assuming specific distribution (often normal distribution).

**Normal Distribution** - Bell-shaped, symmetric distribution; basis for many parametric tests.

**Null Hypothesis (H₀)** - Statement of no effect or no difference; what we test against.

---

## O

**Observation** - Direct measurement or recording of data.

**Observational Study** - Research where researcher observes without manipulating variables.

**Odds Ratio** - Ratio of odds of an outcome in two groups; effect size for categorical outcomes.

**Omega-Squared (ω²)** - Less biased alternative to eta-squared for effect size in ANOVA.

**One-Tailed Test** - Statistical test with directionalprediction (e.g., A > B).

**Operational Definition** - Precise definition of how a variable will be measured.

**Ordinal Scale** - Categorical measurement with meaningful order but unequal intervals (e.g., ratings: poor, fair, good).

**Outlier** - Data point far from other observations; may represent error or true extreme value.

---

## P

**Paired Samples** - Matched observations or measurements on same subjects (before/after, matched pairs).

**Parameter** - Numerical characteristic of a population (e.g., μ, σ).

**Parametric Test** - Statistical test assuming specific distribution (usually normal).

**Pearson Correlation (r)** - Measure of linear relationship between two continuous variables.

**Peer Review** - Evaluation of research by experts before publication.

**Performance Bias** - Systematic differences in care provided to groups.

**P-Hacking** - Manipulating data or analyses to achieve desired p-value.

**Pilot Study** - Small preliminary study to test feasibility and refine methods.

**Placebo** - Inactive treatment that appears identical to active treatment.

**Population** - Complete set of all possible units of interest.

**Post-Hoc Test** - Test conducted after significant ANOVA to determine which groups differ (e.g., Tukey's HSD).

**Power** - Probability of detecting a true effect; 1 - β; typically aim for 0.80 or higher.

**Power Analysis** - Calculation to determine required sample size for desired power, or to calculate achieved power.

**Precision** - Repeatability of measurements; low random error; closeness of replicate measurements.

**Primary Source** - Original research article reporting first-hand findings.

**Pseudoreplication** - Treating non-independent samples as independent replicates; common statistical error.

**Publication Bias** - Tendency for journals to publish positive results more than null/negative results.

**P-Value** - Probability of obtaining results as extreme as observed if null hypothesis is true.

---

## Q

**Qualitative Data** - Non-numerical data (descriptions, categories, observations).

**Quantitative Data** - Numerical data.

**Quartile** - Values dividing ranked data into four equal parts (Q1=25th, Q2=50th, Q3=75th percentile).

**Quasi-Experimental** - Study resembling experiment but lacking full randomization.

---

## R

**R** - Free statistical software; also used as symbol for correlation coefficient.

**R²** - Coefficient of determination; proportion of variance in outcome explained by predictor(s).

**Random Assignment** - Using chance to assign subjects to treatment groups.

**Random Effect** - Effect of a factor representing a random sample from larger population.

**Random Error** - Unpredictable variation in measurements.

**Randomization** - Process of assigning treatments by chance to eliminate bias.

**Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD)** - Experimental design where treatments are randomized within blocks.

**Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT)** - Experimental study with random assignment to treatment/control.

**Range** - Difference between maximum and minimum values.

**Ratio Scale** - Measurement with equal intervals and true zero (e.g., weight, height).

**Reference** - Citation of published source.

**Regression** - Statistical technique for modeling relationships between variables.

**Reliability** - Consistency of results when measurement is repeated.

**Repeated Measures** - Design where same subjects are measured multiple times.

**Replication** - Repeating experimental units under same conditions; essential for estimating variability.

**Reporting Bias** - Selective reporting of outcomes or results.

**Research Design** - Overall plan for answering research question.

**Residual** - Difference between observed and predicted value.

**Response Variable** - See Dependent Variable.

---

## S

**Sample** - Subset selected from population for study.

**Sample Size (n)** - Number of experimental units or observations.

**Sampling Frame** - List of all units in population from which sample is drawn.

**Sampling Error** - Difference between sample statistic and population parameter due to random sampling.

**Secondary Source** - Publication summarizing or synthesizing primary sources (e.g., review article).

**Selection Bias** - Systematic difference in how subjects are selected or assigned to groups.

**Shapiro-Wilk Test** - Statistical test for normality of distribution.

**Significance Level** - See Alpha.

**Simple Random Sampling** - Probability sampling where each unit has equal chance of selection.

**Single-Blind** - Either subjects or researchers (but not both) are unaware of treatment assignment.

**Skewness** - Measure of asymmetry in distribution.

**Spearman Correlation** - Non-parametric alternative to Pearson correlation; based on ranks.

**Split-Plot Design** - Experimental design where some factors are harder to randomize than others.

**Standard Deviation (SD)** - Square root of variance; typical deviation from mean.

**Standard Error (SE)** - Standard deviation of sampling distribution; SD divided by square root of n.

**Standardization** - Keeping procedures constant to reduce variability.

**Statistic** - Numerical characteristic of a sample (e.g., x̄, s).

**Statistical Power** - See Power.

**Statistical Significance** - Result unlikely due to chance alone (p < α); doesn't indicate importance.

**Stratified Sampling** - Probability sampling where population is divided into strata and random sample taken from each.

**Systematic Error** - Consistent bias in one direction.

**Systematic Review** - Comprehensive, structured review of literature using explicit methods.

**Systematic Sampling** - Probability sampling where every kth unit is selected after random start.

---

## T

**t-Test** - Statistical test comparing one or two means.

**Test Statistic** - Value calculated from sample data to make statistical decision (e.g., t, F, χ²).

**Treatment** - Specific level of independent variable applied to experimental unit.

**Triangulation** - Using multiple sources or methods to verify findings.

**Tukey's HSD** - Post-hoc test controlling family-wise error rate for all pairwise comparisons.

**Two-Tailed Test** - Statistical test with non-directional prediction (A ≠ B).

**Type I Error** - Rejecting true null hypothesis; false positive; probability = α.

**Type II Error** - Failing to reject false null hypothesis; false negative; probability = β.

---

## U

**Unbiased** - Estimate whose expected value equals true population parameter.

**Univariate** - Involving one dependent variable.

---

## V

**Validity** - Extent to which test measures what it intends to measure.

**Variable** - Any characteristic that can take different values.

**Variance** - Average squared deviation from mean; measure of spread.

**Verification** - Confirming accuracy of data or procedures.

---

## W

**Welch's t-Test** - Variation of t-test that doesn't assume equal variances.

**Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test** - Non-parametric alternative to paired t-test.

**Within-Subjects Design** - See Repeated Measures.

---

## Z

**Z-Score** - Number of standard deviations a value is from the mean.

**Z-Test** - Statistical test using standard normal distribution; used when population SD is known.

## Agricultural/Aquaponic Terms

**Aquaponics** - Integration of aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation).

**Biofilter** - Component housing beneficial bacteria that convert fish waste (ammonia) into plant-available nutrients.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)** - Production in enclosed structures with environmental control (greenhouses, vertical farms).

**Coupled System** - Aquaponic system where fish and plant components share same water.

**Decoupled System** - Aquaponic system with separate water loops for fish and plants, connected through controlled nutrient transfer.

**Deep Water Culture (DWC)** - Hydroponic/aquaponic method where plant roots are suspended in aerated nutrient solution.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)** - Amount of oxygen dissolved in water; critical for fish and root health.

**Electrical Conductivity (EC)** - Measure of water's ability to conduct electricity; indicator of dissolved salt/nutrient concentration.

**Hydroponics** - Growing plants in nutrient solution without soil.

**Media Bed** - Growing method using inert media (gravel, clay pellets) to support plants and house biofilter.

**Nitrification** - Bacterial conversion of ammonia to nitrite, then nitrate.

**Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)** - Hydroponic method with thin film of nutrient solution flowing over roots.

**Stocking Density** - Mass of fish per unit volume of water (kg/m³) or growing area.

**TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)** - Total concentration of dissolved substances in water.
