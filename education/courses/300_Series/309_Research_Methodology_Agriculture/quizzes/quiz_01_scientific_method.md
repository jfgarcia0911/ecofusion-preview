## Multiple Choice (1 point each)

**1. What is the correct order of steps in the scientific method?**

a) Hypothesis → Observation → Experiment → Conclusion
b) Observation → Question → Hypothesis → Experiment → Analysis → Conclusion
c) Question → Experiment → Hypothesis → Observation
d) Experiment → Observation → Hypothesis → Question

**Answer:** b

---

**2. Which of the following is a testable hypothesis?**

a) Aquaponics is better than hydroponics
b) Fish are important for plant growth
c) Increasing fish stocking density from 10 to 20 kg/m³ will increase lettuce yield by at least 15%
d) Plants need nutrients to grow

**Answer:** c

---

**3. What is the difference between a null hypothesis (H₀) and an alternative hypothesis (H₁)?**

a) H₀ predicts an effect; H₁ predicts no effect
b) H₀ states no effect; H₁ states there is an effect
c) They are the same thing
d) H₀ is for experiments; H₁ is for observations

**Answer:** b

## Short Answer (2 points each)

**7. Distinguish between an observation and an inference. Provide an example of each from aquaponics.**

**Example Answer:**
- **Observation:** Direct measurement or sensory input. Example: "Water temperature is 78°F"
- **Inference:** Interpretation based on observation. Example: "The high temperature is causing fish stress"

---

**8. Why is replication important in agricultural research?**

**Example Answer:**
Replication allows researchers to estimate variability, account for random variation, increase statistical power, and determine if observed effects are consistent and reproducible rather than due to chance.

## Critical Thinking (2 points)

**9. A researcher observes that tomato plants near the entrance of their greenhouse produce smaller fruit than plants in the back. They hypothesize that the difference is due to temperature variation. Describe one potential confounding variable that could explain the difference, and explain how the researcher could control for it.**

**Example Answer:**
**Confounding variable:** Light exposure differences (entrance may have different shading patterns or door opening may let in more natural light)
**Control strategy:** Randomize plant locations across both areas, or block by location and include location as a factor in the experimental design, ensuring all treatments appear in both entrance and back areas.
