## Multiple Choice (1 point each)

**1. Completely Randomized Design (CRD) is most appropriate when:**

a) Experimental units are heterogeneous
b) There are obvious environmental gradients
c) Experimental units are homogeneous
d) You want to study interactions

**Answer:** c

---

**2. In a Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD), treatments are:**

a) Randomized across all units ignoring blocks
b) Assigned systematically within blocks
c) Randomly assigned within each block
d) Never randomized

**Answer:** c

---

**3. A Latin Square design controls for:**

a) One blocking factor
b) Two blocking factors
c) Three blocking factors
d) No blocking factors

**Answer:** b

---

**4. The main advantage of factorial designs over separate experiments is:**

a) They require fewer total experimental units
b) They can detect interaction effects
c) They are easier to analyze
d) They don't require replication

**Answer:** b

---

**5. In a 3×4 factorial design, how many treatment combinations are there?**

a) 3
b) 4
c) 7
d) 12

**Answer:** d

---

**6. Split-plot designs are used when:**

a) All factors are equally easy to randomize
b) Some factors are harder or more expensive to randomize than others
c) You want to avoid blocking
d) You have only one factor

**Answer:** b

---

**7. What does a significant interaction effect in a factorial experiment indicate?**

a) Both main effects are significant
b) The effect of one factor depends on the level of another factor
c) The factors are independent
d) The experiment failed

**Answer:** b

---

**8. Repeated measures designs are most appropriate for:**

a) Comparing independent groups
b) Single time-point measurements
c) Measurements over time on the same subjects
d) Destructive sampling

**Answer:** c

## Design Selection (2 points each)

**9. For each scenario, select the most appropriate design and briefly justify your choice:**

**Scenario A:** Testing 4 LED light spectrums on basil growth. You have 24 identical growth chambers in a controlled environment room.

**Scenario B:** Comparing 2 irrigation levels and 3 fertilizer types on strawberry yield.

**Example Answer:**

**Scenario A:**
**Design:** Completely Randomized Design (CRD)
**Justification:** Growth chambers are identical and in controlled environment (homogeneous conditions), so no blocking needed. Randomly assign 6 chambers to each of 4 light spectrum treatments.

**Scenario B:**
**Design:** 2×3 Factorial Design
**Justification:** Two factors (irrigation and fertilizer) with potential interaction effects. Factorial design allows testing both main effects and whether optimal fertilizer depends on irrigation level. Creates 6 treatment combinations.

---

**10. A researcher calculated the relative efficiency of RCBD vs. CRD and found RE = 145%. Interpret this result and explain what it means for the experimental design.**

**Example Answer:**
Relative efficiency of 145% means the RCBD was 45% more efficient than a CRD would have been. This indicates that blocking was effective in removing variation and improving precision. The MSE (error variance) in the RCBD was lower than in a CRD, meaning the experiment could detect smaller treatment differences with the same sample size. This validates the decision to use blocking and shows the blocking factor (e.g., greenhouse section, time period) did account for meaningful variation.

## Application (2 points)

**11. Design a 4×4 Latin Square for testing 4 hydroponic nutrient formulations (A, B, C, D) across 4 weeks (rows) and 4 greenhouse sections (columns). Show your layout and explain what sources of variation are controlled.**

**Example Answer:**

```
                Column 1   Column 2   Column 3   Column 4
             ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
    Week 1   │    A     │    B     │    C     │    D     │
             ├──────────┼──────────┼──────────┼──────────┤
    Week 2   │    B     │    C     │    D     │    A     │
             ├──────────┼──────────┼──────────┼──────────┤
    Week 3   │    C     │    D     │    A     │    B     │
             ├──────────┼──────────┼──────────┼──────────┤
    Week 4   │    D     │    A     │    B     │    C     │
             └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
```

**Controlled variation:**
- **Row effect (weeks):** Controls for temporal variation, seasonal changes, or learning effects over time
- **Column effect (greenhouse sections):** Controls for spatial variation like light intensity, temperature gradients, or proximity to equipment

Each treatment appears exactly once in each row and once in each column.
