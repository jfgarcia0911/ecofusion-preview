## Short Answer (2 points each)

**7. Explain the difference between a primary source and a secondary source in agricultural research. Provide one example of each.**

**Example Answer:**
- **Primary source:** Original research presenting first-hand data and novel findings. Example: A peer-reviewed article reporting experimental results on nutrient uptake in aquaponic lettuce.
- **Secondary source:** Summarizes or synthesizes information from primary sources. Example: A review article summarizing findings from multiple aquaponic studies.

---

**8. Why is synthesis more valuable than summary in a literature review?**

**Example Answer:**
Synthesis integrates and connects findings across multiple sources to identify patterns, contradictions, and gaps, providing deeper understanding. Summary merely restates what individual studies found without making connections. Synthesis demonstrates critical thinking and helps justify research needs, while summary is descriptive only.

## Critical Thinking (2 points)

**9. You find a study published in 2023 showing that a new nutrient supplement dramatically increases yield with no citation of previous research and no conflict of interest statement. The journal charges authors $2,000 to publish. What red flags do you see, and what would you do before citing this source?**

**Example Answer:**
**Red flags:**
- No citations (suggests poor scholarship or isolated claims)
- No conflict of interest statement (possible undisclosed financial ties)
- High publication fee (possible predatory journal)
- Extraordinary claims without supporting literature

**Actions before citing:**
- Check if journal is peer-reviewed and reputable
- Look for journal on predatory journal lists
- Verify authors' credentials and affiliations
- Search for corroborating research
- Consider alternative, more credible sources
