## Calculation (2 points)

**7. Calculate the required sample size per group to detect a 20g difference in lettuce yield. Assume standard deviation is 25g, desired power is 80%, and α=0.05 (use simplified formula: n = 16 × (σ²/δ²)).**

**Show your work.**

**Example Answer:**
```
Given:
σ = 25 g
δ = 20 g
Formula: n = 16 × (σ²/δ²)

n = 16 × (25²/20²)
n = 16 × (625/400)
n = 16 × 1.5625
n = 25

Answer: 25 plants per treatment group
```

## Short Answer (2 points each)

**8. Explain the difference between a confounding variable and a controlled variable. Provide an agricultural research example of each.**

**Example Answer:**
- **Controlled variable:** A factor deliberately held constant across all treatments to prevent it from affecting results. Example: Using the same plant variety across all nutrient treatments.
- **Confounding variable:** An uncontrolled factor that varies with the treatment and affects the outcome, making it impossible to determine causation. Example: Testing treatment A only in summer and treatment B only in winter—season is confounded with treatment.

---

**9. A researcher has 30 grow beds in a greenhouse with a clear temperature gradient from north (cool) to south (warm). They want to test 3 nutrient formulations with 10 replicates each. Describe how they should use blocking to improve the experiment.**

**Example Answer:**
Use Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) with 10 blocks of 3 grow beds each, positioned across the temperature gradient. Each block contains all three treatments (one of each nutrient formulation), randomly assigned within the block. This removes the temperature gradient effect from experimental error, making treatment comparisons more precise.

Example layout:
- Block 1 (North): [B][A][C]
- Block 2: [C][B][A]
- Block 3: [A][C][B]
...continuing through Block 10 (South)

## Critical Thinking (2 points)

**10. Identify all the design flaws in this proposed experiment: "A student wants to compare organic vs. synthetic fertilizer on tomato yield. They put organic fertilizer on 10 tomato plants on the sunny south bench and synthetic fertilizer on 10 tomato plants on the shaded north bench. They will weigh all tomatoes from all plants at the end and compare the totals." List at least 3 major problems and suggest improvements.**

**Example Answer:**
**Major flaws:**
1. **Confounding:** Light exposure (south sunny vs. north shaded) is confounded with fertilizer type—cannot determine if differences are due to fertilizer or light
2. **Pseudoreplication/wrong experimental unit:** Weighing all tomatoes together gives only n=1 per treatment (one total for organic, one for synthetic). Should analyze individual plants or average per plant.
3. **No randomization:** Systematic assignment of treatments to locations introduces bias
4. **No blocking:** If using both benches, should block by location

**Improvements:**
- Randomly assign both fertilizer types to both benches (half organic and half synthetic on each bench)
- Use individual plants as experimental units, measure separately (n=10 per treatment)
- Consider blocking by bench position if keeping both benches
- Include a control group with no fertilizer or standard fertilizer for baseline comparison
