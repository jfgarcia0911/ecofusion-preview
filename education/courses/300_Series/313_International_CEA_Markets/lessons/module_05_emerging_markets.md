## 5.1 Emerging Markets Overview

### Definition and Characteristics

**Emerging CEA Markets:**
- Early-stage industry development
- Growing but limited infrastructure
- Rising middle class demand
- Government interest developing
- Financing challenges
- Higher risk, higher potential returns

```

================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------

  (non-EU)
```

- First-mover advantages
- Growing urban demand

- Government incentives
- Lower labor costs
- Local production value

## 5.5 Financing Emerging Market Entry

### Funding Sources

**1. Development Finance Institutions (DFIs)**

| Institution | Focus | Typical Investment |
|-------------|-------|-------------------|
| **IFC** (World Bank) | Emerging markets, private sector | $2M-50M+ debt/equity |
| **IDB** (Inter-American) | Latin America | $1M-20M+ |
| **AfDB** (African Development Bank) | Africa | $2M-30M+ |
| **EBRD** (European Bank) | Eastern Europe, Central Asia | $5M-50M+ |
| **CDC** (UK Development Finance) | Africa, South Asia | $2M-25M+ |
| **FMO** (Dutch Development) | Emerging markets | $5M-50M |

**DFI Advantages:**
- Patient capital (longer terms)
- Risk tolerance (emerging markets)
- Below-market rates (development mandate)
- Technical assistance
- Credibility (other investors follow)

**DFI Requirements:**
- Development impact (jobs, food security)
- Environmental/social standards (IFC Performance Standards)
- Governance (anti-corruption)
- Financial sustainability
- Local partnerships preferred

**2. Blended Finance**

**Structure:**
```
Typical Blended Finance Stack:

1. Grant capital (10-20%): Government, foundations
   - Highest risk
   - Technical assistance
   - Catalytic

2. Concessional debt/equity (20-40%): DFIs
   - Below-market returns
   - Patient capital
   - Risk absorption

3. Commercial capital (40-70%): Private investors
   - Market-rate returns
   - Lower risk (protected by 1 & 2)
   - Largest portion
```

**Example Project:**
```
$10M Emerging Market Greenhouse Project:

$1M   Grant (Government aid agency)
      - Feasibility study
      - Training programs
      - Technical assistance

$3M   DFI Concessional Loan (IFC)
      - 5% interest
      - 10-year term
      - 2-year grace period

$6M   Commercial Investment
      - $3M Private equity (20% IRR target)
      - $3M Commercial debt (10% interest)

Total: $10M project financed
Risk: Distributed across capital stack
Return: Appropriate for each investor type
```

**3. Impact Investors**

**Characteristics:**
- Social/environmental + financial returns
- Patient capital (7-12 year hold)
- Emerging market focus
- Below-market expectations (10-15% vs. 20-25%)

**Examples:**
- Acumen Fund
- Root Capital
- Triodos Investment Management
- Oikocredit
- Agri-Business Capital Fund

**4. Government Grants and Incentives**

**Common Programs:**

| Region | Typical Programs | Value |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| Latin America | Tax holidays, land grants | Varies by country |
| Africa | Free trade zones, tax breaks | 5-10 years tax exemption |
| India | Subsidy programs (state level) | 25-50% capital subsidy |
| Southeast Asia | Investment incentives | Tax holidays, grants |

**5. Family Offices and HNWIs**

**Emerging Market Interest:**
- Impact + return
- Long-term horizon
- Patient capital
- Strategic value

**Approach:**
- Direct relationships
- Clear impact narrative
- Realistic returns (12-18%)
- Strong governance

### Risk Mitigation Tools

**1. Political Risk Insurance**

**Providers:**
- MIGA (World Bank)
- OPIC/DFC (US Development Finance)
- National ECAs (export credit agencies)

**Coverage:**
- Expropriation
- Political violence
- Currency inconvertibility
- Contract breach

**Cost:** 0.5-2% of insured amount annually

**2. Currency Hedging**

**Strategies:**
- Forward contracts (1-2 years)
- Natural hedges (local revenue, local costs)
- Hard currency pricing (where possible)
- Currency swaps

**Limitations:**
- Cost (3-7% annually)
- Availability (frontier currencies)
- Tenor (limited to 1-3 years)

**3. Structured Financing**

**Techniques:**
- Offshore holding companies
- Security over assets (legal considerations)
- Cross-default provisions
- Escrow accounts (hard currency)

## 5.6 Business Models for Emerging Markets

### Adapted Models

**1. Training and Technology Transfer**

**Structure:**
- Technology licensing
- Training programs (fee-based)
- Consulting services
- Ongoing support contracts

**Revenue:**
- Upfront licensing fee
- Training fees
- Ongoing royalties (3-7% of revenue)
- Service contracts

**Advantages:**
- Lower capital requirement
- Reduced risk exposure
- Knowledge leverage
- Scalable

**Example:**
```
Technology Transfer Agreement:

Upfront:    $200K licensing fee
Training:   $50K (2-week program, 10 people)
Royalties:  5% of revenue (minimum $20K/year)
Support:    $3K/month service contract

Year 1:     $286K revenue
Year 2-5:   $100K+/year ongoing
Investment: $100K (time, customization)
```

**2. Managed Services**

**Structure:**
- Partner builds facility (local capital)
- Foreign partner manages operations
- Fee-based management contract
- Performance incentives

**Revenue:**
- Base management fee (3-5% of revenue)
- Performance bonuses (profit share)
- Procurement fees (2-3% of purchases)

**Advantages:**
- No capital requirement (partner provides)
- Control of operations (quality)
- Scalable (manage multiple sites)
- Exit flexibility (terminate contract)

**Challenges:**
- Partner dependency
- Profit sharing (not ownership)
- Control limitations
- Contract enforcement

**3. Franchise Model**

**Structure:**
- Standardized system package
- Franchisee investment
- Training and support
- Brand licensing

**Revenue:**
- Franchise fee ($50K-200K)
- Ongoing royalty (5-8% revenue)
- Equipment sales (markup)
- Support services

**Requirements:**
- Proven, standardized system
- Replicable processes
- Strong support infrastructure
- Brand value

**4. Equity Joint Venture**

**Structure:**
- Foreign partner: Technology, expertise (30-49%)
- Local partner: Capital, market access (51-70%)
- Shared governance
- Profit sharing per ownership

**Advantages:**
- Aligned incentives (ownership)
- Local partner commitment
- Market access
- Risk sharing

**Challenges:**
- Partnership management
- Governance complexity
- Exit planning critical
- Cultural fit essential

## 5.7 Success Factors in Emerging Markets

### Critical Elements

**1. Local Partnerships**

**Partner Selection Criteria:**
| Factor | Importance | Evaluation |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Financial capacity | Very High | Audited financials, credit check |
| Market access | Very High | Distribution, relationships |
| Reputation | High | References, background |
| Technical capability | Medium | Can be developed |
| Cultural fit | High | Extended courtship |
| Government connections | High | Track record |

**Partnership Red Flags:**
- Unrealistic promises
- Pressure for fast decisions
- Unwillingness to share information
- Poor references
- Financial opacity
- Weak governance

**2. Adaptation and Flexibility**

**Areas Requiring Adaptation:**
- **Technology**: Simplified, more resilient
- **Crops**: Local preferences
- **Pricing**: Market-appropriate
- **Processes**: Infrastructure constraints
- **Timeline**: Patience required
- **Business model**: Market fit

**3. Phased Investment**

**Recommended Approach:**
```
Phase 1: Pilot (Year 1-2)
- Investment: $500K-2M
- Scale: Small (proof of concept)
- Goal: Learn, adapt, prove
- Location: One site

Phase 2: Expansion (Year 2-4)
- Investment: $2M-10M
- Scale: Commercial scale
- Goal: Profitability, refinement
- Location: 2-3 sites

Phase 3: Scale (Year 4-7)
- Investment: $10M-50M+
- Scale: Regional presence
- Goal: Market leadership
- Location: Multiple sites, countries
```

**4. Infrastructure Resilience**

**Essential Backup Systems:**

**Power:**
- Generator capacity: 100% backup
- Solar + battery (where viable)
- Uninterruptible power supply (UPS)
- Fuel storage (1-2 weeks)

**Water:**
- Storage tanks (1-2 weeks supply)
- Water treatment systems
- Backup well (where possible)
- Rainwater capture

**Internet:**
- Satellite backup
- Multiple ISPs
- Local data storage

**Budget Impact:**
```
Infrastructure Costs (vs. developed markets):
Developed market: 10-15% of capex for backup
Emerging market:  30-50% of capex for backup

Example $5M project:
Developed: $500K-750K backup systems
Emerging:  $1.5M-2.5M backup systems
```

**5. Patient Capital and Realistic Expectations**

**Timeline Expectations:**

| Milestone | Developed Market | Emerging Market |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Permits and approvals | 6-12 months | 12-24 months |
| Construction | 12-18 months | 18-30 months |
| Market acceptance | 12-24 months | 24-48 months |
| Profitability | 3-5 years | 5-8 years |
| Exit opportunity | 5-7 years | 7-12 years |

**Return Expectations:**
- Developed markets: 15-20% IRR
- Emerging markets: 20-30% IRR (higher risk)
- Frontier markets: 25-35% IRR

## Key Takeaways

1. **Emerging Markets**: High potential, higher risk, patient capital required
2. **Latin America**: $1-2B market, Brazil and Chile lead, infrastructure better than Africa
3. **Africa**: $500M-1B, massive long-term potential, infrastructure challenges significant
4. **Financing**: DFIs, blended finance, impact investors key sources
5. **Business Models**: Adapt to market - technology transfer, managed services, JVs
6. **Success Factors**: Local partnerships, phased investment, resilience, patience
7. **Timeline**: 2-3x longer than developed markets for all milestones

## Case Study: Emerging Market Technology Transfer

**Scenario**: Dutch greenhouse technology company entering East Africa

**Approach**:
1. Pilot in Kenya (flower to food diversification)
2. Partner with local flower exporter
3. Technology transfer + training
4. Management services contract
5. Success → expand to Rwanda, Ethiopia

**Outcomes**:
- Year 1: $150K revenue (training, licensing)
- Year 2-3: $200K annual (management fees, royalties)
- Year 4: Second market entry (Ethiopia)
- Year 5: Equity stake option exercised

**Lessons**:
- Start small, prove model
- Partner selection critical
- Adapt technology to context
- Patient capital essential
- Phased commitment reduces risk
