
## 12.3 Sequencing and Timing

### Entry Sequence Strategy

**Waterfall Approach (Sequential):**

```
Timeline:
Year 1: Market A (UAE)
  - Full focus, resources concentrated
  - Prove model in one market
  - Learn and adapt

Year 2: Market B (Singapore)
  - Leverage UAE learnings
  - Maintain UAE operations
  - Two-market operations

Year 3: Market C (Germany)
  - Three-market portfolio
  - Regional diversification
  - Established international capability

Advantages:
✓ Focused execution (one market at a time)
✓ Resource efficiency (no over-stretch)
✓ Learning application (each informs next)
✓ Lower risk (sequential de-risking)

Disadvantages:
✗ Slower growth (patience required)
✗ Opportunity cost (delayed markets)
✗ Single-market dependency (early years)
```

**Parallel Approach (Simultaneous):**

```
Timeline:
Year 1: Markets A, B, C simultaneously
  - UAE, Singapore, Germany all start
  - Rapid global footprint
  - Parallel execution

Advantages:
✓ Fast global presence (brand, credibility)
✓ First-mover advantage (multiple markets)
✓ Revenue growth (faster)
✓ Portfolio diversification (immediate)

Disadvantages:
✗ Resource strain (team, capital, attention)
✗ Execution risk (harder to manage multiple)
✗ Limited learning transfer (no sequential application)
✗ Higher capital requirement (all at once)

When Appropriate:
- Large, well-capitalized companies
- Experienced international management
- Similar markets (minimal adaptation needed)
- Competitive urgency (need to move fast)
```

**Hybrid Approach (Recommended for Most):**

```
Timeline:
Year 1: Market A (UAE) - Direct investment
        Market B (Japan) - Technology licensing (low intensity)

Year 2: Market A expansion
        Market B operations
        Market C (Germany) - Direct investment

Year 3: Markets A, B, C all operating
        Market D (Kenya) - Pilot project
        Markets E, F (Mexico, South Korea) - Licensing

Rationale:
→ Mix of high-touch (direct investment) and low-touch (licensing)
→ Sequential for complex markets, parallel for simpler
→ Diversify by entry mode (reduce resource constraints)
→ Build portfolio with varying timelines to revenue

Balance:
- 2-3 direct investment markets (sequenced)
- 3-5 licensing/franchise markets (can be parallel)
- 1-2 pilot/exploration markets (low commitment)
- Total: 6-10 markets over 5 years
```

## 12.4 Market Entry Planning

### Market Entry Plan Template

**Structure:**

```
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (2 pages)
   - Market opportunity
   - Entry strategy
   - Financial projections
   - Key risks and mitigation
   - Go/no-go recommendation

2. MARKET ANALYSIS (10-15 pages)
   - Market size and growth
   - Customer segments
   - Competitive landscape
   - Regulatory environment
   - Infrastructure assessment
   - SWOT analysis

3. ENTRY STRATEGY (5-10 pages)
   - Entry mode selection (and rationale)
   - Partnership approach (if applicable)
   - Product/service offering
   - Positioning and pricing
   - Go-to-market approach

4. OPERATIONAL PLAN (10-15 pages)
   - Facility/operations plan
   - Supply chain and logistics
   - Organizational structure
   - Staffing and recruitment
   - Technology and systems
   - Quality and compliance

5. FINANCIAL PLAN (5-10 pages)
   - Investment requirements
   - Revenue projections (5 years)
   - Cost structure
   - Profitability timeline
   - Cash flow analysis
   - Sensitivity analysis
   - ROI and IRR

6. IMPLEMENTATION PLAN (5 pages)
   - Milestones and timeline (Gantt chart)
   - Responsibilities (RACI matrix)
   - Resource requirements
   - Critical path
   - Dependencies

7. RISK MANAGEMENT (5 pages)
   - Risk register (identify, assess, mitigate)
   - Political and economic risks
   - Operational risks
   - Financial risks
   - Mitigation strategies
   - Contingency plans

8. APPENDICES
   - Market research data
   - Competitive intelligence
   - Financial models (detailed)
   - Partnership term sheets
   - Legal/regulatory summaries
```

### Talent Strategy

**Staffing Approach:**

```
Expatriate (Home Country → Foreign Assignment):
Roles:
- General Manager (initial 2-3 years)
- Technical experts (startup phase)
- Knowledge transfer (training local team)

Advantages:
✓ Company culture transfer
✓ Trusted leadership (known quantity)
✓ Technical expertise
✓ Direct communication (language, culture)

Disadvantages:
✗ Expensive (2-3x home country cost)
✗ Cultural challenges (may struggle locally)
✗ Family adjustment (spouse, children)
✗ Limited tenure (1-3 years typical)

Best Practice:
→ Use for startup and knowledge transfer
→ Plan transition to local leadership (18-36 months)
→ Support well (housing, schooling, cultural training)

Local Nationals (Hire in Foreign Market):
Roles:
- Most staff (operations, sales, admin)
- Eventually management (if capable)

Advantages:
✓ Cultural fit (understand local market)
✓ Language (native)
✓ Cost-effective (local market rates)
✓ Government preference (employment targets)
✓ Long-term (career opportunity locally)

Disadvantages:
✗ Company culture (need development)
✗ Technical skills (may need training)
✗ International experience (may be limited)

Best Practice:
→ Invest in training and development
→ Rotate to HQ (cultural immersion)
→ Clear career paths (retention)
→ Competitive compensation (local market)

Third-Country Nationals (From Another Foreign Market):
Roles:
- Regional roles (multiple countries)
- Specialized expertise (scarce skills)

Example:
- Dutch greenhouse expert → UAE operations
- Japanese precision agriculture specialist → USA
- Australian sales manager → Southeast Asia region

Advantages:
✓ Specialized expertise (best from anywhere)
✓ International experience (been there before)
✓ Regional perspective (neutral between HQ and local)
✓ Cost (may be lower than HQ expat)

Use When:
→ Specific expertise not available locally or from HQ
→ Regional role (multiple countries)
→ Neutral location advantage (not HQ, not local)
```

## 12.7 Scaling Strategy

### From One to Many Markets

**Scaling Challenges:**

```
CHALLENGE: Maintaining Quality Across Markets
Solution:
→ Standardized operating procedures (global SOPs)
→ Quality management system (ISO or similar)
→ Central training program (all markets)
→ Regular audits (HQ team visits)
→ Technology enablement (remote monitoring)

CHALLENGE: Organizational Capacity
Solution:
→ Phased expansion (not too fast)
→ Talent development (grow capabilities)
→ Strategic hiring (international expertise)
→ Systems and processes (automation, standardization)
→ Partnership leverage (don't do everything yourself)

CHALLENGE: Capital Requirements
Solution:
→ Diversified financing (equity, debt, DFIs, grants)
→ Partnerships (share investment burden)
→ Licensing (capital-light expansion)
→ Phased investment (prove before scaling)
→ Cash flow from early markets (fund new markets)

CHALLENGE: Cultural Complexity
Solution:
→ Regional leadership (cultural expertise)
→ Local partnerships (navigation support)
→ Cultural training (all international staff)
→ Adaptation framework (global standard, local flexibility)
→ Learning networks (markets share best practices)
```

### Replication Framework

**Standardization vs. Adaptation:**

```
STANDARDIZE (Global Consistency):
✓ Technology core (growing systems, sensors)
✓ Quality standards (defect rates, safety)
✓ Financial controls (reporting, approval thresholds)
✓ Brand identity (logo, core messaging)
✓ Values and culture (company principles)
✓ Safety and compliance (non-negotiable minimums)

ADAPT (Local Flexibility):
→ Crop selection (local preferences)
→ Facility design (climate-specific)
→ Pricing (market dynamics)
→ Marketing messaging (cultural relevance)
→ Staffing (local labor markets)
→ Go-to-market (distribution channels)

Framework:
1. Define global standards (what must be consistent)
2. Define adaptation zones (where local flexibility OK)
3. Approval process (when to deviate from standard)
4. Learning loop (local innovations → global standards)
```

## 12.8 Exit and Portfolio Management

### Exit Strategy Planning

**Why Plan Exits from the Beginning:**

```
Reasons to Exit a Market:
- Underperformance (persistent losses, no path to profitability)
- Strategic pivot (focus on better opportunities)
- Partnership breakdown (irreconcilable differences)
- Regulatory changes (market no longer viable)
- Political risk (instability, expropriation threat)
- Better opportunities (capital reallocation)

Exit Options:

1. Sale to Partner (Joint Venture)
   - Trigger: Buy-sell clause, performance failure
   - Timeline: 3-6 months
   - Outcome: Cash exit, clean break

2. Sale to Third Party
   - Process: Find buyer, negotiate, close
   - Timeline: 6-12 months
   - Outcome: Market value (if profitable)

3. Wind Down
   - Process: Cease operations, liquidate assets
   - Timeline: 3-6 months
   - Outcome: Salvage value only (last resort)

4. License/Franchise Conversion
   - Process: Sell operations, retain royalty stream
   - Timeline: 6-12 months
   - Outcome: Convert equity to royalty, reduce risk

Exit Planning Checklist:
☐ Exit provisions in partnership agreements (from start)
☐ Separate legal entities (easier to sell/close)
☐ Asset ownership clear (who owns what)
☐ IP protection (retained by HQ, licensed to local)
☐ Customer contracts (assignment provisions)
☐ Employee obligations (severance, notice)
☐ Financial reporting (clean books, audited if possible)
```

### Portfolio Optimization

**Ongoing Portfolio Review:**

```
Annual Portfolio Assessment:

For Each Market:

1. Financial Performance
   - Revenue vs. target
   - Profitability vs. target
   - Cash flow
   - ROI (actual vs. projected)

2. Strategic Fit
   - Aligns with global strategy?
   - Market size/growth as expected?
   - Competitive position improving?

3. Execution Quality
   - Operational metrics (yield, quality, cost)
   - Team capability
   - Partnership health (if applicable)

4. Risk Profile
   - Political/economic stability
   - Regulatory changes
   - Partnership risks
   - Reputational risks

Classification:

INVEST (Green):
- Meeting or exceeding expectations
- Strategic importance high
- Action: Additional investment, scale

MAINTAIN (Yellow):
- Mixed performance, solvable issues
- Strategic importance moderate
- Action: Improve execution, monitor closely

HARVEST (Orange):
- Underperforming, limited upside
- Strategic importance low
- Action: Minimize investment, maximize cash flow, consider exit

DIVEST (Red):
- Persistent underperformance
- Strategic misalignment
- Action: Exit (sell or wind down)

Reallocation:
→ Capital from Harvest/Divest → Invest markets
→ Management attention → Invest markets
→ Portfolio optimization (annually)
```

## 12.9 Comprehensive Strategy Example

### Case Study: GreenTech Global Expansion

**Company Profile:**
- Current: $60M revenue, USA only
- Goal: $150M revenue, 5 countries by 2030
- Capability: Vertical farming technology

**5-Year Strategy:**

```
PHASE 1 (Years 1-2): FOUNDATION

Year 1:
Markets:
  - UAE (Dubai): Direct investment (JV 50/50)
    * Investment: $15M (50% share = $7.5M)
    * Mode: High-tech greenhouse, 5 ha
    * Timeline: 24 months to operations
    * Target: $8M revenue (Year 3)

  - Japan: Technology licensing
    * Upfront: $300K
    * Royalty: 5% of revenue
    * Partner: Japanese agricultural corporation
    * Timeline: 12 months to first royalties
    * Target: $200K/year royalties

Organizational:
  - Hire VP International
  - Establish international finance capability
  - International legal counsel (retainer)

Investment: $8M (UAE $7.5M + Japan setup $0.5M)
Revenue Impact: $0 (Year 1), $200K (Year 2), $2M+ (Year 3)

PHASE 2 (Years 2-3): EXPANSION

Year 2:
Markets:
  - Germany: Acquisition
    * Target: Mid-size greenhouse operator
    * Acquisition cost: $20M
    * Immediate revenue: $15M
    * Value: Market access, team, customers

  - Singapore: Technology partnership
    * Provide systems to local developer
    * Equipment sales: $5M
    * Ongoing support: $500K/year
    * Showcase for Asia

Year 3:
Markets:
  - Mexico: Franchise model
    * Franchisee invests in facility
    * Franchise fee: $200K
    * Ongoing royalty: 6% of revenue
    * Low capital requirement (franchisee funds)

  - Kenya: Pilot project (test for Africa)
    * Small scale: $2M investment
    * Development finance: 50% grant/concessional
    * Learning opportunity

Organizational:
  - Regional VP (Europe/Middle East)
  - Regional VP (Asia-Pacific)
  - International operations team (5 people)

Investment: $32M (Germany $20M, Singapore $2M, Kenya $1M, Mexico $0.2M)
Revenue Impact: Year 3: $40M international

PHASE 3 (Years 4-5): SCALE & OPTIMIZE

Year 4-5:
- Scale existing markets (UAE, Germany second facilities)
- Add 2-3 licensing markets (South Korea, Australia, Chile)
- Optimize and improve profitability
- Portfolio management (exit underperformers if any)

Total Investment (5 years): $40M
International Revenue (Year 5): $70M
International EBITDA (Year 5): $14M (20% margin)
Markets: 6-8 operating
Entry Modes: Mix (2 direct, 1 acquisition, 3-5 licensing/franchise)
Total Company Revenue (Year 5): $130M (domestic + international)

Strategic Objectives Achieved:
✓ Geographic diversification (multiple continents)
✓ Revenue growth (significant)
✓ Technology commercialization (licensing revenue)
✓ Risk mitigation (portfolio of markets)
✓ Organizational capability (international competence)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Strategic Clarity**: Define clear objectives for internationalization (revenue, diversification, learning)
2. **Market Selection**: Systematic evaluation and prioritization (not opportunistic)
3. **Sequencing**: Phased approach for most companies (waterfall or hybrid, not pure parallel)
4. **Entry Planning**: Comprehensive plans reduce risk (don't wing it)
5. **Capabilities**: Build organizational competence (people, processes, systems)
6. **Performance Management**: Rigorous KPIs and governance (what gets measured gets managed)
7. **Portfolio Approach**: Manage as portfolio, optimize continuously (winners and losers, reallocate)
8. **Exit Planning**: Plan exits from day one (not an afterthought)
