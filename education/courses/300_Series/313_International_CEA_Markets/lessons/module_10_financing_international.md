## 10.2 Equity Financing

### Venture Capital and Private Equity

**VC Characteristics:**

```
Typical VC Investment:
Stage:          Series A to Series C
Amount:         $5M - $50M
Ownership:      15-30%
Return Target:  10x return, 3-7 year exit
Sector Focus:   Technology-enabled, scalable
Geography:      Developed markets preferred

CEA Fit:
✓ Technology-differentiated vertical farms
✓ Strong unit economics
✓ Scalable model (10+ units)
✓ Large addressable market
✗ Single-site greenhouses (not scalable enough)
✗ Commodity production (no differentiation)
✗ Emerging markets (too risky for most VCs)
```

**PE Characteristics:**

```
Typical PE Investment:
Stage:          Growth, mature companies
Amount:         $25M - $500M+
Ownership:      Majority (60-100%) or significant minority
Return Target:  3-5x return, 5-7 year exit
Sector Focus:   Proven business, cash flow positive
Geography:      Flexible (including emerging markets)

CEA Fit:
✓ Established greenhouse operations (cash flowing)
✓ Platform for consolidation
✓ Operational improvement opportunity
✓ Clear exit path (strategic buyer, IPO)
```

**Recent CEA Investment Examples:**

| Company | Type | Amount | Investor Type | Year |
|---------|------|--------|---------------|------|
| Plenty | VC | $400M+ cumulative | SoftBank, others | 2017-2023 |
| Infarm | VC | $600M+ cumulative | LGT, Atomico, others | 2018-2021 |
| AppHarvest | SPAC/IPO | $475M | Public markets | 2021 |
| Pure Harvest | Growth | $180M | Cargill, others | 2023 |
| Village Farms | PE | Multiple rounds | Public + institutional | Ongoing |

**Trends (2024-2025):**
- Focus shifting from pure growth to profitability
- Later-stage rounds more challenging
- Strategic investors more prominent
- Greenhouse consolidation (PE interest)
- Vertical farm funding constrained (after failures)

### Strategic Investors

**Types:**

```
1. Downstream (Customers):
   - Retailers (secure supply, innovation)
   - Food service (dedicated production)
   - Example: Kroger investing in vertical farm

2. Upstream (Suppliers):
   - Equipment manufacturers (sales + equity)
   - Seed companies (genetics + equity)
   - Example: Signify (lighting) investing in growers

3. Related Industry:
   - Agricultural companies (diversification)
   - Food companies (vertical integration)
   - Example: Cargill investing in CEA

4. Cross-Industry:
   - Real estate (asset value)
   - Technology (AI, automation application)
   - Utility companies (demand management)
```

**Strategic Investment Terms:**

```
Typical Structure:
Investment:     $5M - $50M
Ownership:      Minority (10-25%)
Board seat:     Often included
Strategic value:
  - Customer commitments (offtake agreements)
  - Technology access
  - Distribution channels
  - Brand validation

Advantages:
✓ Capital + strategic benefits
✓ Patient capital (longer horizon)
✓ Market access
✓ Credibility

Disadvantages:
✗ Potential conflicts (if competitor customers)
✗ Control limitations (may restrict future options)
✗ May limit future strategic options
```

## 10.3 Debt Financing

### Commercial Bank Lending

**Typical Terms:**

```
Working Capital Line:
Amount:         $500K - $5M
Term:           1 year (revolving)
Rate:           Prime + 2-4% (6-10% total)
Collateral:     Accounts receivable, inventory
Covenants:      Cash flow, working capital minimums
Personal Guarantee: Often required (smaller companies)

Equipment Financing:
Amount:         $1M - $20M
Term:           5-7 years
Rate:           5-9%
Collateral:     Equipment (asset-backed)
LTV:            60-80% of equipment value
Balloon:        Possible (refinance or buy out)

Real Estate/Facility Financing:
Amount:         $5M - $50M+
Term:           10-20 years
Rate:           4-8%
Collateral:     Real estate (mortgage)
LTV:            60-75%
Covenants:      Debt service coverage ratio >1.25x
```

**Requirements:**

```
Documentation:
☐ Business plan (3-5 year projections)
☐ Financial statements (3 years historical)
☐ Tax returns (business + personal)
☐ Proof of equity (your investment)
☐ Collateral valuation
☐ Personal financial statement
☐ Credit report (business + personal)

Credit Criteria:
- EBITDA positive (or clear path within 12-18 months)
- Debt service coverage ratio >1.25x
- Equity contribution: 20-40%
- Strong personal credit (FICO >680)
- Industry experience
- Collateral sufficient (LTV)
```

**International Considerations:**

```
Challenges:
✗ Foreign entity (may not lend)
✗ Currency risk (USD loan, local revenue)
✗ Political risk
✗ Collateral enforcement difficulty
✗ Credit information limited

Solutions:
✓ Local banks (in host country)
✓ International banks (HSBC, Citi, Standard Chartered)
✓ Loan guarantees (parent company)
✓ Currency hedging
✓ Development finance (see below)
```

### Project Finance

**Structure:**

```
Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) Model:

            ┌─────────────────────────────┐
            │   Project Company (SPV)     │
            │   $30M Greenhouse Project   │
            └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                           │
         ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
         │                 │                 │
    ┌────▼────┐      ┌────▼────┐      ┌────▼────┐
    │ Equity  │      │ Sr Debt │      │ Jr Debt │
    │  $6M    │      │  $20M   │      │  $4M    │
    │  20%    │      │  67%    │      │  13%    │
    │ (Sponsor)│     │ (Bank)  │      │ (Mezz)  │
    └─────────┘      └─────────┘      └─────────┘

Project Cash Flow → Debt Service → Equity Returns

Non-Recourse: Lenders only have claim on project assets
               (not sponsor's other assets)
```

**Typical Project Finance Terms:**

```
Senior Debt (Bank):
Amount:         60-70% of project cost
Term:           10-15 years
Rate:           SOFR/LIBOR + 3-5%
Security:       First lien on all assets
Covenants:      DSCR >1.35x, reserve accounts
Grace period:   Construction + 6-12 months

Mezzanine/Subordinated Debt:
Amount:         10-20% of project cost
Term:           10-12 years (shorter than senior)
Rate:           10-15%
Security:       Subordinated to senior debt
Features:       Equity kicker (warrants), PIK interest possible

Equity:
Amount:         20-30% of project cost
Returns:        15-25% IRR target
Risk:           Highest (last in cash flow waterfall)
Control:        Full (sponsor)
```

**Project Finance Due Diligence:**

```
Lender Requirements:
1. Market Study
   - Demand analysis (off-take agreements preferred)
   - Competitive assessment
   - Pricing validation

2. Technical Feasibility
   - Independent engineer review
   - Technology proven (not experimental)
   - Construction plan (EPC contract)
   - Operating plan (experienced operator)

3. Financial Model
   - 20-year projections
   - Sensitivity analysis
   - Multiple scenarios
   - Debt service coverage (1.35x+ required)

4. Legal Structure
   - SPV incorporation
   - Security documentation
   - Permits and licenses
   - Project agreements (EPC, O&M, off-take)

5. Risk Allocation
   - Construction risk (EPC contractor)
   - Operating risk (operator, insurance)
   - Market risk (off-take agreements)
   - Political risk (insurance if needed)

Timeline: 6-12 months (due diligence + documentation)
Cost: 2-5% of loan amount (legal, advisory, engineering)
```

### Accessing DFI Financing

**Process:**

```
Step 1: Pre-Qualification (1-3 months)
- Initial concept note (5-10 pages)
- High-level financials
- Development impact narrative
- Sponsor background
→ DFI feedback: Proceed or not aligned

Step 2: Due Diligence (3-6 months)
- Full business plan
- Financial model (detailed)
- Environmental & Social Impact Assessment (ESIA)
- Technical feasibility study
- Legal structure
→ DFI internal approval process

Step 3: Board Approval (1-3 months)
- Final documentation
- DFI board presentation
- Approval decision
→ Board approval or rejection

Step 4: Documentation (2-4 months)
- Legal agreements (loan, security, covenants)
- Environmental & Social Action Plan (ESAP)
- Disbursement conditions
→ Financial close

Total Timeline: 12-18 months (from initial contact to first disbursement)
```

**Tips for DFI Applications:**

```
✓ Strong development narrative (jobs, food security, environment)
✓ Additionality (IFC filling a gap commercial won't)
✓ Demonstration effect (pioneering, replicable model)
✓ Local partnerships (not just foreign import)
✓ Environmental credentials (sustainability, water, emissions)
✓ Quality sponsor (financial strength, experience)
✓ Commercial viability (not charity, bankable project)
✓ Patience (12-18 month process)

✗ Weak development case (only financial returns)
✗ High environmental/social risk
✗ Weak sponsor
✗ Experimental technology (unproven)
✗ Short-term capital need (DFIs are slow)
```

## 10.5 Blended Finance

### Structure and Approach

**Blended Finance Model:**

```
Capital Stack Example ($20M Project):

Layer 1: Grant (10%)
  - Source: Government aid agency, foundation
  - Amount: $2M
  - Use: Technical assistance, feasibility, first-loss
  - Terms: Non-repayable
  - Purpose: De-risk, enable commercial capital

Layer 2: Concessional Debt/Equity (30%)
  - Source: DFI (IFC, CDC, etc.)
  - Amount: $6M
  - Terms: Below-market rate (5% vs. 12% market)
  - Purpose: Absorb risk, crowd in commercial

Layer 3: Commercial Debt (40%)
  - Source: Commercial banks
  - Amount: $8M
  - Terms: Market rate (10-12%)
  - Purpose: Majority capital at market terms
  - Enabled by: Layers 1 & 2 de-risking

Layer 4: Equity (20%)
  - Source: Sponsor, impact investors
  - Amount: $4M
  - Returns: 18-25% IRR target
  - Purpose: Sponsor skin in game
  - Risk: Highest, but protected by others

Total: $20M with risk distributed across capital stack
Result: Project viable that wouldn't be with commercial capital alone
```

**Real-World Example:**

```
Greenhouse Project, Ethiopia

Total Cost:          $15M
Grant (USAID):       $1.5M (10%) - Feasibility, training
DFI (IFC):           $5M (33%) - 6% debt
Commercial Bank:     $6M (40%) - 11% debt
Sponsor Equity:      $2.5M (17%) - 20%+ IRR target

Without Blended Finance:
Commercial bank would require:
- 40% equity ($6M - sponsor can't provide)
- 14% interest (not viable)
- Shorter term (7 years vs. 12)
→ Project not feasible

With Blended Finance:
- Grant covers pre-development risk
- DFI provides patient, cheap capital
- Commercial bank comfortable (IFC participation)
- Returns viable for all parties
→ Project proceeds

Development Outcome:
- 180 jobs created
- 5,000 tons/year production
- Import substitution
- Technology demonstration (others follow)
- Food security contribution
```

## 10.6 Currency and Financial Risk Management

### Currency Risk

**Types of Currency Exposure:**

```
1. Transaction Risk:
   - USD equipment purchase, local currency revenue
   - Example: Buy $5M equipment, sell produce in KES (Kenyan Shilling)

2. Translation Risk:
   - Consolidating foreign subsidiary financials
   - Example: UAE subsidiary AED profits → USD parent reporting

3. Economic Risk:
   - Long-term competitiveness due to exchange rates
   - Example: Mexico production costs rise vs. USD (MXN weakens)
```

**Currency Hedge Mechanisms:**

```
1. Forward Contracts:
   Lock in exchange rate for future transaction

   Example:
   Today:  Need to pay $2M for equipment in 6 months
   Concern: USD strengthening (local currency weakening)
   Solution: Forward contract at 1.20 (lock in rate)
   Cost: 1-3% of transaction

   6 Months Later:
   Market rate: 1.35 (currency weakened 12.5%)
   Your rate: 1.20 (locked in)
   Savings: 11% ($220K on $2M transaction)

2. Natural Hedges:
   Match currency of revenues and costs

   Example:
   - Borrow in local currency (if revenues in local)
   - Purchase inputs locally (vs. import)
   - Price in hard currency (if customers accept)

   Result: Currency moves affect revenues and costs similarly

3. Currency Options:
   Right (not obligation) to exchange at set rate

   Example:
   Buy USD call option (right to buy USD at 1.20)
   If USD strengthens: Exercise option (save money)
   If USD weakens: Don't exercise (benefit from weak USD)
   Cost: Premium (2-5% of notional)

   Use: When direction uncertain, want protection + upside
```

**Currency Strategy by Market:**

```
Stable Currency Markets (USA, EU, Japan):
- Minimal hedging needed
- Natural hedges sufficient
- Forward contracts for large transactions

Moderate Volatility (Mexico, South Africa, Brazil):
- Natural hedges primary strategy
- Forward contracts for major capex
- Maintain local currency debt (match revenues)
- Review hedging quarterly

High Volatility / Emerging Markets (Nigeria, Argentina, etc.):
- Hard currency pricing where possible
- Offshore accounts (hard currency)
- Short forward contracts (3-6 months max)
- Natural hedges critical
- Avoid long-term local currency debt (inflation)
```

### Political Risk Management

**Political Risk Insurance:**

```
Coverage Types:

1. Expropriation:
   - Government seizes assets
   - Nationalization
   - Creeping expropriation (regulation destroys value)

2. Political Violence:
   - War, civil unrest
   - Terrorism
   - Strikes

3. Currency Inconvertibility:
   - Cannot convert local currency to hard currency
   - Transfer restrictions
   - Capital controls

4. Contract Breach:
   - Government breaches contract
   - No judicial recourse
   - Breach by state-owned entity

Providers:
- MIGA (World Bank)
- OPIC/DFC (US government)
- National ECAs (export credit agencies)
- Private insurers (Lloyd's, AIG, etc.)

Cost: 0.5-2% of insured value per year
Coverage: Up to 90% of investment
Term: Up to 15-20 years

Example:
Investment: $20M (Nigeria)
Coverage: Expropriation, currency inconvertibility
Amount: $18M (90%)
Term: 15 years
Premium: $200K/year (1% of insured amount)
```

**Practical Risk Mitigation:**

```
Structural Protections:
✓ Offshore holding company (neutral jurisdiction)
✓ Offshore accounts (receive hard currency payments)
✓ International arbitration clause (ICSID, ICC)
✓ Government agreements (investment protection)
✓ Local partner (political connections)
✓ Community engagement (local support)

Operational Protections:
✓ Maintain control of IP (license, not transfer)
✓ Phased investment (prove model before scaling)
✓ Insurance (political risk coverage)
✓ Diversification (multiple countries)
✓ Exit strategy (clear from beginning)

Monitoring:
✓ Political risk monitoring services
✓ Embassy/consulate relationships
✓ Industry associations
✓ Legal counsel (local + international)
✓ Regular risk assessments (quarterly)
```

## 10.7 Financial Structuring Examples

### Case Study 1: Netherlands Expansion (Low Risk)

```
Project: 10 hectare greenhouse, Netherlands
Cost: €40M

Financing Structure:

Bank Debt (60%):         €24M
  - Term: 15 years
  - Rate: 3.5%
  - Collateral: Facility mortgage
  - Covenants: DSCR >1.25x

Government Grant (10%):  €4M
  - Source: Dutch innovation fund
  - Use: Renewable energy (geothermal, solar)
  - Non-repayable

  - From: Company retained earnings + PE investor
  - Returns: 15% IRR target

Risk Level: Low (stable country, proven model)
Currency Risk: Minimal (EUR stable)
Political Risk: None
```

### Case Study 2: UAE Expansion (Medium Risk)

```
Project: 6 hectare high-tech greenhouse, Abu Dhabi
Cost: $35M

Financing Structure:

  - Term: 12 years, 2-year grace
  - Rate: 5%
  - Purpose: Anchor financing, mobilize others

Commercial Debt (30%):   $10.5M (Local UAE bank)
  - Term: 10 years
  - Rate: 7%
  - Enabled by: IFC participation

  - Use: Technology import, training
  - Condition: Local hiring

Equity (25%):            $9M
  - Sponsor: $4M
  - Local partner: $5M (strategic investor)

Risk Level: Medium (government-backed, but foreign jurisdiction)
Currency Risk: Low (AED pegged to USD)

```

### Case Study 3: Kenya Expansion (High Risk)

```
Project: 3 hectare greenhouse, Nairobi outskirts
Cost: $12M

Financing Structure:

Grant (15%):             $1.8M
  - Source: USAID, EU development funds
  - Use: Feasibility, training, first-loss capital

DFI Debt (40%):          $4.8M (IFC + AfDB)
  - Term: 12 years, 3-year grace
  - Rate: 6%
  - Currency: USD with KES option (70/30)

Local Debt (15%):        $1.8M (Kenyan bank)
  - Term: 7 years
  - Rate: 12% (local rates)
  - Purpose: Working capital, local purchases

Equity (30%):            $3.6M
  - Sponsor: $2M
  - Impact investor: $1M
  - Local partner: $0.6M (contributed land)

Risk Mitigation:
- Political risk insurance ($200K): $10M coverage
- Currency hedge: Forward contracts (equipment purchases)
- Local partnership: Political connections, market access
- Phased investment: Prove model at small scale first

Risk Level: High (emerging market, infrastructure challenges)
Currency Risk: High (KES volatility)
Political Risk: Medium (mitigated by insurance, DFI)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Diverse Sources**: Equity, debt, DFIs, grants, blended finance - use appropriate mix for market/project
2. **DFIs**: Essential for emerging markets; patient capital, below-market rates, political risk mitigation
3. **Blended Finance**: Enables difficult projects; combines grant, concessional, and commercial capital
4. **Currency Risk**: Significant in emerging markets; natural hedges and forward contracts critical
5. **Political Risk**: Insurance available; structural protections important
6. **Risk-Return**: Higher risk markets require higher returns, but blended finance can bridge gap
7. **Timeline**: International financing slow (12-18 months DFI); plan accordingly
