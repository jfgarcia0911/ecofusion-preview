## Questions

### 1. What is a phytosanitary certificate?
A) A business license
B) A document certifying plants are free from pests and diseases
C) An organic certification
D) A tax document

### 2. Which organization sets international standards for phytosanitary measures?
A) WHO
B) WTO
C) IPPC (International Plant Protection Convention)
D) FAO only

### 3. What is the primary purpose of customs documentation in international trade?
A) Marketing
B) Declare goods, assess duties, and ensure compliance
C) Product design
D) Employee verification

### 4. What does "HS Code" stand for in international trade?
A) High Security Code
B) Harmonized System Code
C) Harvest Schedule Code
D) Health Safety Code

### 5. Which factor most affects the shelf life of fresh produce during export?
A) Package color
B) Temperature control and cold chain management
C) Brand name
D) Country of origin

### 6. What is a common barrier to exporting fresh CEA produce internationally?
A) Product quality is too high
B) Short shelf life and regulatory requirements
C) Excessive profits
D) Too much demand

### 7. What documentation is typically required for organic certification in export markets?
A) None needed
B) Certification from recognized organic certifying body
C) Self-declaration
D) Company letterhead

### 8. Which mode of transportation is most common for international fresh produce export?
A) Air freight
B) Ocean freight
C) Rail only
D) Truck only

### 9. What is "Incoterms" in international trade?
A) Insurance terms
B) Standardized trade terms defining responsibilities
C) Internet terminology
D) Organic standards

### 10. Which is a key consideration for export packaging?
A) Artistic design only
B) Protection, temperature control, and regulatory compliance
C) Maximum weight
D) Bright colors

---

## Answer Key

1. **B) A document certifying plants are free from pests and diseases**
   *Explanation: Phytosanitary certificates verify that plants and plant products are free from quarantine pests and diseases.*

2. **C) IPPC (International Plant Protection Convention)**
   *Explanation: The IPPC sets international standards for phytosanitary measures to protect plant health in international trade.*

3. **B) Declare goods, assess duties, and ensure compliance**
   *Explanation: Customs documentation declares goods, allows assessment of duties and taxes, and ensures regulatory compliance.*

4. **B) Harmonized System Code**
   *Explanation: HS Code (Harmonized System Code) is an internationally standardized system for classifying traded products.*

5. **B) Temperature control and cold chain management**
   *Explanation: Proper temperature control and unbroken cold chain management are critical for maintaining produce quality during export.*

6. **B) Short shelf life and regulatory requirements**
   *Explanation: Fresh produce has limited shelf life and faces complex regulatory requirements including phytosanitary certification.*

7. **B) Certification from recognized organic certifying body**
   *Explanation: Organic exports require certification from bodies recognized in the destination market (USDA, EU Organic, etc.).*

8. **A) Air freight**
   *Explanation: High-value fresh produce is typically exported via air freight to maintain quality and reduce transit time.*

9. **B) Standardized trade terms defining responsibilities**
   *Explanation: Incoterms define the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in international trade (FOB, CIF, etc.).*

10. **B) Protection, temperature control, and regulatory compliance**
    *Explanation: Export packaging must protect products, maintain temperature, comply with regulations, and facilitate handling.*
