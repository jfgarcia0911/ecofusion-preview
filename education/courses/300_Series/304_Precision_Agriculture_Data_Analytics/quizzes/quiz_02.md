### Question 1
Which sensor technology provides the highest accuracy for measuring air temperature in controlled environment agriculture?

A) Thermistor
B) Thermocouple
C) RTD (Resistance Temperature Detector)
D) Infrared sensor

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** RTDs offer ±0.05°C accuracy, making them the gold standard for precision agriculture. While more expensive, they provide the accuracy needed for optimal climate control where 1°C can significantly impact plant growth.

### Question 2
What is the recommended measurement range for dissolved oxygen (DO) sensors in aquaponics fish tanks?

A) 0-5 mg/L
B) 0-10 mg/L
C) 0-20 mg/L
D) 0-50 mg/L

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A 0-20 mg/L range is ideal for aquaponics. While fish require 5-8 mg/L, the sensor needs headroom for supersaturation events and calibration. The 0-20 mg/L range balances accuracy in the critical zone with the ability to detect extreme conditions.

### Question 3
How often should pH probes be calibrated in commercial aquaponics systems?

A) Daily
B) Weekly
C) Monthly
D) Quarterly

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Weekly calibration is the industry standard for commercial operations. pH probes drift over time, and weekly calibration ensures accuracy within ±0.1 pH units. Critical systems may require twice-weekly calibration, while less critical applications can extend to bi-weekly.

### Question 4
What is the primary advantage of using PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density) sensors over lux meters?

A) Lower cost
B) Measures plant-usable light (400-700nm)
C) Easier to calibrate
D) Works in all light conditions

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** PPFD sensors measure only the 400-700nm wavelengths that plants use for photosynthesis, while lux meters are calibrated for human vision. This makes PPFD the correct metric for optimizing plant growth, especially under LED lighting with specific spectrums.

### Question 5
Which water quality parameter is most affected by temperature and requires temperature compensation?

A) pH
B) Electrical Conductivity (EC)
C) Dissolved Oxygen (DO)
D) All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** All three parameters are temperature-dependent. pH changes ~0.003 units per °C, EC changes ~2% per °C, and DO solubility decreases as temperature increases. Quality sensors include automatic temperature compensation (ATC) to provide accurate readings across temperature ranges.

### Question 6
What is the typical accuracy requirement for EC sensors in nutrient solution management?

A) ±1%
B) ±5%
C) ±10%
D) ±20%

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ±5% accuracy is standard for commercial hydroponic/aquaponic systems. For example, at 1500 µS/cm, this allows ±75 µS/cm variation, which is acceptable for most crops. Critical applications may require ±2% accuracy, while ±10% is insufficient for precision nutrient management.

### Question 7
Which sensor placement strategy is recommended for monitoring greenhouse air temperature?

A) Single sensor at the center
B) One sensor per 1000 sq ft, aspirated, at canopy height
C) Sensors along one wall
D) Single sensor near the door

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The industry standard is one sensor per 1000 sq ft, placed at canopy height in aspirated shields to avoid radiant heat effects. Multiple sensors account for microclimates, and aspiration ensures the sensor measures air temperature, not radiant energy from lights or surfaces.

### Question 8
What is the primary disadvantage of ion-selective electrode (ISE) sensors for measuring nitrate in aquaponics?

A) High cost
B) Requires frequent calibration and maintenance
C) Slow response time
D) Cannot measure in water

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISE sensors require weekly calibration, regular electrode replacement, and are sensitive to interference from other ions. While they provide real-time data (unlike lab tests), the maintenance burden is significant. This is why many operations still use weekly lab tests instead of continuous ISE monitoring.

### Question 9
For a 5,000 sq ft aquaponics facility, what is the recommended minimum number of pH sensors?

A) 1 (fish tank only)
B) 2 (fish tank and system output)
C) 3 (fish tank, biofilter, plant beds)
D) 5+ (multiple zones)

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Three sensors monitor the critical points: fish tank (animal welfare), biofilter (nitrification efficiency), and plant bed output (nutrient uptake). This allows detection of localized pH issues and verification that the entire system is stable. Single-sensor systems miss zone-specific problems.

### Question 10
What is the typical service life of a dissolved oxygen membrane before replacement?

A) 1-3 months
B) 6-12 months
C) 2-3 years
D) 5+ years

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** DO membranes typically last 6-12 months in aquaponics applications before degradation affects accuracy. Factors affecting lifespan include biofilm growth, physical damage, and chemical exposure. Budget for annual membrane replacement as part of operational costs, plus electrolyte refills every 3-6 months.
