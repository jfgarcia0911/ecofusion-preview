## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
1. Assess your current leadership capabilities comprehensively

## Introduction

Leadership development is a lifelong journey, not a destination. The most effective leaders are intentional and systematic about their own growth. They assess honestly, set ambitious goals, seek development experiences, and reflect on their learning.

This module guides you in creating your Personal Leadership Development Plan—a roadmap for becoming the leader you aspire to be.

### 360-Degree Feedback

**Why it matters:** You have blind spots. Others see your impact more clearly than you do.

**Who to ask:**
- Your manager/board
- Direct reports
- Peers and colleagues
- Customers or stakeholders
- Mentors or advisors

**What to ask:**
- What are my greatest leadership strengths?
- Where do I need to develop?
- What's one thing I could do differently that would make me more effective?
- How do I come across to others?
- What do I do that helps or hinders the team?

**How to ask:**
- **Formal:** Use 360 assessment tool
- **Informal:** Direct conversations or anonymous survey
- **Frame it:** "I'm working on my leadership development and would value your honest feedback"
- **Create safety:** Assure them you want honesty, won't be defensive
- **Follow up:** Thank them, share what you're taking from it, report on your progress

## Setting Development Goals

### 3-Year Leadership Goals

Based on your vision and self-assessment, what do you want to achieve in the next 3 years?

**Career/Role Goals:**
- Position or role advancement
- Scope or responsibility expansion
- New areas or challenges
- Example: "Become COO of our organization, leading operations and 50-person team"

**Capability Goals:**
- Competencies to develop
- Skills to build
- Behaviors to strengthen
- Example: "Become highly skilled at strategic planning and change leadership"

**Impact Goals:**
- Results you'll achieve
- People you'll develop
- Change you'll create
- Example: "Successfully lead our regenerative transition and mentor 5 emerging farmers"

**Make them SMART:**
- Specific
- Measurable
- Achievable (but ambitious)
- Relevant (aligned with vision)
- Time-bound

### Priority Development Areas

**From your assessment, choose 2-4 areas for focused development:**

Consider:
- What would have the biggest positive impact?
- What aligns with your goals and vision?
- What's developable (vs. unchangeable)?
- Where do you have energy to develop?

**For each priority area, define:**
- **Current state:** Where are you now?
- **Desired state:** Where do you want to be?
- **Why it matters:** Impact on your effectiveness
- **Development strategies:** How will you develop this?

**Example:**

**Priority:** Strategic Thinking and Planning

**Current:** I'm strong operationally but tend to be tactical and reactive. I don't spend enough time on long-term thinking and proactive strategy.

**Desired:** I consistently think 3-5 years ahead, anticipate trends and opportunities, and lead strategic planning for our organization.

**Impact:** Will enable us to be proactive rather than reactive, position us for growth, and free me from day-to-day operations.

**Strategies:**
- Read 3 books on strategic thinking
- Find mentor who's strong strategic leader
- Block 2 hours weekly for strategic thinking
- Lead our annual strategic planning process
- Attend strategic planning workshop

## Obstacles and Strategies

### Common Obstacles

**1. "I don't have time"**
- Development feels like "extra" on top of full workload
- Urgent crowds out important
- Strategy: Development IS the work of leadership. Schedule it like any critical meeting. Start small (1 hour/week) and build.

**2. "I don't know what to work on"**
- Unclear priorities or too many
- Analysis paralysis
- Strategy: Start with self-assessment and feedback. Pick just 1-2 things. Action creates clarity.

**3. "I'm not seeing progress"**
- Development is gradual and non-linear
- Hard to see own growth
- Strategy: Track specific behaviors and incidents. Seek external perspective. Patience and persistence.

**4. "I don't have support"**
- Manager not invested
- No budget or resources
- Isolated
- Strategy: Self-directed development possible. Build peer support. Invest your own time and money. Create accountability for yourself.

**5. "I'm afraid to change"**
- Comfortable with current self
- Fear of failure or judgment
- Imposter syndrome
- Strategy: Growth requires discomfort. Start small. Focus on learning, not perfection. Find supportive community.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Leadership development is intentional** - doesn't happen by accident, requires systematic approach.

2. **Self-awareness is the starting point** - honest assessment of current state enables targeted development.

3. **Vision provides direction** - knowing where you're going guides what to develop.

4. **Focus on 2-4 priorities** - trying to develop everything develops nothing.

5. **Experience is the primary teacher** - 70% of development comes from challenging assignments.

6. **Support accelerates growth** - mentors, coaches, peers, and community multiply learning.

7. **Commitment and accountability are essential** - development requires discipline and follow-through.
