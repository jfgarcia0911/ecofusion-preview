## Questions

### 1. What characterizes good decision-making processes?
a) Making decisions as quickly as possible
b) Gathering relevant information and considering alternatives
c) Always deciding alone
d) Avoiding difficult decisions

### 2. The OODA Loop decision-making model stands for:
a) Observe, Orient, Decide, Act
b) Order, Organize, Deliver, Analyze
c) Opinion, Option, Decision, Action
d) Outline, Operate, Decide, Adjust

### 3. When should leaders make decisions autocratically?
a) Always, to show authority
b) In crisis requiring immediate action
c) When seeking buy-in is important
d) Never

### 4. Participative decision-making is most appropriate when:
a) Time is extremely limited
b) You need team buy-in and diverse perspectives
c) The decision is confidential
d) You want to avoid responsibility

### 5. What is analysis paralysis?
a) Making decisions too quickly
b) Overthinking and delaying decision due to excessive analysis
c) Physical inability to move
d) Delegating all decisions

### 6. Which cognitive bias involves seeking only confirming information?
a) Anchoring bias
b) Confirmation bias
c) Sunk cost fallacy
d) Availability bias

### 7. The sunk cost fallacy is:
a) Continuing investment based on past costs rather than future value
b) Avoiding all financial risks
c) Calculating total expenses accurately
d) Investing in proven opportunities

### 8. When making high-stakes decisions, leaders should:
a) Decide immediately without input
b) Gather data, consider alternatives, and assess risks
c) Avoid making the decision
d) Flip a coin

### 9. After making a decision, effective leaders:
a) Never revisit or adjust it
b) Monitor results and adjust as needed
c) Blame others if it doesn't work
d) Hide the decision from the team

### 10. Intuitive decision-making:
a) Should never be used in business
b) Can complement analytical thinking based on experience
c) Is always superior to analysis
d) Is just random guessing

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) Gathering relevant information and considering alternatives**
   *Explanation:* Good decision-making involves gathering relevant information, considering multiple alternatives, evaluating consequences, and making informed choices.

2. **a) Observe, Orient, Decide, Act**
   *Explanation:* The OODA Loop (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act) is a decision-making framework emphasizing rapid, iterative cycles.

3. **b) In crisis requiring immediate action**
   *Explanation:* Autocratic decisions are appropriate in crises, when expertise is clear, or when quick action is essential - not as a default approach.

4. **b) You need team buy-in and diverse perspectives**
   *Explanation:* Participative decision-making builds buy-in, leverages diverse perspectives, and develops team capabilities - best when time allows and input adds value.

5. **b) Overthinking and delaying decision due to excessive analysis**
   *Explanation:* Analysis paralysis occurs when excessive analysis and overthinking prevent timely decision-making and action.

6. **b) Confirmation bias**
   *Explanation:* Confirmation bias is the tendency to seek, interpret, and recall information that confirms existing beliefs while ignoring contradicting evidence.

7. **a) Continuing investment based on past costs rather than future value**
   *Explanation:* The sunk cost fallacy is continuing to invest in something because of past investment, rather than evaluating based on future value and opportunity cost.

8. **b) Gather data, consider alternatives, and assess risks**
   *Explanation:* High-stakes decisions warrant thorough processes: gather data, identify alternatives, assess risks and benefits, consult experts, and evaluate consequences.

9. **b) Monitor results and adjust as needed**
   *Explanation:* After decisions, effective leaders monitor outcomes, gather feedback, and adjust course as needed - decision-making is iterative.

10. **b) Can complement analytical thinking based on experience**
    *Explanation:* Intuition, based on deep experience and pattern recognition, can complement analytical thinking - especially valuable when data is incomplete or time is limited.
