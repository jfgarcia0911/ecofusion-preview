## Questions

### 1. Organizational culture is best defined as:
a) The formal rules and policies of an organization
b) The shared values, beliefs, and norms that shape behavior
c) The organizational chart and reporting structure
d) The physical workspace and facilities

### 2. Which element is NOT typically part of organizational culture?
a) Shared assumptions
b) Behavioral norms
c) Financial statements
d) Rituals and traditions

### 3. Leaders shape culture primarily through:
a) Memos and policy documents
b) Their own behavior and what they reward
c) Hiring consultants
d) Ignoring employee concerns

### 4. What is psychological safety in a team?
a) Physical safety equipment and protocols
b) Job security and tenure
c) Feeling safe to take interpersonal risks without fear
d) Health insurance benefits

### 5. A culture of innovation is characterized by:
a) Punishing all failures harshly
b) Following only proven methods
c) Encouraging experimentation and learning from failures
d) Avoiding any risk-taking

### 6. Which is a sign of a healthy organizational culture?
a) High employee turnover
b) Open communication and trust
c) Blame and finger-pointing
d) Resistance to new ideas

### 7. To change organizational culture, leaders should:
a) Focus only on changing policies
b) Model desired behaviors and align systems with values
c) Expect instant transformation
d) Ignore existing culture

### 8. What role do stories play in organizational culture?
a) They waste time and distract from work
b) They communicate values and shape behavior
c) They should be avoided in professional settings
d) They only matter for marketing

### 9. A culture aligned with sustainability values would emphasize:
a) Short-term profits above all else
b) Triple bottom line: people, planet, profit
c) Minimal environmental consideration
d) Extractive resource use

### 10. Onboarding new employees is important for culture because:
a) It's required by law
b) It's the first opportunity to immerse them in cultural norms
c) It reduces paperwork
d) It has no impact on culture

---

## Answer Key

1. **b) The shared values, beliefs, and norms that shape behavior**
   *Explanation:* Organizational culture consists of shared values, beliefs, assumptions, and behavioral norms that guide how people act.

2. **c) Financial statements**
   *Explanation:* While financial performance may reflect culture, financial statements are not a component of culture itself. Culture includes assumptions, norms, and rituals.

3. **b) Their own behavior and what they reward**
   *Explanation:* Leaders shape culture most powerfully through role modeling behavior and what they recognize, reward, and tolerate.

4. **c) Feeling safe to take interpersonal risks without fear**
   *Explanation:* Psychological safety means team members feel safe to speak up, share ideas, admit mistakes, and take risks without fear of punishment or embarrassment.

5. **c) Encouraging experimentation and learning from failures**
   *Explanation:* Innovation cultures embrace calculated risk-taking, experimentation, and treat failures as learning opportunities.

6. **b) Open communication and trust**
   *Explanation:* Healthy cultures feature open communication, trust, collaboration, and psychological safety - not turnover, blame, or resistance.

7. **b) Model desired behaviors and align systems with values**
   *Explanation:* Culture change requires leaders to model behaviors, align hiring/rewards/systems with desired culture, and sustain effort over time.

8. **b) They communicate values and shape behavior**
   *Explanation:* Stories are powerful cultural tools that communicate values, illustrate desired behaviors, and shape organizational identity.

9. **b) Triple bottom line: people, planet, profit**
   *Explanation:* Sustainability-aligned cultures emphasize balanced attention to social, environmental, and economic outcomes.

10. **b) It's the first opportunity to immerse them in cultural norms**
    *Explanation:* Onboarding is critical for culture transmission, introducing new employees to values, norms, stories, and expected behaviors.
