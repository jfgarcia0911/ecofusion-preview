# Energy Quick Wins for CEA Facilities

**Course 305: Energy Systems for CEA**
**Immediate Action Handout**

---

## Low-Cost / No-Cost Measures

These improvements require minimal investment but can deliver significant savings immediately.

---

## 1. Optimize Heating Setpoints

**What to Do:**
- Lower nighttime setpoint by 4-6°F
- Reduce daytime setpoint by 2°F if crops tolerate
- Implement temperature setback schedule

**Typical Savings:** 10-20% on heating costs
**Cost:** $0 (just reprogram thermostats)
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** 30 minutes

**Example:**
```
Before: Constant 70°F day and night
After:  Day 68°F / Night 62°F
Annual savings: $2,500-5,000 (typical greenhouse)
```

---

## 2. Fix Air Leaks

**What to Do:**
- Weather-strip all doors
- Install door sweeps
- Seal poly film attachment points
- Caulk around penetrations
- Fix damaged glazing

**Typical Savings:** 15-30% on heating costs
**Cost:** $300-1,500 (materials only)
**Payback:** 2-6 months
**Time to Implement:** 1-2 days

**Priority Areas:**
1. Main entry doors (biggest culprit)
2. Ventilation fan shutters
3. Roof-to-wall connections
4. Poly clips and wiggle wire
5. Electrical/plumbing penetrations

---

## 3. Optimize Lighting Schedules

**What to Do:**
- Set timers for exact photoperiod needed (no extra hours)
- Turn off lights during non-production hours
- Implement automatic controls
- Eliminate after-hours lighting waste

**Typical Savings:** 5-15% on lighting costs
**Cost:** $0-500 (basic timers)
**Payback:** 1-3 months
**Time to Implement:** 2-4 hours

**Example:**
```
Before: Lights on 18 hrs/day (excessive)
After:  Lights on 16 hrs/day (optimal for crops)
Reduction: 11% in lighting energy
Annual savings: $1,500-3,000
```

---

## 4. Reduce Phantom Loads

**What to Do:**
- Unplug unused equipment
- Use power strips to fully disconnect devices
- Turn off computers and monitors when not in use
- Disable unnecessary "always-on" features

**Typical Savings:** $200-800/year
**Cost:** $0-100 (power strips)
**Payback:** Immediate to 2 months
**Time to Implement:** 1 hour

**Common Phantom Load Sources:**
- Office equipment
- Chargers (always plugged in)
- Older lighting ballasts
- Standby electronics

---

## 5. Clean and Maintain Equipment

**What to Do:**
- Clean lighting fixtures (restore 5-15% output)
- Replace HVAC filters monthly
- Clean coils and heat exchangers
- Lubricate motors and fans
- Check and fix refrigerant leaks

**Typical Savings:** 5-15% across systems
**Cost:** $200-500/year (materials)
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** Ongoing (monthly tasks)

**Impact:**
```
Dirty lighting: 10-20% output loss
Dirty HVAC coils: 15-25% efficiency loss
Poor maintenance: 10-30% energy penalty
```

---

## 6. Optimize Pump Schedules

**What to Do:**
- Run irrigation pumps only when needed (not 24/7)
- Schedule pumping during off-peak hours
- Reduce runtime for recirculation pumps
- Fix leaks (every drip wastes pump energy)

**Typical Savings:** 20-40% on pumping energy
**Cost:** $0
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** 1 day

**Example:**
```
Before: Pump runs 24/7 = 8,760 hrs/year
After:  Pump runs 8 hrs/day = 2,920 hrs/year
Reduction: 67% in pumping energy
Annual savings: $800-2,000
```

---

## 7. Use Natural Ventilation

**What to Do:**
- Open vents during mild weather instead of using fans
- Use thermal buoyancy (passive ventilation)
- Strategic vent placement for airflow
- Turn off mechanical cooling when outdoor temp appropriate

**Typical Savings:** 30-60% on cooling fan energy
**Cost:** $0
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** Daily operational change

**When to Use:**
- Outdoor temp < indoor setpoint + 5°F
- Low humidity days
- Mild spring/fall weather

---

## 8. Manage Peak Demand

**What to Do:**
- Stagger equipment startup times
- Avoid running all high-load equipment simultaneously
- Schedule non-critical loads during off-peak
- Monitor real-time demand

**Typical Savings:** 10-25% reduction in demand charges
**Cost:** $0
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** Implement new procedures

**Simple Strategy:**
```
Instead of all lights on at 6:00am:
  Zone 1: 5:45am
  Zone 2: 6:00am
  Zone 3: 6:15am
  Zone 4: 6:30am

Result: 20-30% lower peak demand
Savings: $1,000-3,000/year typical
```

---

## 9. Improve Power Factor

**What to Do:**
- Install capacitor bank
- Reduce motor loading (right-size equipment)
- Replace old magnetic ballasts

**Typical Savings:** 5-15% on demand charges
**Cost:** $2,000-10,000 (capacitors)
**Payback:** 1-3 years
**Time to Implement:** 1 day installation

**Check if needed:**
- Look at utility bill for power factor or kVA charges
- Target: PF > 0.95
- If PF < 0.85, definitely worth correcting

---

## 10. Conduct Staff Training

**What to Do:**
- Train staff on energy-efficient practices
- Post energy-saving reminders
- Assign energy champion
- Review energy reports monthly
- Incentivize efficiency improvements

**Typical Savings:** 5-10% across all systems
**Cost:** $0 (time investment)
**Payback:** Immediate
**Time to Implement:** Ongoing

**Key Training Topics:**
- Proper thermostat use
- Turning off unnecessary equipment
- Reporting leaks and issues promptly
- Understanding energy goals
- Personal stake in efficiency
