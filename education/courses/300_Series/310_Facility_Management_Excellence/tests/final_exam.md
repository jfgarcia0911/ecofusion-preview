## Section 1: Operations Excellence Framework (Questions 1-5)

### 1. The Operations Excellence Pyramid consists of five levels. Which statement correctly describes the sequential nature of building excellence?

A) You can jump to any level if you have sufficient budget
B) Each level requires the foundation of the level below it
C) All levels should be developed simultaneously
D) Start with optimization first, then build the foundation

**Answer: B**

---

### 2. A facility assessment reveals the following maturity levels: Safety (Level 3), Quality (Level 2), Equipment (Level 1), People (Level 3), Processes (Level 2). What is the overall facility maturity level?

A) Level 3
B) Level 2
C) Level 1
D) Average of all levels (Level 2.2)

**Answer: C**

---

### 3. Which characteristic best distinguishes a Level 2 (Stability) facility from a Level 1 (Foundation) facility?

A) Has world-class benchmarks
B) Equipment runs reliably with >95% uptime and trained workforce
C) Just meets basic safety requirements
D) Implements data-driven optimization

**Answer: B**

---

### 4. According to the 80/20 Rule of Excellence, what represents 80% of the work but 20% of the glory?

A) Optimization and innovation (Levels 4-5)
B) Marketing and sales
C) Foundation and stability work (Levels 1-2)
D) Technology implementation

**Answer: C**

---

### 5. What is the primary impact of attempting Level 4 optimization without first achieving Level 2 stability?

A) Faster results and immediate ROI
B) Improvements don't stick; data reflects unstable processes
C) Employee satisfaction increases
D) Costs decrease immediately

**Answer: B**

---

## Section 3: Quality Management (Questions 13-17)

### 13. In the quality hierarchy, which approach is most effective and cost-efficient?

A) Detection through inspection
B) Prevention through design
C) Correction after defects found
D) Acceptance of defects

**Answer: B**

---

### 14. What does HACCP stand for, and what is its primary purpose?

A) Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points - food safety management
B) High Accuracy Crop Control Process - yield optimization
C) Health and Cleanliness Compliance Program - facility sanitation
D) Hazardous Chemical Control Protocol - chemical safety

**Answer: A**

---

### 15. A Critical Control Point (CCP) in HACCP is:

A) Any step in the production process
B) A point where control is essential to prevent or eliminate food safety hazards
C) The most expensive equipment
D) Where the manager stands

**Answer: B**

---

### 16. What is the purpose of the "5 Whys" root cause analysis technique?

A) To ask five different people
B) To dig deeper into causes by repeatedly asking "why" to find root cause
C) To create five different solutions
D) To delay corrective action

**Answer: B**

---

### 17. When a process consistently produces out-of-specification product, what is the correct action?

A) Continue production and inspect more carefully
B) Stop production, identify root cause, correct, then resume
C) Lower quality standards to match current output
D) Blame operators and continue

**Answer: B**
