## What is Standard Work?

### Definition

**Standard Work** is the current best-known method for performing a task, documented and followed consistently by all team members.

**Key Principles:**
- **Best practice** - Not just any way, but the best way we know today
- **Documented** - Written down clearly and accessibly
- **Trained** - Everyone knows the standard
- **Followed** - Actual practice matches the standard
- **Improvable** - Standards evolve as we learn better methods

### Why Standard Work Matters in CEA

| Without Standards | With Standards |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Each person does it their way | Everyone follows best practice |
| Inconsistent results | Predictable outcomes |
| Difficult to train new staff | Clear training path |
| Can't identify problems | Deviations are visible |
| No baseline for improvement | Clear starting point |
| Tribal knowledge | Documented process |

**Real Impact:**
```
Example: Nutrient Mixing

Without Standard:
- Operator A: EC 2.2, pH 6.1 (high EC)
- Operator B: EC 1.8, pH 5.8 (low EC)
- Operator C: EC 2.0, pH 6.3 (high pH)
Result: Crop stress, inconsistent quality

With Standard:
- All operators: EC 2.0 ± 0.1, pH 6.0 ± 0.2
Result: Consistent crop performance
```

## The Three Elements of Standard Work

### 1. Takt Time

**Definition:** The pace of customer demand; how often you must complete one unit.

**Formula:**
```
Takt Time = Available Time / Customer Demand

Example:
- Available time: 8 hours = 480 minutes
- Customer demand: 240 packages per day
- Takt Time = 480 / 240 = 2 minutes per package

Meaning: Must complete one package every 2 minutes to meet demand
```

**CEA Applications:**
- Harvest pace to fill orders
- Transplant rate to maintain pipeline
- Packaging throughput for shipping windows

### 2. Work Sequence

**Definition:** The specific order of steps that must be performed.

**Elements:**
- Sequence of operations
- Location of work
- Movement of materials
- Quality checks
- Safety checks

**Example: Transplant Work Sequence**
```
1. Retrieve tray of seedlings from germination area
2. Place tray on workstation
3. Prepare grow channels (verify clean, filled)
4. Remove seedling from plug (check root development)
5. Place seedling in net pot with growing media
6. Insert net pot into channel hole
7. Repeat steps 4-6 for entire tray
8. Move completed channel to grow area
9. Return empty tray to wash station
10. Record completion on production board
```

### 3. Standard In-Process Inventory

**Definition:** The minimum amount of work-in-process needed to maintain flow.

**CEA Examples:**
- Seedlings in germination: 3 days worth
- Plants in nursery: 7 days worth
- Unharvested mature plants: 1 day worth
- Packaged product: 2 hours worth

**Purpose:**
- Buffer against variability
- Enable continuous flow
- Visual indicator of problems (too much/too little)

### Example SOP: Daily System Check

```
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
                    STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
                        DAILY SYSTEM CHECK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SOP NUMBER: OPS-001
VERSION: 2.1
EFFECTIVE DATE: 2025-01-15
DEPARTMENT: Operations

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PURPOSE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Verify all systems are operating within normal parameters
at the start of each shift to prevent crop loss and ensure
safe, efficient operations.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. SCOPE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Covers all critical systems: water, nutrients, lighting,
climate control, and monitoring systems.

Frequency: Beginning of each shift (3x daily)
Duration: 15-20 minutes

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. RESPONSIBILITIES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Shift Lead: Ensures check is completed
- Operator: Performs check and records results
- Manager: Reviews daily logs

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. MATERIALS & EQUIPMENT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Daily check sheet (form OPS-001A)
- Clipboard
- Handheld thermometer/hygrometer
- pH/EC calibration solutions (weekly)
- Flashlight

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Non-slip footwear required
- Caution around electrical panels
- Report any unusual sounds or smells immediately
- Do not attempt repairs - notify maintenance

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. PROCEDURE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

STEP | ACTION | SPECIFICATION | CHECK
-----|--------|---------------|-------
1 | Review overnight logs | Check for alarms, notes | Initial log
2 | Check main water pump | Running, no leaks | Visual & sound
3 | Verify water pressure | 30-40 PSI | Read gauge
4 | Check reservoir level | 75-90% full | Visual
5 | Measure reservoir EC | 1.8-2.2 mS/cm | Meter reading
6 | Measure reservoir pH | 5.8-6.2 | Meter reading
7 | Check reservoir temp | 65-72°F | Thermometer
8 | Verify all grow lights on | 100% operational | Visual count
9 | Check light intensity | PPFD 200-250 μmol/m²/s | Meter sample
10 | Measure air temp | 68-75°F | Digital display
11 | Measure humidity | 55-65% RH | Digital display
12 | Verify CO2 level | 800-1200 ppm | Digital display
13 | Check HVAC operation | Running per schedule | Listen/feel
14 | Inspect pumps/plumbing | No leaks, proper flow | Visual/listen
15 | Check backup systems | Ready status | Indicator lights
16 | Record all readings | Complete form OPS-001A | Sign & file

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. QUALITY CHECKS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Check | Specification | Frequency | Action if Fail
------|---------------|-----------|----------------
Sensor calibration | ±5% accuracy | Weekly | Recalibrate
Visual crop health | No wilting/stress | Daily | Notify manager
Water clarity | Clear, no debris | Daily | Clean filters
Equipment noise | Normal sound | Daily | Notify maintenance

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. TROUBLESHOOTING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Problem | Likely Cause | Solution
--------|--------------|----------
EC too high | Over-concentration | Add water, notify manager
EC too low | Dilution, depletion | Add nutrients per SOP-012
pH too high | Nutrient depletion | Add pH down per SOP-013
pH too low | Over-acidification | Add pH up per SOP-013
Low water pressure | Clogged filter | Clean/replace filter
High temp | HVAC issue | Check settings, call maint.
Low humidity | Dehumidifier issue | Check unit, add water
No light section | Bulb out, ballast | Note location, call maint.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. RECORDS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Complete form OPS-001A each shift
- File in daily log binder
- Flag any out-of-spec readings
- Retain logs for 2 years

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. RELATED DOCUMENTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- SOP-012: Nutrient Adjustment
- SOP-013: pH Adjustment
- SOP-025: Sensor Calibration
- Emergency Response Plan

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
APPROVALS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Written by: J. Smith, Ops Manager     Date: 01/15/2025
Reviewed by: M. Lee, Grower           Date: 01/15/2025
Approved by: R. Johnson, GM           Date: 01/15/2025

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## Visual Management Systems

### The Power of Visual Controls

**Principle:** Make abnormalities visible at a glance.

**Visual Management Hierarchy:**
1. **Visual Display** - Shows status
2. **Visual Control** - Prevents errors
3. **Visual Guarantee** - Makes errors impossible

### Types of Visual Management

#### 1. Color Coding

```
Standard Color System for CEA:

Piping:
- RED: Nutrient solution supply
- BLUE: Fresh water supply
- YELLOW: Drain/waste water
- GREEN: Air supply
- ORANGE: Electrical conduit

Zones:
- GREEN: Ready to harvest
- YELLOW: Growing (not ready)
- BLUE: Nursery/young plants
- WHITE: Germination

Quality:
- GREEN: Grade A (premium)
- YELLOW: Grade B (standard)
- RED: Reject (compost)
```

#### 2. Floor Markings

```
Floor Marking Standards:

Yellow Lines (2 inches):
- Walkways and aisles
- Safety zones around equipment
- Staging areas

Red Lines (2 inches):
- No-go zones (safety)
- Emergency equipment zones

White Lines (2 inches):
- Work areas
- Equipment placement

Arrows:
- Flow direction
- One-way paths
```

## Implementing Standard Work

### Implementation Roadmap

**Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-4)**
1. Select pilot area
2. Form SOP development team
3. Document current state
4. Train team on SOP format

**Phase 2: Development (Weeks 5-12)**
1. Create SOPs for core processes
2. Design visual management systems
3. Pilot test in selected area
4. Gather feedback and revise

**Phase 3: Training (Weeks 13-16)**
1. Train all operators on SOPs
2. Install visual management
3. Begin compliance audits
4. Collect improvement ideas

**Phase 4: Rollout (Weeks 17-24)**
1. Expand to all areas
2. Establish audit schedule
3. Create improvement process
4. Measure results

**Phase 5: Sustain (Ongoing)**
1. Regular audits
2. Continuous updates
3. New hire training
4. Performance tracking

### Training on Standard Work

**Effective Training Method: Job Instruction (JI)**

**Four-Step Method:**

**Step 1: Prepare**
- Put learner at ease
- State the job
- Find out what they know
- Get them interested

**Step 2: Present**
- Tell, show, and illustrate
- One important step at a time
- Stress key points
- Instruct clearly and completely

**Step 3: Try Out**
- Have them do the job
- Have them explain key points
- Correct errors immediately
- Continue until you know they know

**Step 4: Follow Up**
- Put them on their own
- Check frequently
- Encourage questions
- Taper off coaching

**Training Checklist:**
```
□ SOP review with trainer
□ Demonstration by expert
□ Practice by learner (supervised)
□ Independent performance (observed)
□ Competency verification
□ Sign-off on training record
```

## Auditing Standard Work

### Purpose of Audits

**Audits verify:**
- SOPs are being followed
- Visual management is maintained
- Training is effective
- Standards are still valid
- Opportunities for improvement

**Audit Frequency:**
- Daily: Leader gemba walks
- Weekly: Focused process audits
- Monthly: Comprehensive system audits
- Quarterly: Management audits

### Audit Response Process

**When non-conformances are found:**

1. **Immediate:** Correct the current situation
2. **Short-term:** Retrain if needed
3. **Root cause:** Why did deviation occur?
4. **Long-term:** Update SOP or training if needed

**Audit Metrics:**
- Compliance rate: % of items passed
- Trend: Improving, stable, or declining?
- Common issues: What fails most often?
- Effectiveness: Does training work?

## Improving Standard Work

### Kaizen (Continuous Improvement) of Standards

**Key Principle:** Standard work is not static - it should improve.

**Improvement Sources:**
1. **Operator ideas** - People doing the work know best
2. **Problem-solving** - Root cause investigations
3. **Benchmarking** - Learning from others
4. **Technology** - New tools and methods
5. **Customer feedback** - Market requirements change

### Standard Work Improvement Process

```
Current Standard → Idea for Improvement → Test New Method
      ↑                                          ↓
Update Standard ← Validate Results ← Document New Method
```

**Example Improvement Cycle:**

```
Week 1: Current standard for transplanting
- Time: 3 minutes per tray
- Operator stands at bench
- Tools on shelf

Week 2: Operator suggests improvement
- Idea: Tool holder at waist level
- Test: Build prototype holder

Week 3: Trial period
- Time: 2.5 minutes per tray (17% faster)
- Less reaching and bending
- Operator feedback: positive

Week 4: Update standard
- Add tool holder to SOP
- Update workstation photo
- Train all operators
- New standard: 2.5 min per tray
```

## Common Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge 1: "We Don't Have Time to Document"

**Reality:** You don't have time NOT to document.

**Cost of No Standards:**
- Training new hires: 3x longer
- Error rates: 5x higher
- Rework and waste: $$ daily
- Knowledge loss when people leave

**Solution:**
- Start with most critical processes
- Use simple format (one-page SOPs)
- Involve operators in creating SOPs
- Make it part of job expectations

### Challenge 2: "Every Situation is Different"

**Reality:** 80% of work is repeatable.

**Approach:**
- Document the standard 80%
- Develop guidelines for variables
- Train judgment for exceptions
- Review exceptions to improve standard

### Challenge 3: "People Won't Follow SOPs"

**Root Causes:**
- SOPs are unrealistic or outdated
- SOPs not accessible or understandable
- No training on the standard
- No accountability or enforcement
- SOPs don't reflect best practice

**Solutions:**
- Involve operators in SOP creation
- Make SOPs visual and simple
- Train thoroughly
- Audit regularly and give feedback
- Update standards based on reality

### Challenge 4: "Standards Stifle Creativity"

**Reality:** Standards enable innovation.

**Explanation:**
- Standards provide baseline for improvement
- Can't improve what isn't standardized
- Innovation should update the standard
- Standards free mental energy for problem-solving

**Example:**
```
Without Standard:
- Everyone reinvents the wheel
- No common baseline
- Can't measure improvement

With Standard:
- Follow standard for routine work
- Focus creativity on improvements
- Test improvements systematically
- Raise the bar for everyone
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Standard work is the foundation** - Without it, improvement is impossible
2. **Best known method** - Standards reflect current best practice, not rigid rules
3. **Document, train, follow** - All three are essential
4. **Visual management** - Make standards and status visible
5. **Audit for compliance** - Regular verification ensures adherence
6. **Continuous improvement** - Standards should evolve
7. **Engage operators** - Those doing the work must own the standards
8. **Start simple** - One-page SOPs better than 50-page documents
