## Crisis Management Framework

### What is a Crisis?

**Definition:** An unexpected event that threatens operations, safety, reputation, or financial viability and requires immediate response

**Characteristics:**
- Sudden onset (little warning)
- High stakes (serious consequences)
- Time pressure (must act fast)
- Uncertainty (limited information)
- High stress (emotional intensity)

### CEA Crisis Categories

| Type | Examples | Impact |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Operational** | Equipment failure, power outage | Production loss, crop damage |
| **Environmental** | Extreme weather, flooding | Infrastructure damage |
| **Biological** | Disease outbreak, pest infestation | Crop loss, contamination |
| **Human** | Serious injury, death, workplace violence | Safety, legal, morale |
| **Reputational** | Food safety incident, contamination | Customer loss, liability |
| **Financial** | Major customer loss, funding gap | Cash flow, layoffs |
| **Cyber** | Ransomware, system breach | Data loss, operations down |

## Crisis Preparedness

### The Four Phases of Crisis Management

```
1. MITIGATION (Before)
   └─ Reduce likelihood and impact
   └─ Redundancy, maintenance, training

2. PREPAREDNESS (Before)
   └─ Plans, teams, drills
   └─ Resources pre-positioned

3. RESPONSE (During)
   └─ Activate plan
   └─ Execute, adapt, communicate

4. RECOVERY (After)
   └─ Return to normal
   └─ Learn and improve
```

### Risk Assessment

**Identify Potential Crises:**
```
For each potential crisis:
├─ Likelihood (1-5)
├─ Impact (1-5)
├─ Risk Score (Likelihood × Impact)
└─ Priority for planning (High risk = priority)

Example:
Crisis: Extended Power Outage
├─ Likelihood: 3 (happens occasionally)
├─ Impact: 5 (critical - crop loss in 24-48 hrs)
├─ Risk Score: 15
└─ Priority: HIGH - needs detailed plan
```

### Business Continuity Planning

**Critical Functions:**
```
Priority 1 (Restore immediately):
├─ Fish/animal life support
├─ Safety systems
├─ Critical communications
└─ Security

Priority 2 (Restore within 24 hrs):
├─ Environmental control (temp, humidity)
├─ Lighting (extend tolerance if needed)
├─ Water circulation
└─ Monitoring systems

Priority 3 (Restore within 72 hrs):
├─ Full production
├─ Office functions
├─ Non-critical systems
└─ Normal operations
```

## Crisis Response Plans

### Plan Components

**Essential Elements:**
```
1. PURPOSE & SCOPE
   - What crisis does this plan address?
   - When to activate?

2. CRISIS MANAGEMENT TEAM
   - Roles and responsibilities
   - Contact information
   - Chain of command

3. NOTIFICATION PROCEDURES
   - Who to notify and when
   - Communication channels
   - Escalation protocols

4. RESPONSE ACTIONS
   - Step-by-step procedures
   - Decision trees
   - Resource lists

5. COMMUNICATION TEMPLATES
   - Internal communications
   - Customer notifications
   - Media statements

6. RECOVERY PROCEDURES
   - Damage assessment
   - Restoration priorities
   - Return to operations

7. APPENDICES
   - Contact lists
   - Site maps
   - Equipment manuals
   - Vendor information
```

## Crisis Management Team

### Team Structure

**Incident Commander:**
- Overall authority and decision-making
- Usually Facility Manager or designee
- Coordinates all response activities

**Operations Chief:**
- Manages tactical operations
- Implements response actions
- Assigns tasks to teams

**Planning Chief:**
- Assesses situation
- Develops action plans
- Tracks resources

**Logistics Chief:**
- Procures resources
- Manages supplies and equipment
- Coordinates external support

**Communications Chief:**
- Internal communications
- External stakeholder updates
- Media relations

**Safety Officer:**
- Monitors responder safety
- Stops unsafe actions
- Ensures compliance

### Team Activation

**Notification Tree:**
```
Incident Detected
      ↓
First Responder/Operator
      ↓
Supervisor (assess severity)
      ↓
   ┌──┴──┐
   ↓     ↓
Minor   Major
(handle (activate
locally) CMT)
         ↓
    Facility Manager (Incident Commander)
         ↓
    ┌────┴─────┬─────────┬──────────┐
    ↓          ↓         ↓          ↓
Operations  Planning  Logistics  Comms
```

## Crisis Communication

### Internal Communication

**During Crisis:**
- Frequent updates (hourly if needed)
- Consistent message
- Transparent (share what you know, admit what you don't)
- Two-way (listen to frontline)
- Multiple channels (meeting, email, text)

**Communication Template:**
```
SITUATION UPDATE #3
Date/Time: Dec 10, 2025 / 14:00

CURRENT STATUS:
- Power still out, generator operating normally
- All critical systems functioning
- Crop conditions stable
- Estimated restoration: 18:00 (4 hours from now)

ACTIONS TAKEN:
- Fuel delivery arranged for 16:00
- Harvest team released early (non-essential)
- Monitoring intensified (every 30 min)

WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU:
- Standby for potential evening shift coverage
- Report any system abnormalities immediately
- Conserve non-essential power usage

NEXT UPDATE:
- 16:00 or when power restored

Questions? Contact Incident Commander: [phone]
```

### External Communication

**Customer Communication:**
```
Key Principles:
- Proactive (don't let them hear elsewhere)
- Honest (explain situation clearly)
- Solutionsfocused (what you're doing)
- Impact-specific (how it affects them)
- Timeline (when you'll update again)

Example:
"Dear [Customer],

We're experiencing a temporary power outage at our facility
due to a storm in the area. Our backup systems are functioning
normally and we do not expect any impact to crop quality.

Your order scheduled for tomorrow will be fulfilled as planned.
We'll provide another update this evening or immediately if
the situation changes.

Thank you for your understanding. Please contact me directly
with any questions: [contact info]"
```

**Media/Public:**
```
Guidelines:
- Designate single spokesperson
- Stick to facts (no speculation)
- Express concern/empathy
- Explain actions being taken
- Avoid "no comment" (use "we're investigating")

Hold Messaging:
"We are currently managing [situation] at our facility.
Our top priority is [safety/quality/etc.]. We have activated
our emergency response plan and [actions being taken].
We will provide updates as we have confirmed information."
```

## Specific Crisis Scenarios

### Food Safety Incident

**Triggers:**
- Pathogen detected in testing
- Customer illness report
- Contamination discovered

**Immediate Actions:**
```
1. CONTAIN (within 1 hour)
   - Stop all production and shipments
   - Isolate affected lots
   - Secure samples for testing

2. ASSESS (within 4 hours)
   - Scope of contamination
   - Products affected
   - Customers who received product

3. DECIDE (within 8 hours)
   - Recall necessary?
   - Level of recall (public vs. silent)
   - Regulatory notification required?

4. EXECUTE
   - Notify customers
   - Retrieve product
   - Root cause investigation
   - Corrective actions
   - Regulatory compliance
```

### Severe Weather Event

**Hurricane/Tornado:**
```
BEFORE (Watch):
- Review plan, assign roles
- Secure outdoor items
- Stage emergency supplies
- Top off generator fuel
- Harvest what you can

DURING (Warning):
- Shelter in safe location
- No outside activity
- Monitor conditions
- Equipment shutdowns if ordered

AFTER:
- Safety check (injuries, hazards)
- Damage assessment
- Restore critical systems
- Document damage (insurance)
- Begin recovery operations
```

### Workplace Violence

**Response:**
```
ACTIVE THREAT:
- Run: Escape if possible
- Hide: Lock/barricade, stay quiet
- Fight: Last resort, use anything as weapon
- Call 911 when safe

POST-INCIDENT:
- Ensure scene is secure
- Render first aid if trained
- Preserve evidence
- Support affected employees
- Investigate and prevent recurrence
```

## Post-Crisis Recovery

### After-Action Review

**Conduct within 48-72 hours**

**Agenda:**
```
1. TIMELINE REVIEW
   - What happened and when?
   - Sequence of events
   - Decision points

2. WHAT WENT WELL
   - Effective actions
   - Heroes and highlights
   - Systems that worked

3. WHAT COULD IMPROVE
   - Gaps identified
   - Confusion points
   - Resources needed

4. LESSONS LEARNED
   - Key takeaways
   - Unexpected discoveries
   - Best practices

5. ACTION ITEMS
   - Plan updates
   - Training needs
   - Equipment/supplies
   - Policy changes
```

### Psychological Recovery

**Supporting the Team:**
- Acknowledge trauma/stress
- Provide counseling if needed
- Allow time to process
- Gradual return to normal
- Monitor for ongoing distress

## Key Takeaways

1. **Prepare before crisis** - Plans, teams, resources, drills
2. **Activate decisively** - Don't delay, mobilize quickly
3. **Communicate constantly** - Internal and external, transparency
4. **Prioritize safety** - People first, then assets
5. **Document everything** - Decisions, actions, expenses
6. **Learn and improve** - Every crisis improves next response
7. **Support the team** - Crisis is traumatic, provide care
8. **Test your plans** - Drills reveal gaps before real crisis
