## Leadership vs. Management

### Understanding the Difference

**Management:** Doing things right
- Planning and budgeting
- Organizing and staffing
- Controlling and problem-solving
- Maintaining order and consistency
- Focus: Systems and processes

**Leadership:** Doing the right things
- Setting direction and vision
- Aligning and inspiring people
- Motivating and empowering
- Creating change and innovation
- Focus: People and culture

**Both Are Needed:**
```
Great Facility Managers = Strong Management + Strong Leadership

Management ensures efficient operations
Leadership inspires excellence and growth
```

## Core Leadership Competencies

### 1. Vision and Direction

**Setting the Course:**
- Paint a compelling picture of the future
- Connect daily work to bigger purpose
- Make goals clear and achievable
- Inspire commitment and effort

**Example Vision Statement:**
```
"We will be the region's premier provider of premium,
sustainably-grown produce, known for unmatched quality,
reliability, and innovation - creating value for customers,
meaningful work for employees, and positive environmental impact."

Translation to Daily Work:
"Every head of lettuce we grow represents our commitment
to quality. When you carefully monitor EC and pH, when you
gently handle crops at harvest, when you ensure perfect
packaging - you're making our vision real."
```

### 2. Communication

**Effective Communication:**
- Clear and concise messaging
- Active listening
- Appropriate frequency
- Two-way dialogue
- Multiple channels

**Communication Modes:**

| Mode | When to Use | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Face-to-face** | Important issues, feedback | Performance review |
| **Team meetings** | Alignment, updates | Daily huddle |
| **Written** | Documentation, detail | SOP update |
| **Visual** | Status, metrics | Dashboard |
| **One-on-one** | Coaching, sensitive topics | Career development |

### 3. Decision Making

**Decision-Making Framework:**

```
┌─ Assess Situation
│  ├─ Gather facts and data
│  ├─ Identify stakeholders
│  └─ Understand constraints
│
├─ Generate Options
│  ├─ Brainstorm alternatives
│  ├─ Evaluate pros/cons
│  └─ Consider risks
│
├─ Decide
│  ├─ Apply criteria
│  ├─ Choose best option
│  └─ Commit to decision
│
├─ Implement
│  ├─ Create action plan
│  ├─ Communicate decision
│  └─ Execute
│
└─ Review
   ├─ Monitor results
   ├─ Learn from outcome
   └─ Adjust if needed
```

**When to Decide Yourself vs. Involve Team:**

**Decide Yourself:**
- Emergency/time-critical
- Highly sensitive/confidential
- You have unique expertise
- Decision is yours to make (non-negotiable)

**Involve Team:**
- Implementation depends on their buy-in
- They have knowledge/expertise
- Good development opportunity
- Multiple valid approaches exist

### 4. Accountability

**Creating Accountable Culture:**

**Clear Expectations:**
- Specific goals and standards
- Defined responsibilities
- Measurable outcomes
- Agreed-upon timeline

**Follow-Up:**
- Regular check-ins
- Track commitments
- Provide support
- Address shortfalls promptly

**Consequences:**
- Positive: Recognition, rewards
- Negative: Coaching, corrective action
- Consistent application
- Fair and transparent

## Building High-Performing Teams

### Stages of Team Development (Tuckman)

**1. Forming:**
- Getting to know each other
- Polite, uncertain
- Leader-dependent
- **Leader Role:** Provide structure, set expectations

**2. Storming:**
- Conflict emerges
- Challenging each other
- Testing boundaries
- **Leader Role:** Facilitate conflict resolution, reinforce goals

**3. Norming:**
- Establishing routines
- Building trust
- Resolving differences
- **Leader Role:** Support collaboration, develop team norms

**4. Performing:**
- High productivity
- Self-managing
- Collaborative problem-solving
- **Leader Role:** Delegate, remove obstacles, stretch goals

**5. Adjourning:**
- Task complete/team disbands
- Reflection and celebration
- **Leader Role:** Recognize accomplishment, capture learnings

### Characteristics of High-Performing Teams

**What Makes Teams Effective:**

```
HIGH-PERFORMING TEAM CHARACTERISTICS

1. CLEAR PURPOSE
   ✓ Shared goals understood by all
   ✓ Know how success is measured
   ✓ See how work contributes to bigger picture

2. DEFINED ROLES
   ✓ Clear responsibilities
   ✓ Complementary skills
   ✓ Mutual respect for expertise

3. OPEN COMMUNICATION
   ✓ Psychological safety
   ✓ Active listening
   ✓ Constructive disagreement welcome

4. MUTUAL ACCOUNTABILITY
   ✓ Commitment to team goals
   ✓ Hold each other accountable
   ✓ Collective ownership of results

5. COLLABORATIVE CULTURE
   ✓ Trust and respect
   ✓ Support each other
   ✓ Celebrate wins together

6. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
   ✓ Learn from mistakes
   ✓ Seek feedback
   ✓ Innovate and experiment
```

### Team-Building Strategies

**Daily Practices:**
- Start-of-shift huddles (alignment)
- Recognize contributions publicly
- Encourage peer-to-peer help
- Solve problems together

**Periodic Activities:**
- Team lunches or celebrations
- Cross-training rotations
- Kaizen events (collaborative improvement)
- Team goal-setting sessions
- Skill development workshops

**Building Trust:**
1. **Reliability:** Do what you say
2. **Competence:** Demonstrate capability
3. **Openness:** Share information
4. **Respect:** Value everyone's contribution
5. **Integrity:** Consistent values and actions

## Coaching and Development

### Coaching Mindset

**Coach vs. Tell:**

| Telling (Traditional) | Coaching (Developmental) |
|----------------------|---------------------------|
| "Here's what to do" | "What do you think you should do?" |
| Gives answers | Asks questions |
| Creates dependency | Builds capability |
| Fast short-term | Sustainable long-term |
| One-way | Two-way dialogue |

**When to Coach vs. Tell:**
- **Tell:** Emergency, safety-critical, new employee, no time
- **Coach:** Development opportunity, person has capacity, non-urgent

### GROW Coaching Model

**Framework for Coaching Conversations:**

```
G - GOAL
   What do you want to achieve?
   What would success look like?
   Why is this important?

R - REALITY
   What's happening now?
   What have you tried?
   What's working/not working?

O - OPTIONS
   What could you do?
   What else?
   What if you had no constraints?

W - WAY FORWARD
   What will you do?
   When will you do it?
   How will I support you?
   How will we measure progress?
```

**Example Coaching Conversation:**

```
Scenario: Operator struggling with transplant efficiency

GOAL:
Leader: "What would you like to improve?"
Operator: "I want to speed up my transplanting. I'm at 1,200 plants/hr
          but the standard is 1,500."
Leader: "Why is that important to you?"
Operator: "I want to keep up with the team and meet our production targets."

REALITY:
Leader: "What's your current process?"
Operator: "I pick up each seedling, place in net pot, add media, then
          insert into channel."
Leader: "Where do you think time is being lost?"
Operator: "Maybe when I'm reaching for media? And sometimes the seedlings
          stick in the plug tray."

OPTIONS:
Leader: "What could you try to address those issues?"
Operator: "I could stage media closer... maybe use the moistening spray
          on sticky plugs like Sarah does."
Leader: "What else might help?"
Operator: "I could batch the media addition - do 10 at a time before inserting."

WAY FORWARD:
Leader: "Which will you try first?"
Operator: "Let me test the media staging and Sarah's spray technique tomorrow."
Leader: "Great. I'll check in with you at break. What will tell you it's working?"
Operator: "If I can get to 1,400 plants/hr by end of week."
Leader: "Sounds like a plan. Let me know if you need anything."
```

### Giving Effective Feedback

**SBI-I Model:**

**S - Situation:** Describe when and where
**B - Behavior:** Specific observable action
**I - Impact:** Effect of the behavior
**I - Intent/Improvement:** Reinforce or redirect

**Positive Feedback Example:**
```
S: "During yesterday's harvest..."
B: "...I noticed you double-checked every package weight..."
I: "...which resulted in zero customer complaints about short weights."
I: "Please continue this attention to detail - it ensures customer trust."
```

**Constructive Feedback Example:**
```
S: "In this morning's team meeting..."
B: "...you interrupted Maria twice while she was speaking..."
I: "...which shut down her contribution and made the team uncomfortable."
I: "In future meetings, please let others finish their thoughts. Your input
    is valuable, and so is everyone else's."
```

**Feedback Best Practices:**
- Timely (soon after behavior)
- Specific (not vague generalizations)
- Focused on behavior (not personality)
- Private for constructive (public for positive)
- Balanced (recognize strengths, address gaps)
- Two-way dialogue (listen to their perspective)

## Delegation and Empowerment

### Why Delegate?

**Benefits:**
- **For you:** Free time for strategic work
- **For them:** Development opportunity, engagement
- **For organization:** Build bench strength, succession planning

**What to Delegate:**
- Routine tasks (you've done many times)
- Learning opportunities (stretch assignments)
- Decisions at their level (empower)

**What NOT to Delegate:**
- Performance management
- Sensitive/confidential issues
- Crisis situations (until they're ready)
- Your core responsibilities

### Delegation Process

**Steps:**

**1. Select the Right Person**
- Has capacity (time, bandwidth)
- Has capability (skills or can learn)
- Motivated to take it on

**2. Define the Assignment**
- Clear outcome expected
- Authority level granted
- Resources available
- Deadline

**3. Provide Support**
- Initial training/guidance
- Check-in points
- Availability for questions
- Remove obstacles

**4. Monitor Progress**
- Regular updates
- Course-correct if needed
- Don't micromanage

**5. Review Results**
- Evaluate outcome
- Provide feedback
- Recognize achievement
- Capture learnings

**Authority Levels:**
```
Level 1: Gather information, I'll decide
Level 2: Recommend action, I'll decide
Level 3: Decide and inform me
Level 4: Decide and act, inform me afterward
Level 5: Decide and act, no need to inform me
```

### Empowerment Culture

**Creating Empowered Teams:**

**Clarity:**
- Clear goals and boundaries
- Understood decision rights
- Transparent expectations

**Capability:**
- Skills and knowledge
- Training and development
- Access to information

**Confidence:**
- Trust from leadership
- Support when needed
- Safe to take risks/fail

**Example:**
```
Task: Daily harvest planning

Traditional (Not Empowered):
- Manager creates harvest schedule
- Tells team what to harvest
- Team executes without input
- Issues escalated to manager

Empowered:
- Team reviews orders and crop maturity
- Creates harvest plan within guidelines
- Makes adjustments during day as needed
- Escalates only unusual situations
- Manager reviews results, provides feedback
```

## Managing Conflict

### Types of Conflict

**1. Task Conflict:** Disagreement about what/how to do work
- Can be productive (leads to better solutions)
- Manage by focusing on data and goals

**2. Process Conflict:** Disagreement about workflow/procedures
- Often stems from unclear roles
- Resolve by clarifying and standardizing

**3. Relationship Conflict:** Personal friction between people
- Usually destructive
- Address promptly before escalation

### Conflict Resolution Strategies

**Thomas-Kilmann Model:**

```
                     Assertiveness
                          ↑
                          │
               COMPETING  │  COLLABORATING
    (Win-Lose)            │        (Win-Win)
                          │
         ─────────────────┼─────────────────→
                          │               Cooperativeness
               AVOIDING   │  ACCOMMODATING
           (Lose-Lose)    │     (Lose-Win)
                          │
                COMPROMISING
                (Split Difference)
```

**When to Use Each:**

**Avoiding:** Low stakes, need time to cool off
**Accommodating:** Preserve relationship, not important to you
**Competing:** Emergency, need quick decision, you're right on critical issue
**Compromising:** Both have valid points, time pressure
**Collaborating:** Important to both, time available, win-win possible

### Difficult Conversations

**Prepare:**
- Clarify your intent (help, not punish)
- Gather facts
- Consider their perspective
- Choose right time/place (private, unrushed)

**Conduct:**
```
1. State purpose
   "I'd like to discuss attendance. Can we talk now?"

2. Share observations
   "I've noticed you've been late 4 times in past 2 weeks."

3. Seek their perspective
   "What's going on? This isn't typical for you."

4. Listen actively
   (Let them explain without interrupting)

5. Problem-solve together
   "How can we address this? What support do you need?"

6. Agree on action
   "So you'll adjust your morning routine and aim for 15 minutes early?"

7. Confirm understanding
   "Let's check in next week. Sound good?"

8. Document
   (Note conversation and agreements)
```

**Stay Calm:**
- Breathe, pause before responding
- Focus on facts, not emotions
- Assume positive intent
- Separate person from problem

## Motivating and Engaging Teams

### Understanding Motivation

**Intrinsic Motivators (more powerful):**
- Purpose (meaningful work)
- Autonomy (control over work)
- Mastery (getting better)
- Connection (relationships)

**Extrinsic Motivators:**
- Pay and bonuses
- Benefits
- Recognition and awards
- Promotion

**Both Matter, Intrinsic More Sustainable**

### Recognition Strategies

**Types of Recognition:**

**Formal:**
- Employee of the month
- Annual awards
- Bonuses and raises
- Promotion

**Informal:**
- Verbal thank you
- Handwritten note
- Public acknowledgment
- Small gift cards

**Characteristics of Effective Recognition:**
- **Specific:** "Great job" vs. "Your quick action to fix the pump prevented crop loss"
- **Timely:** Soon after the behavior
- **Sincere:** Genuine appreciation
- **Public (usually):** In front of peers
- **Fair:** Everyone has opportunity

### Creating Engaging Work

**Job Characteristics Model:**

**1. Skill Variety:** Use different skills
- Cross-training, job rotation

**2. Task Identity:** Complete whole task
- Avoid excessive fragmentation

**3. Task Significance:** Meaningful impact
- Connect work to customer/mission

**4. Autonomy:** Control over how to work
- Within boundaries, let them decide

**5. Feedback:** Know how you're doing
- Regular, specific feedback

**Example:**
```
Low Engagement Job Design:
- Repeat same task all day (low variety)
- Only do one small step (low identity)
- Don't see final product (low significance)
- Told exactly how to do it (low autonomy)
- No feedback on quality (low feedback)

High Engagement Job Design:
- Rotate through harvest, pack, QC (high variety)
- Responsible for complete order (high identity)
- See customer reactions (high significance)
- Determine best approach (high autonomy)
- Daily quality metrics visible (high feedback)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Leadership is about people** - Inspire, develop, empower
2. **Build high-performing teams** - Clear goals, trust, accountability
3. **Coach, don't just tell** - Develop capability
4. **Give frequent feedback** - Specific, timely, balanced
5. **Delegate effectively** - Free yourself, grow others
6. **Address conflict early** - Don't let it fester
7. **Recognize and motivate** - Intrinsic and extrinsic
8. **Create engaging work** - Meaningful, varied, autonomous
