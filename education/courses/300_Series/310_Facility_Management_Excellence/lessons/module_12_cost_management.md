## CEA Cost Structure

### Operating Cost Categories

**Fixed Costs (Don't vary with production):**
- Facility lease/mortgage
- Insurance
- Management salaries
- Property taxes
- Depreciation

**Variable Costs (Vary with production):**
- Seeds
- Nutrients
- Growing media
- Packaging
- Direct labor (harvest, pack)
- Utilities (energy, water)

**Semi-Variable Costs:**
- Maintenance
- Quality control
- Supervision
- Some utilities (baseload + variable)

## Cost Calculation Methods

### Cost Per Pound (Standard Metric)

```
Cost per Pound = Total Operating Costs / Pounds Produced

Example (Monthly):
Operating costs: $45,000
Production: 30,000 lbs
Cost/lb = $45,000 / 30,000 = $1.50/lb
```

### Cost Per Square Foot

```
Cost per Sq Ft = Total Costs / Growing Area Sq Ft

Annual Example:
Total costs: $540,000
Growing area: 10,000 sq ft
Cost/sq ft = $540,000 / 10,000 = $54/sq ft/year
```

### Unit Economics

```
Unit: 1 head of lettuce

Revenue: $2.50
Costs:
├─ Seeds: $0.08
├─ Nutrients: $0.07
├─ Media: $0.05
├─ Energy: $0.38
├─ Labor: $0.53
├─ Packaging: $0.10
├─ Overhead: $0.30
└─ Total: $1.51

Contribution Margin: $0.99 per head (40%)
```

## Cost Drivers and Optimization

### 1. Labor Optimization

**Current State Analysis:**
```
Total labor hours: 800 hrs/week
Production: 10,000 lbs/week
Labor productivity: 12.5 lbs/hr
Labor rate: $18/hr average
Labor cost: $14,400/week = $1.44/lb
```

**Improvement Strategies:**

**A. Eliminate Waste:**
- Remove non-value activities
- Reduce waiting time
- Improve workflow
- Target: +15% productivity

**B. Cross-Training:**
- Flexible workforce
- Reduce idle time
- Better coverage
- Target: -10% overtime

**C. Automation:**
- Mechanize repetitive tasks
- Seeders, packaging
- ROI analysis required
- Target: -20% manual labor

**Improved State:**
```
Productivity: 14.4 lbs/hr (+15%)
Reduced overtime: Save $1,500/week
Labor cost: $1.29/lb (10% reduction)
Annual savings: $78,000
```

### 2. Energy Optimization

**Energy Audit:**
```
Monthly Energy Use: 75,000 kWh
Cost: $0.12/kWh = $9,000/month
Production: 30,000 lbs
Energy cost: $0.30/lb

Breakdown:
├─ Lighting: 50,000 kWh (67%)
├─ HVAC: 20,000 kWh (27%)
├─ Pumps: 3,000 kWh (4%)
└─ Other: 2,000 kWh (2%)
```

**Reduction Strategies:**

**Lighting (Largest Opportunity):**
```
Current: 1,000W fixtures × 50 = 50 kW
Hours: 18 hrs/day × 30 days = 540 hrs
Monthly: 50 kW × 540 hrs = 27,000 kWh

Upgrade to LED (600W):
New: 600W × 50 = 30 kW
Monthly: 30 × 540 = 16,200 kWh
Savings: 10,800 kWh/month = $1,296/month

Investment: $25,000
Payback: 25,000 / 1,296 = 19 months
```

**HVAC Optimization:**
- Insulation improvements
- Setpoint optimization (±1°F can save 5%)
- Maintenance (clean coils, filters)
- Target: 10% reduction = 2,000 kWh/month

**Total Energy Savings:**
- Lighting: $1,296/month
- HVAC: $240/month
- Total: $1,536/month = $18,432/year
- New energy cost: $0.25/lb (17% reduction)

### 3. Material Cost Reduction

**Strategies:**

**A. Supplier Negotiation:**
```
Current: Seeds $0.08/lb produced
Volume: 360,000 lbs/year
Annual spend: $28,800

Negotiation:
├─ Volume discount (10%): Save $2,880
├─ Annual contract pricing: Save $1,440
└─ Total savings: $4,320/year

New seed cost: $0.068/lb
```

**B. Waste Reduction:**
```
Current: 5% of materials wasted
Target: 2% waste (better handling, storage)
Savings: 3% of $108,000 = $3,240/year
```

**C. Alternative Suppliers:**
- Multi-source critical items
- Test lower-cost alternatives
- Balance cost vs. quality

### 4. Yield Improvement (Biggest Lever)

**Impact:**
```
Current: 30 lbs/sq ft/year
Fixed costs allocated: $540,000/year
Growing area: 10,000 sq ft
Production: 300,000 lbs
Fixed cost per lb: $540,000 / 300,000 = $1.80/lb

Improved: 33 lbs/sq ft/year (+10%)
Production: 330,000 lbs
Fixed cost per lb: $540,000 / 330,000 = $1.64/lb

Savings: $0.16/lb on fixed costs
Annual value: $0.16 × 330,000 = $52,800

Plus: 30,000 additional lbs to sell
Revenue: 30,000 × $2.50 = $75,000
Variable cost: 30,000 × $0.80 = $24,000
Contribution: $51,000

Total Annual Benefit: $103,800
```

**Yield Improvement Methods:**
- Genetics (better varieties)
- Environmental optimization
- Crop scheduling
- Disease prevention
- Training and skill development

## Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

### Traditional vs. ABC

**Traditional Costing:**
- Allocates overhead based on direct labor or machine hours
- Can distort true costs
- Example: All products charged same overhead rate

**Activity-Based Costing:**
- Traces costs to activities
- Assigns activity costs to products based on consumption
- More accurate product costs

### ABC Implementation Example

**Activities and Cost Pools:**
```
Activity          │ Cost Pool │ Cost Driver          │ Rate
──────────────────┼───────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────
Seeding           │ $12,000   │ Trays seeded         │ $2/tray
Quality testing   │ $8,000    │ Batches tested       │ $40/batch
Packaging         │ $15,000   │ Units packaged       │ $0.05/unit
Order processing  │ $6,000    │ Orders processed     │ $30/order
Equipment setup   │ $4,000    │ Setup hours          │ $50/hour
```

**Product Cost Comparison:**

```
Product A (Romaine):
Direct costs: $1.00/lb
Activities consumed:
├─ Seeding: 0.5 trays = $1.00
├─ QC: 0.1 batches = $4.00
├─ Packaging: 4 units = $0.20
├─ Orders: 0.05 orders = $1.50
└─ Setup: 0.02 hrs = $1.00
ABC overhead: $7.70 per 12 lbs = $0.64/lb
Total ABC cost: $1.64/lb

Traditional overhead (30% of direct): $0.30/lb
Traditional total: $1.30/lb

ABC reveals Romaine is more expensive than thought
```

## Budgeting and Variance Analysis

### Creating Operating Budget

**Annual Budget Process:**
```
1. Sales Forecast
   ├─ Volume by product
   ├─ Pricing assumptions
   └─ Revenue projection

2. Production Plan
   ├─ Production volume needed
   ├─ Capacity requirements
   └─ Schedule

3. Cost Budget
   ├─ Variable costs (materials, labor)
   ├─ Fixed costs (facility, equipment)
   └─ Capital expenditures

4. Profit Projection
   └─ Revenue - Costs = Profit
```

## Cost Reduction Guidelines

### Principles

**1. Understand Before Cutting:**
- Analyze data thoroughly
- Identify true drivers
- Consider unintended consequences

**2. Cut Fat, Not Muscle:**
- Eliminate waste first
- Preserve value-creating activities
- Don't compromise safety or quality

**3. Involve the Team:**
- Frontline knows where waste is
- Engagement improves implementation
- Reduces resistance

**4. Measure and Track:**
- Verify savings are real
- Monitor for negative impacts
- Adjust as needed

### Cost Reduction Checklist

```
□ LABOR
  □ Optimize scheduling
  □ Cross-train for flexibility
  □ Eliminate waiting/idle time
  □ Improve processes (lean)
  □ Appropriate automation

□ ENERGY
  □ LED lighting upgrade
  □ Insulation improvements
  □ Efficient HVAC scheduling
  □ Sensor calibration
  □ Demand management

□ MATERIALS
  □ Supplier negotiation
  □ Volume discounts
  □ Reduce waste
  □ Alternative suppliers
  □ Just-in-time delivery

□ OVERHEAD
  □ Eliminate redundancies
  □ Automate admin tasks
  □ Reduce inventory
  □ Optimize space utilization
  □ Review subscriptions/services

□ YIELD IMPROVEMENT
  □ Better genetics
  □ Environmental optimization
  □ Operator training
  □ Quality management
  □ Preventive maintenance
```

## Capital Investment Decisions

### ROI Analysis

**Formula:**
```
ROI = (Gain from Investment - Cost of Investment) / Cost of Investment × 100

Example: LED Lighting Upgrade
Cost: $25,000
Annual savings: $15,552
ROI = ($15,552 - $0) / $25,000 × 100 = 62% first year
```

### Payback Period

```
Payback Period = Investment Cost / Annual Savings

Example:
Investment: $25,000
Annual savings: $15,552
Payback = $25,000 / $15,552 = 1.6 years

Guideline: <2-3 years for equipment upgrades
```

### Net Present Value (NPV)

```
NPV = Σ (Cash Flow / (1 + r)^t) - Initial Investment

Where:
r = discount rate (cost of capital)
t = time period

Example: 5-year equipment life, 10% discount rate
Year 0: -$25,000 (investment)
Year 1-5: +$15,552 (savings)

NPV = -25,000 + 15,552/(1.1) + 15,552/(1.1)² + ... + 15,552/(1.1)⁵
    = -25,000 + 58,943
    = $33,943

Decision: NPV > 0, invest!
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Know your costs** - Track cost per pound and understand drivers
2. **Biggest levers** - Yield improvement, labor productivity, energy efficiency
3. **Data-driven decisions** - Use ABC and variance analysis
4. **ROI focus** - Evaluate investments rigorously
5. **Involve team** - They know where waste is
6. **Balance** - Don't sacrifice quality or safety for cost savings
7. **Continuous improvement** - Small gains compound over time
8. **Benchmark** - Compare to industry standards
