## What is Lean?

### Lean Defined

**Lean** is a systematic approach to identifying and eliminating waste through continuous improvement, creating flow and producing only what is needed by the customer

**Core Concept:** Maximize customer value while minimizing waste

**Origins:**
- Developed at Toyota (Toyota Production System)
- Adapted to manufacturing worldwide
- Applied to healthcare, services, agriculture
- Increasingly relevant to CEA operations

### The Five Principles of Lean

```
1. DEFINE VALUE
   └─ What does the customer value?
      └─ Willing to pay for

2. MAP THE VALUE STREAM
   └─ Identify all steps in process
      └─ Separate value-added from waste

3. CREATE FLOW
   └─ Make value-creating steps flow
      └─ Eliminate delays and batching

4. ESTABLISH PULL
   └─ Produce only what customer wants
      └─ When they want it

5. SEEK PERFECTION
   └─ Continuous improvement
      └─ Never-ending process
```

### Lean Thinking in CEA

- Labor intensive (improve productivity)

**CEA Opportunity Areas:**

- Material handling and logistics
- Energy and resource consumption
- Quality control processes
- Maintenance and changeover

## The 8 Wastes (DOWNTIME)

### Identifying Waste

**Definition of Waste:** Any activity that consumes resources but creates no value for the customer

### 1. Defects

**Description:** Products or services that don't meet specifications

**CEA Examples:**
- Tip burn on lettuce → downgraded or scrapped
- Pest damage → unmarketable
- Mechanical damage during harvest
- Contamination during processing
- Labeling errors

**Impact:**
```
Example:
5% defect rate
Weekly production: 10,000 lbs
Cost per lb: $2.50

Waste: 10,000 × 0.05 × $2.50 = $1,250/week
Annual: $65,000
Plus: Labor, materials, utilities wasted on defects
```

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Root cause analysis and correction
- Poka-yoke (error-proofing)
- Standard work
- Training and skill development
- Quality at the source
- Process capability improvement

### 2. Overproduction

**Description:** Producing more, sooner, or faster than the next process needs

**CEA Examples:**
- Growing more lettuce than customer orders
- Seeding too many plants for available space
- Harvesting before orders confirmed
- Packaging ahead of shipping schedule

**Impact:**
- Spoilage and waste
- Excessive inventory holding costs
- Refrigeration energy waste
- Capital tied up
- Storage space consumed

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Just-in-time production
- Demand-based scheduling
- Smaller batch sizes
- Pull systems (kanban)
- Better forecasting
- Takt time pacing

### 3. Waiting

**Description:** Idle time when work, materials, or information is not ready

**CEA Examples:**
- Operators waiting for harvest to arrive for packing
- Equipment idle waiting for maintenance
- Crops ready but no orders to harvest
- Delayed shipments waiting for labels
- Staff waiting for assignments

**Impact:**
```
Example:
Packing team of 3 operators
Waits 30 minutes/day for harvest
$15/hour labor rate

Daily waste: 3 × 0.5 hrs × $15 = $22.50
Annual: $6,750 (plus lost production)
```

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Balance workflow
- Improve scheduling
- Cross-training for flexibility
- Standardized work
- Visual management
- Buffer management

### 4. Non-Utilized Talent

**Description:** Not fully utilizing people's skills, knowledge, and abilities

**CEA Examples:**
- Experienced growers doing only manual tasks
- Operators not asked for improvement ideas
- Skills not matched to roles
- Lack of training and development
- Top-down decision making only
- No problem-solving involvement

**Impact:**
- Missed improvement opportunities
- Low morale and engagement
- High turnover
- Slower learning organization
- Innovation stagnation

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Suggestion systems
- Kaizen teams
- Cross-functional problem solving
- Skills development programs
- Empowerment and autonomy
- Recognition systems

### 5. Transportation

**Description:** Unnecessary movement of materials or products

**CEA Examples:**
- Moving harvested crops long distances to pack area
- Transporting supplies from distant storage
- Multiple handling of packaged products
- Inefficient facility layout
- Poor location of materials

**Impact:**
```
Example:
Harvest area 100 ft from packing
10 trips/day with cart
5 minutes per round trip
$15/hour labor

Daily waste: (10 × 5 min / 60) × $15 = $12.50
Annual: $3,750
Plus: Equipment, damage risk, fatigue
```

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Optimize facility layout
- Point-of-use storage
- Continuous flow lines
- Reduce batch sizes
- Gravity and conveyors
- Cellular layouts

### 6. Inventory

**Description:** Excess raw materials, WIP, or finished goods beyond what's needed

**CEA Examples:**
- Excess seedlings in germination
- Too many plants ready for harvest
- Overstocked packaging materials
- Excess nutrients and supplies
- Unharvested mature crops

**Impact:**
- Space consumption
- Refrigeration energy
- Spoilage and obsolescence
- Capital tied up
- Hides problems (quality, capacity)

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Pull systems
- Smaller order quantities
- Improved demand forecasting
- Reduce lead times
- Supplier partnerships
- Just-in-time delivery

### 7. Motion

**Description:** Unnecessary movement of people

**CEA Examples:**
- Reaching for tools not within easy access
- Walking to get supplies during a task
- Bending, twisting, stretching excessively
- Searching for items
- Poor workstation layout

**Impact:**
```
Example:
Transplant operation
Worker walks 20 steps to get media (2× per tray)
100 trays/day
Each trip = 20 seconds

Daily waste: 100 × 2 × 20 sec = 4,000 sec = 1.1 hours
Annual: 330 hours of productive time lost
Plus: Ergonomic injuries, fatigue
```

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Ergonomic workstation design
- Shadow boards and tool organization
- Point-of-use storage
- Standard work with minimal motion
- Continuous improvement of layout
- Time and motion studies

### 8. Extra Processing

**Description:** Doing more work than customer requires or values

**CEA Examples:**
- Over-washing already-clean produce
- Excessive quality inspections
- Redundant data entry
- Multiple handling and re-sorting
- Over-packaging
- Unnecessary paperwork

**Impact:**
- Wasted labor time
- Equipment usage
- Material consumption
- Slower throughput
- Higher costs without value addition

**Elimination Strategies:**
- Understand customer requirements
- Eliminate non-value steps
- Simplify processes
- Automation where appropriate
- Standard work
- Value stream mapping

## 5S Workplace Organization

### The Five Steps of 5S

**5S Framework:**
```
1S: SORT (Seiri)
   └─ Separate needed from unneeded
      └─ Remove the unneeded

2S: SET IN ORDER (Seiton)
   └─ A place for everything
      └─ Everything in its place

3S: SHINE (Seiso)
   └─ Clean and inspect
      └─ Maintain standards

4S: STANDARDIZE (Seiketsu)
   └─ Establish standards
      └─ Visual management

5S: SUSTAIN (Shitsuke)
   └─ Maintain discipline
      └─ Continuous improvement
```

### Implementing 5S in CEA

**1S: Sort (Seiri)**

**Goal:** Remove unnecessary items from workspace

**Process:**
1. Red tag items not used regularly
2. Evaluate each red-tagged item
3. Dispose, relocate, or store
4. Keep only what's needed daily

**Example - Harvest Area:**
```
Before Sort:
- 5 harvest knives (only need 2)
- Broken scale (not used)
- Empty boxes (no current need)
- Old paperwork (outdated)
- Extra hand tools (rarely used)

After Sort:
✓ 2 harvest knives (daily use)
✓ Working scale
✓ Current day's boxes only
✓ Today's paperwork only
✓ Essential tools only

Red tagged items → Relocated to central tool crib
```

**2S: Set in Order (Seiton)**

**Goal:** Organize items for efficiency

**Principles:**
- Frequently used items closest
- Visual indicators for location
- Ergonomic placement
- Reduce search time to zero

**Tools:**
- Shadow boards
- Color coding
- Labels and signs
- Floor markings
- Designated locations

**Example - Packing Station:**
```
PACKING STATION LAYOUT:

     [Shadow Board]          [Incoming Product]
     - Scale                      ↓
     - Tape gun              ┌─────────┐
     - Scissors              │ Lettuce │
     - Marker                │ Staging │
                             └─────────┘
                                  ↓
   [Packaging Supplies]      [Work Zone]
   ├─ Boxes (right)         Operator Here
   ├─ Labels (front)              ↓
   └─ Bags (left)          [Finished Goods]
                                  ↓
                            [Shipping Area]

Key:
- Everything within arm's reach
- No walking during packing
- Visual restocking signals
- Ergonomic height
```

**3S: Shine (Seiso)**

**Goal:** Clean and inspect regularly

**Activities:**
- Daily cleaning
- Equipment inspection during cleaning
- Identify abnormalities
- Immediate response to issues

**Cleaning = Inspection**

**Example - Daily Shine Schedule:**
```
GROWING AREA SHINE CHECKLIST

Start of Shift (15 minutes):
□ Sweep floors
□ Wipe down grow tables
□ Clean walkways
□ Check for leaks (note location)
□ Inspect pumps (listen for unusual noise)
□ Check lighting (note any out bulbs)
□ Verify sensors clean (wipe if needed)
□ Empty trash

End of Shift (10 minutes):
□ Return tools to shadow boards
□ Wipe work surfaces
□ Sweep traffic areas
□ Dispose of plant waste
□ Stock supplies for next shift

Weekly Deep Clean (30 minutes):
□ Pressure wash floors
□ Clean reservoirs
□ Wipe down equipment
□ Organize storage areas
□ Deep clean harvest tools
```

**Goal:** Create standards to maintain 1S-3S

**Methods:**

- Schedules
- Responsibility assignments
- Visual management

## Value Stream Mapping

### What is a Value Stream Map?

**Value Stream Map (VSM):** Visual representation of all activities (value-added and non-value-added) required to bring a product from raw material to customer

**Purpose:**
- See the entire flow
- Identify waste
- Find improvement opportunities
- Create future state vision

### Creating a Value Stream Map

**Steps:**
1. Select product/family to map
2. Walk the process (Gemba)
3. Document current state
4. Analyze for waste
5. Design future state
6. Create implementation plan

### Future State Vision

**Improvement Principles:**
1. **Produce to takt time** - Match customer demand pace
2. **Create continuous flow** - Eliminate batching where possible
3. **Use pull systems** - Produce only what's needed
4. **Level the workload** - Smooth production
5. **Improve processes first** - Before automation

**Future State Improvements:**
```
Target Improvements:
- Reduce harvest wait: 2 hrs → 30 min (better scheduling)
- Eliminate package wait: 4 hrs → 0 (kanban system)
- Improve harvest uptime: 95% → 98% (better maintenance)

Impact:
- Reduced lead time: 12.3 hrs → 6.9 hrs (44% reduction)

- Better freshness: Less time from harvest to customer
```

## Pull Systems and Kanban

### Push vs. Pull

**Push System:**
- Produce based on forecast
- Build inventory "just in case"
- Risk of overproduction

**Pull System:**
- Produce based on actual demand
- Replenish only what was consumed
- Minimize inventory

### Kanban System

**Kanban:** Signal to produce or move items

**Types:**
- **Production kanban:** Authorization to produce
- **Withdrawal kanban:** Authorization to move/withdraw
- **Signal kanban:** Visual trigger to reorder

**CEA Kanban Example - Transplant Station:**

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║              TRANSPLANT KANBAN BOARD                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                      ║
║  Romaine Lettuce                                     ║
║                                                      ║
║  Red Zone (0-2 days supply):  [  ] [  ] [  ]        ║
║  ↑ URGENT - Transplant immediately                   ║
║                                                      ║
║  Yellow Zone (3-4 days):      [  ] [  ] [XX] [  ]   ║
║  ↑ WARNING - Schedule soon                           ║
║                                                      ║
║  Green Zone (5-7 days):       [XX] [XX] [XX]        ║
║  ↑ OK - Normal operations                            ║
║                                                      ║
║  Current Status: 6 days supply ✓                     ║
║  Action: Continue normal schedule                    ║
║                                                      ║
║  XX = Cohort in nursery                              ║
║  [  ] = Empty slot - need to transplant              ║
║                                                      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

How it works:
1. Each card (XX) represents one day's worth of transplants
2. As cohorts move to growing area, cards removed
3. When yellow zone reached, increase transplant priority
4. When red zone reached, urgent transplant needed
5. Never let board go empty (stockout)
```

## Kaizen (Continuous Improvement)

### What is Kaizen?

**Kaizen:** Change for the better; continuous improvement involving everyone

**Types:**
- **Daily kaizen:** Small improvements by individuals
- **Point kaizen:** Quick fixes (minutes to hours)
- **Kaizen events:** Focused improvement projects (3-5 days)

### Conducting a Kaizen Event

**Typical 5-Day Kaizen Event:**

```
Day 1: UNDERSTAND
- Training on lean tools
- Current state data collection
- Gemba walk
- Problem definition

Day 2: ANALYZE
- Value stream mapping
- Root cause analysis
- Waste identification
- Brainstorm solutions

Day 3: DESIGN
- Develop future state
- Pilot testing
- Refine solutions
- Standard work development

Day 4: IMPLEMENT
- Make changes
- Train operators
- Verify improvements
- Adjust as needed

Day 5: VERIFY & REPORT
- Measure results
- Document new standards
- Create sustainment plan
- Present to leadership
```

**Kaizen Event Example - Harvest Process:**

```
KAIZEN EVENT RESULTS

Area: Harvest Operations
Goal: Reduce harvest time per 100 lbs

Current State:
- Time: 45 minutes / 100 lbs
- Labor: 2 operators
- Issues: Walking, searching, rework

Improvements Implemented:
1. Reorganized harvest tools (shadow board)
2. Staged harvest containers at point of use
3. Improved cutting technique training
4. Added mobile scale to eliminate transport
5. Created harvest sequence standard work

Future State:
- Time: 32 minutes / 100 lbs (29% reduction!)
- Labor: 2 operators
- Quality: Improved (less damage)

Annual Impact:
- Labor savings: 260 hours
- Capacity increase: +40%
- Cost savings: $6,500/year
```

## Lean Metrics

### Key Lean Indicators

**1. Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)**
```
OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
Target: >85%
```

**2. Lead Time**
```
Lead Time = Time from customer order to delivery
Target: Minimize while maintaining quality
```

**3. Inventory Turns**
```
Inventory Turns = Annual Sales / Average Inventory
Target: Maximize (indicates efficiency)

Example:
Annual sales: $1,200,000
Average inventory: $100,000
Turns = $1.2M / $100K = 12 turns/year
Average inventory age = 365 / 12 = 30 days
```

**4. First Pass Yield (FPY)**
```
FPY = Units produced right first time / Total units
Target: >95%

Example:
Produced: 10,000 lbs
Rework: 300 lbs
Scrap: 200 lbs
FPY = (10,000 - 300 - 200) / 10,000 = 95%
```

**5. Value-Added Ratio**
```
VA Ratio = Value-Added Time / Total Lead Time
Target: Maximize

Example:
Total lead time: 32 days
Value-added time: 31 days (growing)
VA Ratio = 31 / 32 = 96.9% (good for CEA!)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Lean is about waste elimination** - Identify and remove non-value activities
2. **8 wastes framework** - DOWNTIME provides systematic approach
3. **5S creates foundation** - Organized workplace enables all other improvements
4. **Value stream thinking** - See the big picture, not just individual processes
5. **Pull over push** - Produce to demand, not forecast
6. **Kaizen culture** - Everyone improving, every day
7. **Measure progress** - Lean metrics show the way
8. **Respect for people** - Engage team in improvement
