## Questions

### 1. What is the typical price premium for medicinal herbs compared to culinary herbs?
a) Same price
b) 50-100% higher
c) 200-600% higher
d) 1000% higher

### 2. Which certification is most valued by medicinal herb buyers?
a) FDA approval
b) Organic certification
c) Hydroponic certification
d) Local certification

### 3. What is a key consideration when growing medicinal herbs for commercial sale?
a) They must be dried before sale
b) Understanding regulatory compliance and making no medical claims
c) They require special licenses in all states
d) They can only be sold to pharmacies

### 4. Which medicinal herb is also known as Tulsi?
a) Lemon balm
b) Chamomile
c) Holy basil
d) Echinacea

### 5. What is the optimal harvest time for medicinal herbs to maximize active compounds?
a) Early morning after dew dries
b) Midday when sun is strongest
c) Late afternoon
d) Evening after sunset

### 6. Which medicinal herb is known for immune support properties?
a) Mint
b) Basil
c) Echinacea
d) Parsley

### 7. What is a primary market channel for medicinal herbs?
a) Restaurants
b) Wellness market, herbalists, and tea companies
c) Grocery stores only
d) Farmers markets exclusively

### 8. What percentage of medicinal herb sales require drying and processing?
a) Less than 25%
b) 25-50%
c) 50-75%
d) 75-100%

### 9. Which growing condition is particularly important for medicinal herb quality?
a) High nitrogen levels
b) Low stress to maximize active compound production
c) Maximum growth rate
d) Dense planting

### 10. What is the typical wholesale price range for premium dried medicinal herbs?
a) $8-15/lb
b) $15-30/lb
c) $20-60/lb
d) $60-100/lb

---

## Answer Key

1. **c) 200-600% higher**
   *Explanation: Medicinal herbs command significant premiums of 200-600% over standard culinary herbs due to specialized markets and processing requirements.*

2. **b) Organic certification**
   *Explanation: Organic certification is highly valued in the medicinal herb market as buyers prioritize purity and absence of synthetic chemicals.*

3. **b) Understanding regulatory compliance and making no medical claims**
   *Explanation: Growers must understand regulations and avoid making medical claims unless properly certified, as this is legally required.*

4. **c) Holy basil**
   *Explanation: Holy basil (Ocimum sanctum) is also known as Tulsi and is valued for its medicinal properties in Ayurvedic medicine.*

5. **a) Early morning after dew dries**
   *Explanation: Early morning harvest, after dew dries but before heat stress, maximizes essential oils and active compounds in medicinal herbs.*

6. **c) Echinacea**
   *Explanation: Echinacea is widely recognized for immune support properties and is a popular medicinal herb.*

7. **b) Wellness market, herbalists, and tea companies**
   *Explanation: Medicinal herbs are primarily sold to wellness markets, herbalists, tea companies, and supplement manufacturers rather than food service.*

8. **d) 75-100%**
   *Explanation: The majority of medicinal herbs (75-100%) require drying and processing for storage stability and concentrated active compounds.*

9. **b) Low stress to maximize active compound production**
   *Explanation: Controlled, low-stress growing conditions help maximize production of beneficial active compounds in medicinal herbs.*

10. **c) $20-60/lb**
    *Explanation: Premium dried medicinal herbs command wholesale prices of $20-60/lb, reflecting their specialized markets and processing requirements.*
