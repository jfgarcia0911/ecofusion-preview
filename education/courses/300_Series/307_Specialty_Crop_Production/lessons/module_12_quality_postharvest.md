## Harvest Timing for Quality

### Time of Day Matters

**Early Morning Harvest (Preferred):**
```
Timing: After dew dries, before 10 AM

Advantages:
- Maximum turgidity (overnight water uptake)
- Cool temperatures
- Peak essential oil content (herbs)
- Longer shelf life (30-50% better)
- Less field heat to remove

Best for: Greens, herbs, edible flowers, delicate crops
```

**Evening Harvest (Acceptable):**
```
Timing: After 5 PM, as temperatures cool

Advantages:
- Plants have cooled from day heat
- Alternative if morning not possible

Best for: Sturdier crops (cucumbers, peppers, tomatoes)
```

**Avoid Midday Harvest:**
```
Issues:
- High field heat (harder to cool)
- Plants stressed from heat/sun
- Reduced turgidity
- Shorter shelf life
- Lower quality

Exception: Only if immediate refrigeration available
```

## Field Heat Removal (Pre-Cooling)

### Critical Importance

**The "Hour Rule":**
```
Goal: Reduce product temperature from field temperature to storage temperature within 1 hour of harvest

Impact on Shelf Life:
- Pre-cooled within 1 hour: 100% shelf life potential
- Pre-cooled within 2 hours: 80-90% shelf life
- Pre-cooled within 4 hours: 60-70% shelf life
- No pre-cooling (>4 hours): 40-50% shelf life

Conclusion: Immediate cooling is critical for premium quality
```

### Pre-Cooling Methods

**Cold Water Immersion (Fast, Effective):**
```
Method:
- Harvest into containers
- Immediately immerse in cold water bath (34-40°F)
- 5-15 minutes (product dependent)
- Product temperature drops to near water temp

Advantages:
- Very fast cooling
- Removes field heat AND field debris
- Rehydrates slightly wilted products
- Simple, low-tech

Disadvantages:
- Water quality critical (clean, food-safe)
- Some products don't tolerate (basil, flowers)
- Need drain/dry afterward

Best for: Greens, Asian vegetables, herbs (most), root crops
```

**Refrigerated Air (Standard):**
```
Method:
- Place harvested product directly in cooler/refrigerator
- 34-40°F temperature
- High humidity (90-95%)
- Allow 2-4 hours for cooling

Advantages:
- Simple equipment (just cooler)
- Good for delicate products
- No water contact

Disadvantages:
- Slower than water immersion
- Requires cooler space

Best for: Edible flowers, tomatoes, peppers, lettuce heads
```

**Hydro-Cooling (Commercial Scale):**
```
Method:
- Conveyor system with cold water shower
- Product passes under water spray
- 34-40°F water
- Continuous process

Advantages:
- Very fast (seconds to minutes)
- High throughput
- Automated

Disadvantages:
- Expensive equipment ($5,000-20,000)
- Only viable for larger operations

Best for: High-volume operations (500+ lbs/day)
```

## Washing Protocols

### When to Wash

**Do Not Wash (Store Dry):**
```
Products:
- Tomatoes
- Peppers
- Most herbs (except if visibly dirty)
- Edible flowers (usually)

Reason:
- Moisture promotes decay
- Natural protective coating removed
- Reduced shelf life

Exception: If visibly dirty, wash gently and dry thoroughly
```

**Wash Before Storage:**
```
Products:
- Leafy greens (if soil/debris present)
- Root vegetables
- Crops from outdoor/soil systems

Process:
- MUST dry thoroughly before packaging
- Surface moisture = bacterial growth
```

**Wash Before Sale/Delivery:**
```
Products:
- Baby leaf salad mix
- Herbs (if requested by customer)
- Products sold as "ready-to-eat"

Process:
- Wash immediately before packaging
- Triple-wash system
- Spin dry completely
```

### Triple-Wash System (Food Safety Best Practice)

```
STATION 1: Pre-Rinse
- Cold water (38-42°F)
- Remove loose debris and field heat
- Gentle agitation
- 30-60 seconds

STATION 2: Wash
- Cold water (38-42°F)
- Optional: Food-safe sanitizer
  * Chlorine: 50-100 ppm
  * Peroxyacetic acid: 40-80 ppm
  * Hydrogen peroxide: 1% solution
- 1-2 minutes contact time
- Gentle agitation

STATION 3: Rinse
- Clean cold water only
- Remove any sanitizer residue
- 30-60 seconds
- Final rinse

DRYING:
- Salad spinner (small scale)
- Commercial spinner (larger scale)
- Must be surface-dry for packaging
- Remaining moisture = decay
```

## Storage Conditions by Crop Category

### Leafy Greens & Herbs (Most)

```
Temperature: 34-38°F
Humidity: 95-98%
Ethylene sensitivity: Moderate
Shelf life: 7-14 days (variety dependent)

Storage tips:
- Store in perforated bags or vented containers
- High humidity critical (prevent wilting)
- Good air circulation
- Check daily for any decay
```

### Basil (Exception to Above)

```
Temperature: 50-55°F (WARMER)
Humidity: 90-95%
Ethylene sensitivity: High
Shelf life: 7-10 days

Critical:
- DO NOT refrigerate (below 50°F = blackening)
- Keep away from tomatoes/fruits (ethylene)
- Stand stems in water OR humid environment
- Basil is tropical herb, needs warmer storage
```

### Tomatoes

```
Temperature: 55-60°F (Room temperature acceptable)
Humidity: 90-95%
Ethylene: Producer (keep separate from other crops)
Shelf life: 5-10 days (ripeness dependent)

Critical:
- DO NOT refrigerate (flavor loss, mealy texture)
- Refrigeration acceptable for over-ripe fruit (slows decay)
- Store stem-scar up (less bruising)
```

### Peppers

```
Temperature: 45-50°F
Humidity: 90-95%
Ethylene sensitivity: Low
Shelf life: 7-14 days

Notes:
- Chill-sensitive (below 45°F = pitting, discoloration)
- Warmer than greens, cooler than tomatoes
```

### Cucumbers & Squash

```
Temperature: 50-55°F
Humidity: 90-95%
Ethylene sensitivity: High
Shelf life: 5-7 days (cucumbers), 4-7 days (summer squash)

Notes:
- Chill-sensitive (below 50°F)
- Very sensitive to ethylene
- Relatively short shelf life
- Harvest to use quickly
```

### Edible Flowers

```
Temperature: 34-38°F
Humidity: 90-95%
Ethylene sensitivity: Very high
Shelf life: 2-10 days (species dependent)

Storage:
- Single layer only (no stacking)
- Clamshells with vents
- Away from all ethylene sources
- Most delicate products - handle minimally
```

## Quality Grading Standards

### Premium Grade (100% Price)

**Universal Criteria:**
```
Appearance:
- Perfect or near-perfect visual appeal
- Appropriate color for variety
- No blemishes, damage, or discoloration
- Uniform size within grade
- Clean (no soil, debris, insects)

Quality:
- Crisp, turgid (not wilted)
- Fresh appearance
- No decay or disease
- Appropriate ripeness/maturity

Suitable for:
- Fine dining restaurants
- Premium retail
- High-end farmers markets
- Commanding maximum price
```

### Choice Grade (75-90% Price)

**Criteria:**
```
Appearance:
- Good visual appeal
- Minor cosmetic imperfections acceptable
- Slight size variation OK
- Generally clean

Quality:
- Good condition
- Minor wilting acceptable
- No significant decay
- Suitable for intended use

Suitable for:
- Standard restaurants
- Retail
- Farmers markets
- General commercial use
```

### Standard/Processing Grade (40-65% Price)

**Criteria:**
```
Appearance:
- Significant cosmetic issues
- Size variation
- Some damage acceptable

Quality:
- Still food-safe and edible
- May have slight decay (removed areas)
- Not suitable for fresh market presentation
- Good for processing, cooking

Suitable for:
- Value-conscious buyers
- Processing/value-added
- Farmers market "seconds" discount
- Food banks (donation)
```

## Packaging by Market Channel

### Restaurant Wholesale

```
Packaging:
- Reusable containers (cost-effective)
- Perforated plastic bags
- Breathable containers
- Minimal packaging (reduce waste)

Labeling:
- Farm name
- Product name/variety
- Pack date
- Weight
- Contact information
- Simple, functional

Delivery:
- Coolers with ice packs
- Insulated bags
- Maintain 34-40°F during transport
- Minimize time out of refrigeration
```

### Retail (Grocery Stores)

```
Packaging:
- Consumer-ready clamshells or bags
- Professional appearance critical
- Clear view of product
- Proper venting (no condensation)

Labeling Requirements:
- Product name (common and variety)
- Weight (net weight)
- Farm name and address
- Growing method (Organic, Hydroponic, etc.)
- PLU or UPC code (larger stores require)
- Harvest/pack date or use-by date
- Storage instructions
- Nutritional info (optional but valued)

Quality:
- Must be premium grade
- Consistent sizing
- Attractive presentation
```

### Farmers Market

```
Packaging:
- Minimal packaging (eco-friendly appeal)
- Paper bags, reusable containers, or baskets
- Attractive display containers
- Easy for customers to see product

Labeling:
- Simple tags or signs
- Farm name and product
- Price
- Growing story/methods
- Recipe cards or usage info

Presentation:
- Visual appeal critical
- Samples (where appropriate)
- Educational signage
- Engage customers directly
```

## Cold Chain Management

### The Unbroken Cold Chain

```
CONCEPT: Product temperature never rises above optimal storage temperature from harvest to consumer

CRITICAL POINTS:
1. Harvest → Pre-cooling: <1 hour
2. Pre-cooling → Storage: Immediate
3. Storage → Packing: Work in cooler or quickly
4. Packing → Delivery: Insulated, ice packs/refrigeration
5. Delivery → Customer storage: Minimal time

WEAK LINK PRINCIPLE:
- Cold chain is only as strong as weakest link
- One breakdown can negate all other efforts
- Every temperature spike reduces shelf life

INVESTMENT AREAS:
- Adequate cooler space
- Backup cooling (power outages)
- Insulated transport containers
- Ice packs or refrigerated vehicle
- Temperature monitoring (thermometers)
```

## Common Post-Harvest Problems and Solutions

### Issue: Rapid Wilting After Harvest

**Causes:**
```
1. Harvested during heat of day (high field heat)
2. Delayed pre-cooling
3. Low humidity in storage
4. Ethylene exposure
5. Product over-mature at harvest
```

**Solutions:**
```
- Harvest early morning
- Immediate pre-cooling (<1 hour)
- Ensure 90-95% humidity in storage
- Separate ethylene-sensitive crops
- Harvest at proper maturity
```

---

### Issue: Decay/Mold During Storage

**Causes:**
```
1. Moisture on product in packaging
2. Damaged tissue (entry point for pathogens)
3. Diseased product mixed with healthy
4. Poor air circulation in storage
5. Temperature too warm
```

**Solutions:**
```
- Dry thoroughly before packaging
- Handle gently (minimize bruising)
- Cull any diseased/damaged product
- Ensure air circulation in cooler
- Verify temperature (34-38°F for most)
- Use perforated/vented packaging
```

---

### Issue: Premature Yellowing

**Causes:**
```
1. Ethylene exposure
2. Temperature too warm
3. Over-mature at harvest
4. Nitrogen deficiency in growing
```

**Solutions:**
```
- Store away from ethylene producers
- Verify storage temperature
- Harvest at proper stage
- Ensure adequate nutrition during growing
```

---

### Issue: Short Shelf Life (Unexpectedly)

**Causes:**
```
1. Delayed cooling after harvest
2. Temperature fluctuations in storage
3. Low humidity
4. Product was stressed during growth
5. Variety selection (some inherently shorter)
```

**Solutions:**
```
- Immediate pre-cooling protocol
- Stable storage temperature
- Monitor and maintain humidity
- Optimal growing conditions
- Choose varieties known for shelf life
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Pre-cooling within 1 hour of harvest is critical** - determines 50-100% of achievable shelf life
2. **Storage temperature varies by crop** - tomatoes and basil require warmer storage than greens
3. **Moisture management is critical** - too much causes decay, too little causes wilting
4. **Cold chain must be unbroken** - every temperature spike reduces quality and shelf life
5. **Quality grading impacts pricing** - premium grade commands 25-60% higher prices than choice grade
6. **Packaging must match market channel** - restaurant, retail, and farmers market have different requirements
7. **Daily inspection prevents problems** - catch and remove decaying product before it spreads
