## Business Model Selection

### Scale and Focus Options

**Micro-Scale (100-200 sq ft):**
```
Best Model: Direct sales focus
- Farmers markets
- Direct to chef
- CSA (small, 10-20 members)
- Specialty products (microgreens, edible flowers)

Challenges:
- Labor efficiency difficult
- Limited economies of scale
- Must command premium prices

Success Factor: Highest-value crops only
```

**Small-Scale (200-1,000 sq ft):**
```
Best Model: Mixed sales channels
- Restaurant wholesale (primary)
- Farmers markets (secondary)
- Retail accounts (2-3 stores)
- Online sales

Advantages:
- Diversification reduces risk
- Better labor efficiency
- Room for crop variety
- Sustainable profitability possible

Success Factor: Balanced crop mix and market development
```

**Medium-Scale (1,000-5,000 sq ft):**
```
Best Model: Wholesale focus with specialty positioning
- Multiple restaurant accounts (10-30)
- Retail chain (regional)
- Food service distributor
- Some direct sales

Advantages:
- Economies of scale
- Justify staff/equipment
- Consistent demand
- Professional operation

Success Factor: Volume production with quality control
```

## Crop Mix Optimization

### Profitable Crop Mix Formula

**Allocation Strategy:**
```
60% - Reliable cash crops (weekly turnover)
- Baby leaf salad mix
- Standard culinary herbs
- Lettuce varieties
- Goal: Consistent revenue, fund operations

25% - Premium specialty crops (higher margins)
- Specialty herbs (Thai basil, shiso)
- Heirloom varieties
- Asian greens mixes
- Goal: Profit margin, differentiation

10% - Ultra-premium crops (highest pricing)
- Edible flowers
- Microgreens
- Exotic crops
- Goal: Maximum margin, brand positioning

5% - Trial crops (market testing)
- New varieties
- Experimental products
- Potential future additions
- Goal: Innovation, market development
```

### Example 500 sq ft Optimal Mix

```
ALLOCATION:

300 sq ft - Baby Leaf Mix (60%)
  Turnover: 3 weeks
  Annual production: 5,200 lbs
  Price: $14/lb average
  Revenue: $72,800

125 sq ft - Specialty Herbs (25%)
  Mix of basil, mint, specialty varieties
  Annual production: 650 lbs
  Price: $20/lb average
  Revenue: $13,000

50 sq ft - Microgreens & Edible Flowers (10%)
  High-value, fast-turn
  Annual production: 400 lbs (microgreens), 80 oz (flowers)
  Revenue: $10,000

25 sq ft - Trial Crops (5%)
  Rotating experimental varieties
  Revenue: Variable, $0-2,000

TOTAL ANNUAL REVENUE: $95,800-97,800

This mix provides:
- Consistent base revenue (baby leaf)
- Premium pricing (herbs, micros, flowers)
- Market differentiation (specialty focus)
- Innovation pipeline (trials)
```

## Cash Flow Management

### Monthly Cash Flow (Example: Moderate Scenario)

**Monthly Revenue Pattern:**
```
Months 1-3: $3,000-5,000 (ramp-up)
Months 4-6: $6,500-7,500 (building)
Months 7-12: $8,000-9,000 (full production)

Average: $8,200/month (after ramp-up)
```

**Monthly Expenses:**
```
Fixed costs: $3,800/month
- Rent, insurance, utilities base: $2,000
- Owner draw (minimum): $1,800

Variable costs: $4,100/month
- Labor (part-time): $650
- Seeds, nutrients: $350
- Electricity (climate, lights): $650
- Packaging and delivery: $700
- Marketing: $250
- Miscellaneous: $1,500

Total: $7,900/month
```

**Cash Flow Requirement:**
```
Breakeven: $7,900/month revenue
Target: $8,200/month (matches moderate scenario)
Margin: $300/month positive cash flow

CRITICAL: Months 1-6 have negative cash flow
Need working capital: $20,000-30,000 to bridge startup period
```

### Strategies for Cash Flow Management

**Reduce Startup Cash Drain:**
```
1. Start small, scale up (begin with 200 sq ft, expand to 500)
2. Focus on fastest-turn crops initially (baby leaf, microgreens)
3. Delay slow-payback crops (perennial herbs, fruiting crops)
4. Secure first customers BEFORE production starts
5. Pre-sell (CSA model, deposits from restaurants)
```

**Accelerate Revenue:**
```
1. Farmers markets (immediate payment)
2. Direct sales (no payment terms)
3. Require deposits for large standing orders
4. Offer early-pay discounts
5. Multiple small customers vs. few large (diversify risk)
```

**Manage Expenses:**
```
1. Owner labor initially (defer salary)
2. Use existing infrastructure where possible
3. Buy used equipment (coolers, processing)
4. Negotiate payment terms with suppliers
5. Start without full automation (add as profitable)
```

## Risk Assessment and Mitigation

### Major Business Risks

**Production Risks:**
```
Risk: Crop failure due to disease, pests, or environmental issues
Likelihood: Medium
Impact: High

Mitigation:
- Backup power supply
- Redundant environmental controls
- Diverse crop portfolio
- Crop insurance (if available)
- Emergency fund (10% of annual operating costs)
```

**Market Risks:**
```
Risk: Loss of major customer(s)
Likelihood: Medium-High
Impact: High

Mitigation:
- Diversify customer base (no single customer >20% revenue)
- Multiple market channels
- Written agreements/contracts
- Consistent quality and service
- Regular communication and relationship building
```

**Financial Risks:**
```
Risk: Insufficient cash flow for operations
Likelihood: High (startup phase)
Impact: Critical

Mitigation:
- Adequate startup capital (6-12 months operating expenses)
- Line of credit established
- Gradual scale-up
- Maintain emergency fund
- Conservative financial projections
```

**Operational Risks:**
```
Risk: Owner illness/inability to work
Likelihood: Low-Medium
Impact: High

Mitigation:
- Cross-train any staff
- Document all processes (SOPs)
- Backup personnel identified
- Business interruption insurance
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Adequate startup capital is critical** - plan for $50,000-70,000 investment plus 6-12 months working capital
2. **Profitability requires premium pricing and efficient production** - wholesale commodity pricing will not support small-scale specialty operations
3. **Diversified crop mix balances revenue stability and profit margins** - 60% reliable crops, 30% premium crops, 10% ultra-premium
4. **Cash flow planning is essential** - first 6-12 months typically negative, plan accordingly
5. **Customer diversification reduces risk** - no single customer should exceed 20% of revenue
6. **Start small and scale methodically** - prove concept before major expansion
7. **Track KPIs religiously** - data-driven decisions improve profitability and reduce risk
