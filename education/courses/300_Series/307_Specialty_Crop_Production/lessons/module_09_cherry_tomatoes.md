## Production Systems

### High-Wire System (Recommended)

**Configuration:**
```
Overhead wire: 7-8 ft high
Plant spacing: 18" in row, 4-5 ft between rows
Density: 2-3 plants per sq ft (vertical growing)
Support: Twine lowered as plant grows

Advantages:
- Maximum yield per sq ft (15-25 lbs/plant/year)
- Efficient harvest (fruit at waist height)
- Good air circulation (disease prevention)
- Professional appearance
```

**Pruning Strategy:**
```
Single-leader system:
- Remove all suckers weekly
- Maintain one main stem
- Lower leaves removed as plant grows
- Top plant at 6-7 ft or when reaches wire

Result:
- Focus energy on fruit production
- Easier pest/disease management
- Larger, more uniform fruit
- Longer production season
```

### Dutch Bucket System

**Good for Tomatoes:**
```
Bucket size: 5 gallon minimum
Media: Coconut coir or perlite/vermiculite
Irrigation: Drip, 4-8x daily
Drainage: Critical (no standing water)

Benefits:
- Individual plant control
- Easy to adjust nutrients
- Moveable for management
- Suitable for long-season crop
```

## Nutrient Management

### Growth Stage Nutrition

**Vegetative (Weeks 1-4):**
```
EC: 2.0-2.5 mS/cm
N-P-K: 3-1-2
Goal: Establish strong plant structure
```

**Early Fruiting (Weeks 5-10):**
```
EC: 2.5-3.0 mS/cm
N-P-K: 2-1-3
Increase: K, Ca for fruit development
```

**Peak Production (Weeks 10+):**
```
EC: 3.0-4.0 mS/cm
N-P-K: 1-1-3
High potassium for fruit quality and flavor
Calcium: 180-220 ppm (blossom end rot prevention)
```

### Flavor Optimization

**Higher EC = Better Flavor:**
```
Standard EC: 2.5-3.0 mS/cm (good yield, moderate flavor)
High EC: 3.5-4.5 mS/cm (lower yield, excellent flavor)

For premium market:
- Run higher EC (3.5-4.0)
- Accept 15-20% lower yield
- Charge 50-100% premium for superior flavor
- Market as "intensely flavored" specialty tomatoes
```

**Environmental Factors:**
```
Light: Maximum light = better flavor
- DLI: 20-30 mol/m²/day optimal
- Greenhouse or supplemental lighting

Temperature: Moderate stress improves flavor
- Day: 75-80°F
- Night: 62-68°F (cool nights = better flavor)
- Avoid excessive heat (>85°F)

Humidity: 60-75%
- Lower humidity = more concentrated flavor
- But watch for blossom end rot
```

## Pollination

### Hand Pollination (Recommended in Controlled Environment)

**Method:**
```
Electric pollinator (vibrating wand):
- Visit each flower cluster
- 2-3 seconds of vibration
- 3x per week minimum
- Best between 10 AM - 2 PM

Manual alternative:
- Shake plant gently
- Or tap flower clusters with finger
- Less effective than electric pollinator

Results:
- Better fruit set (80-95% vs. 60-70% natural)
- More uniform fruit size
- Increased yield (20-30%)
```

## Harvest and Quality Standards

### Optimal Harvest Timing

**Maturity Indicators:**
```
Color: Full varietal color development
Feel: Slight give when squeezed gently
Taste: Sample for sweetness (best indicator)
Stem: Beginning to separate from vine

For Maximum Flavor:
- Wait until fully ripe (color + taste)
- Vine-ripened superior to picked early
- Worth slight reduction in shelf life
```

### Quality Grading

**Premium Grade (100% price):**
```
Criteria:
- Perfect shape and color
- No blemishes or cracks
- Uniform size
- Excellent flavor
- Stem attached (preferred)
- Clean, no debris

Market: Fine dining, premium retail
```

**Choice Grade (75-85% price):**
```
Criteria:
- Minor cosmetic imperfections
- Small cracks acceptable (at stem end)
- Slight size variation
- Good flavor

Market: Standard restaurants, retail
```

**Seconds (40-60% price):**
```
Criteria:
- Cosmetic issues (cracks, scars, odd shapes)
- Still good flavor
- Food-safe

Market: Sauce/processing, farmers market discount
```

### Post-Harvest Handling

**DO NOT REFRIGERATE:**
```
Critical: Tomatoes are chill-sensitive
- Refrigeration (<55°F) = flavor loss, mealy texture
- Store at 60-70°F
- Counter temperature acceptable

Shelf life:
- Room temp: 5-7 days
- Refrigerated: 7-10 days but poor flavor
```

**Packaging:**
```
Restaurant:
- Pint or quart containers (reusable)
- Single layer (prevent bruising)
- Deliver within 24 hours

Retail:
- Clear clamshells (show fruit)
- Pint size most common
- Attractive presentation
- Varietal label
- Price: $4-8 per pint retail

Farmers Market:
- Pint or quart baskets
- Multiple varieties displayed
- Sampling essential
- Price by volume or weight
```

## Pest and Disease Management

### Major Pests

**Whiteflies:**
```
Damage: Sap feeding, honeydew, virus vectors
Control:
- Yellow sticky traps (monitoring + control)
- Encarsia formosa (parasitic wasp)
- Insecticidal soap
- Neem oil
```

**Aphids:**
```
Control:
- Ladybugs
- Lacewings
- Insecticidal soap
- Monitor new growth
```

### Common Diseases

**Powdery Mildew:**
```
Symptoms: White powder on leaves
Prevention:
- Good air circulation
- Lower humidity (<80%)
- Resistant varieties
Control:
- Sulfur spray
- Milk spray (1:9 dilution)
- Remove affected leaves
```

**Blossom End Rot:**
```
Symptoms: Dark, sunken spot on fruit bottom
Cause: Calcium deficiency or uptake issue
Prevention:
- Consistent watering (no stress)
- Adequate calcium (180-220 ppm)
- Avoid excess nitrogen
- Maintain proper pH (5.8-6.3)
```

**Leaf Mold:**
```
Symptoms: Yellow spots on upper leaf, fuzzy growth below
Prevention:
- Lower humidity
- Increase air circulation
- Remove lower leaves as plant grows
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Specialty cherry tomatoes command 200-400% premiums** over standard varieties through superior flavor and unique colors
2. **High-wire systems maximize profitability** achieving 15-25 lbs per plant annually with proper management
3. **Higher EC (3.5-4.0) produces superior flavor** worth premium pricing despite slightly lower yields
4. **Pollination management increases yields 20-30%** through consistent hand/electric pollination
5. **Never refrigerate tomatoes** - chill damage ruins flavor; store at room temperature
6. **Heirloom varieties command highest prices** but may have lower yields than hybrids
7. **Year-round production requires supplemental lighting** but enables consistent revenue stream
