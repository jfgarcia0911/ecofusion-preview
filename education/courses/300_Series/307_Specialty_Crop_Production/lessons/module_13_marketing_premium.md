## Value-Based Pricing Strategy

### Premium Pricing Formula

```
Base Cost + Quality Premium + Scarcity Premium + Service Premium = Market Price

Example - Specialty Baby Leaf Mix:
Production cost: $6/lb
+ Quality premium (organic, perfect): $4/lb
+ Scarcity premium (limited local availability): $3/lb
+ Service premium (same-day delivery): $3/lb
= Market price: $16/lb wholesale

Compared to commodity greens ($3-4/lb wholesale)
Premium: 300-430%
```

### Pricing Tiers by Market Channel

| Channel | Price Level | Volume | Relationship |
|---------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| **Fine Dining Direct** | Highest (100%) | Low | Essential |
| **Farmers Market** | High (90-95%) | Medium | Direct |
| **Specialty Retail** | Medium-High (65-75%) | Medium-High | Professional |
| **Farm Stands/CSA** | Medium-High (70-85%) | Medium | Community |
| **Distributors** | Medium (50-65%) | High | Transactional |
| **Wholesale Bulk** | Baseline (40-55%) | Very High | Price-sensitive |

## Restaurant Sales Strategy

### Target Customer Profiling

**Tier 1: White-Tablecloth Fine Dining**
```
Characteristics:
- $$$$  price point
- Chef-driven menu
- Seasonal/changing menu
- Farm-to-table philosophy
- Quality paramount over price

Approach:
- Research chef and current menu
- Prepare targeted sample box
- Request brief in-person meeting
- Emphasize quality and story
- Flexible to specific needs

Pricing:
- Premium pricing accepted
- 0-10% discount for volume
- Worth time investment

Product Mix:
- Edible flowers
- Microgreens
- Specialty herbs
- Heirloom varieties
- Perfect quality only
```

**Tier 2: Farm-to-Table Casual**
```
Characteristics:
- $$-$$$ price point
- Casual atmosphere, quality food
- Local sourcing important
- Value-conscious but quality-focused
- Consistent needs

Approach:
- Emphasize local, sustainable
- Consistent availability critical
- Competitive but fair pricing
- Reliable service

Pricing:
- Standard premium pricing
- 10-15% volume discount
- Good margins possible

Product Mix:
- Specialty lettuces
- Fresh herbs
- Asian greens
- Seasonal specialties
- Good quality, some imperfections OK
```

**Tier 3: Pizza/Casual Italian**
```
Characteristics:
- $-$$ price point
- High volume potential
- Consistent product needs
- Price-sensitive

Approach:
- Focus on volume items (basil, tomatoes)
- Competitive pricing essential
- Reliability and consistency
- Professional relationships

Pricing:
- Lower premiums (20-60% over commodity)
- Volume discounts (15-25%)
- Thin margins, volume game

Product Mix:
- Basil (year-round)
- Cherry tomatoes
- Standard herbs
- Some specialty greens
```

### Initial Contact Workflow

**Step 1: Research (Before Contact)**
```
- Review online menu (current)
- Check social media (ingredient photos)
- Read reviews (what are they known for?)
- Identify chef name
- Note cuisine style
- List potential products that fit their needs
```

**Step 2: Prepare Sample Box**
```
Contents:
- 3-5 products matching their cuisine
- Small amounts (2-4 oz each)
- Perfect quality specimens
- Variety information cards
- Price list
- Farm business card
- Your contact info

Packaging:
- Professional appearance
- Each item labeled
- Attractive presentation
- Delivered cold
```

**Step 3: Initial Call/Email**
```
Template:

"Hello [Chef Name],

I'm [Your Name] from [Farm Name], a local specialty crop grower in [Location]. I grow [high-quality/organic/hydroponic] [specialty greens/herbs/etc.] specifically for discerning restaurants like yours.

I've been following [Restaurant Name] and love your approach to [seasonal cuisine/local ingredients/etc.]. I think some of our products would complement your menu beautifully.

Would you be open to receiving a small sample box of [specific items that match their menu]? There's no obligation - I'd just love to introduce you to what we grow.

[If yes, schedule delivery within 2-3 days]

Thank you for your time,
[Your Name]
[Farm Name]
[Phone]
[Email]
```

**Step 4: Sample Delivery**
```
Timing:
- Mid-morning (9-11 AM) or mid-afternoon (2-4 PM)
- Avoid lunch rush
- Call ahead to confirm timing

Delivery:
- Arrive on time
- Professional appearance
- Brief introduction (5 minutes max)
- Leave sample box and materials
- Don't pressure for immediate decision
- "I'll follow up in 2-3 days"
```

**Step 5: Follow-Up**
```
Timing: 2-3 days after sample delivery

Call or email:
"Hi [Chef Name], just following up on the samples I dropped off [day]. I wanted to see if you had a chance to try them and if you have any questions.

[If positive response:]
Great! What products are you interested in? What quantities do you typically need? How often would you like deliveries?

[If neutral/need more time:]
No problem. Can I check back with you next week? Or would you prefer I send you our availability list each week?

[If negative:]
Thank you for trying them. If your needs change or you'd like to try anything else in the future, please keep my contact info.
```

## Building Long-Term Customer Relationships

### Communication Best Practices

**Weekly Availability Lists:**
```
Send every Monday (or consistent day)

Format:
- Clear subject: "[Farm Name] - Week of [Date] Availability"
- List products available
- Quantities available
- Pricing
- Delivery schedule
- Order deadline (e.g., "Order by Wednesday for Friday delivery")
- Contact info for questions

Example:

=================================
[FARM NAME] - WEEKLY AVAILABILITY
Week of May 15-21
=================================

BABY LEAF SALAD MIXES:
□ Spring Medley - 20 lbs available - $16/lb
□ Spicy Asian Mix - 15 lbs available - $18/lb

SPECIALTY HERBS:
□ Thai Basil - 8 lbs available - $24/lb
□ Lemon Basil - 6 lbs available - $24/lb
□ Shiso (Green) - 3 lbs available - $30/lb

EDIBLE FLOWERS:
□ Nasturtiums (mixed) - 12 oz available - $22/oz
□ Violas (mixed) - 8 oz available - $28/oz

DELIVERY: Thursday & Friday this week
ORDER DEADLINE: Wednesday 5 PM
CONTACT: [Phone] or reply to this email

Thank you!
[Your Name], [Farm Name]
=================================
```

**Seasonal Planning Meetings:**
```
Frequency: Quarterly

Purpose:
- Discuss upcoming menu changes
- Preview new varieties you're trialing
- Get advance notice of needs
- Coordinate seasonal transitions
- Build relationship beyond transactions

Benefits:
- Anticipate demand
- Adjust production
- Demonstrate commitment
- Strengthen partnership
```

### Handling Common Situations

**When You're Out of Stock:**
```
Immediate communication:
"Hi [Chef], I wanted to let you know we're currently out of [product] due to [reason]. I expect to have more available [timeframe].

In the meantime, can I suggest [alternative product]? Or would you prefer to wait until [product] is back in stock?"

Never:
- Ghost customers when out of stock
- Over-promise and under-deliver
- Try to substitute without asking

Always:
- Communicate proactively
- Provide realistic timeframes
- Offer alternatives
- Maintain trust
```

**When Quality Isn't Perfect:**
```
Options:
1. Don't deliver sub-par product (best for reputation)
2. Offer at discount with full disclosure

Example:
"Hi [Chef], This week's [product] isn't up to our usual standard due to [weather/pest issue/etc.]. It's still good quality but has [minor cosmetic issues]. I can offer it at 20% off, or I can skip this delivery and have perfect product next week. What would you prefer?"

Key: Transparency builds trust
```

## Farmers Market Strategy

### Market Selection

**Criteria for Choosing Markets:**
```
Demographics:
- High-income area (can afford premium)
- Food-conscious population
- Foot traffic volume

Timing:
- Day/time that fits your schedule
- Doesn't conflict with deliveries
- Allows fresh harvest

Competition:
- How many other greens/herb vendors?
- What do they offer?
- Is there room for premium positioning?

Fees:
- Booth fee reasonable vs. expected sales?
- Annual vs. per-market options

Requirements:
- Insurance requirements
- Licensing
- Organic certification needed?
```

### Display and Presentation

**Visual Appeal Principles:**
```
Height Variation:
- Use crates, stands, risers
- Create visual interest
- Eye-level products get most attention

Color Blocking:
- Group by color
- Create visual impact
- Green/red/purple sections

Abundant Appearance:
- Product looks plentiful (even if limited)
- Continuous restocking
- Never looks picked-over

Cleanliness:
- Professional table covering
- Clean containers
- No wilted/damaged product visible
```

**Signage Best Practices:**
```
Product Signs:
- Large, clear variety names
- Brief description (flavor, use)
- Price (prominent)
- "ORGANIC" or "HYDROPONIC" if applicable

Farm Story:
- Farm name banner
- Location/story
- Growing methods
- Contact info for wholesale inquiries

Educational:
- "How to Store" tips
- Recipe suggestions
- Sampling encouragement
```

### Customer Engagement

**Sampling Strategy:**
```
What to Sample:
- Unique varieties (unfamiliar to customers)
- Flavor highlights (Sungold tomatoes, specialty basil)
- Combat skepticism ("Does it really taste different?")

How:
- Small, prepared samples
- Invite customers to try
- Discuss flavor while they taste
- Connect taste to use suggestions

Impact:
- 30-50% increase in sales of sampled items
- Converts browsers to buyers
- Creates memorable experience
- Word-of-mouth marketing
```

**Recipe Cards:**
```
Design:
- Farm-branded
- Simple 3-5 ingredient recipes
- Quick preparation (15 minutes or less)
- Uses products you're selling
- Include farm contact info

Distribution:
- Free with purchase
- Available at table
- Specific to products purchased

Benefits:
- Helps customers use unfamiliar products
- Reduces barriers to purchase
- Provides value-add
- Encourages return visits
```

### Pricing Strategy

**Premium Positioning:**
```
Principle: Don't compete on price, compete on quality

Approach:
- Price 20-40% above commodity equivalent
- Price on par with or slightly below specialty stores
- Bundle deals for multiple items
- Loyalty programs (punch cards)

Example Pricing:
- Baby leaf mix (4 oz): $6-8
- Specialty herbs (bunch): $4-6
- Cherry tomatoes (pint): $6-10
- Edible flowers (0.25 oz): $8-12

Justification (on signage/conversation):
- Harvested this morning
- Pesticide-free/organic
- Locally grown
- Specialty varieties
- Superior flavor
```

## Retail Store Partnerships

### Approaching Specialty Stores

**Target Stores:**
```
Best fit:
- Independent specialty grocers
- Natural food stores
- Co-ops
- High-end markets
- Farm stands (other farms)

Less ideal:
- Big box grocery (requirements too complex for small scale)
- Discount stores (price-focused)
```

**Meeting with Produce Manager:**
```
Preparation:
- Sample products
- Price sheet
- Delivery schedule
- Liability insurance proof
- Business license
- Any certifications (organic, etc.)

Questions to Ask:
1. What are your current specialty greens/herbs suppliers?
2. What products are customers requesting that you don't have?
3. What delivery schedule works for you?
4. What are your packaging/labeling requirements?
5. What are payment terms?
6. Is there a trial period?

Questions They'll Ask:
1. Can you supply consistently year-round?
2. What's your backup plan if crop fails?
3. What's your liability insurance?
4. Can you provide organic certification?
5. What are your wholesale prices?
6. How do you handle product that doesn't sell?
```

### Retail Success Factors

**Critical Requirements:**
```
Consistency:
- Same day/time delivery every week
- Consistent product quality
- Reliable quantities
- Professional communication

Packaging:
- Consumer-ready
- Professional labeling
- UPC/PLU codes (many stores)
- Proper venting/moisture control

Quality:
- Premium grade only
- Shelf life must meet expectations (minimum 5-7 days)
- No wilted, damaged, or substandard product

Service:
- Prompt responses to issues
- Flexible to reasonable requests
- Product rotation assistance (some stores)
- Signage/POS materials
```

## Online and Direct Sales

### CSA (Community Supported Agriculture)

**Model Overview:**
```
Structure:
- Members pay upfront for season
- Weekly share pickup
- Share contents vary by harvest
- Commitment from both parties

Pricing:
- Full season (20-24 weeks): $400-800
- Half season: $250-450
- Weekly equivalent: $20-40

Products:
- Diversified vegetables/herbs
- Seasonal variation
- Regular staples + specialties
- Sometimes add-ons (flowers, extras)
```

**Pros & Cons:**
```
Advantages:
- Upfront cash flow
- Committed customer base
- Predictable demand
- Community connection
- Direct relationship

Disadvantages:
- Requires product diversity
- Fixed pricing (can't adjust for market)
- Weather/crop failures affect members
- Distribution logistics
- Customer management time

Best for:
- Diversified growers
- Customer relationship focus
- Stable cash flow priority
```

## Branding and Marketing Materials

### Farm Brand Development

**Brand Elements:**
```
Farm Name:
- Memorable
- Reflects values/location
- Easy to spell and say

Logo:
- Simple, recognizable
- Works in color and black/white
- Scalable (business card to banner)

Tagline:
- Optional but helpful
- Communicates what you do
- Examples:
  * "Fresh from our farm to your table"
  * "Specialty greens, grown with care"
  * "Hydroponic herbs and vegetables"

Visual Identity:
- Color scheme (consistent)
- Font choices
- Photo style
```

## Sales Tracking and Customer Management

### Record Keeping

**Customer Database:**
```
Track for each customer:
- Business name and type
- Contact person and title
- Phone, email, address
- Products typically ordered
- Order frequency and quantities
- Pricing/discount level
- Payment terms
- Delivery preferences
- Notes (preferences, issues, opportunities)

Tools:
- Simple spreadsheet (start)
- CRM software (as you grow)
- Order management system (larger operations)
```

**Sales Tracking:**
```
Record each sale:
- Date
- Customer
- Products and quantities
- Pricing
- Payment method and status
- Delivery date

Benefits:
- Track best customers
- Forecast demand
- Identify trends
- Calculate profitability by customer/product
- Tax records
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Premium pricing requires justification** through superior quality, service, or scarcity
2. **Restaurant relationships are built on reliability and communication** - deliver what you promise, when you promise
3. **Farmers markets require engagement** - sampling, education, and relationship building drive sales
4. **Retail partnerships demand consistency** - same quality, timing, and packaging every delivery
5. **Branding differentiates your farm** - professional materials and consistent identity build trust
6. **Multiple sales channels reduce risk** - don't depend on single customer type
7. **Customer retention is more valuable than acquisition** - nurture existing relationships for long-term success
