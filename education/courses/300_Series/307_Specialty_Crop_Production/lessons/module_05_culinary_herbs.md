## Introduction to Culinary Herb Production

Fresh culinary herbs represent one of the most profitable specialty crop categories, with typical wholesale prices of $12-40/lb compared to $2-4/lb for standard greens. Many herbs are perennial or long-season crops, providing continuous harvest from established plants.

### Market Overview

**Price Comparison (Wholesale):**
```
Standard Herbs:
  Basil (Italian): $12-18/lb
  Parsley: $8-14/lb
  Cilantro: $8-14/lb

Specialty Herbs:
  Thai basil: $18-28/lb
  Lemon basil: $18-28/lb
  Purple basil: $20-32/lb
  French tarragon: $24-40/lb
  Specialty mint varieties: $16-28/lb

Premium: 150-400% over standard varieties
```

**Market Advantages:**
- Year-round demand from restaurants
- High price per pound
- Long shelf life (7-14 days for most)
- Continuous harvest (perennials)
- Low shipping weight
- Strong farmers market sales

## Annual Herbs

### Basil (Ocimum basilicum)

**Overview:**
The king of culinary herbs, basil offers numerous specialty varieties commanding premium prices. Fast-growing and productive, but frost-sensitive.

#### Variety Selection

| Variety | Characteristics | Market Price | Best Use |
|---------|----------------|--------------|----------|
| **Genovese** | Classic Italian, large leaves | $12-18/lb | Standard workhorse |
| **Italian Large Leaf** | Very large (3-4"), sweet | $14-20/lb | Restaurant favorite |
| **Thai** | Anise flavor, purple stems | $18-28/lb | Asian cuisine, premium |
| **Lemon** | Citrus aroma, bright flavor | $18-28/lb | Fish, salads, desserts |
| **Purple/Dark Opal** | Deep purple, ornamental | $20-32/lb | Garnish, color accent |
| **Cinnamon** | Spicy, cinnamon notes | $20-28/lb | Specialty cocktails, desserts |
| **Holy Basil (Tulsi)** | Peppery, medicinal | $22-32/lb | Wellness market, tea |
| **Lime** | Strong lime flavor | $18-28/lb | Mexican, Thai cuisine |
| **African Blue** | Perennial, purple flowers | $24-36/lb | Edible flowers, unique |

---

**Growing Parameters:**

```
Temperature: 70-85°F (21-29°C)
  - Warm season crop (critical)
  - Below 50°F: stunted, damaged
  - Optimal: 75-80°F day, 65-70°F night

pH: 5.5-6.5
EC: 1.6-2.4 mS/cm
  - Higher EC = more intense flavor
  - Lower EC = faster growth, milder

Light: HIGH requirement
  - DLI: 15-20 mol/m²/day
  - 14-16 hours photoperiod
  - Full sun equivalent
  - Low light = leggy, poor flavor

Spacing:
  - Intensive: 6" × 6" (4 plants/sq ft)
  - Standard: 8" × 8" (2.25/sq ft)
  - Allows air circulation (critical)
```

---

**Production Strategy:**

**Method 1: Direct Seed to Harvest**
```
TIMELINE:
  - Seed to germination: 5-7 days
  - Germination to transplant: 14-21 days
  - Transplant to first harvest: 21-28 days
  - Total: 40-56 days

HARVEST SCHEDULE:
  - First harvest: Cut 4-6" tips
  - Regrowth: 14-21 days
  - Second harvest: Cut above 2-3 leaf nodes
  - Continue: 3-5 harvests per plant
  - Total production: 12-16 weeks
```

**Method 2: Mother Stock + Cuttings**
```
ESTABLISHMENT (Weeks 1-8):
  - Grow 10-20 mother plants from seed
  - Allow to reach 12-16" height
  - Maintain vegetative (pinch flowers)

PROPAGATION (Ongoing):
  - Take 4-6" tip cuttings weekly
  - Root in water or media (7-14 days)
  - Transplant to production system
  - First harvest: 21-28 days

ADVANTAGES:
  - Continuous supply of new plants
  - Bypass seed stage
  - Clone best-performing plants
  - Faster to production
```

---

**Harvest Protocol:**

```
FIRST HARVEST:
  - Height: 8-12"
  - Cut 4-6" tips
  - Leave 2-3 nodes (4-6 leaves) on plant
  - Encourages branching

SUBSEQUENT HARVESTS:
  - Every 2-3 weeks
  - Cut branches above leaf nodes
  - Maintain plant height 12-18"
  - Always leave growing points

FLOWER MANAGEMENT:
  - Pinch flowers immediately (prevents bitterness)
  - Basil flowers quickly in stress
  - Remove flower buds daily
  - Exception: African Blue (edible flowers)

TIME OF DAY:
  - Early morning (peak oil content)
  - After dew has dried
  - Before heat of day
```

---

**Post-Harvest:**

```
IMMEDIATE:
  - Place stems in cold water
  - Keep in shade
  - Target: Below 45°F within 30 minutes

WASHING:
  - Optional (if clean production)
  - Very gentle if needed
  - Cold water rinse
  - Pat dry carefully

STORAGE:
  - 45-50°F (NOT refrigerator temp)
  - Basil is chill-sensitive
  - Below 40°F = blackening
  - Stand in water OR
  - Humid environment (90-95%)

PACKAGING:
  - Bunches in water tubes
  - Wrapped in damp paper towel
  - Perforated plastic bag
  - Never sealed bags (decay)

SHELF LIFE:
  - Optimal handling: 7-10 days
  - Standard: 5-7 days
  - Poor handling: 2-3 days
```

---

**Market Positioning:**

```
RESTAURANTS (Primary):
  - Italian: Genovese, Italian Large Leaf
  - Asian: Thai, Lemon, Holy
  - Fine dining: Specialty varieties, perfect leaves
  - Pricing: $14-28/lb wholesale

RETAIL:
  - 2-3 oz clamshells
  - Living plants in pots
  - Pricing: $3.99-5.99 per package

FARMERS MARKETS:
  - Fresh-cut bunches (1-2 oz)
  - Multiple varieties
  - Pricing: $3-6 per bunch
  - Sampling important
```

### Cilantro (Coriandrum sativum)

**Market Position:**
Essential herb for Mexican, Asian, and fusion cuisine. Steady demand, moderate pricing, fast turnover.

**Varieties:**

| Variety | Bolting Resistance | Days to Harvest | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Santo** | Excellent | 50-55 | Industry standard, reliable |
| **Slow Bolt** | Very good | 55-60 | Best for warm conditions |
| **Calypso** | Excellent | 50-55 | Large leaves, productive |
| **Leisure** | Very good | 55-60 | Long harvest window |

**Growing Parameters:**
```
Temperature: 60-75°F (cooler than basil)
  - Cool season preference
  - Bolts quickly above 80°F
  - Can tolerate light frost

pH: 6.0-7.0
EC: 1.8-2.5 mS/cm

Light: Moderate
  - DLI: 12-15 mol/m²/day
  - Lower light = slower bolting
  - 12-14 hour photoperiod

Spacing: 4" × 4" (9/sq ft)
  - Dense planting for leaf production
```

**Production Notes:**
```
SUCCESSION PLANTING:
  - Plant every 2-3 weeks
  - Short harvest window (10-14 days)
  - Bolts quickly when mature
  - Continuous planting essential

HARVEST:
  - Cut-and-come-again possible
  - First cut: 6-8" height (30-40 days)
  - Second cut: 14 days later
  - Then replant

BOLTING MANAGEMENT:
  - Cool temperatures
  - Shorter photoperiod helps
  - Harvest before flower stems appear
  - Once bolting starts, quality declines
```

**Pricing:**
```
Wholesale: $8-14/lb
Retail (bunched): $2-4/bunch (1-2 oz)
Farmers market: $3-5/bunch
```

### Parsley (Petroselinum crispum)

**Types:**

| Type | Characteristics | Market | Price |
|------|----------------|--------|-------|
| **Italian (Flat-leaf)** | Strong flavor, culinary preferred | Restaurant, retail | $10-16/lb |
| **Curly** | Mild, garnish traditional | Garnish market | $8-14/lb |
| **Hamburg** (root) | Grown for root, niche | Specialty | $6-10/lb |

**Growing Parameters:**
```
Temperature: 60-75°F
  - Biennial (grown as annual)
  - Cold tolerant
  - Good winter crop

pH: 6.0-7.0
EC: 1.8-2.5 mS/cm

Germination: SLOW (14-21 days)
  - Soak seeds 24 hours before planting
  - Or use transplants

Days to Harvest: 60-75 days from seed
  - 35-40 days from transplant

Spacing: 6" × 6" (4/sq ft)
```

**Production Strategy:**
```
LONG-SEASON CROP:
  - Slower than basil or cilantro
  - But continuous harvest once established
  - Can produce for 6-9 months
  - Multiple cuttings per plant

HARVEST:
  - Cut outer stems
  - Leave center growing point
  - Harvest every 2-3 weeks
  - 4-6 harvests per plant typical
```

## Perennial Herbs

### Mint (Mentha spp.)

**Overview:**
Perennial herb family with numerous specialty varieties. Once established, provides continuous harvest for years.

**Specialty Varieties:**

| Variety | Flavor Profile | Market | Price Premium |
|---------|----------------|--------|---------------|
| **Spearmint** | Classic mint | Standard | Baseline ($12-16/lb) |
| **Peppermint** | Strong, menthol | Cocktails, tea | 1.2x ($14-20/lb) |
| **Chocolate Mint** | Chocolate notes | Desserts, specialty | 1.5-2x ($18-28/lb) |
| **Orange/Citrus Mint** | Citrus aroma | Cocktails, fish | 1.5-2x ($18-28/lb) |
| **Mojito Mint** | Mild, sweet | Cocktail programs | 1.5-2x ($18-26/lb) |
| **Pineapple Mint** | Variegated, fruity | Garnish, premium | 2x ($24-32/lb) |
| **Apple Mint** | Fruity, mild | Tea, salads | 1.3-1.5x ($16-22/lb) |

---

**Growing Parameters:**
```
Temperature: 65-75°F optimal
  - Tolerates 55-85°F
  - Perennial (cold hardy to Zone 3-4)

pH: 6.0-7.0
EC: 1.6-2.2 mS/cm

Light: Moderate
  - DLI: 12-16 mol/m²/day
  - Tolerates partial shade
  - Full sun = more intense flavor

Spacing: 8" × 8" to 12" × 12"
  - Spreads vigorously
  - Needs containment (runners)
  - 1-2 plants per sq ft
```

**Propagation:**
```
CUTTINGS (Preferred):
  - Take 4-6" tip cuttings
  - Root in water or media (7-10 days)
  - Transplant to system
  - First harvest: 21-28 days
  - Advantage: Clone exact variety

DIVISION:
  - Divide established plants
  - Every 3-4 months to maintain vigor
  - Replant divisions
  - Good for stock renewal

FROM SEED:
  - Not recommended (variable)
  - Varieties don't come true from seed
  - Exception: Some species mints
```

**Long-Term Management:**
```
ESTABLISHMENT (Months 1-2):
  - Plant from cuttings
  - Allow to reach 8-12" height
  - Pinch tips to encourage branching
  - No harvest (build plant)

PRODUCTION (Months 3-24+):
  - Harvest every 2-3 weeks
  - Cut stems to 2-3" height
  - Continuous regrowth
  - Very productive

RENEWAL:
  - Every 6-12 months:
    * Take new cuttings from best plants
    * Discard old plants
    * Replant with vigorous stock
  - Maintains productivity and quality
```

**Market Applications:**
```
RESTAURANT:
  - Cocktail programs (Mojito mint, Pineapple)
  - Desserts (Chocolate, Orange)
  - Lamb dishes (Spearmint)
  - Fish (Citrus mints)
  - Pricing: $16-32/lb wholesale

RETAIL:
  - Fresh-cut bunches
  - Living plants (4" pots)
  - Specialty varieties preferred
  - Pricing: $3-6/bunch, $5-8/plant

TEA MARKET:
  - Fresh or dried
  - Organic premium
  - Bulk packs
  - Pricing: $18-30/lb fresh, $40-80/lb dried
```

### Thyme (Thymus vulgaris)

**Specialty Varieties:**

| Variety | Characteristics | Market | Price |
|---------|----------------|--------|-------|
| **Common/English** | Standard culinary | General | $14-20/lb |
| **Lemon** | Citrus aroma | Premium, fish/poultry | $18-28/lb |
| **Orange** | Orange scent | Specialty cocktails | $20-30/lb |
| **Creeping** | Low-growing, mild | Garnish, ground cover | $16-24/lb |
| **Caraway** | Caraway flavor | Specialty | $18-26/lb |

**Growing Parameters:**
```
Temperature: 60-75°F
  - Mediterranean herb
  - Heat tolerant
  - Cold hardy (Zone 5-9)

pH: 6.0-7.5
EC: 1.4-2.0 mS/cm
  - Lower than most herbs
  - Don't overwater (prefers drier)

Light: High
  - DLI: 14-18 mol/m²/day
  - Full sun preference
  - High light = intense flavor

Spacing: 8" × 8" (2.25/sq ft)
  - Slow initial growth
  - Fills in over time
```

**Production Timeline:**
```
ESTABLISHMENT (3-4 months):
  - Slow initial growth
  - Build root system
  - Minimal harvest

PRODUCTION (Months 4-24+):
  - Harvest stems 4-6" long
  - Cut above woody growth
  - Regrowth in 3-4 weeks
  - Continuous production

REJUVENATION:
  - Prune hard annually (cut back 1/3)
  - Maintains compact growth
  - Prevents woodiness
  - Encourages tender new growth
```

### Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)

**Varieties:**

| Variety | Growth Habit | Characteristics | Market |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Tuscan Blue** | Upright (2-4 ft) | Large leaves, strong | Standard culinary |
| **Arp** | Upright, cold hardy | Zone 6 hardy | Cold climate |
| **BBQ** | Upright, thick stems | Grilling skewers | Specialty |
| **Trailing** | Cascading | Ornamental, mild | Retail plant sales |

**Growing Parameters:**
```
Temperature: 65-75°F
  - Mediterranean herb
  - Heat tolerant
  - Cold sensitive (Zone 8-10 typically)

pH: 6.0-7.0
EC: 1.4-2.0 mS/cm
  - Prefers drier conditions
  - Don't overwater

Light: HIGH
  - DLI: 16-20 mol/m²/day
  - Maximum light for best growth
  - Essential oil production

Spacing: 12" × 12" or larger
  - Large, woody plant
  - Can grow 2-4 ft tall
  - Long-term crop (2-3 years)
```

**Production Considerations:**
```
CHALLENGES:
  - Very slow growth from seed (not recommended)
  - Start from cuttings (3-4 month rooting)
  - Long establishment period (6-9 months)
  - Requires significant space

ADVANTAGES:
  - Once established, very productive
  - High market value
  - Multiple harvests per year
  - 2-3 year production from single plant
  - Good dried herb market

ECONOMICS:
  - Space-intensive (lower plant density)
  - Long ROI timeline
  - Better for larger operations
  - Consider opportunity cost vs. faster herbs
```

**Pricing:**
```
Fresh wholesale: $16-24/lb
Fresh retail: $4-6 per bunch (1-2 oz)
Dried: $50-80/lb
Living plants (6" pot): $8-15
```

**Overview:**
Premium herb for French cuisine, command highest prices among common herbs.

```
CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Anise/licorice flavor
  - Essential in French cooking
  - Cannot grow from seed (only cuttings)

MARKET VALUE:
  - $24-40/lb wholesale
  - $6-10 per bunch (1 oz) retail
  - Limited commercial availability
  - High demand from fine dining

GROWING:
  Temperature: 60-75°F
  pH: 6.0-7.0
  EC: 1.6-2.2 mS/cm
  Light: Moderate (DLI 12-16)
  Spacing: 12" × 12"

PRODUCTION:
  - Propagate from cuttings only
  - Establishment: 3-4 months
  - Production: 12-18 months
  - Harvest: Cut stems 4-6" long
  - Regrowth: 3-4 weeks

CAUTION:
  - Russian tarragon (from seed) is inferior
  - No culinary value
  - Always verify variety as French
```

## Economics and Profitability

### Production Scenarios

#### Small-Scale (100 sq ft) - Mixed Herbs

```
PLANT ALLOCATION:
  40 sq ft - Basil (160 plants, mixed varieties)
  25 sq ft - Mint (25 plants, specialty varieties)
  15 sq ft - Cilantro (135 plants, succession)
  10 sq ft - Parsley (40 plants)
  10 sq ft - Specialty (Tarragon, Thyme, etc.)

ANNUAL PRODUCTION:
  Basil: 320 lbs (2 harvests/plant, 1 lb each)
  Mint: 100 lbs (continuous, 4 lbs/plant/year)
  Cilantro: 135 lbs (3 cycles, 1 lb per cycle)
  Parsley: 80 lbs (4 harvests/plant, 0.5 lb each)
  Specialty: 40 lbs (mixed)
  Total: 675 lbs

REVENUE:
  Basil: 320 lbs × $16/lb = $5,120
  Mint: 100 lbs × $20/lb = $2,000
  Cilantro: 135 lbs × $10/lb = $1,350
  Parsley: 80 lbs × $12/lb = $960
  Specialty: 40 lbs × $28/lb = $1,120
  Gross Annual Revenue: $10,550

COSTS:
  Seeds/cuttings: $250
  Nutrients: $300
  Electricity: $400
  Packaging: $300
  Labor (8 hrs/wk × $20 × 52): $8,320
  Miscellaneous: $200
  Total Costs: $9,770

  Net Profit: $780
  Profit per sq ft: $7.80/year
```

**Analysis:** Small-scale herb production for direct sales (restaurants, farmers markets) has tight margins due to high labor. Best as part of diversified operation.

---

#### Medium-Scale (500 sq ft) - Basil Focus

```
PLANT ALLOCATION:
  400 sq ft - Basil (multiple varieties)
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Culinary herbs offer exceptional pricing** with wholesale prices of $12-40/lb compared to $2-4/lb for standard greens.

2. **Specialty varieties command premiums** - Thai basil, lemon basil, chocolate mint, and French tarragon can sell for 150-400% above standard varieties.

3. **Perennial herbs provide long-term value** - mint, thyme, oregano, and rosemary produce continuously for 1-3 years from single planting.

4. **Propagation strategy impacts profitability** - mother stock systems for basil and cloning for perennials reduce costs and speed production.

5. **Post-harvest handling is critical** - basil requires warmer storage (45-50°F) while most herbs store at 34-38°F for maximum shelf life.

6. **Market diversification reduces risk** - combining restaurant wholesale, retail packages, and farmers market sales provides stability.

7. **Value-added products increase margins** - herb blends, living plants, and dried herbs can double or triple profitability per pound.
