## Pricing Strategy Formulas

### Cost-Plus Pricing

```
Production Cost per lb: $___
× Markup Factor: 2.5-4.0 (depending on market)
= Wholesale Price: $___

Example:
Baby leaf cost: $6/lb
× 2.5 markup
= $15/lb wholesale price
```

### Market-Based Pricing

```
Research competitor pricing:
Competitor A: $___
Competitor B: $___
Market average: $___

Your quality level:
□ Below average: -10-20%
□ Average: Market price
□ Above average: +10-30%
□ Premium: +30-60%

Your price: $___
```

### Value-Based Pricing

```
Customer value perception:
Standard product value: $___
+ Quality premium: $___
+ Uniqueness premium: $___
+ Service premium: $___
+ Story/local premium: $___
= Maximum price customer will pay: $___

Set price at 80-90% of maximum for value perception
```

## Price Adjustment Factors

### Increase Price For:
- Organic certification (+$2-4/lb)
- Same-day harvest/delivery (+$1-3/lb)
- Perfect premium grade (+20-40%)
- Rare/unique varieties (+30-100%)
- Small/custom orders (+10-20%)
- Weekend delivery (+$1-2/lb)

### Decrease Price For:
- Volume commitments (-10-25%)
- Choice grade vs. premium (-15-30%)
- Long payment terms not accepted (Cash = discount)
- Regular standing orders (-5-15%)
- Self-pickup by customer (-10-15%)

---

## Seasonal Pricing Strategy

### High Demand Seasons (prices ↑ 10-30%)
- Spring (April-May): Fresh greens peak demand
- Fall (Sept-Nov): Cool-season crops, return to farmers markets
- Holiday periods: Premium for special events

### Lower Demand (prices ↓ or stable)
- Mid-summer heat: Greens less popular, tomatoes peak
- Post-holiday (Jan-Feb): Lower restaurant traffic
- Weather extremes: Adjust for market conditions

## Profitability Quick Check

```
Minimum Viable Pricing:

Production cost per lb: $_____
+ Packaging & delivery: $_____
+ Labor allocation: $_____
= Total cost: $_____

× 1.4 (minimum 40% margin) = Break-even price
× 2.0 (100% markup) = Target wholesale price
× 2.5-3.0 = Sustainable business price

If market won't support 2.5x cost, reconsider crop viability.
```

## Pro Tips

1. **Start high, adjust down easier than up** - It's hard to raise prices on existing customers
2. **Test pricing at farmers markets** - Direct feedback on what customers will pay
3. **Quality justifies premium** - But only if quality is actually superior
4. **Document your costs accurately** - Know your true cost per pound
5. **Review pricing quarterly** - Adjust for costs, market, and competition
6. **Different prices for different channels** - Wholesale ≠ retail ≠ farmers market
7. **Bundle pricing** - Multi-product orders can have better margins
8. **Be transparent with good customers** - Build trust through honesty about costs and value
