# Climate Adaptation Glossary

**Course 312: Climate Change Adaptation**

---

## A

**Adaptation:** Actions taken to adjust to actual or expected climate change and its effects, reducing vulnerability and building resilience.

**Adaptive Capacity:** The ability of a system to adjust to climate change, moderate potential damages, take advantage of opportunities, or cope with consequences.

**Anthropogenic:** Caused or produced by humans (e.g., anthropogenic climate change from greenhouse gas emissions).

**Aquifer:** Underground layer of permeable rock, sediment, or soil that holds groundwater. Many aquifers are being depleted faster than they can recharge.

---

## B

**Baseline:** Reference point for measuring changes. In climate context, often the pre-industrial period (1850-1900) or recent historical average (1981-2010).

**Biochar:** Charcoal produced from plant matter and stored in soil. Sequesters carbon for centuries while improving soil properties.

**Business Continuity:** Planning and preparation to ensure an organization can continue operating during and after a disruption (e.g., extreme weather event).

---

## C

**Carbon Footprint:** Total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an individual, organization, event, or product, expressed in CO2 equivalents (CO2e).

**Carbon Sequestration:** Long-term storage of carbon dioxide or other forms of carbon to mitigate climate change.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture):** Food production in structures like greenhouses or indoor farms where environmental factors are controlled.

**Climate:** Long-term average weather patterns in a region (typically 30+ years of data), including temperature, precipitation, wind, etc.

**Climate Change:** Long-term shift in global or regional climate patterns, particularly increased average temperatures due to human activities.

**Climate Hub (USDA):** Regional centers providing climate science and adaptation resources to farmers, ranchers, and land managers.

**CO2e (Carbon Dioxide Equivalent):** Universal unit for measuring greenhouse gas emissions, converting all GHGs to equivalent amount of CO2 based on global warming potential.

---

## D

**Desertification:** Land degradation in arid/semi-arid areas, often accelerated by climate change and poor land management.

**Drought:** Extended period with below-average precipitation, causing water scarcity.

**Downscaling:** Method of translating global climate projections to regional or local scale for planning purposes.

---

## E

**Emission:** Release of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. Sources include fossil fuel combustion, agriculture, deforestation.

**EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program):** USDA program providing financial assistance for conservation practices, including water and energy efficiency.

**Evapotranspiration:** Combined water loss from evaporation (surfaces) and transpiration (plants). Increases with temperature.

**Extreme Weather Event:** Unusual, severe, or unseasonal weather (heat waves, heavy rainfall, hurricanes, etc.). Climate change is increasing frequency and intensity.

---

## F

**Feedback Loop:** Process where initial change triggers effects that either amplify (positive feedback) or dampen (negative feedback) the original change.
- **Positive Example:** Ice melts → less reflection → more heat absorbed → more ice melts
- **Negative Example:** More CO2 → more plant growth → more CO2 absorbed

**Flood Zone:** Area designated by FEMA based on flood risk. Zones A/AE/V have 1% annual flood probability ("100-year flood").

**Food Security:** Condition where all people have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food. Four pillars: availability, access, utilization, stability.

---

## G

**GHG (Greenhouse Gas):** Gases that trap heat in the atmosphere (CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, fluorinated gases, water vapor).

**Global Warming Potential (GWP):** Measure of how much a greenhouse gas warms the atmosphere over a time period (usually 100 years) compared to CO2.
- CO2 = 1 (baseline)
- CH4 = 28-36
- N2O = 298

**Grid:** Electrical power distribution network. "On-grid" = connected to utility; "off-grid" = independent power.

---

## H

**Heat Island Effect:** Urban areas significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas due to human activities and built environment.

**Heat Wave:** Period of excessively hot weather, typically 3+ days above local threshold temperature.

**Hurricane:** Tropical cyclone with sustained winds ≥74 mph. Categories 1-5 based on wind speed (Saffir-Simpson scale).

**Humidity:** Amount of water vapor in air. Affects plant transpiration, disease pressure, human comfort, and evaporative cooling effectiveness.

---

## I

**IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change):** UN body assessing scientific information on climate change. Produces comprehensive assessment reports every 6-7 years.

**ITC (Investment Tax Credit):** Federal tax credit for renewable energy investments (currently 30% for solar).

---

## L

**Lifecycle Assessment (LCA):** Method for assessing environmental impacts of a product or service throughout its lifecycle (production, use, disposal).

---

## M

**Methane (CH4):** Greenhouse gas with 28-36x warming potential of CO2 over 100 years. Agricultural sources include livestock, manure, rice paddies.

**Mitigation (Climate):** Actions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions or enhance carbon sinks, addressing root causes of climate change.

**Modular Design:** System designed in independent modules that can operate separately, enhancing resilience and flexibility.

---

## N

**Net Zero:** Achieving balance between greenhouse gas emissions produced and removed from atmosphere. CEA operations can achieve this with renewable energy and carbon sequestration.

**Nitrous Oxide (N2O):** Powerful greenhouse gas (298x CO2) from fertilizer application and soil processes. CEA dramatically reduces N2O emissions through precision nutrient delivery.

**NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration):** U.S. agency providing weather forecasts, climate data, and ocean information.

---

## P

**Paris Agreement:** 2015 international climate treaty with goal to limit global warming to well below 2°C, preferably 1.5°C, compared to pre-industrial levels.

**Permafrost:** Ground that remains frozen for 2+ consecutive years. Thawing permafrost releases CO2 and methane, creating positive feedback loop.

**Photovoltaic (PV):** Technology that converts sunlight directly into electricity (solar panels).

**Pollinator:** Animal (bee, butterfly, bird, bat) that transfers pollen between flowers, enabling reproduction. Climate change threatens pollinators through habitat loss, temperature stress, and phenological mismatch.

**ppm (Parts Per Million):** Unit for measuring atmospheric gas concentration. Current CO2: ~420 ppm (vs. pre-industrial 280 ppm).

**Precipitation:** Water falling from sky (rain, snow, sleet, hail). Climate change affects amount, intensity, and patterns.

---

## R

**REAP (Rural Energy for America Program):** USDA program providing grants and loan guarantees for renewable energy and energy efficiency projects in rural areas.

**Recirculation:** Recovering and reusing water (or nutrients) in a closed-loop system. CEA achieves 90-95%+ recirculation, dramatically reducing water use.

**Redundancy:** Having backup systems or capacity to maintain function if primary system fails. Critical for resilience (e.g., backup generator).

**Renewable Energy:** Energy from naturally replenishing sources (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass) that don't deplete resources or emit greenhouse gases during operation.

**Resilience:** Capacity to absorb disturbance, reorganize, and retain essential function. Resilient systems bounce back quickly from shocks.

**RPS (Renewable Portfolio Standard):** State policies requiring utilities to source a percentage of electricity from renewable sources.

---

## S

**Scenario:** Plausible description of how the future may develop based on assumptions about key driving forces (e.g., emission scenarios SSP1-1.9 through SSP5-8.5).

**Sensitivity:** Degree to which a system is affected by climate change. Cool-season crops have high sensitivity to heat; heat-tolerant crops have low sensitivity.

**Sequestration (Carbon):** Long-term removal of CO2 from atmosphere and storage in sinks (forests, soil, biochar, geological formations).

**SSP (Shared Socioeconomic Pathway):** Scenarios used by IPCC describing alternative futures of societal development and emissions.
- SSP1-1.9: Very low emissions (rapid action)
- SSP5-8.5: Very high emissions (no action)

---

## T

**Thermal Mass:** Materials that absorb and store heat energy (water, concrete, brick). Used in greenhouses to moderate temperature swings.

**Tipping Point:** Threshold beyond which a climate system reorganizes, often irreversibly, into a different state (e.g., ice sheet collapse, Amazon dieback).

**Transpiration:** Water movement through plant from roots to leaves where it evaporates. Increases with temperature and decreases with humidity.

---

## U

**UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply):** Battery backup system providing emergency power when grid fails. Critical for aquaponics (fish aeration) and monitoring systems.

**USDA (United States Department of Agriculture):** Federal agency supporting farmers through programs including REAP, EQIP, Climate Hubs, and research.

---

## V

**Vulnerability:** Propensity to be adversely affected by climate change. Function of exposure, sensitivity, and (lack of) adaptive capacity.
- **Formula:** Vulnerability = (Exposure × Sensitivity) - Adaptive Capacity

---

## W

**Water Stress:** Condition where water demand exceeds available supply or quality constraints limit use. Climate change is increasing water stress globally.

**Weather:** Short-term atmospheric conditions (hours to weeks) in a specific place and time. Distinct from climate (long-term patterns).

**Wildfire:** Uncontrolled fire in wildland vegetation. Climate change increases frequency and intensity through hotter, drier conditions.

**Wind Load:** Force exerted by wind on structures. Greenhouses must be designed for local wind loads (increases in hurricane/tornado zones).

## Units & Conversions

**Temperature:**
- Fahrenheit to Celsius: (°F - 32) × 5/9
- Celsius to Fahrenheit: (°C × 9/5) + 32

**Energy:**
- 1 kWh = 3,412 BTU
- 1 ton of cooling = 12,000 BTU/hour

**Water:**
- 1 cubic foot = 7.48 gallons
- 1 gallon = 8.34 pounds (water weight)
- 1 acre-inch = 27,154 gallons

**Greenhouse Gases:**
- 1 ton = 2,000 pounds
- CO2e = emissions × GWP (e.g., 1 ton CH4 = 28-36 tons CO2e)
