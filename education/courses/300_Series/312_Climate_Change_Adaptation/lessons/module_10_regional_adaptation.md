## 10.2 Southwest Adaptation Strategies

### Climate Challenges

**Current Conditions:**
- Annual precipitation: 5-15 inches
- Summer temps: Routinely >100°F (Phoenix: 110-120°F)
- Evaporation exceeds precipitation
- Colorado River and aquifer depletion
- Megadrought (2000-present)

**Projected Changes (2050):**
- Temperature: +3-6°F
- Precipitation: -10 to -30%
- Days >100°F: +50-100%
- Water availability: -20 to -40%

### Adaptation Priorities

**1. Water Management (CRITICAL)**

| Strategy | Implementation | Priority |
|----------|---------------|----------|
| **Maximum recirculation** | 95%+ closed-loop systems | ESSENTIAL |
| **Rainwater harvest** | Capture all rainfall (though limited) | HIGH |
| **Condensate recovery** | HVAC dehumidification capture | HIGH |
| **Well as backup** | Deep wells, test quality | MEDIUM |
| **Greywater** | Non-potable uses | MEDIUM |

**Example System:** 10,000 sq ft greenhouse
- Daily water use: 300 gallons (95% recirculation)
- Rainwater capture: 12,000 gal/year (12" rainfall × 10K sq ft × 80%)
- Condensate: 6,000 gal/year
- Municipal makeup: 90,000 gal/year
- **Total: 108,000 gal/year (vs. field: 2+ million gallons)**

**2. Cooling Management (CRITICAL)**

```
SOUTHWEST COOLING SYSTEM DESIGN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TIER 1: Passive (Essential)
├─► Shade cloth: 50-70% (retractable for winter)
├─► Reflective coating: Reduce solar load 30-50%
├─► Insulated glazing: Double-wall poly or polycarbonate
├─► Proper orientation: Minimize east-west exposure
└─► Thermal mass: Cool overnight, buffer daytime heat

TIER 2: Evaporative Cooling (Highly Effective in Arid Climate)
├─► Evaporative pads: 20-30°F cooling
├─► Exhaust fans: High air exchange rate (60+ per hour)
├─► Water cost: ~$500-1,500/year
└─► ROI: Excellent (low cost, high effectiveness)

TIER 3: Active Cooling (For High-Value Crops or Extreme Heat)
├─► Supplemental AC: For peak heat periods
├─► Ground-source cooling: High upfront, low operating
├─► Thermal storage: Ice at night, use during day
└─► Cost: $2-5/sq ft/year operating

SEASONAL STRATEGY:
• Winter (Nov-Mar): Minimal cooling, some heating
• Spring/Fall: Evaporative cooling sufficient
• Summer (Jun-Sep): Full evaporative + possible AC
• Consider reduced production in extreme summer heat
```

**3. Solar Energy (EXCELLENT OPPORTUNITY)**

- Solar resource: 6-7 kWh/m²/day (best in U.S.)
- Can meet 100%+ of greenhouse electrical needs
- Federal + state incentives: 40-60% cost reduction
- Payback: 4-7 years
- **PRIORITY: Plan solar from the beginning**

**4. Crop Selection**

| Season | Recommended Crops | Strategy |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| **Winter (Nov-Mar)** | All leafy greens, herbs, tomatoes, peppers | PEAK SEASON - outdoor too cool |
| **Spring/Fall** | Lettuce, kale, basil, tomatoes | GOOD SEASON - moderate temps |
| **Summer (Jun-Sep)** | Basil, heat-tolerant tomatoes/peppers, reduce greens | CHALLENGING - consider partial shutdown |

## 10.3 Southeast Adaptation Strategies

### Climate Challenges

**Current Conditions:**
- Hot, humid summers (80-95°F + 70-90% humidity)
- Hurricane risk (coastal areas)
- Heavy rainfall events (tropical systems)
- Mild winters (coastal: no freeze; inland: occasional frost)

**Projected Changes (2050):**
- Temperature: +2-4°F
- Precipitation: +5-15% (more intense events)
- Hurricane intensity: Increase
- Sea level rise: 1-3 feet (coastal flooding)

### Adaptation Priorities

**1. Humidity and Disease Management**

```
SOUTHEAST HUMIDITY CONTROL STRATEGIES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CHALLENGE: High humidity → Fungal/bacterial diseases

SOLUTIONS:

Air Circulation:
├─► Fans: Continuous air movement (prevent stagnant air)
├─► Spacing: Wider plant spacing (better airflow)
└─► Cost: Low, essential

Dehumidification:
├─► Dehumidifiers: For closed environments
├─► Condensate capture: Bonus water supply
├─► Cost: $1-3/sq ft/year operating
└─► ROI: Disease prevention + water capture

Ventilation:
├─► High air exchange rate (60+ per hour)
├─► Automated vents: Humidity-triggered
└─► Trade-off: Cooling costs (letting in hot, humid air)

Crop Management:
├─► Resistant varieties
├─► Preventive biocontrols
├─► Spacing and pruning
└─► Early detection and removal

BEST PRACTICE: Combination approach
• Ventilation + fans (air movement)
• Dehumidify when AC is running (summer)
• Crop selection and management
• Monitoring (humidity sensors with alerts)
```

**2. Hurricane Preparation (Coastal)**

| Risk Level | Design Standard | Cost Premium | Recommended Action |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Low** (inland) | Standard code | Baseline | Basic prep plan |
| **Moderate** (50-100 mi coast) | Enhanced | +15-25% | Reinforced structure, prep plan |
| **High** (coastal) | Hurricane-rated | +30-50% | Engineered structure, full protocol |
| **Very High** (<10 mi coast, <10 ft elevation) | Maximum | +50%+ | Consider if viable location |

**3. Extended Growing Season Opportunity**

- Frost-free period: 250-365 days
- Winter production highly viable
- **Market advantage:** Produce fresh greens when northern states cannot
- **Strategy:** Year-round production with seasonal crop rotation

## 10.4 Midwest/Great Plains Adaptation

### Climate Challenges

**Current Conditions:**
- Temperature extremes: -20°F winter to 100°F summer
- Variable precipitation: Drought-flood cycles
- Severe storms: Tornadoes, hail, high winds
- Spring flooding increasing
- Aquifer depletion (High Plains Aquifer)

**Projected Changes (2050):**
- Winter temps: +4-6°F (fewer extreme cold snaps)
- Summer temps: +3-5°F (more heat waves)
- Precipitation: +5-15% annual (but more variable)
- Spring flooding: Increase
- Extreme weather: More frequent/intense

### Adaptation Priorities

**1. Structural Resilience (Severe Weather)**

```
MIDWEST STRUCTURAL DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Wind Loads:
├─► Design for 90-110 mph winds (higher in tornado alleys)
├─► Reinforced connections, anchor bolts
├─► Impact-resistant glazing (hail)
└─► Premium: +10-20% over baseline

Snow Loads:
├─► Design for 30-60 lb/sq ft (varies by state)
├─► Pitched roof to shed snow
├─► Heating to prevent buildup
└─► Premium: +5-15%

Foundation:
├─► Below frost line (3-4 feet deep)
├─► Reinforced concrete
├─► Proper drainage (spring flooding)
└─► Premium: +10-20%

TOTAL STRUCTURAL PREMIUM: +25-50% vs. mild climate
ROI: Avoids catastrophic loss, insurance savings
```

**2. Energy Efficiency (Temperature Extremes)**

| System | Winter Challenge | Solution | Summer Challenge | Solution |
|--------|-----------------|----------|------------------|----------|
| **Insulation** | Heat loss | Double-wall poly, thermal curtains | Heat gain | Shade cloth, reflective coatings |
| **Heating** | -20°F nights | Efficient heat pumps + backup | N/A | N/A |
| **Cooling** | N/A | N/A | 95-105°F days | Evaporative cooling, ventilation |
| **Backup power** | Ice storms | Generator essential | Severe storms | Generator essential |

**3. Water Conservation (Aquifer Depletion)**

- High Plains Aquifer declining 1-3 feet per year (some areas)
- CEA offers 90-95% water savings vs. field irrigation
- **Regional significance:** CEA can reduce agricultural water demand dramatically
- **Positioning:** Aquifer conservation + local food production

## 10.5 Northeast Adaptation

### Climate Challenges

**Current Conditions:**
- Short growing season: 120-180 days
- Cold winters: Below freezing Nov-Mar
- Variable weather: Unpredictable springs/falls
- Coastal flooding risk (sea level rise + nor'easters)

**Projected Changes (2050):**
- Winter temps: +3-5°F (but still freezing)
- Precipitation: +5-10% (more as rain vs. snow)
- Growing season: +2-3 weeks
- Coastal flooding: Increase
- Extreme rain events: +50-100%

### Adaptation Priorities

**1. Season Extension (MAJOR OPPORTUNITY)**

```
NORTHEAST SEASON EXTENSION VALUE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

FIELD AGRICULTURE:
├─► Growing season: May-September (5 months)
├─► Winter: Zero production
└─► Market: Seasonal only, imported produce

PASSIVE GREENHOUSE:
├─► Growing season: March-November (8-9 months)
├─► Winter: Minimal production (cold-hardy crops only)
└─► Market: Extended season, winter imports

HEATED GREENHOUSE:
├─► Growing season: Year-round (12 months)
├─► Winter: Full production (heating costs moderate)
└─► Market: Year-round local, premium winter prices

BUSINESS CASE:
• Winter produce commands 2-3x price premium
• Customer demand: Strong local food movement
• Competition: Limited winter production
• ROI: Heating costs offset by premium prices
```

**2. Energy Efficiency (Heating Costs)**

| Strategy | Heating Reduction | Cost | Payback |
|----------|------------------|------|---------|
| **Thermal curtains** | 30-60% | Medium | 2-5 years |
| **Double-wall glazing** | 40-50% | High (new builds) | 5-10 years |
| **Insulated north wall** | 10-20% | Medium | 3-7 years |
| **Efficient heating** (heat pumps) | 40-60% vs. resistance | High | 5-10 years |
| **LED lighting** (reduce heat need) | 20-30% | Medium | 2-4 years |

**3. Strong Local Food Movement**

- Northeast leads U.S. in local food demand
- Farm-to-table restaurant culture
- CSA memberships high
- Institutional procurement mandates (local)
- **Opportunity:** Premium pricing, reliable markets

## 10.6 Northwest Adaptation

### Climate Challenges

**Current Conditions:**
- Mild, wet winters (40-50°F, rainy)
- Dry summers (increasing drought)
- Low summer sun (clouds, marine layer)
- Wildfire risk increasing
- Snowpack declining (water supply)

**Projected Changes (2050):**
- Winter: +2-4°F, similar or more precip
- Summer: +3-6°F, -20-40% precipitation
- Drought intensity/duration: Increase
- Wildfire season: Longer, more intense
- Snowpack: -30-50% (water shortages)

### Adaptation Priorities

**1. Summer Water Management**

- Rainwater harvest: EXCELLENT (40-80" annual rainfall)
- Capture winter rain → use in dry summer
- Storage sizing: 60-120 days summer use
- **Can meet 100%+ of needs with proper storage**

**Example:** 10,000 sq ft greenhouse, Seattle area (37" rainfall)
- Annual capture: 10,000 sq ft × (37"/12) × 7.48 gal/cu ft × 80% = 184,000 gallons
- Annual use: 110,000 gallons
- **Result: Surplus, fully rain-fed possible**

**2. Natural Light Greenhouse Optimization**

```
NORTHWEST GREENHOUSE DESIGN FOR LOW LIGHT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CHALLENGE: Cloudy, low winter sun

STRATEGIES:

Maximize Light Transmission:
├─► Glass or clear poly (not diffused)
├─► Minimal structure (thin framing)
├─► Regular cleaning (remove algae, dirt)
├─► Proper orientation (south-facing)
└─► Light-reflective interior surfaces

Supplemental Lighting (Winter):
├─► LED fixtures for photoperiod extension
├─► 4-8 hours/day (early morning, evening)
├─► Cost: $0.50-1.50/sq ft/month
└─► Necessary for quality winter greens

Crop Selection:
├─► Shade-tolerant crops (lettuce, spinach, kale)
├─► Avoid high-light crops in winter (tomatoes)
├─► Microgreens (short cycle, lower light needs)
└─► Mushrooms (no light needed!)

RESULT: Year-round production with moderate lighting cost
```

**3. Wildfire Preparedness**

- Defensible space (see Module 8)
- Ember-resistant vents
- Irrigation as fire suppression
- Evacuation plan
- Insurance (fire coverage)

## 10.8 Key Takeaways

### Regional Adaptation is Essential

1. **No One-Size-Fits-All Solution**
   - Each region faces unique challenges
   - Adaptation strategies must be tailored
   - What works in Arizona won't work in Maine

2. **Prioritize Based on Greatest Risks**
   - Southwest: Water scarcity and heat
   - Southeast: Humidity and hurricanes
   - Midwest/Plains: Severe weather and extremes
   - Northeast: Heating costs and season extension
   - Northwest: Summer drought and wildfire

3. **Leverage Regional Opportunities**
   - Southwest: Solar energy (best in U.S.)
   - Southeast: Year-round production
   - Midwest: Water conservation (aquifer relief)
   - Northeast: Winter premium pricing
   - Northwest: Rainwater abundance

4. **Climate Change Varies Regionally**
   - Some regions warming faster (Southwest)
   - Some getting wetter (Northeast), some drier (Southwest)
   - Extremes increasing everywhere
   - Plan for 2050-2080 conditions, not current

5. **Resources Available Regionally**
   - USDA Climate Hubs
   - State climatologists
   - University extension services
   - Regional grower networks
   - **Use them!**
