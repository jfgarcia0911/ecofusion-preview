## 9.2 Cooling Strategies for CEA

### Passive Cooling Methods

**Shading:**

```
SHADE CLOTH SELECTION FOR HEAT MANAGEMENT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Shade Level    Light         Temp          Best Application
               Reduction     Reduction
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

30% Shade      30%           3-5°F         Moderate heat, light-loving crops
                                           (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers)

50% Shade      50%           5-8°F         High heat, moderate light needs
                                           (lettuce, herbs in summer)

70% Shade      70%           8-12°F        Extreme heat, shade-tolerant
                                           (leafy greens, microgreens)

90% Shade      90%           12-15°F       Extreme heat + emergency cooling
                                           (Temporary, limits growth)

DEPLOYMENT OPTIONS:
├─► Fixed (all season): Simple, but inflexible
├─► Retractable (manual): Moderate cost, flexible
├─► Motorized/automated: High cost, optimal control
└─► Interior vs Exterior: Exterior more effective (blocks heat before entry)

MATERIAL CHOICES:
├─► Knitted shade cloth: Durable, doesn't fray
├─► Woven shade cloth: Less expensive, can fray
├─► Aluminum/reflective: Maximum heat rejection
└─► White vs Black: White reflects, black absorbs (both provide shade)
```

**Thermal Mass:**
- Water barrels, tanks, or tubes inside greenhouse
- Concrete floors or walls
- Absorb heat during day, release at night
- Effectiveness: 5-10°F night warming, 3-5°F day cooling
- Cost: Low (use what you have)

**Natural Ventilation:**

| Method | Air Exchanges per Hour | Cooling Effectiveness | Cost |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|------|
| **Side vents only** | 10-20 | Moderate | Low |
| **Roof vents only** | 15-30 | Good (hot air rises) | Medium |
| **Side + roof** (cross-ventilation) | 30-60 | Very good | Medium |
| **Ridge vent + side walls** | 40-80 | Excellent | Medium-High |

**Whitewash/Reflective Coatings:**
- Apply to greenhouse exterior (temporary or permanent)
- Reflects 30-70% of solar radiation
- Reduces cooling load significantly
- Removable (wash off after hot season)
- Cost: Very low ($50-200 for 10K sq ft)

### Active Cooling Systems

**Evaporative Cooling:**

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                  EVAPORATIVE COOLING SYSTEMS                                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                              ║
║  EVAPORATIVE PAD SYSTEM (Most common greenhouse cooling)                    ║
║                                                                              ║
║   [Evap Pads]  ──►  [Greenhouse]  ──►  [Exhaust Fans]                       ║
║   │  ↓  │                                                                    ║
║   Water drips                                                                ║
║   through pads                                                               ║
║                                                                              ║
║   How it works:                                                              ║
║   1. Water pumped to pads (cellulose or aspen)                              ║
║   2. Exhaust fans pull air through wet pads                                 ║
║   3. Evaporation cools incoming air by 10-30°F                              ║
║   4. Cooled air flows through greenhouse                                    ║
║                                                                              ║
║   Effectiveness: Depends on HUMIDITY                                         ║
║   ├─► Arid climates (10-20% RH): 20-30°F cooling                            ║
║   ├─► Dry climates (30-40% RH): 15-25°F cooling                             ║
║   ├─► Moderate (50-60% RH): 10-15°F cooling                                 ║
║   └─► Humid climates (>70% RH): <5°F cooling (not effective)                ║
║                                                                              ║
║   Cost: $1-3/sq ft installed                                                 ║
║   Operating: Very low (pump + fans)                                          ║
║                                                                              ║
║  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────    ║
║                                                                              ║
║  MISTING/FOGGING SYSTEMS                                                     ║
║   • High-pressure nozzles create fine mist                                  ║
║   • Evaporates in air (vs. on pads)                                         ║
║   • 5-15°F cooling (less than pads)                                         ║
║   • Can increase humidity (good for plants, can be issue for some crops)    ║
║   • Cost: $0.50-2/sq ft                                                      ║
║                                                                              ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

**Air Conditioning:**
- Most effective but most expensive
- Use only when evaporative cooling insufficient
- Typical: 1 ton AC per 300-600 sq ft (varies by climate, insulation)
- Operating cost: $0.50-2/sq ft/month (summer)
- Best for: High-value crops, humid climates, indoor farms

**Ground-Source Cooling:**
- Circulate water through underground pipes (50-60°F stable temp)
- Use for cooling via heat exchanger
- High upfront cost, low operating cost
- Best for: New construction, moderate climates

## 9.3 Heat-Tolerant Crop Selection

### Variety Selection

**Breeding for Heat Tolerance:**

| Crop | Standard Varieties | Heat-Tolerant Varieties | Benefit |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|---------|
| **Lettuce** | Buttercrunch, Bibb | Jericho, Muir, Nevada | 5-10°F higher tolerance |
| **Tomatoes** | Beefsteak, Early Girl | Phoenix, Heatwave II, Surefire | Fruit set at 90-100°F |
| **Basil** | Genovese | Aromatto, Eleonora | Better in heat, less bolting |
| **Spinach** | Bloomsdale | Tyee, Space, Indian Summer | Slower bolting |

**Crop Substitution Strategy:**

```
SEASONAL CROP ROTATION FOR HOT CLIMATES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

COOL SEASON (Oct-Apr): 60-75°F
├─► Lettuce (all types)
├─► Spinach
├─► Kale, chard
├─► Arugula
├─► Pac choi
└─► Herbs: Cilantro, parsley

TRANSITION (Apr-May, Sep-Oct): 70-85°F
├─► Heat-tolerant lettuce varieties
├─► Basil (starts producing)
├─► Young greens (harvest before heat)
├─► Tomatoes, peppers (planting/establishment)
└─► Herbs: Basil, oregano

HOT SEASON (Jun-Sep): 80-95°F+
├─► Basil (peak season)
├─► Tomatoes, peppers (if cooled adequately)
├─► Thai basil, other heat-lovers
├─► Edible flowers (nasturtium, calendula)
├─► Microgreens (short cycle, temperature control)
└─► Consider: Reduce production, focus on cooling

STRATEGY: Match crop to season, maximize profitability year-round
```

### Temperature Management

**Cooling Methods for Fish Tanks:**

| Method | Cooling Capacity | Cost | Best Application |
|--------|-----------------|------|-----------------|
| **Shade cloth over tanks** | 3-5°F | Very low | Outdoor tanks, mild heat |
| **Evaporative cooling (misters)** | 5-10°F | Low | Dry climates |
| **Water chillers** (small) | 10-20°F | Medium ($500-2K) | <500 gal systems |
| **Water chillers** (commercial) | 20-30°F | High ($3-15K) | Large systems |
| **Ground-source cooling** | 10-15°F | Very high | New builds, large scale |
| **Ice (emergency)** | Variable | Low (ongoing) | Temporary emergency |

### Heat-Related Illness

```
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### OSHA Heat Safety Requirements

**Prevention Measures (Required in many jurisdictions):**

1. **Water**
   - Potable water available at all times
   - Encourage drinking 1 cup every 15-20 minutes
   - Cool water preferred (not ice cold)

2. **Rest**
   - Frequent rest breaks in cool/shaded areas
   - At 90°F: 15 minutes rest per hour
   - At 100°F: 15 minutes rest every 30 minutes
   - Adjust based on heat index, workload

3. **Shade**
   - Shaded rest areas available
   - Workers can access at any time
   - Adequately sized for crew

4. **Training**
   - Recognize heat illness symptoms
   - Importance of hydration
   - Emergency response procedures
   - Buddy system (watch each other)

5. **Acclimatization**
   - New workers: Gradually increase workload over 1-2 weeks
   - All workers: After time off, re-acclimatize
   - Most heat illness occurs in first few days

6. **Monitoring**
   - Check on workers regularly
   - Heat index monitoring
   - Modify schedule when heat index >90°F

### Heat Stress Prevention Plan

**Operational Strategies:**

| Heat Index | Work Modification | Frequency of Breaks |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| **<80°F** | Normal operations | As needed |
| **80-90°F** | Increase water intake, monitor | Every hour |
| **90-100°F** | Reduce work intensity, increase breaks | Every 30-45 min |
| **100-110°F** | Essential tasks only, frequent breaks | Every 15-30 min |
| **>110°F** | Consider suspending outdoor work | Continuous monitoring |

**Engineering Controls:**
- Portable fans in work areas
- Evaporative cooling for break areas
- Minimize work in hottest part of greenhouse
- Schedule intense tasks for cooler parts of day (early morning)

**Personal Protective Equipment:**
- Light-colored, loose-fitting clothing
- Wide-brim hats (outdoor work)
- Cooling vests (available, but expensive)
- Sunscreen (outdoor work)

### Seasonal Production Adjustments

**Hot Season Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Trade-off |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Reduce production** | Grow 50-70% of cool-season capacity | Lost revenue vs. cooling costs |
| **Shift to heat-tolerant crops** | Basil, peppers instead of lettuce | Different market, may be less profitable |
| **Increase cooling investment** | AC, chillers | High cost, but maintain production |
| **Take summer break** | Shut down June-August, deep maintenance | Zero revenue, but zero cost |
| **Microgreens focus** | Short cycle, controlled environment | Good margins, smaller volume |

## 9.8 Key Takeaways

### Heat Management is Critical for Climate Adaptation

1. **Plant Responses to Heat are Nonlinear**
   - Each crop has critical thresholds
   - Beyond threshold, rapid decline
   - Quality suffers before quantity (lettuce bitterness, flower drop)

2. **Cooling Strategies are Hierarchical**
   - Passive first (shade, ventilation, thermal mass): Low cost
   - Evaporative second (pads, misters): Moderate cost, climate-dependent
   - Active third (AC, chillers): High cost, last resort

3. **Crop Selection Matters**
   - Match crops to season (cool-season crops in winter, heat-tolerant in summer)
   - Use heat-tolerant varieties
   - Shift production calendar to avoid worst heat

4. **Fish Temperature Management is Critical**
   - Monitoring essential (2-3x daily during heat)
   - Choose warm-water species for hot climates (tilapia, catfish)
   - Cooling systems may be necessary (chillers, ground-source)

5. **Worker Safety is Legal and Ethical Obligation**
   - OSHA requirements (water, rest, shade)
   - Heat illness can be fatal
   - Modify schedules (early morning, evening)
   - Engineering controls (fans, shade, cooling areas)

6. **Climate Change Demands Forward-Thinking Design**
   - Size systems for future heat (2050-2080), not current
   - Add capacity margin (25-50%)
   - Assume heat waves will be hotter, longer, more frequent
