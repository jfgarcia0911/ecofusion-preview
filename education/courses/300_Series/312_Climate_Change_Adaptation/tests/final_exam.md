### Question 32 (5 points)
**Describe the vulnerability framework (three components) and how it applies to assessing a CEA operation's climate risk. Include an example calculation.**

**Sample Answer (full credit):**
The vulnerability framework consists of:
1. **Exposure:** Degree of contact with climate hazard (e.g., greenhouse exposed to 100°F heat waves)
2. **Sensitivity:** Degree of impact from exposure (e.g., lettuce dies above 85°F = high sensitivity)
3. **Adaptive Capacity:** Ability to respond (e.g., evaporative cooling system = high capacity)

**Calculation:** Vulnerability = (Exposure × Sensitivity) - Adaptive Capacity
Example: (High heat exposure: 4) × (Lettuce sensitivity: 5) - (Cooling system: 3) = 20 - 3 = 17 (moderate vulnerability)

CEA typically scores LOW vulnerability due to controlled environment (low exposure) and high adaptive capacity.

### Question 33 (5 points)
**Compare the carbon footprint of field agriculture vs. CEA. What are the key factors that determine whether CEA has a lower or higher carbon footprint? Provide THREE specific factors.**

**Sample Answer (full credit):**
**Key factors:**
1. **Energy source:** Renewable energy (solar, wind) makes CEA 80-90% lower carbon; fossil fuel grid makes it higher
2. **System type:** Natural light greenhouse (lower) vs. full artificial light indoor farm (higher energy intensity)
3. **Comparison baseline:** Displacing shipped produce (CEA advantage) vs. local seasonal field (less advantage)

**Additional factors:** Transportation distance avoided, N2O/CH4 elimination, efficiency of equipment (LED vs. HPS).

**Conclusion:** CEA with renewable energy and displacing long-distance produce has significantly lower footprint; CEA with fossil fuels and competing with local field crops may have higher footprint.
