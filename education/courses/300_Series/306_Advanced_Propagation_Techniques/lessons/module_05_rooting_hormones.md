## 1. Plant Hormone Basics

### Hormones Involved in Rooting

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                    PLANT HORMONES IN PROPAGATION                             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║                                                                              ║
║  AUXINS (Primary Rooting Hormones)                                           ║
║  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ ║
║  │ Natural:                                                                │ ║
║  │  └─ IAA (Indole-3-Acetic Acid) - produced naturally in plants          │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Synthetic (used commercially):                                         │ ║
║  │  ├─ IBA (Indole-3-Butyric Acid) - Most common, stable, effective      │ ║
║  │  ├─ NAA (Naphthaleneacetic Acid) - Strong, long-lasting               │ ║
║  │  └─ 2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic Acid) - Very strong, limited use │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Functions:                                                              │ ║
║  │  ├─ Stimulate cell division in cambium/pericycle                       │ ║
║  │  ├─ Initiate root primordia formation                                  │ ║
║  │  ├─ Promote vascular connection                                        │ ║
║  │  └─ Suppress shoot development (apical dominance)                      │ ║
║  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ ║
║                                                                              ║
║  CYTOKININS (Supporting Role)                                                ║
║  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ ║
║  │ Types: Kinetin, BAP (6-Benzylaminopurine), Zeatin                      │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Functions:                                                              │ ║
║  │  ├─ Cell division promotion                                            │ ║
║  │  ├─ Shoot development (opposite of auxin)                              │ ║
║  │  ├─ Delay senescence of leaves on cutting                              │ ║
║  │  └─ Work synergistically with auxins at correct ratio                  │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Ratio: High auxin:low cytokinin → Root formation                       │ ║
║  │        Low auxin:high cytokinin → Shoot formation                      │ ║
║  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ ║
║                                                                              ║
║  OTHER HORMONES                                                              ║
║  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ ║
║  │ Ethylene:                                                               │ ║
║  │  ├─ Can inhibit rooting at high concentrations                         │ ║
║  │  └─ May enhance rooting at very low levels                             │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Gibberellins:                                                           │ ║
║  │  ├─ Generally inhibit rooting                                          │ ║
║  │  └─ Promote shoot elongation (unwanted in cuttings)                    │ ║
║  │                                                                         │ ║
║  │ Abscisic Acid (ABA):                                                    │ ║
║  │  ├─ Stress hormone, inhibits growth                                    │ ║
║  │  └─ Can reduce rooting under water stress                              │ ║
║  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ ║
║                                                                              ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

### IBA vs. NAA Comparison

| Characteristic | IBA (Indole-3-Butyric Acid) | NAA (Naphthaleneacetic Acid) |
|----------------|----------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Stability** | Very stable in solution | Very stable |
| **Translocation** | Stays localized at application site | Moves readily in plant |
| **Activity** | Moderate, consistent | Strong, variable |
| **Phytotoxicity** | Low (safer) | Higher (can burn tissue) |
| **Root quality** | Better lateral roots | May produce dense, short roots |
| **Commercial use** | Most popular (80% of products) | Less common, specific uses |
| **Best for** | General propagation, all species | Difficult species, quick results |
| **Concentration range** | 500-20,000 ppm typical | 100-5,000 ppm typical |

## 4. Preparing Custom Hormone Solutions

### DIY Liquid Rooting Hormone

```
PREPARING IBA STOCK SOLUTION (10,000 ppm)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

MATERIALS:
├─ Pure IBA powder (99% purity)
├─ 95% Ethanol or isopropyl alcohol
├─ Graduated cylinder or precise measuring tool
├─ Glass container with tight lid
├─ Scale accurate to 0.01g
└─ Safety equipment (gloves, goggles)

STOCK SOLUTION FORMULA:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                          │
│ For 10,000 ppm (1%) IBA stock solution:                                 │
│                                                                          │
│ IBA needed = (Desired ppm × Volume in mL) ÷ 1,000,000                   │
│                                                                          │
│ Example: 1 liter (1000 mL) at 10,000 ppm                                │
│ = (10,000 × 1000) ÷ 1,000,000                                           │
│ = 10 grams IBA per liter alcohol                                        │
│                                                                          │
│ PROCEDURE:                                                               │
│ 1. Weigh 10.0 grams IBA powder                                          │
│ 2. Add to 900 mL alcohol in glass container                             │
│ 3. Stir or shake until completely dissolved (may take 10-30 min)        │
│ 4. Add alcohol to bring total volume to 1000 mL                         │
│ 5. Label clearly: "IBA 10,000 ppm - DATE"                               │
│ 6. Store in dark glass bottle in cool location                          │
│                                                                          │
│ SHELF LIFE: 6-12 months if stored properly (cool, dark, sealed)         │
│                                                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

DILUTION GUIDE (from 10,000 ppm stock)
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                          │
│ To make working solutions from stock:                                   │
│                                                                          │
│ C₁ × V₁ = C₂ × V₂                                                        │
│ Where: C₁ = Stock concentration                                         │
│        V₁ = Volume of stock needed                                      │
│        C₂ = Desired concentration                                       │
│        V₂ = Final volume needed                                         │
│                                                                          │
│ EXAMPLES:                                                                │
│                                                                          │
│ Make 500 mL of 1,000 ppm quick dip:                                     │
│ V₁ = (1,000 × 500) ÷ 10,000 = 50 mL stock + 450 mL water/alcohol        │
│                                                                          │
│ Make 500 mL of 5,000 ppm quick dip:                                     │
│ V₁ = (5,000 × 500) ÷ 10,000 = 250 mL stock + 250 mL water/alcohol       │
│                                                                          │
│ Make 1 liter of 100 ppm long soak:                                      │
│ V₁ = (100 × 1000) ÷ 10,000 = 10 mL stock + 990 mL water                │
│                                                                          │
│ NOTE: For quick dip (alcohol-based), use alcohol as diluent             │
│       For long soak (aqueous), use water as diluent                     │
│                                                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

SAFETY PRECAUTIONS:
├─ ⚠ IBA powder is irritant - avoid contact with skin/eyes
├─ ⚠ Work in well-ventilated area (alcohol fumes)
├─ ⚠ Wear gloves and safety glasses
├─ ⚠ Keep away from heat/flames (alcohol flammable)
├─ ⚠ Label all solutions clearly
└─ ⚠ Dispose of properly (not down drain)
```

### Combined Hormone Formulations

```
ADVANCED FORMULATION: IBA + NAA COMBINATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SYNERGISTIC EFFECTS:
├─ IBA: Produces higher quality, more fibrous roots
├─ NAA: Faster root initiation, good for difficult species
└─ Combination: Best of both - fast initiation + quality roots

RECOMMENDED RATIOS:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Application Type      │ IBA:NAA Ratio │ Total Concentration            │
│───────────────────────┼───────────────┼────────────────────────────────│
│ General purpose       │ 2:1 or 3:1    │ 3,000 ppm IBA + 1,000 ppm NAA  │
│ Difficult species     │ 1:1           │ 5,000 ppm IBA + 5,000 ppm NAA  │
│ Herbaceous cuttings   │ 4:1           │ 2,000 ppm IBA + 500 ppm NAA    │
│ Woody cuttings        │ 2:1           │ 8,000 ppm IBA + 4,000 ppm NAA  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

EXAMPLE: Commercial-style combination (Dip'N Grow clone)
├─ 10 g IBA + 5 g NAA per liter alcohol
├─ = 10,000 ppm IBA + 5,000 ppm NAA stock
├─ Dilute 1:10 for softwood (1,000 ppm IBA + 500 ppm NAA)
└─ Dilute 1:1 for hardwood (5,000 ppm IBA + 2,500 ppm NAA)
```

│                  HORMONE APPLICATION TROUBLESHOOTING                        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                             │
│  PROBLEM: No improvement in rooting with hormone treatment                  │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │
│  │ Possible Causes:                                                       │ │
│  │  ├─ Hormone degraded (old, improperly stored)                         │ │

│  │  ├─ Environmental factors limiting (temp, humidity)                   │ │

│  │  └─ Wrong timing (tissue too mature)                                  │ │
│  │                                                                        │ │
│  │ Solutions:                                                             │ │
│  │  ├─ Test hormone freshness (positive control species)                 │ │
│  │  ├─ Increase concentration incrementally                              │ │
│  │  ├─ Optimize environment first                                        │ │
│  │  ├─ Improve stock plant management                                    │ │
│  │  └─ Adjust cutting timing                                             │ │
│  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│                                                                             │
│  PROBLEM: Tissue damage/burning at application site                         │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │

│  │  ├─ Alcohol burn from quick dip                                       │ │

│  │  ├─ Reduce concentration by 25-50%                                    │ │
│  │  ├─ Shorten dip time                                                  │ │

│  │  ├─ Remove excess powder                                              │ │

│  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│                                                                             │
│  PROBLEM: Excessive callus, few roots                                       │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │

│                                                                             │
│  PROBLEM: Uneven rooting across batch                                       │

│  │ Possible Causes:                                                       │ │

## 7. Advanced Applications

### Co-factors and Additives

```
HORMONE ENHANCERS AND SYNERGISTS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PHENOLIC COMPOUNDS
├─ Caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid
├─ Function: Enhance auxin activity, antioxidant
├─ Natural source: Plant extracts (willow water)
└─ Application: Add to hormone solution (100-500 ppm)

VITAMINS
├─ Thiamine (B1): 1-10 ppm
├─ Niacin (B3): 0.5-5 ppm
├─ Pyridoxine (B6): 0.5-1 ppm
├─ Function: Cofactors for root metabolism
└─ Often included in commercial rooting gels

CARBOHYDRATES
├─ Sucrose: 1-3% in long soak solution
├─ Function: Energy source during rooting
└─ Especially helpful for leafless cuttings

BENEFICIAL MICROBES
├─ Trichoderma, Mycorrhizae
├─ Function: Disease protection, nutrient uptake
├─ Many commercial gels include these
└─ Can mix powder into rooting medium

ANTI-TRANSPIRANTS
├─ Wilt-Pruf, Vapor Gard
├─ Function: Reduce water loss during rooting
└─ Foliar spray before or after taking cuttings
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **IBA is the most reliable and widely used** rooting hormone for commercial propagation
2. **Concentration matters** - too high causes damage, too low is ineffective
3. **Application method** should match scale and species requirements
4. **Fresh hormones** are critical - old or improperly stored products lose efficacy
5. **Hormones are tools, not magic** - environmental conditions must still be optimal
6. **Custom solutions** offer flexibility and cost savings at commercial scale

## Practical Exercise

**Hormone Concentration Experiment:**

Select 2 species (one easy-rooting, one moderate-difficult)

Test 4 IBA concentrations per species:
- 0 ppm (control)
- Low (species-appropriate)
- Medium (2× low)
- High (4× low)

25 cuttings per treatment (200 total)

**Measure:**
- % rooting at 2, 4, 6 weeks
- Number of roots per cutting
- Root length
- Presence of phytotoxicity
- Time to transplant readiness

**Deliverables:**
- Data analysis with dose-response curves
- Optimal concentration recommendations
- Cost analysis (hormone cost per successful cutting)
