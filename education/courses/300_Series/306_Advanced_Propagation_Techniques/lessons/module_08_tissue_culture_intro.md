## 1. Tissue Culture Fundamentals

### What is Tissue Culture?

**Definition**: Growing plants from cells, tissues, or organs in sterile conditions on artificial growth medium

### Types of Culture

| Type | Explant Source | Applications | Difficulty |
|------|---------------|--------------|------------|
| **Meristem culture** | Growing tip (0.1-0.5mm) | Virus elimination | Very difficult |
| **Shoot tip culture** | Terminal bud (2-5mm) | Rapid multiplication | Moderate |
| **Node culture** | Stem sections with bud | Propagation | Easy |
| **Callus culture** | Any tissue | Research, breeding | Moderate |
| **Embryo culture** | Seed embryo | Hybrid rescue | Moderate |
| **Protoplast culture** | Individual cells | Genetic engineering | Very difficult |

### Advantages vs. Traditional Methods

**Advantages:**
- Extremely rapid multiplication (1 → 1,000,000+ in 1 year)
- Year-round production
- Disease-free plants
- Genetic uniformity
- Space-efficient
- Long-distance shipping easy

**Disadvantages:**
- High initial investment
- Requires specialized skills
- Contamination risk
- Labor-intensive
- Expensive per plant (if small scale)
- Acclimation challenges

## 3. Culture Media

### MS (Murashige & Skoog) Medium - Standard Formula

```
MS BASAL SALTS (per liter)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

MACRONUTRIENTS:
├─ NH₄NO₃: 1,650 mg/L
├─ KNO₃: 1,900 mg/L
├─ CaCl₂·2H₂O: 440 mg/L
├─ MgSO₄·7H₂O: 370 mg/L
├─ KH₂PO₄: 170 mg/L

MICRONUTRIENTS:
├─ MnSO₄·4H₂O: 22.3 mg/L
├─ ZnSO₄·7H₂O: 8.6 mg/L
├─ H₃BO₃: 6.2 mg/L
├─ KI: 0.83 mg/L
├─ Na₂MoO₄·2H₂O: 0.25 mg/L
├─ CuSO₄·5H₂O: 0.025 mg/L
├─ CoCl₂·6H₂O: 0.025 mg/L

IRON SOURCE:
└─ Fe-EDTA: 36.7 mg/L

VITAMINS:
├─ Myo-inositol: 100 mg/L
├─ Thiamine-HCl: 0.1 mg/L
├─ Pyridoxine-HCl: 0.5 mg/L
└─ Nicotinic acid: 0.5 mg/L

CARBON SOURCE:
└─ Sucrose: 30 g/L (3%)

GELLING AGENT:
└─ Agar: 7-8 g/L

pH: Adjust to 5.7-5.8 before autoclaving
```

**Note**: Most labs purchase pre-made powder mix to save time and ensure consistency

### Growth Regulators

| Hormone | Function | Typical Concentration |
|---------|----------|---------------------|
| **BAP (cytokinin)** | Shoot multiplication | 0.5-5.0 mg/L |
| **Kinetin (cytokinin)** | Shoot formation | 0.1-2.0 mg/L |
| **IAA (auxin)** | Root induction | 0.1-1.0 mg/L |
| **IBA (auxin)** | Rooting | 0.5-3.0 mg/L |
| **NAA (auxin)** | Rooting | 0.1-2.0 mg/L |
| **GA₃ (gibberellin)** | Stem elongation | 0.1-1.0 mg/L |

**Key Principle**:
- High cytokinin:auxin ratio → Shoot formation
- Low cytokinin:auxin ratio → Root formation
- Balanced → Callus formation

## 4. Sterile Technique

### Contamination Sources

1. **Airborne**: Fungal spores, bacteria from air
2. **Surface**: Unsterilized tools, work surfaces
3. **Explant**: Internal/external plant pathogens
4. **Personnel**: Hands, clothing, breath
5. **Media**: Improper sterilization, contaminated ingredients

### Surface Sterilization Protocol

```
STANDARD EXPLANT STERILIZATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

STEP 1: WASH
├─ Rinse explants under running tap water (1-2 min)
├─ Use gentle dish soap if very dirty
└─ Removes gross contamination, debris

STEP 2: SURFACE STERILANT (choose one)

OPTION A: Bleach Method (most common)
├─ 10-20% commercial bleach (0.5-1.0% NaOCl)
├─ Add 1-2 drops Tween-20 (surfactant)
├─ Soak 10-20 minutes with gentle agitation
├─ Time varies by tissue thickness

OPTION B: Alcohol Method
├─ 70% ethanol: 30 seconds - 2 minutes
├─ Followed by bleach treatment (above)
├─ Better penetration for waxy surfaces

OPTION C: Hydrogen Peroxide
├─ 3-10% H₂O₂: 5-15 minutes
├─ Gentler, but less effective
└─ Good for delicate tissues

STEP 3: RINSE (in laminar flow hood)
├─ Rinse 3-5 times with sterile distilled water
├─ Each rinse: 1-2 minutes
├─ Critical: Must remove all sterilant
└─ Final rinse water should be bacteria-free

STEP 4: DISSECTION
├─ Transfer to sterile petri dish
├─ Trim damaged/sterilized edges
├─ Cut to appropriate size (5-10mm pieces)
└─ Transfer to culture medium immediately
```

### Laminar Flow Hood Usage

```
WORKING IN LAMINAR FLOW HOOD
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

BEFORE STARTING:
├─ Turn on blower 15-30 min before use
├─ Wipe down all surfaces with 70% ethanol
├─ UV sterilize for 15-30 min (if equipped)
├─ Organize all materials within hood
└─ Flame sterilize tools (or use 70% ethanol)

DURING WORK:
├─ Work at least 6" inside hood opening
├─ Never pass hands/materials above open cultures
├─ Keep movements slow and deliberate
├─ Flame tools between plants
├─ Minimize talking/air disturbance
└─ Work from clean to dirty

CONTAMINATION RATE GOALS:
├─ Beginner: <20% contamination acceptable
├─ Intermediate: 5-10% contamination
├─ Expert: <5% contamination
└─ Production: <2% contamination required
```

## 5. Culture Stages

### Stage I: Establishment

```
INITIATION PROTOCOL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Media: MS + 0.5-2.0 mg/L BAP + 0.1 mg/L IAA

Process:
├─ Surface sterilize explants
├─ Dissect to remove damaged tissue
├─ Place 1 explant per vessel
├─ Label with species, date, treatment
└─ Incubate in growth room

Duration: 2-4 weeks
Success criteria: Green, growing tissue
Common issues: Contamination (bacterial, fungal), browning (phenolic oxidation)

Solutions to browning:
├─ Add antioxidants (ascorbic acid 50-150 mg/L)
├─ Use activated charcoal (0.5-2 g/L)
└─ Transfer to fresh medium after 3-7 days
```

### Stage II: Multiplication

```
SHOOT PROLIFERATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Media: MS + 1.0-3.0 mg/L BAP (higher cytokinin)

Process:
├─ Separate shoots from Stage I cultures
├─ Transfer 3-5 shoots per vessel
├─ Subculture every 4-6 weeks
└─ Expect 3-10× multiplication per cycle

Multiplication rates (examples):
├─ Strawberry: 5-10× per month
├─ Banana: 4-8× per month
├─ Potato: 5-15× per month
├─ Orchid: 3-5× per month
└─ Blueberry: 3-6× per month

Production calculation:
1 initial explant → 5× multiplication × 8 cycles = 390,625 plants per year
(theoretical - actual is lower due to losses)
```

### Stage III: Rooting

```
ROOT INDUCTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Media: 1/2 MS + 0.5-2.0 mg/L IBA (low cytokinin, higher auxin)

Process:
├─ Select well-developed shoots (2-3 cm)
├─ Transfer to rooting medium
├─ Reduce light intensity (50%)
├─ Incubate 2-4 weeks
└─ Expect >80% rooting

Alternative: Ex vitro rooting
├─ Dip shoots in IBA solution (as with cuttings)
├─ Stick directly in rooting medium
├─ Saves time and labor
└─ Higher losses but faster overall
```

### Stage IV: Acclimatization

```
HARDENING OFF PROTOCOL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CHALLENGE: Tissue culture plants adapted to:
├─ 100% humidity
├─ Low light
├─ Sterile environment
├─ Sugar-fed (not photosynthesizing fully)
└─ Fragile cuticle and stomata

WEEK 1: Transition
├─ Remove from vessel, wash off agar
├─ Plant in sterile soilless mix
├─ Cover with humidity dome or plastic
├─ Humidity: 95-100%
├─ Light: 500-1000 fc
├─ Temperature: 70-75°F
└─ No fertilizer yet

WEEK 2-3: Acclimation
├─ Gradually open vents in dome
├─ Reduce humidity by 10-15% per day
├─ Increase light to 1500-2500 fc
├─ Begin light fertilization (50 ppm N)
└─ Monitor for wilting - slow down if needed

WEEK 3-4: Establishment
├─ Remove dome completely
├─ Full ambient humidity (50-70%)
├─ Normal light levels
├─ Standard fertilization
└─ Transplant to final containers

Survival rates:
├─ Beginner: 60-70%
├─ Experienced: 80-90%
└─ Automated systems: 90-95%
```

## 6. Commercial Applications

### Cost Comparison

```
TISSUE CULTURE VS. CONVENTIONAL PROPAGATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TISSUE CULTURE (commercial lab, 100,000 plants/year)
├─ Lab setup: $50,000 (amortized)
├─ Cost per plant: $0.15-0.50
├─ Time: 3-6 months from initiation
├─ Space: Very efficient (100,000 plants in 500 sq ft lab)
├─ Labor: Skilled but efficient at scale
└─ Best for: Difficult-to-root species, rapid scaling, disease elimination

CONVENTIONAL CUTTINGS (100,000 plants/year)
├─ Greenhouse setup: $30,000 (amortized)
├─ Cost per plant: $0.10-0.30
├─ Time: 4-12 weeks
├─ Space: Large (100,000 cuttings = 10,000+ sq ft)
├─ Labor: High if manual
└─ Best for: Easy-rooters, established operations

BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS:
Tissue culture economical when:
├─ Multiplication rate >5× per cycle
├─ Species difficult to propagate conventionally
├─ Need for disease-free stock
├─ Rapid scaling required
└─ Export/long-distance shipping
```

### Species Suitability

**Excellent for tissue culture:**
- Orchids, banana, strawberry, potato, pineapple
- Difficult-to-root woody ornamentals
- Plants with systemic diseases (viruses)

**Poor candidates:**
- Easy-rooting species (basil, coleus, willow)
- Species with high contamination rates
- Recalcitrant species (walnut, oak, chestnuts)

## 7. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| **High contamination** | Poor sterile technique | Review protocol, upgrade equipment |
| **Browning (explant death)** | Phenolic oxidation | Add antioxidants, frequent transfers |
| **No shoot formation** | Wrong hormone ratio | Increase cytokinin, reduce auxin |
| **Hyperhydricity ("glassy" plants)** | Excess moisture, high cytokinin | Reduce humidity, lower cytokinins, add ventilation |
| **Poor rooting** | Insufficient auxin | Increase IBA/NAA concentration |
| **High mortality in acclimation** | Too-rapid transition | Slower hardening, maintain high humidity longer |
| **Slow growth** | Nutrient depletion | Fresh medium, check pH, verify sterilization |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Tissue culture enables rapid multiplication** impossible with conventional methods
2. **Sterile technique is critical** - contamination is the main challenge
3. **Media composition** (especially hormone ratios) controls development
4. **Commercial viability** depends on species, scale, and alternative methods
5. **Acclimatization** requires careful attention to prevent losses
6. **Investment required** but pays off for high-value or difficult species
