# Propagation Glossary

**Course 306:** Advanced Propagation Techniques

---

## A

**Acclimatization** - The process of gradually adapting tissue-cultured or greenhouse-grown plants to ambient environmental conditions (lower humidity, higher light, temperature fluctuations).

**Adventitious roots** - Roots that develop from stems, leaves, or other non-root tissue, as opposed to roots developing from the primary root system.

**Agar** - Gelatinous substance derived from seaweed, used to solidify tissue culture media.

**Apical dominance** - Suppression of lateral bud growth by hormones produced in the growing tip (apex).

**Aseptic** - Free from contamination by microorganisms; sterile.

**Auxin** - Class of plant hormones that promote cell elongation, root formation, and other growth processes. Common synthetic auxins: IBA, NAA, 2,4-D.

---

## B

**Basal** - Referring to the base or bottom of a cutting (the end that was closest to roots on the parent plant).

**Bottom heat** - Heating system that warms the rooting medium/root zone, promoting faster rooting and germination.

**Bud union** - The point where scion and rootstock join in a grafted plant.

---

## C

**Callus** - Undifferentiated tissue that forms over wounds; precursor to root or shoot formation in many propagation methods.

**Cambium** - Thin layer of actively dividing cells between bark and wood; critical for graft union formation.

**Chilling requirement** - Period of cold temperature needed by some seeds or plants to break dormancy or induce flowering.

**Clone** - Genetically identical plant produced by vegetative propagation.

**Cotyledon** - Seed leaf; first leaf/leaves to emerge during germination (one in monocots, two in dicots).

**Cutting** - Portion of stem, root, or leaf removed from parent plant for propagation.

**Cytokinin** - Class of plant hormones that promote cell division and shoot formation. Examples: BAP, kinetin, zeatin.

---

## D

**Damping-off** - Disease of seedlings caused by soil-borne fungi (Pythium, Rhizoctonia) resulting in collapse at soil line.

**Dehiscence** - Natural opening/splitting of seed pods, fruits, or anthers to release contents.

**Desiccation** - Excessive water loss; drying out.

**DIF** - Difference between day and night temperatures; affects stem elongation (positive DIF promotes stretch, negative DIF reduces it).

**Dormancy** - State of suspended growth or development; in seeds, prevents germination even under favorable conditions.

**DROP** - Morning temperature drop technique; brief (2-3 hr) temperature reduction at dawn to reduce stem elongation.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)** - Measure of dissolved salts in water or growing medium; indicates nutrient/fertilizer concentration. Units: mS/cm or dS/m.

**Embryo** - Immature plant within a seed; consists of radicle, hypocotyl, cotyledons, and epicotyl.

**Endosperm** - Food storage tissue in seeds; nourishes embryo during germination.

**Epicotyl** - Portion of embryo/seedling above the cotyledons; becomes the shoot.

**Etiolation** - Abnormal growth in absence of light; characterized by elongated, pale, weak stems.

**Explant** - Piece of plant tissue used to initiate tissue culture.

---

## F

**F1 hybrid** - First filial generation from cross between two parent lines; exhibits hybrid vigor but doesn't breed true.

**Hardening off** - see Acclimatization

---

## G

**Germination** - Process by which a seed develops into a seedling; begins with water uptake (imbibition) and ends with radicle emergence.

**Gibberellin (GA)** - Plant hormone that promotes stem elongation, seed germination, and breaking of dormancy.

**Graft** - Union of two plant parts (scion and rootstock) to grow as one plant.

**Graft compatibility** - Ability of scion and rootstock to form successful union and grow normally long-term.

---

## H

**Hardwood cutting** - Cutting taken from dormant, fully mature wood (winter).

**Hilum** - Scar on seed marking point of attachment to parent plant.

**Hormone** - Chemical substance produced by plants that regulates growth and development at low concentrations.

**Humidity dome** - Clear plastic cover used to maintain high humidity around cuttings or seedlings.

**Hypocotyl** - Portion of embryo/seedling between radicle and cotyledons; becomes lower stem.

**Hyperhydricity** - Excessive water content in tissue culture plants; also called "vitrification" or "glassiness."

---

## I

**IAA (Indole-3-Acetic Acid)** - Natural auxin produced by plants.

**IBA (Indole-3-Butyric Acid)** - Synthetic auxin most commonly used for rooting cuttings.

**Imbibition** - Uptake of water by dry seed; first step of germination.

**In vitro** - In glass; refers to tissue culture (growing plants in laboratory containers).

**Internode** - Stem section between two nodes.

**Interstem** - Piece of stem grafted between rootstock and scion to improve compatibility or add characteristics.

---

## J

**Juvenile** - Young phase of plant development; often roots more easily than mature tissue.

---

## L

**Laminar flow hood** - Equipment that provides sterile air flow for aseptic tissue culture work.

**Leaching** - Washing soluble materials from substrate by passing water through it; used to reduce salt buildup.

**Leaf-bud cutting** - Cutting consisting of leaf, petiole, and short stem section with axillary bud.

---

## M

**Medium (plural: media)** - Substrate for growing plants; can be soil, soilless mix, or sterile culture medium.

**Meristem** - Region of actively dividing cells; growing points of shoots and roots.

**Meristem culture** - Tissue culture using very small shoot tip (0.1-0.5mm) to eliminate viruses.

**Micropyle** - Small opening in seed coat through which pollen tube entered ovule; often water entry point.

**Mist propagation** - Method using fine water spray to maintain high humidity around leafy cuttings.

**Morphological dormancy** - Dormancy caused by underdeveloped embryo at time of seed dispersal.

**MS medium** - Murashige & Skoog medium; standard nutrient formulation for tissue culture.

---

## N

**NAA (Naphthaleneacetic Acid)** - Synthetic auxin used for rooting; more mobile than IBA but higher phytotoxicity risk.

**Node** - Point on stem where leaf attaches; contains axillary bud.

---

## O

**Orthodox seed** - Seed that can be dried and stored at low temperatures without losing viability (most crop seeds).

**Osmopriming** - Seed priming using osmotic solutions (PEG, KNO₃) to control water uptake rate.

---

## P

**Peat** - Partially decomposed organic matter harvested from bogs; common component of growing media.

**Perlite** - Expanded volcanic glass used in growing media for aeration and drainage.

**pH** - Measure of acidity/alkalinity; scale 0-14, with 7 neutral. Optimal for most plants: 5.5-6.5.

**Phenolic compounds** - Plant chemicals that oxidize (turn brown) when tissues are wounded; can inhibit rooting.

**Phytotoxicity** - Plant damage caused by chemicals, including excessive fertilizer or rooting hormones.

**Physiological dormancy** - Dormancy caused by internal conditions in embryo requiring environmental cues to break.

**Phytochrome** - Light-sensitive plant pigment that regulates many processes including germination and stem elongation.

**Plug** - Small cell or container for starting seedlings; also refers to seedling grown in such container.

**ppm** - Parts per million; unit for expressing very dilute concentrations (1 ppm = 1 mg/L).

**Priming** - Seed treatment involving controlled hydration to speed germination.

**Primordia** - Earliest stage of development of organs (root primordia → roots).

**Propagation** - Multiplication of plants; can be sexual (seed) or asexual (vegetative).

---

## R

**Radicle** - Embryonic root; first structure to emerge during germination.

**Recalcitrant seed** - Seed that cannot withstand drying or freezing; must be kept moist (tropical species).

**Rejuvenation** - Techniques to restore juvenile characteristics to mature plant tissue, improving rooting ability.

**Rooting medium** - Substrate used for rooting cuttings; typically well-drained, sterile, low-nutrient.

**Rootstock** - Root system portion of grafted plant; selected for disease resistance, vigor, or adaptation.

---

## S

**Scarification** - Process of breaking or weakening hard seed coat to allow water entry and germination.

**Scion** - Shoot portion of grafted plant; selected for desired fruit, flower, or foliage characteristics.

**Semi-hardwood cutting** - Cutting from current season's growth that is partially mature (late summer/fall).

**Softwood cutting** - Cutting from soft, actively growing current season's shoot tips (spring/early summer).

**Soilless medium** - Growing substrate without soil; typically peat/coir + perlite/vermiculite.

**SPAD** - Measurement of leaf chlorophyll content/greenness using SPAD meter; indicates nitrogen status.

**Stock plant** - Parent plant maintained to provide cuttings for propagation; also called "mother plant."

**Stratification** - Cold or warm temperature treatment to break seed dormancy.

**Sub-irrigation** - Irrigation system where water is supplied from below, wetting medium by capillary action.

**Substrate** - Growing medium; material in which plants are rooted.

---

## T

**Terminal cutting** - Cutting that includes the growing tip of the shoot.

**Testa** - Seed coat; protective outer covering of seed.

**Tetrazolium (TZ) test** - Rapid viability test using tetrazolium salt that stains living tissue red.

**Tissue culture** - Aseptic culture of plant cells, tissues, or organs on artificial nutrient medium.

**True leaves** - Leaves with characteristics of mature plant; appear after cotyledons.

---

## U

**Uniformity** - Consistency in size, appearance, and quality within a crop; important for commercial production.

---

## V

**Vermiculite** - Expanded mica used in growing media for water retention and aeration.

**Viable** - Alive and capable of germination or growth.

**Vigor** - Rate and strength of growth; energetic, healthy growth.

**Vitrification** - see Hyperhydricity.

---

## W

**Water potential** - Measure of water availability to plants; gradient drives water movement.

**Whip and tongue graft** - Grafting method using interlocking cuts for same-diameter stems; common for fruit trees and grapes.

---

## Units of Measurement

**Length:**
- mm (millimeter) = 0.1 cm = 0.039 inches
- cm (centimeter) = 10 mm = 0.39 inches
- in (inch) = 2.54 cm
- ft (foot) = 12 inches = 30.48 cm

**Temperature:**
- °F (Fahrenheit) = (°C × 9/5) + 32
- °C (Celsius) = (°F - 32) × 5/9

**Volume:**
- mL (milliliter) = 1 cm³
- L (liter) = 1000 mL
- gal (gallon) = 3.785 L

**Concentration:**
- % = parts per hundred
- ppm = parts per million = mg/L
- ppb = parts per billion = μg/L

**Light:**
- fc (foot-candle) ≈ 10.76 lux
- μmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ (photosynthetic photon flux density)
- DLI (mol·m⁻²·d⁻¹) = μmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ × hours × 0.0036
