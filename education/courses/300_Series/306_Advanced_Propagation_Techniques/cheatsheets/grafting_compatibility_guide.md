## Grafting Compatibility by Botanical Relationship

### General Rules

| Relationship | Compatibility | Success Rate | Examples |
|--------------|---------------|--------------|----------|
| **Same species** | Excellent | 95-100% | 'Beefsteak' tomato on 'Maxifort' tomato |
| **Same genus** | Good to excellent | 80-95% | Apple on apple, tomato on eggplant |
| **Same family** | Variable | 30-70% | Tomato family intergeneric grafts |
| **Different families** | Poor | <10% | Rarely attempted commercially |

## Vegetable Grafting Compatibility

### Solanaceae Family (Nightshades)

#### Tomato Rootstocks

| Scion | Compatible Rootstocks | Purpose | Success Rate |
|-------|----------------------|---------|--------------|
| **Tomato** | Tomato (any variety) | Disease resistance, vigor | 90-95% |
| | Selected rootstock varieties (Maxifort, Beaufort, etc.) | Specific disease resistance | 90-95% |
| **Eggplant** | Tomato rootstock | Bacterial wilt resistance | 85-90% |
| | Eggplant rootstock | Disease resistance | 90-95% |
| **Pepper** | Pepper | Disease resistance | 85-90% |
| | Hot pepper on sweet pepper | Vigor | 80-85% |

**Note:** Tomato and eggplant are most compatible within Solanaceae for interspecific grafting.

#### Common Tomato Rootstock Varieties

| Rootstock | Resistances* | Vigor | Best Scion Types |
|-----------|-------------|-------|------------------|
| **Maxifort** | V, F, ToMV, N | High | Indeterminate, large fruit |
| **Beaufort** | V, F, ToMV, For, Pl | Very High | All types, high-wire systems |
| **Estamino** | V, F, ToMV, N | Medium | Determinate, processing |
| **Arnold** | V, F, ToMV | High | Beefsteak, heirlooms |

*V=Verticillium, F=Fusarium, ToMV=Tobacco Mosaic Virus, N=Nematodes, For=Fusarium crown/root rot, Pl=Corky root

## Fruit Tree Grafting Compatibility

### Pome Fruits (Rosaceae, Subfamily Maloideae)

#### Apple

| Rootstock Type | Size Control | Soil Adaptation | Precocity | Common Varieties |
|----------------|--------------|-----------------|-----------|------------------|
| **Seedling** | Standard (100%) | Wide range | Late (8-10 yrs) | Antonovka |
| **Semi-dwarf** | 50-80% | Moderate | Medium (4-6 yrs) | MM106, MM111, M7 |
| **Dwarf** | 30-50% | Good drainage needed | Early (2-3 yrs) | M9, M26, G16, G41 |
| **Extreme dwarf** | 20-30% | Excellent drainage | Very early | M27 |

**Compatibility Notes:**
- All apple varieties compatible with apple rootstocks
- Pear can be grafted on some apple rootstocks (short-lived, not recommended)
- Incompatibility rare within *Malus* genus

#### Pear

| Rootstock | Compatibility | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| **Seedling pear** | Excellent | Standard size, late bearing |
| **Quince (Cydonia)** | Good with interstems | Dwarfing, requires compatible interstem for many varieties |
| **OHxF series** | Excellent | Disease resistant, various sizes |

## Incompatibility Symptoms

### Watch For:

**Early Signs (1-3 years):**
- Excessive suckering from rootstock
- Overgrowth at union (scion or rootstock growing faster)
- Premature leaf drop on scion
- Poor growth/vigor

**Late Signs (3-10 years):**
- Sudden collapse/death
- Union breakage at graft
- Nutrient deficiency symptoms
- Reduced productivity

### Types of Incompatibility:

1. **Translocated** - Toxins/viruses move across graft
2. **Localized** - Poor vascular connection at union
3. **Delayed** - Symptoms appear years later

## Grafting Method by Compatibility

| Situation | Recommended Method | Success Rate |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| Same diameter stems | Whip & tongue | 85-95% |
| Rootstock much larger | Cleft, bark, or side graft | 75-90% |
| Vegetables (herbaceous) | Splice, tube, hole insertion | 85-95% |
| Topworking mature tree | Bark graft (spring), cleft | 70-85% |
| Difficult compatibility | Approach graft, bridge graft | 60-80% |

## Vegetable Rootstock Selection Decision Tree

```
START: What is your primary goal?

Disease Resistance?
├─ Fusarium/Verticillium → Resistant tomato rootstock
├─ Bacterial wilt → Eggplant rootstock for eggplant
└─ Soil-borne pathogens → Interspecific cucurbit for cucumber/melon

Vigor/Yield?
├─ Extended season → High-vigor rootstock (Beaufort, etc.)
├─ Poor soil conditions → Vigorous, adapted rootstock
└─ Cold tolerance → Cold-tolerant rootstock

Fruit Quality?
├─ Must maintain → Test rootstock impact before large scale
└─ Less critical → Focus on disease/vigor

Multi-resistance?
└─ → Modern hybrid rootstocks (Maxifort, Beaufort)
```

## Testing Unknown Compatibility

**Before Large-Scale Grafting:**

1. **Trial Run** (Year 1)
   - Graft 10-25 plants
   - Monitor closely for 1 full season
   - Evaluate: Success rate, growth, union quality, productivity

2. **Expanded Trial** (Year 2)
   - Graft 100-500 plants if Year 1 successful
   - Compare to non-grafted controls
   - Economic analysis

3. **Full Implementation** (Year 3+)
   - Only after proven success and profitability

**Red Flags - Don't Proceed:**
- <70% successful unions
- Obvious overgrowth at union
- Significant yield reduction
- Quality problems

## Quick Compatibility Check

**Before Grafting, Verify:**

- [ ] Same family (minimum)
- [ ] Same genus (preferred for fruit trees)
- [ ] Rootstock disease resistance matches needs
- [ ] Compatible vigor levels
- [ ] Proven combination (check references) OR small test first
- [ ] Seasonal timing appropriate (dormant season for woody plants)
