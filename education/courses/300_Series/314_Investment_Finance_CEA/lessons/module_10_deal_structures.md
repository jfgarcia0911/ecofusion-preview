# Module 10: Deal Structures and Negotiations

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Structure investment deals effectively
- Negotiate key deal terms
- Navigate term sheet negotiations
- Understand deal breakers vs. negotiable items
- Close transactions efficiently
- Handle common negotiation scenarios

---

## 10.1 Deal Structure Fundamentals

### Common Deal Structures

```
INVESTMENT STRUCTURE OPTIONS

1. STRAIGHT EQUITY
└── Investor receives ownership % for capital

2. CONVERTIBLE INSTRUMENTS
├── Convertible Notes
├── SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
└── Converts to equity at future event

3. PREFERRED EQUITY
├── Series Preferred Stock
├── With special rights and protections
└── Most common for VC/institutional

4. DEBT + EQUITY
├── Debt portion: Lower cost, tax-deductible
├── Equity portion: Upside participation
└── Mezzanine structure

5. REVENUE/PROFIT SHARING
├── % of revenue or profits
├── Until cap reached
└── No ownership transfer

6. JOINT VENTURE
├── New entity co-owned
├── Shared governance
└── Contributed assets and capital
```

### Deal Structure Decision Factors

| Factor | Straight Equity | Convertible Note | Preferred Equity | Debt + Equity |
|--------|----------------|------------------|------------------|---------------|
| **Complexity** | Low | Low | High | Very High |
| **Speed to Close** | Fast | Fastest | Slow | Slowest |
| **Investor Protection** | Minimal | Minimal | Strong | Strong |
| **Flexibility** | Limited | High | Moderate | Moderate |
| **Cost** | Low | Lowest | High | Highest |
| **Best Stage** | Pre-seed | Seed/Bridge | Series A+ | Growth+ |

## 10.2 Negotiation Strategy

### Preparation Phase

```
PRE-NEGOTIATION CHECKLIST

KNOW YOUR POSITION:
□ Minimum acceptable valuation
□ Maximum dilution tolerance
□ Must-have terms vs. nice-to-have
□ Walk-away scenarios
□ Alternative options (BATNA)

KNOW THE INVESTOR:
□ Investment thesis and focus
□ Portfolio companies and outcomes
□ Typical deal terms
□ Reputation and references
□ Decision-making process

PREPARE MATERIALS:
□ Financial model (multiple scenarios)
□ Market comparables
□ Term sheet comparisons
□ Due diligence documents ready
□ Legal counsel engaged

TEAM ALIGNMENT:
□ Founder agreement on terms
□ Board alignment
□ Existing investor input
□ Legal and financial advisors briefed
```

### Negotiation Principles

**The BATNA Framework:**

```
BEST ALTERNATIVE TO NEGOTIATED AGREEMENT

Your Position:
├── Raising $8M Series A
├── Lead offer: $25M pre-money valuation
└── Alternative: Bootstrap + revenue growth

BATNA Analysis:

Option A: Accept Lead Offer
├── Valuation: $25M pre
├── Dilution: 24% ($8M / $33M post)
├── Cash: $8M in bank
├── Timeline: Close in 60 days
└── Outcome: Fund growth, hire team, 2nd facility

Option B: Bootstrap
├── Valuation: Maintain higher for next round
├── Dilution: None now
├── Cash: Current $500K, burn $250K/month
├── Timeline: Need revenue in 90 days or trouble
└── Outcome: Uncertain, high risk

Option C: Wait for Better Offer
├── Valuation: Potential $30M+ if wait
├── Dilution: 21% vs. 24%
├── Cash: Burns $750K waiting 3 months
├── Timeline: Uncertain if better offer comes
└── Outcome: Risk current offer walks

DECISION:
Option A (Accept) is strongest:
├── Bird in hand
├── Reasonable valuation
├── Reduces execution risk
└── BATNA (Option B) is weak

Negotiate on terms, not valuation
```

### Common Negotiation Tactics

```
INVESTOR TACTICS & RESPONSES

TACTIC: "Exploding Offer"
├── Investor: "Offer expires in 48 hours"
└── Response: "We need reasonable time for diligence.
              One week is standard and fair."

TACTIC: "Take It or Leave It"
├── Investor: "These are our standard terms, non-negotiable"
└── Response: "We understand you have standards. Here are
              3 specific terms that don't work for us and why..."

TACTIC: "Industry Standard"
├── Investor: "3x participating preferred is market"
└── Response: "Our research shows 1x non-participating is
              standard for Series A. Here's data from 20 deals..."

TACTIC: "Future Round Protection"
├── Investor: "We need 2x preference to protect against down round"
└── Response: "We'll agree to 1x participating that converts
              at 2x return. Aligns incentives better."

TACTIC: "Last Minute Changes"
├── Investor: Adds term at final negotiation
└── Response: "That wasn't in term sheet. Either revert or
              we need to reconsider entire deal."

FOUNDER TACTICS

TACTIC: "Competitive Tension"
├── Create perception of multiple interested investors
├── Don't lie, but emphasize other discussions
└── "We're talking to several firms. Timeline matters."

TACTIC: "Anchor High"
├── Start with higher valuation expectation
├── Provides negotiating room
└── "Based on comps, we think $35M pre is appropriate..."

TACTIC: "Trade-offs"
├── Give on less important terms
├── Get on critical items
└── "We'll accept your board seat request if we can
     keep protective provisions more limited."

TACTIC: "Time Pressure"
├── Use external deadlines legitimately
├── Accelerate decision-making
└── "Our supplier needs commitment by month-end for
     equipment order at this price."
```

## 10.3 Key Negotiable Terms

### Valuation

```
VALUATION NEGOTIATION

SCENARIO: Series A Negotiation
├── Company: Greenhouse operator, $8M revenue, breakeven
├── Raise Amount: $10M
├── Company Ask: $40M pre-money (5x revenue)
└── Investor Offer: $25M pre-money (3.1x revenue)

NEGOTIATION PROGRESSION:

Round 1:
Company: $40M pre
├── Rationale: Comps trading at 5-6x revenue
├── High growth rate (50% YoY)
└── Technology IP valuable

Investor: $25M pre
├── Rationale: Private company discount (30%)
├── Early-stage risk
└── Market volatility

Round 2:
Company: $35M pre
├── Concession: $5M (12.5%)
├── "We'll meet you closer to middle"

Investor: $27M pre
├── Concession: $2M (8%)
├── "Based on diligence, some upward movement"

Round 3:
Company: $32M pre
├── Additional: $3M down
├── Approaching limit

Investor: $30M pre
├── Additional: $3M up
├── "This is our final. Consistent with our model."

FINAL DEAL:
├── Pre-money: $30M (25% down from ask, 20% up from offer)
├── Post-money: $40M
├── Dilution: 25%
└── Both sides compromise

ALTERNATIVE: MILESTONE-BASED VALUATION
├── Base: $28M pre
├── Tranche 1: $5M at close
├── Tranche 2: $5M when hit $12M revenue (12 months)
│   └── Adjust valuation to $32M if achieved
└── Benefits: Reduces risk for investor, upside for company
```

### Liquidation Preferences

```
LIQUIDATION PREFERENCE NEGOTIATION

INVESTOR INITIAL PROPOSAL:
├── 2x Participating Preferred
├── Example: $10M investment
└── On $50M exit:
    ├── Preference: $20M (2x)
    ├── Plus pro-rata: $7.5M (25% of remaining $30M)
    └── Total: $27.5M (55% of proceeds despite 25% ownership!)

COMPANY COUNTER:
├── 1x Non-participating
└── On $50M exit:
    ├── Option 1: $10M preference
    ├── Option 2: $12.5M (25% pro-rata)
    └── Take Option 2: $12.5M (fair 25%)

COMPROMISE OPTIONS:

Option A: 1x Participating with Cap
├── Preference + participation
├── Until total return = 2.5x investment
├── Then convert to common
└── On $50M exit:
    ├── $10M + $10M participation = $20M (2x, below cap)
    ├── Investor: $20M
    └── More than non-participating, less than 2x participating

Option B: Tiered Preference
├── 1x participating if exit < $40M
├── Convert to common if exit > $40M
└── Incentivizes driving higher value

FINAL DEAL: 1x Non-participating
├── Industry standard
├── Fair alignment
├── Clean cap table
└── Investor accepts based on:
    ├── Confidence in upside
    ├── Reasonable valuation
    └── Strong founder relationship
```

### Board Composition

```
BOARD SEAT NEGOTIATION

INVESTOR PROPOSAL:
5-person board:
├── 2 investor seats (40%)
├── 2 founder seats (40%)
└── 1 independent (investor chooses)
Result: Investor controls 3-2

FOUNDER COUNTER:
7-person board:
├── 3 founder seats
├── 1 investor seat
├── 1 seed investor seat
├── 1 employee representative
└── 1 independent (mutually agreed)
Result: Balanced, no single control

COMPROMISE:
5-person board:
├── 2 founder seats
├── 1 Series A investor seat
├── 1 seed/angel representative
└── 1 independent (requires both founder + investor agreement)

Plus Board Observer Rights for Series A
Plus Protective Provisions for major decisions

OUTCOME:
├── No single party control
├── Independent has meaningful voice
├── All stakeholders represented
└── Protective provisions give investor veto on key items

BOARD EVOLUTION CLAUSE:
├── Board expands to 7 at Series B
├── Series B gets 2 seats
├── Maintain representation ratios
└── Plan for future scales
```

### Protective Provisions

```
PROTECTIVE PROVISION NEGOTIATION

INVESTOR PROPOSAL (Extensive):
Require Series A majority approval for:
├── Any issuance of securities
├── Any acquisition or sale
├── Any new debt >$500K
├── Annual budget and plan
├── Hiring/firing any executive
├── Opening new facilities
├── Any transaction >$100K
├── Changing business direction
├── Amending bylaws
└── Paying dividends

FOUNDER POSITION:
Too restrictive, prevents normal operations

NEGOTIATED OUTCOME:
Require Series A majority for:
├── Issuing senior or parity securities
├── Acquisition or sale of company
├── New debt >$5M
├── Annual budget (material deviations >20%)
├── CEO hire/fire only
├── Amending certificate/bylaws affecting preferred
└── Paying dividends on junior securities

Day-to-day operations:
├── Board approval via normal majority
├── CEO has authority up to approved budget
└── Quarterly updates to investors

RATIONALE:
├── Protects investor's investment
├── Doesn't impede operations
└── Focuses on truly major decisions
```

## 10.4 Negotiation Scenarios

### Down Round Scenario

```
DOWN ROUND NEGOTIATION

Situation:
├── Series A raised at $30M pre, $10M invested
├── Company struggling: 50% of revenue targets
├── Series B needed: $8M
├── Investor offer: $20M pre (33% down from Series A)

Series A Holders:
├── Own 25% post-Series A
├── Anti-dilution protection (weighted average)
└── Concerned about further dilution

New Investor (Series B):
├── Wants fair entry price
├── Demands: Pay-to-play provision
└── Structure: Series A must invest pro-rata or convert to common

NEGOTIATION:

Series A Position:
├── "Down round reflects market, not execution"
├── Offer: Will invest $2M pro-rata at $22M pre (10% up)
└── Request: No automatic conversion to common

Series B Position:
├── "We're taking the risk at this valuation"
├── Must have protection: Pay-to-play
└── Series A must participate or lose preferences

Founder Position:
├── Need the capital to survive
├── Want to treat Series A fairly (good investors)
└── But need to close Series B

COMPROMISE STRUCTURE:

Pay-to-Play Terms:
├── Series A investors who participate pro-rata:
│   └── Keep all preferences and rights
├── Series A investors who don't participate:
│   ├── Retain liquidation preference
│   ├── Lose anti-dilution protection
│   └── Lose voting rights and board seat
└── Provides incentive to participate, not punitive

Valuation: $21M pre (split difference)

Anti-dilution Adjustment:
├── Series A price adjusts from $3.00 to $2.65
├── Conversion ratio improves
└── Partially offsets dilution

OUTCOME:
├── Series B: $8M at $21M pre (27.6% ownership)
├── Series A: $2M additional (maintain ~25% post-new money)
├── Founders: Diluted to ~40% (from 50%)
└── All parties compromised, deal closes
```

### Multiple Term Sheets

```
COMPETING OFFERS SCENARIO

Company: Vertical farm, $12M Series B raise

OFFER 1: Top-Tier VC
├── Valuation: $45M pre-money
├── Amount: $12M
├── Terms: 1x non-participating preferred
├── Board: 2 of 5 seats
├── Liquidation: Standard
├── Timeline: 90 days due diligence
└── Value-add: Strong brand, deep expertise

OFFER 2: Growth Equity Firm
├── Valuation: $50M pre-money
├── Amount: $12M
├── Terms: 1.5x participating preferred
├── Board: 1 of 5 seats
├── Liquidation: Senior to Series A
├── Timeline: 60 days close
└── Value-add: Operational support

OFFER 3: Strategic Corporate
├── Valuation: $52M pre-money
├── Amount: $12M
├── Terms: 1x non-participating
├── Board: 1 of 5 seats, observer
├── Liquidation: Pari passu with Series A
├── Timeline: 45 days close
├── Value-add: Offtake agreement, distribution
└── Strategic fit concerns

ANALYSIS:

Economic Value (at $100M exit):

Offer 1: $23.2M to Series B (23.2% of $100M)
├── Converts to common (better than $12M preference)

Offer 2: $26.3M to Series B
├── Preference: $18M (1.5x)
├── Participation: $8.3M (20.7% of $82M remainder)
└── Economic dilution worse for founders

Offer 3: $20.7M to Series B
├── Converts to common (20.7%)
├── Plus offtake adds $5M value
└── Effective value: $25.7M

Non-Economic Factors:
├── Offer 1: Best reputation, network value
├── Offer 2: Faster close, operational help
├── Offer 3: Strategic value, customer locked in

NEGOTIATION STRATEGY:

Use Offer 2 & 3 to improve Offer 1:
├── "Firm X offered $50M pre-money"
├── Ask VC to move to $48M pre
├── Match 60-day close timeline
└── Highlight strategic offering has offtake

VC Response:
├── Move to $47M pre (split difference)
├── 75-day timeline (meet in middle)
├── Offer to facilitate customer introductions
└── Final offer

DECISION:
Accept Offer 1 (VC) at improved terms:
├── Best long-term partner
├── Fair valuation
├── Clean terms
└── Strategic value over time worth more than extra $3-5M valuation
```

## 10.5 Closing Process

### Due Diligence Phase

```
DUE DILIGENCE TIMELINE

WEEKS 1-2: Information Request
Investor sends diligence request list:
├── Legal documents
├── Financial records
├── Contracts and agreements
├── IP and technology
├── Operations and facilities
└── Team and HR

Company Response:
├── Create virtual data room
├── Upload documents
├── Organize by category
└── Provide index

WEEKS 3-4: Document Review
Investor team reviews:
├── Legal: Counsel reviews corporate docs, contracts
├── Financial: Accountants verify financials
├── Technical: Engineers assess technology
├── Market: Analysts validate market assumptions
└── Operations: Site visit and facility tour

WEEKS 5-6: Follow-Up Questions
├── Investor submits questions
├── Company provides answers
├── Back-and-forth clarifications
└── Additional documents requested

WEEKS 7-8: Final Diligence
├── Reference calls (customers, partners)
├── Background checks (executives)
├── Facility inspection
├── Final financial review
└── Investment committee presentation

RED FLAGS THAT EMERGE:

Legal Issues Discovered:
├── Problem: Intellectual property not properly assigned
├── Resolution: Immediate assignment agreements executed
└── Impact: 1-week delay, no deal impact

Financial Discrepancy:
├── Problem: Revenue recognition issues ($500K)
├── Resolution: Restate financials, reduce valuation $2M
└── Impact: Re-negotiate valuation

Customer Concentration:
├── Problem: Top customer 60% of revenue (undisclosed)
├── Resolution: Require offtake agreement
└── Impact: Additional covenant added

SUCCESSFUL COMPLETION:
├── All issues resolved
├── Final valuation: $45M pre (adjusted from $47M)
├── Proceed to documentation
```

### Documentation and Closing

```
DEFINITIVE DOCUMENTS

WEEK 9-10: Document Drafting
Lawyers draft:
├── Stock Purchase Agreement
├── Amended & Restated Certificate of Incorporation
├── Investors' Rights Agreement
├── Right of First Refusal & Co-Sale Agreement
├── Voting Agreement
└── Management Rights Letter

WEEK 11: Negotiation
├── Company counsel reviews
├── Redlines and comments
├── Negotiate specific provisions
└── Multiple drafts exchanged

WEEK 12: Final Review
├── Signature pages circulated
├── Board approvals
├── Stockholder approvals (if required)
└── Legal opinions prepared

CLOSING DAY:
├── Final signature pages collected
├── Closing conditions verified
├── Wire transfer sent
├── Stock certificates issued
├── File documents with state
└── Press release (if applicable)

POST-CLOSING (Week 13):
├── Cap table updated
├── Board meeting scheduled
├── Investor onboarding
├── Deploy capital per plan
└── Update stakeholders

TOTAL TIMELINE: 12-13 weeks from term sheet to closing
```

## 10.6 Common Deal Breakers

### Issues That Kill Deals

```
TOP DEAL KILLERS

1. VALUATION GAP TOO WIDE
├── Company wants: $50M pre
├── Investor offers: $25M pre
└── 100% gap cannot be bridged

Prevention:
├── Realistic expectations from start
├── Market comparable research
└── Flexibility in structure if not valuation

2. UNDISCLOSED MATERIAL ISSUES
├── Lawsuit not disclosed
├── Customer loss hidden
├── Financial misrepresentation
└── Immediate deal termination

Prevention:
├── Full disclosure from start
├── Anticipate difficult questions
└── Proactive transparency

3. FOUNDER MISALIGNMENT
├── Co-founders disagree on deal
├── One founder wants out
├── Vesting disputes
└── Investor walks away

Prevention:
├── Align founders before fundraising
├── Clear roles and equity
└── Commitment agreements

4. COMPETITIVE PROCESS GONE WRONG
├── Company plays investors against each other aggressively
├── Investor feels disrespected
├── Trust broken
└── All investors walk

Prevention:
├── Professional process
├── Honest communication
├── Respect timelines
└── Choose lead, don't over-shop

5. REGULATORY/LEGAL ISSUES
├── IP not owned by company
├── Permit violations
├── Compliance issues
└── Risk too high

Prevention:
├── Clean up legal issues before fundraising
├── Invest in proper legal structure
└── Disclose known issues early

6. TEAM CONCERNS
├── Background check issues
├── Key person wants to leave
├── Skill gaps unfillable
└── Execution risk too high

Prevention:
├── Build strong complete team
├── Address gaps proactively
└── Retain key people

7. MARKET SHIFTS
├── Macro environment changes
├── Competitor raises large round
├── Regulatory change
└── Investment thesis invalidated

Response:
├── Adapt quickly
├── Pivot if necessary
└── May need to pause fundraise
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Preparation Critical**: Thorough preparation before negotiation improves outcomes; know your BATNA

2. **Everything Negotiable**: Valuation, terms, structure, timing all negotiable; find creative solutions

3. **Alignment Matters**: Choose investors aligned with vision and values, not just highest price

4. **Trade-Offs Strategic**: Give on less important terms to gain on critical items

5. **Professional Process**: Maintain professionalism and honesty throughout; reputation matters

6. **Due Diligence Thorough**: Expect extensive review; prepare materials in advance

7. **Close Efficiently**: 12-13 weeks typical from term sheet to close; manage timeline actively

8. **Avoid Deal Breakers**: Full disclosure, team alignment, and realistic expectations prevent most deal failures
