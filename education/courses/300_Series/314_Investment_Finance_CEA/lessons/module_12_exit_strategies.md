# Module 12: Exit Strategies and Planning

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Understand different exit options for CEA businesses
- Plan and prepare for eventual exits
- Maximize company value at exit
- Navigate the M&A process
- Evaluate exit timing and options
- Structure exits to optimize returns

---

## 12.1 Exit Strategy Overview

### Why Exit Planning Matters

**For Founders:**
- Liquidity and wealth creation
- Next opportunity or retirement
- Strategic rationale (better owner for next phase)
- Investor return obligations

**For Investors:**
- Required returns (typically 3-10x in 7-10 years)
- Fund lifecycle (10-12 year funds need exits)
- Portfolio company liquidity
- Return capital to LPs

### Exit Options for CEA Companies

```
EXIT STRATEGY LANDSCAPE

1. STRATEGIC ACQUISITION
├── Buyer: Larger food/ag company
├── Rationale: Technology, facilities, market share, talent
├── Typical Multiple: 1.5-4.0x revenue, 8-15x EBITDA
├── Timeline: 6-12 months process
└── Probability: Most common exit for CEA

Examples:
├── BrightFarms → Cox Enterprises ($100M, 2020)
├── Little Leaf Farms → Greenheart Farms ($32M, 2022)
└── 80 Acres → Creadev ($160M, 2021)

2. FINANCIAL BUYER (Private Equity)
├── Buyer: PE firm seeking platform or add-on
├── Rationale: Cash flow, consolidation opportunity
├── Typical Multiple: 6-12x EBITDA
├── Timeline: 4-8 months
└── Probability: Increasing for profitable operations

Requirements:
├── EBITDA >$5M typically
├── Stable cash flows
├── Management team in place
└── Scalable platform

3. IPO (Initial Public Offering)
├── Public markets via stock listing
├── Rationale: Access to capital, liquidity, currency for M&A
├── Typical Size: $100M+ market cap minimum
├── Timeline: 12-18 months preparation
└── Probability: Low (very few CEA companies)

Challenges:
├── Limited CEA public company track record
├── Market volatility and skepticism
├── Ongoing public company costs/requirements
└── Need for profitability and scale

Recent Examples:
├── AppHarvest (SPAC 2021, struggled, restructured)
├── Local Bounti (SPAC 2021, trading below IPO)
└── Village Farms (legacy public company)

4. MANAGEMENT BUYOUT (MBO)
├── Management team buys company from investors
├── Rationale: Team wants control, investors want liquidity
├── Typical Multiple: Lower (limited capital)
├── Timeline: 3-6 months
└── Probability: Moderate for smaller operations

Structure:
├── Management equity: 20-40%
├── Debt financing: 50-70%
├── Seller financing: 10-30%
└── Rollover equity: Sometimes

5. RECAPITALIZATION
├── Restructure ownership without full sale
├── Rationale: Partial liquidity for founders/early investors
├── Typical: New investor buys 60-80%, existing roll forward
├── Timeline: 3-6 months
└── Probability: Option for growing companies

Benefits:
├── Founder partial liquidity
├── Founder stays involved
├── Existing investors can exit or roll
└── New capital for growth

6. STRATEGIC MERGER / CONSOLIDATION
├── Merge with peer or competitor
├── Rationale: Scale, cost synergies, market power
├── Typical: Stock for stock, cash + stock
├── Timeline: 6-12 months
└── Probability: Lower currently (industry still fragmented)

Example (Hypothetical):
├── Two regional greenhouse operators merge
├── Combined entity has geographic coverage, scale
├── Cost savings through shared services
└── Better positioned for eventual strategic sale
```

## 12.2 Exit Planning and Preparation

### Building to Exit

```
EXIT PREPARATION TIMELINE

YEARS 5-7 BEFORE EXIT: FOUNDATION
├── Build strong management team (reduce key person risk)
├── Implement scalable systems and processes
├── Document everything (SOPs, recipes, protocols)
├── Build brand and customer base
├── Achieve consistent profitability
└── Clean cap table and corporate structure

YEARS 3-5: POSITIONING
├── Accelerate growth (revenue and profitability)
├── Build competitive moats (IP, contracts, brand)
├── Reduce customer concentration (<20% any customer)
├── Implement enterprise systems (ERP, CRM)
├── Achieve meaningful scale ($20M+ revenue target)
└── Clean up any legal/regulatory issues

YEARS 1-3: OPTIMIZATION
├── Focus on EBITDA margin improvement
├── Sign long-term contracts (demonstrates stability)
├── Build pipeline for growth (but don't over-invest)
├── Audit financials (clean financials = higher value)
├── Engage investment banker
└── Begin informal buyer discussions

YEAR 1: ACTIVE PROCESS
├── Prepare marketing materials (CIM)
├── Financial and legal due diligence preparation
├── Hire transaction counsel
├── Contact potential buyers
├── Run competitive process
└── Negotiate and close

EXIT VALUE DRIVERS:

Financial:
├── Revenue growth rate (20-30%+ = premium)
├── EBITDA margin (25%+ excellent)
├── Consistency and predictability
└── Working capital efficiency

Operational:
├── Technology differentiation
├── Proven unit economics
├── Scalability and replicability
└── Strong management team

Market:
├── Large addressable market
├── Favorable market position
├── Long-term contracts/relationships
└── Barriers to entry

Strategic:
├── Fit with buyer's strategy
├── Synergy opportunities
├── Unique assets (facilities, IP, brand)
└── Competitive threat (remove competitor)
```

### Common Value Destroyers

```
FACTORS THAT REDUCE EXIT VALUE

OPERATIONAL RED FLAGS:
├── Customer concentration (>30% from top 3)
├── Supplier dependency (single source)
├── Key person risk (founder critical to operations)
├── Inconsistent quality or yields
├── High employee turnover
├── Regulatory violations
└── Environmental issues

Fix before exit:
├── Diversify customer base 2+ years before
├── Develop alternative suppliers
├── Hire strong #2, transition leadership
├── Implement quality systems and controls
├── Build stable culture and compensation
└── Remediate any compliance issues

FINANCIAL RED FLAGS:
├── Declining revenue or margins
├── Aggressive revenue recognition
├── Off-balance sheet liabilities
├── Outstanding litigation
├── Tax issues
└── Poor working capital management

Fix before exit:
├── Return to growth or stabilize
├── Conservative accounting policies
├── Settle litigation
├── Clean up tax issues (costly but necessary)
└── Optimize working capital (shows as cash at close)

STRUCTURAL RED FLAGS:
├── Complex cap table (100+ shareholders)
├── Unusual investor rights (blocking provisions)
├── IP not properly owned by company
├── Missing contracts or documentation
├── Related party transactions
└── Shared services with other entities

Fix before exit:
├── Consolidate cap table (reverse stock splits)
├── Negotiate cleanups with investors
├── Assign all IP to company
├── Document everything
└── Unwind related party issues

TIMING ISSUES:
├── Entering process with <12 months cash
├── Must-sell desperation
├── Poor market conditions
└── Internal conflict/instability

Avoid by:
├── Start process with 18+ months runway
├── Explore exits from strength, not weakness
├── Be patient for right opportunity
└── Resolve internal issues first
```

## 12.3 The M&A Process

### Sale Process Timeline

```
M&A TRANSACTION TIMELINE (6-12 months typical)

MONTH 1-2: PREPARATION
├── Engage investment banker (sell-side advisor)
├── Prepare confidential information memorandum (CIM)
├── Develop target buyer list (50-100+ names)
├── Clean up financials and legal issues
├── Organize data room
└── Engage legal counsel

MONTH 2-3: OUTREACH
├── Contact potential buyers (confidential teasers)
├── Execute NDAs with interested parties
├── Distribute CIM to qualified buyers
├── Begin buyer meetings and Q&A
└── Manage process and timeline

MONTH 3-4: INDICATIONS OF INTEREST (IOI)
├── Receive non-binding IOIs from interested buyers
├── Evaluate offers (price, terms, certainty)
├── Select 3-5 buyers to advance
├── Provide access to data room
└── Site visits and management presentations

MONTH 4-5: DUE DILIGENCE
├── Buyers conduct detailed diligence
├── Q&A and document requests
├── Additional site visits
├── Reference calls
└── Synergy analysis

MONTH 5-6: LETTERS OF INTENT (LOI)
├── Receive binding or semi-binding LOIs
├── Negotiate key terms (price, structure, conditions)
├── Select final buyer
├── Enter exclusivity period (30-60 days)
└── Continue diligence

MONTH 6-9: DEFINITIVE AGREEMENT
├── Negotiate purchase agreement
├── Complete all due diligence
├── Resolve contingencies
├── Obtain approvals (board, shareholders, regulators)
└── Finalize all transaction documents

MONTH 9-12: CLOSING
├── Satisfy all closing conditions
├── Final regulatory approvals if needed
├── Wire transfer funds
├── Execute all closing documents
└── Announce transaction

POST-CLOSING (Months 12-24+):
├── Transition period (founder typically stays 12-24 months)
├── Integration with buyer
├── Earnout period (if applicable)
└── Non-compete period
```

### Valuation in M&A Context

```
M&A VALUATION FRAMEWORK

STRATEGIC BUYER VALUATION:

Base Value (Standalone):
├── Revenue: $30M
├── EBITDA: $7.5M (25% margin)
├── Multiple: 10x EBITDA (market rate)
└── Base Value: $75M

Synergy Value (Buyer-Specific):
Cost Synergies:
├── Eliminate redundant overhead: $1.5M/year
├── Purchasing scale savings: $0.5M/year
├── Shared logistics: $0.3M/year
└── Total Cost Synergies: $2.3M/year × 8x = $18.4M

Revenue Synergies:
├── Cross-sell to buyer's customers: $5M new revenue
│   × 25% EBITDA margin = $1.25M EBITDA
└── Revenue Synergies: $1.25M × 10x = $12.5M

Strategic Value:
├── Remove competitor: $5M
├── Acquire technology: $8M
├── Geographic expansion: $4M
└── Strategic Premium: $17M

TOTAL BUYER VALUE:
├── Base: $75M
├── Cost synergies: $18.4M
├── Revenue synergies: $12.5M
├── Strategic premium: $17M
└── Total Value to Buyer: $122.9M

Buyer typically pays 50-70% of synergy value:
Offer Range: $90M - $105M (1.2-1.4x standalone value)

NEGOTIATED PRICE: $95M
├── 12.7x EBITDA (vs. 10x market)
├── 3.2x Revenue
├── Premium reflects strategic fit
└── Seller captures portion of synergies

STRUCTURE:
├── Cash at close: $85M
├── Earnout (2 years): Up to $10M
│   └── Based on revenue targets
└── Total potential: $95M

WATERFALL ANALYSIS ($85M cash at close):

Proceeds Distribution:
├── Debt payoff: $15M
├── Transaction expenses: $3M
├── Net to equity: $67M

Preferred Stock (Series A, B):
├── Series B: $20M invested, 1.5x pref = $30M
├── Series A: $10M invested, 1x pref = $10M
├── Total preferences: $40M (paid first)
└── Remaining for common: $27M

Common Stock Distribution:
├── Founders: 40% = $10.8M
├── Employees (options): 10% = $2.7M
├── Early investors: 5% = $1.35M
└── Series A/B conversion: $12.15M
    (take pro-rata vs. preference)

Final Distribution:
├── Founders: $10.8M (11.4% of total)
├── Employees: $2.7M (2.8%)
├── Series B: $30M (31.6%)
├── Series A: $10M + $5M = $15M (15.8%)
├── Early investors: $6.5M (6.8%)
├── Expenses: $3M
└── Debt: $15M

Plus earnout potential (allocated pro-rata if paid)
```

## 12.4 Negotiating the Sale

### Key Deal Terms

```
PURCHASE AGREEMENT STRUCTURE

PURCHASE PRICE
├── Base purchase price: $95M
├── Working capital adjustment: True-up at close
├── Earnout: Up to $10M over 2 years
└── Escrow: $5M held for 18 months (indemnification)

Working Capital Adjustment:
├── Target NWC: $3M (normalized level)
├── Actual at close: $3.5M
└── Seller receives additional $500K
    (or pays if below target)

Earnout Structure:
Year 1: Up to $5M
├── $3M if revenue > $35M
├── $5M if revenue > $37M
└── Interpolated between

Year 2: Up to $5M
├── $3M if revenue > $40M
├── $5M if revenue > $43M
└── Interpolated between

REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES
Seller represents and warrants:
├── Financial statements accurate
├── Own all assets being sold
├── No undisclosed liabilities
├── All contracts valid and enforceable
├── No litigation (or disclosed)
├── Compliance with all laws
├── IP owned by company
├── Employee matters disclosed
└── Environmental compliance

Survival Period:
├── General reps: 18 months
├── Tax reps: Statute of limitations
├── Title to assets: Indefinite
└── Fundamental reps: Indefinite

INDEMNIFICATION
Seller indemnifies buyer for:
├── Breach of representations
├── Pre-closing liabilities
├── Undisclosed matters
└── Specific identified risks

Caps and Baskets:
├── Basket: $500K (buyer bears first $500K of losses)
├── Cap: $15M (15% of purchase price)
├── Exception: No cap on fraud or fundamental reps
└── Escrow: $5M available for claims (18 months)

CLOSING CONDITIONS
Buyer conditions:
├── Reps true at closing
├── No material adverse change
├── Regulatory approvals obtained
├── Key employees sign employment agreements
└── Customers/contracts confirmed

Seller conditions:
├── Buyer reps true
├── Financing confirmed
├── No buyer material adverse change
└── Purchase price payment ready

EMPLOYMENT AND TRANSITION
Founder/CEO:
├── 2-year employment agreement
├── Base salary: $250K
├── Bonus: Up to $150K (based on performance)
├── Reports to buyer's division president
└── Can be terminated for cause

Key Employees:
├── Retention bonuses ($2M pool)
├── Paid 50% at 12 months, 50% at 24 months
├── Conditional on employment
└── At-will employment maintained

NON-COMPETE / NON-SOLICIT
Founder agrees:
├── 3-year non-compete (CEA in North America)
├── 3-year non-solicit (customers, employees)
├── Perpetual confidentiality
└── Enforcement: Injunctive relief + damages

Exceptions:
├── Can invest passively (<5% ownership)
├── Can consult/advise (with notice)
└── Can start unrelated business
```

### Negotiation Strategy

```
COMMON M&A NEGOTIATION POINTS

PURCHASE PRICE
Buyer tactic: "Recut" price after diligence
├── "We found issues that reduce value by $10M"
└── Response: Expected diligence findings, already reflected
    in price. Material issues only justify re-trade.

Strategy:
├── Anticipate buyer will find 10-20 issues
├── Disclose known issues upfront (less impact)
├── Only material, undisclosed issues justify price change
└── Have breakup point and be willing to walk

EARNOUTS
Buyer position: Large earnout to share risk
├── $70M cash + $30M earnout
└── Seller bears all post-close risk

Seller position: Minimize earnout
├── $95M cash, no earnout
└── Clean break, no ongoing uncertainty

Compromise:
├── $85M cash + $10M earnout
├── Earnout based on objective metrics (revenue, EBITDA)
├── Seller maintains some control over earnout drivers
├── Short earnout period (12-24 months)
└── Buyer can't manipulate (most favored nations clause)

INDEMNIFICATION
Buyer push: Broad indemnification, high cap
├── Cap: 100% of purchase price
├── Survival: 5+ years
└── Basket: $0 (dollar one)

Seller push: Narrow indemnification, low cap
├── Cap: 10% of purchase price
├── Survival: 12 months
└── Basket: 2-3% of purchase price

Market:
├── Cap: 10-20% of purchase price
├── Survival: 12-24 months (general), longer for taxes
├── Basket: 0.5-1% of purchase price
└── Escrow: 10-15% of purchase price

EMPLOYMENT TERMS
Buyer wants: Long-term lock-up of founder
├── 3-5 year employment
├── Below-market compensation
├── Strict non-compete
└── Can terminate and keep non-compete

Seller wants: Flexibility and market comp
├── 12-24 month transition
├── Market compensation
├── Narrow non-compete
└── Non-compete only if employed

Compromise:
├── 2-year employment (with severance if terminated)
├── Market-rate compensation
├── Non-compete during employment + 1 year after
└── Good leaver / bad leaver provisions

CLOSING CONDITIONS
Buyer adds: Extensive conditions
├── All customers sign new contracts
├── All employees stay
├── No competitor new facilities announced
└── Create "MAC" (material adverse change) outs

Seller push: Minimize conditions
├── No financing contingency (require committed financing)
├── Limited MAC definition
├── No new business conditions
└── Clear process and timeline

Outcome:
├── Committed financing (not contingent)
├── MAC defined specifically (>20% EBITDA drop)
├── Commercially reasonable customer confirmations
└── Fixed timeline (closes in 60 days or either party can walk)
```

## 12.5 Alternative Exit Strategies

### Secondary Sales

```
SECONDARY SALE STRUCTURE

Purpose: Provide partial liquidity without full exit

Scenario:
├── Company raised $30M over 3 rounds
├── Now worth $150M (estimated)
├── Founder owns 35%, worth $52.5M
├── Founder wants liquidity but not to sell company
└── Secondary sale: Sell 20% of holdings

Structure:
├── Founder sells 7% of company (20% of 35%)
├── Buyer: Late-stage VC or growth equity fund
├── Price: $150M valuation
├── Proceeds to founder: $10.5M
└── Founder retains 28% ownership

Process:
├── Existing investors have ROFR (right of first refusal)
├── Offer to existing investors first
├── If declined, can offer to third parties
├── Board approval typically required
└── Company doesn't receive capital (goes to founder)

Advantages:
├── Founder gets liquidity
├── Founder stays motivated and engaged
├── Company continues growth trajectory
└── De-risks founder's wealth concentration

Disadvantages:
├── May signal founder losing confidence
├── Dilutes founder voting power
├── May complicate future fundraising/exits
└── Tax implications (ordinary income vs. capital gains)

Typical Situations:
├── Fast-growing pre-exit companies
├── Founders with significant wealth tied up
├── Long time to expected exit (5+ years)
└── Company doesn't need capital
```

### Dividend Recapitalization

```
DIVIDEND RECAP STRUCTURE

Purpose: Return capital to shareholders while maintaining ownership

How It Works:
├── Company raises new debt
├── Uses proceeds to pay dividend to shareholders
├── Shareholders retain ownership
└── Company has higher leverage, but no dilution

Example:
Company Profile:
├── EBITDA: $15M
├── Existing debt: $10M
├── Enterprise value: $120M (8x EBITDA)
└── Equity value: $110M

Dividend Recap:
├── Raise $40M new debt (total debt: $50M = 3.3x EBITDA)
├── Pay $40M dividend to shareholders
├── Equity value: $70M (unchanged ownership %)
└── Leverage: 3.3x (elevated but manageable)

Proceeds Distribution:
├── Series B (25%): $10M
├── Series A (15%): $6M
├── Founder (50%): $20M
├── Employees (10%): $4M

Post-Recap:
├── Everyone retains same ownership %
├── Partial liquidity achieved
├── Company continues operating
└── Eventual exit provides additional upside

When Appropriate:
├── Mature, profitable operations
├── Consistent cash flow
├── Moderate existing leverage
├── No imminent acquisition opportunity
└── Shareholders want liquidity

Risks:
├── Higher debt service burden
├── Reduced flexibility
├── Covenant compliance challenges
└── Limits future debt capacity
```

## 12.6 Maximizing Exit Value

### Value Creation Playbook

```
18-MONTH PRE-EXIT VALUE CREATION PLAN

MONTHS 18-15: STRATEGIC POSITIONING
Q: What story are we telling?
├── Define our differentiation clearly
├── Build competitive moats
├── Highlight strategic value to buyers
└── Position as platform vs. single facility

Actions:
├── File patent applications for technology
├── Sign 3-5 year customer contracts
├── Expand into adjacent geographies/products
├── Build brand recognition
└── Publish thought leadership

Expected Impact: +15-25% value (strategic premium)

MONTHS 15-12: FINANCIAL OPTIMIZATION
Q: How do we maximize EBITDA?
├── Improve margins through efficiency
├── Reduce unnecessary expenses
├── Optimize pricing
└── Show consistent growth

Actions:
├── Implement cost reduction program ($2M target)
├── Price increases where possible (+3%)
├── Automate to reduce labor
├── Renegotiate supplier contracts
└── Eliminate non-core expenses

Expected Impact: +10-15% value (higher EBITDA multiple)

MONTHS 12-9: OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE
Q: How do we reduce buyer risk?
├── Implement systems and processes
├── Build management team
├── Document everything
└── Demonstrate scalability

Actions:
├── Hire CFO (strong financial systems)
├── Implement ERP system
├── Create operations playbook
├── Achieve key certifications (food safety, organic, etc.)
└── Cross-train to eliminate key person risk

Expected Impact: +5-10% value (reduced risk premium)

MONTHS 9-6: CLEAN-UP
Q: What will scare buyers away?
├── Legal and regulatory issues
├── Customer concentration
├── Outstanding litigation
└── Messy cap table

Actions:
├── Resolve any regulatory issues
├── Settle outstanding litigation
├── Diversify top 3 customers to <50% of revenue
├── Consolidate cap table if needed
└── Organize clean data room

Expected Impact: +5-10% value (avoided discounts)

MONTHS 6-3: PREPARE MATERIALS
Q: How do we tell our story?
├── Create compelling investment memorandum
├── Prepare Q&A and FAQs
├── Get financials audited
└── Build buyer list

Actions:
├── Engage investment banker
├── Draft confidential information memorandum (CIM)
├── Audit last 3 years financials
├── Identify 50-100 potential buyers
└── Prepare management presentations

Expected Impact: +10-20% value (competitive process)

MONTHS 3-0: ACTIVE PROCESS
Q: How do we maximize competitive tension?
├── Contact multiple buyers simultaneously
├── Create sense of scarcity
├── Maintain leverage
└── Negotiate aggressively

Actions:
├── Reach out to 50+ potential buyers
├── Advance 5-10 to detailed diligence
├── Get 3-5 LOIs
├── Negotiate best and final offers
└── Select winner and close

Expected Impact: +15-30% value (competitive bidding)

TOTAL POTENTIAL VALUE CREATION: +60-120%

Example:
├── Starting value (do nothing): $80M
├── Execute 18-month plan: $128M-$176M
├── Value created: $48M-$96M
└── Worth the effort!
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Plan Early**: Exit preparation should begin years before actual sale; value is created over time

2. **Multiple Options**: Strategic sale, PE, IPO, MBO, recap each suit different situations and goals

3. **Process Matters**: Competitive M&A process with multiple bidders maximizes value

4. **Clean Up Issues**: Legal, financial, and operational problems reduce value; fix them early

5. **Tell Your Story**: Compelling narrative about strategic value, growth potential, and differentiation commands premium

6. **Know Your Value**: Understand drivers of value and how buyers will evaluate; prepare accordingly

7. **Negotiate Strategically**: Price is important but structure, terms, and certainty matter too

8. **Consider Timing**: Market conditions, company trajectory, and personal goals all factor into optimal exit timing
