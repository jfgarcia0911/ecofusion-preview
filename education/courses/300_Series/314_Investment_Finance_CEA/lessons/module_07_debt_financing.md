## 7.1 Debt Financing Overview

### Advantages and Disadvantages of Debt

**Advantages:**
- No ownership dilution
- Interest is tax-deductible
- Fixed obligations (predictable)
- Lower cost of capital than equity
- Maintains upside for equity holders
- Can leverage returns to equity

**Disadvantages:**
- Required payments regardless of performance
- Risk of default and bankruptcy
- Covenants restrict operational flexibility
- Requires collateral or guarantees
- May be difficult to obtain for early-stage
- Limits future borrowing capacity

### Types of Debt Financing

```
DEBT FINANCING HIERARCHY

SENIOR SECURED DEBT
├── Lowest cost (6-9%)
├── First lien on assets
├── Strictest covenants
└── Traditional banks, commercial lenders

SENIOR UNSECURED DEBT
├── Moderate cost (8-11%)
├── No specific collateral
├── Moderate covenants
└── Institutional lenders, bonds

SUBORDINATED/MEZZANINE DEBT
├── Higher cost (10-15%)
├── Junior to senior debt
├── May include equity kicker (warrants)
└── Specialty mezzanine funds

CONVERTIBLE DEBT
├── Variable cost (6-12% + conversion)
├── Converts to equity at trigger
├── Bridge financing often
└── VCs, angels, growth investors
```

## 7.3 Government-Backed Lending

### SBA Loans

```
SBA 7(a) LOAN PROGRAM

Loan Size:                  Up to $5,000,000
SBA Guarantee:              75-85% (depending on size)
Use:                        Working capital, equipment,
                           real estate, refinancing
Term:                       Up to 25 years (real estate)
                           Up to 10 years (equipment)
Interest Rate:              Prime + 2.75% (typical)

ELIGIBILITY:
├── Small business (<$15M revenue, <500 employees)
├── For-profit operation
├── U.S. based
├── Owner equity injection required
└── Reasonable credit history

SBA 504 LOAN PROGRAM

Structure:                  Two-loan package
├── Bank loan: 50% of project cost
├── CDC loan: 40% of project cost (SBA-backed)
└── Borrower equity: 10% of project cost

Use:                        Fixed assets only
                           - Land and buildings
                           - Major equipment
                           - Improvements

EXAMPLE: $4,000,000 GREENHOUSE PROJECT

Bank Loan (50%):           $2,000,000 @ Prime + 2.25%
CDC Loan (40%):            $1,600,000 @ 5.5% fixed (20 years)
Equity (10%):              $400,000

Benefits:
├── Low down payment (10% vs. 25-30%)
├── Long-term fixed rate on CDC portion
├── Below-market rates
└── Frees up working capital

Drawbacks:
├── Extensive paperwork
├── Longer approval process (60-90 days)
├── Job creation requirements
├── Personal guarantee required
└── Use restrictions

USDA LOAN PROGRAMS

Rural Energy for America Program (REAP):
├── Grants up to 25% of project cost
├── Loan guarantees up to 75%
├── Energy efficiency and renewable energy
└── Must be in rural area

Example: Solar installation for greenhouse
Project Cost: $500,000
├── REAP Grant: $125,000 (25%)
├── REAP Loan Guarantee: $281,250 (75% of remainder)
└── Equity: $93,750 (25% of remainder)

Effective equity requirement: 18.75% vs. 30% conventional
```

## 7.4 Alternative Debt Structures

### Mezzanine Debt

```
MEZZANINE FINANCING STRUCTURE

Use Case: Growth capital for proven operator
├── Too risky for senior debt alone
├── Founders want to minimize equity dilution
└── Bridge to eventual exit

TERMS:
Amount:                     $5,000,000
Interest Rate:              12% (8% cash, 4% PIK)
Term:                       5 years
Subordination:              Junior to senior debt
Security:                   Second lien on assets
Equity Kicker:              Warrants for 5% equity
                           at $20M valuation

CAPITAL STRUCTURE:
Senior Debt:               $8,000,000 (8% rate)
Mezzanine Debt:            $5,000,000 (12% rate)
Equity:                    $7,000,000
                           ──────────
Total:                     $20,000,000

PAYMENT IN KIND (PIK):
├── 8% paid in cash quarterly
├── 4% added to principal (PIK)
├── Reduces cash burden during growth
└── Higher total interest cost

Year-by-Year Principal (PIK accumulation):
Year 1: $5,200,000 ($5M + $200K PIK interest)
Year 2: $5,408,000 ($200K + 4% of $5.2M)
Year 3: $5,624,320
Year 4: $5,849,293
Year 5: $6,083,265 (balloon payment due)

EQUITY KICKER (Warrants):
├── Right to purchase 5% equity
├── Strike price: $1M ($20M × 5%)
├── Exercisable any time before maturity
└── Adds upside participation

Example Exit Scenario:
Company sold for $50M in Year 5
├── Senior debt payoff: $5M
├── Mezzanine principal: $6.08M
├── Warrant exercise: Mezzanine lender pays $1M
│   to buy 5% = $2.5M value
├── Mezzanine return: ($6.08M + $2.5M - $1M) - $5M
│   = $2.58M gain + interest
└── IRR: ~18% (attractive for mezzanine)

ADVANTAGES:
├── Lower equity dilution than pure equity
├── No control rights (vs. VC)
├── Flexible payment structure (PIK option)
└── Long-term capital (5-7 years)

DISADVANTAGES:
├── Expensive (12-15% typical)
├── Still dilutive (warrants)
├── Subordinated (higher risk to lender)
└── Covenants similar to senior debt
```

### Convertible Notes

```
CONVERTIBLE NOTE - BRIDGE FINANCING

Use Case: Series A fundraise in 6 months
├── Need working capital now
├── Don't want to set valuation today
└── Convert to equity at Series A

TERMS:
Principal:                  $2,000,000
Interest Rate:              6% annually
Maturity:                   18 months
Conversion:                 Automatic at qualified financing
                           >$5M
Discount:                   20% discount to Series A price
Valuation Cap:              $15,000,000
Security:                   Unsecured

CONVERSION SCENARIOS:

Scenario 1: Series A at $20M pre-money
├── Series A price per share: $2.00
├── Note conversion price: $1.60 (20% discount)
├── OR price implied by cap: $1.50 ($15M cap)
├── Note holder uses better: $1.50
├── Shares issued: $2M / $1.50 = 1,333,333 shares
└── Note holder ownership: 6.3%

Scenario 2: Series A at $10M pre-money
├── Series A price per share: $1.00
├── Note conversion price: $0.80 (20% discount)
├── OR price implied by cap: $1.00 ($15M cap > $10M)
├── Note holder uses better: $0.80
├── Shares issued: $2M / $0.80 = 2,500,000 shares
└── Note holder ownership: ~20%

Scenario 3: No qualified financing (maturity)
├── Note matures after 18 months
├── Options:
│   ├── Repay principal + interest
│   ├── Extend maturity (negotiate)
│   └── Convert at cap ($15M valuation)
└── Often negotiated based on circumstances

ADVANTAGES:
├── Fast to close (less negotiation)
├── Delays valuation discussion
├── Standard terms (SAFE note evolution)
└── Founder-friendly (in balanced market)

DISADVANTAGES:
├── Dilution uncertain until conversion
├── Debt overhang if no equity round
├── Multiple notes with different terms = complex cap table
└── Can be expensive if valuation rises significantly
```

BEST FOR:
├── Established, profitable operations
├── Need capital but want to avoid dilution
├── Strong cash flow to support rent
└── Focus on operations, not real estate

## 7.5 Debt Capacity Analysis

### Determining Optimal Leverage

```
DEBT CAPACITY CALCULATION

Step 1: Project EBITDA at Stabilization
Projected Stabilized EBITDA: $8,500,000

Step 2: Apply Lender Coverage Requirements
Minimum DSCR: 1.25x
Maximum Debt Service = EBITDA / 1.25
                     = $8,500,000 / 1.25
                     = $6,800,000

Step 3: Calculate Supportable Debt
Interest Rate: 8%
Amortization: 15 years
Payment as % of principal: 11.68%

Supportable Debt = Max Debt Service / Payment %
                 = $6,800,000 / 0.1168
                 = $58,219,178

Step 4: Apply Additional Constraints

Loan-to-Value Test:
├── Appraised value: $65,000,000
├── Maximum LTV: 75%
└── Max debt: $48,750,000 ✓ (lower than DSCR test)

Debt-to-EBITDA Test:
├── Stabilized EBITDA: $8,500,000
├── Maximum multiple: 4.5x
└── Max debt: $38,250,000 ✓ (most restrictive)

CONCLUSION:
Maximum sustainable debt: $38,250,000
(Limited by Debt-to-EBITDA covenant)

CAPITAL STRUCTURE:
Total Project Cost:     $55,000,000
├── Senior Debt:        $38,250,000 (70%)
└── Equity Required:    $16,750,000 (30%)

SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS:

If EBITDA is 10% lower ($7,650K):
├── DSCR test: $52,397,000
├── Debt/EBITDA test (4.5x): $34,425,000 ← Binding
└── Equity required: $20,575,000 (+$3.8M)

If EBITDA is 10% higher ($9,350K):
├── DSCR test: $64,041,000
├── Debt/EBITDA test (4.5x): $42,075,000 ← Binding
└── Equity required: $12,925,000 (-$3.8M)

EBITDA accuracy is critical for debt sizing!
```

## 7.6 Loan Documentation and Terms

### Key Loan Documents

```
LOAN DOCUMENT PACKAGE

1. LOAN AGREEMENT
   ├── Principal terms
   ├── Interest rate and fees
   ├── Repayment schedule
   ├── Events of default
   └── Remedies

2. PROMISSORY NOTE
   ├── Borrower's promise to pay
   ├── Amount, rate, term
   └── Enforceable obligation

3. SECURITY AGREEMENT
   ├── Collateral description
   ├── UCC-1 financing statement
   ├── Perfection of security interest
   └── Rights upon default

4. MORTGAGE/DEED OF TRUST (if real estate)
   ├── Lien on real property
   ├── Legal description
   ├── Foreclosure rights
   └── Title insurance

5. GUARANTY
   ├── Personal or corporate guarantees
   ├── Limited vs. unlimited
   ├── Joint and several liability
   └── Continuing guaranty

6. SUBORDINATION AGREEMENT
   ├── Junior lender acknowledges senior position
   ├── Standstill provisions
   └── Intercreditor agreement

7. CERTIFICATES AND EXHIBITS
   ├── Articles of incorporation
   ├── Operating agreement
   ├── Board resolutions
   ├── Insurance certificates
   ├── Environmental reports
   └── Financial statements
```

### Critical Loan Covenants

```
AFFIRMATIVE COVENANTS (Must Do):

Financial Reporting:
├── Quarterly financial statements (45 days)
├── Annual audited statements (90 days)
├── Annual budget and projections
├── Borrowing base certificates (if revolver)
└── Compliance certificates

Insurance:
├── Property insurance (replacement value)
├── General liability ($2M minimum)
├── Business interruption
├── Crop insurance
└── Key person life insurance

Operations:
├── Maintain facilities in good condition
├── Comply with all laws and regulations
├── Pay taxes when due
├── Maintain corporate existence
└── Allow lender inspections

NEGATIVE COVENANTS (Must Not Do):

Restricted Actions:
├── No additional debt without consent
├── No asset sales >$500K
├── No change in business
├── No distributions if DSCR <1.50x
├── No liens except permitted
└── No mergers or acquisitions

Limitations:
├── Capital expenditures <$750K/year
├── Operating leases <$250K/year
└── Investments/loans to affiliates prohibited

FINANCIAL COVENANTS (Maintain):

Minimum DSCR:               1.25x (tested quarterly)
├── Calculation: (EBITDA - CapEx - Taxes) / Debt Service
├── Measurement: Trailing 12 months
└── Cure: 30 days with equity injection

Maximum Leverage:           4.0x Debt/EBITDA
├── Total debt / EBITDA (TTM)
├── Tested: Quarterly
└── Steps down over time (4.0x → 3.5x → 3.0x)

Minimum Liquidity:          $1,000,000
├── Cash + available revolver
├── Tested: Monthly
└── Cushion for operations

Minimum Net Worth:          $5,000,000
├── Tangible net worth
├── Grows with retained earnings
└── Tested: Quarterly

Maximum CapEx:              $1,000,000/year
├── Without lender consent
├── Maintenance vs. expansion
└── Carry-forward allowed
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Debt Advantages**: Lower cost of capital than equity, tax benefits, and no dilution make debt attractive for established CEA operations

2. **Multiple Options**: Senior debt, mezzanine, convertible notes, and sale-leasebacks each serve different needs and stages

3. **Government Programs**: SBA, USDA, and Farm Credit programs can provide lower-cost capital for qualifying CEA operations

4. **Debt Capacity**: Determined by cash flow (DSCR), asset values (LTV), and leverage ratios (Debt/EBITDA)

5. **Covenants Critical**: Understanding and managing loan covenants is essential to maintain flexibility and avoid default

6. **Leverage Amplifies**: Debt magnifies returns in good times but increases risk in downturns; balance is key

7. **Process Takes Time**: Allow 3-4 months for debt financing process; preparation and documentation are critical

8. **Relationship Matters**: Building strong lender relationships facilitates future financing needs and provides support during challenges
