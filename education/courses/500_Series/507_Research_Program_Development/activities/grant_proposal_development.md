# Activity: Grant Proposal Development

**Course 507: Research Program Development**
**Activity Type**: Hands-on Grant Writing Exercise
**Estimated Time**: 15-20 hours
**Deliverables**: Complete grant proposal package

---

## Activity Overview

This hands-on activity guides you through developing a complete grant proposal for a research project in controlled environment agriculture or aquaponics. You will practice all aspects of proposal development from concept through final submission-ready document.

---

## Learning Objectives

By completing this activity, you will:
1. Develop a compelling research concept with clear objectives
2. Write a complete grant proposal narrative
3. Create a realistic budget and justification
4. Assemble all required supporting materials
5. Apply grant writing best practices
6. Produce a submission-ready proposal package

---

## Part 2: Proposal Narrative (8-10 hours)

Write a complete proposal narrative following this structure. **Total length: 10 pages maximum** (single-spaced, 1" margins, 11pt font).

### Section 1: Abstract (250 words max)

Write a compelling abstract that includes:
- Problem statement and significance
- Research objectives
- Approach/methodology
- Expected outcomes and impact
- Clear and accessible to non-specialists

**Tips**:
- Draft this LAST after rest of proposal written
- Every sentence must be essential
- Make first sentence grab attention
- End with impact statement

---

### Section 2: Introduction and Background (2 pages)

**Structure**:
1. **Opening Hook** (1 paragraph): Why is this important? What's the challenge?
2. **Background** (2-3 paragraphs): What do we currently know? Cite 10-15 key references
3. **Knowledge Gap** (1 paragraph): What's missing? What hasn't been solved?
4. **Your Solution** (1 paragraph): What will you do? Why is this promising?
5. **Objectives** (1 paragraph): What specifically will you accomplish?

**Tips**:
- Establish significance clearly in first paragraph
- Cite high-quality recent literature (last 5 years)
- Identify clear gap your research will fill
- Connect gap to your specific objectives

---

### Section 3: Research Objectives and Hypotheses (1 page)

**Format for Each Objective**:

**Objective 1**: [Specific, measurable objective statement]

**Hypothesis 1.1**: If [condition], then [outcome], because [mechanism].

**Rationale**: [Why this objective/hypothesis? What preliminary evidence?]

Repeat for 3-4 objectives with associated hypotheses.

**Tips**:
- Use SMART framework (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- Quantify where possible
- Ensure each objective builds toward overall goal
- Testable hypotheses with mechanisms

---

### Section 4: Research Approach and Methods (5 pages)

For each objective, provide:

**4.1 Objective 1: [Title]**

**Experimental Design**
- Treatments and controls
- Replication (biological and technical)
- Randomization approach
- Sample sizes (with justification)

**Detailed Procedures**
- Step-by-step methods
- Equipment and materials
- Growing conditions / system parameters
- Measurement techniques

**Data Collection**
- What will be measured?
- When and how often?
- Instruments and protocols
- Quality control measures

**Statistical Analysis**
- Analytical approach (ANOVA, regression, etc.)
- Software to be used
- Significance levels
- Power analysis if appropriate

**Expected Outcomes**
- What results would support hypothesis?
- What would refute it?
- How will you interpret ambiguous results?

**Potential Challenges and Alternative Approaches**
- What could go wrong?
- Contingency plans
- Alternative methods if primary approach fails

Repeat for each objective.

**Tips**:
- Include enough detail that expert could replicate
- Demonstrate feasibility
- Show you've thought through potential problems
- Include preliminary data if available (even small pilot)
- Use figures for experimental designs

---

### Section 5: Expected Outcomes and Impact (1.5 pages)

**Anticipated Results**
- What do you expect to discover?
- How will this advance knowledge?
- What are alternative possible outcomes?

**Scientific Significance**
- How will this change understanding in your field?
- What new questions will it enable?
- How will methods/tools benefit other researchers?

**Broader Impacts**
- Who will benefit and how? (industry, growers, consumers, society)
- Economic impacts (quantify if possible)
- Social or environmental benefits
- Training and education outcomes

**Dissemination Plan**
- Publications (target 3-5 papers; list potential journals)
- Conferences (2-3 per year; list key conferences)
- Extension outputs (fact sheets, webinars, field days)
- Industry engagement

**Tips**:
- Be specific about impacts
- Quantify where possible
- Show pathway from research to impact
- Address all review criteria (intellectual merit and broader impacts)

### Budget Justification (2-3 pages)

Write justification for EVERY line item:

**Personnel** (for each position):
- Who (or "Name TBD" for new hire)
- What they will do
- Why necessary for project
- How effort calculated
- Basis for salary (institutional scales)

**Equipment** (for each item):
- Specific model and specifications
- Why needed (can't use existing)
- How it will be used in project
- Quote or basis for cost estimate

**Materials** (by category or major items):
- What will be purchased
- Quantities needed
- Basis for cost estimates
- Why necessary for experiments

Continue for all budget categories.

**Tips**:
- Be specific and detailed
- Connect every item to project activities
- Show your calculations
- Use realistic cost estimates
- Justify, don't just restate

## Part 4: Supporting Materials (2-3 hours)

### Biographical Sketch (yours or create hypothetical)

**Format**: NSF-style or NIH-style (2 pages max)

**Sections**:
1. Professional Preparation (education)
2. Appointments (positions held)
3. Products (10-15 most relevant publications)
4. Synergistic Activities (service, outreach, etc.)
5. Collaborators and Affiliations

**Tips**:
- Highlight expertise relevant to this proposal
- Show track record of productivity
- Include collaborations demonstrating teamwork

---

### Facilities and Equipment (1 page)

Describe available resources:
- Laboratory space and description
- Greenhouse or growing facilities
- Major equipment already available
- Computing resources
- Institutional support (TTO, statistics consulting, etc.)

**Tips**:
- Show you have what's needed (that's not in budget)
- Demonstrate feasibility
- Highlight unique capabilities

---

### Data Management Plan (1-2 pages)

**Required sections**:
1. Types of data generated
2. Metadata and documentation standards
3. Storage and backup during research
4. Data sharing plan (where, when, how)
5. Retention and archiving (timeline)
6. Roles and responsibilities

**Tips**:
- Follow FAIR principles
- Name specific repositories (e.g., Dryad, Figshare)
- Commit to sharing upon publication
- Address any restrictions (e.g., patentable results)

---

### Letters of Support (1-2 letters)

**If you have collaborators or partners**, create realistic letters:
- On official letterhead
- Specific commitments stated
- What they will provide (expertise, facilities, funding, materials)
- Signed by appropriate authority
- Recent date

**Example Opening**:
"I am writing to express my strong support for Dr. [Name]'s proposal titled '[Title]'. Our company, [Company Name], has worked with Dr. [Name] on [previous project] and found [him/her/them] to be an excellent collaborator. We are pleased to commit [specific resources] to support this important research."

## Part 5: Assembly and Review (1-2 hours)

### Assemble Complete Package

Organize your proposal:
1. Cover page (if required)
2. Abstract
3. Narrative (Sections 1-6)
4. References Cited
5. Budget (all years)
6. Budget Justification
7. Biographical Sketches
8. Current and Pending Support
9. Facilities and Equipment
10. Data Management Plan
11. Letters of Support

### Self-Review Checklist

Go through the Grant Proposal Checklist (cheatsheet) and verify:
- [ ] All sections complete
- [ ] Page limits respected
- [ ] Format requirements met
- [ ] All review criteria addressed
- [ ] Budget matches narrative
- [ ] Writing is clear and compelling
- [ ] Proofread for errors
- [ ] Figures and tables effective
- [ ] References complete and formatted

### Peer Review (if possible)

Exchange proposals with classmate or colleague:
- Read their proposal
- Provide constructive feedback
- Use reviewer perspective
- Be specific and helpful

## Evaluation Rubric

Your proposal will be evaluated on:

### Scientific Merit (40 points)
- Clear, important research question (10 pts)
- Sound approach and methods (15 pts)
- Feasibility demonstrated (10 pts)
- Innovation and originality (5 pts)

### Broader Impacts (15 points)
- Clear societal benefits (8 pts)
- Dissemination plan (4 pts)
- Training components (3 pts)

### Writing Quality (20 points)
- Clarity and organization (10 pts)
- Compelling narrative (5 pts)
- Grammar and style (5 pts)

### Budget (15 points)
- Realistic and appropriate (8 pts)
- Well-justified (7 pts)

### Completeness (10 points)
- All required sections (5 pts)
- Supporting materials (3 pts)
- Format requirements (2 pts)

**Total: 100 points**

## Tips for Success

1. **Start Early**: Don't underestimate time required
2. **Be Specific**: Vague proposals don't get funded
3. **Show, Don't Just Tell**: Evidence for all claims
4. **Make It Easy for Reviewers**: Clear, organized, addresses criteria
5. **Get Feedback**: Fresh eyes catch issues you miss
6. **Proofread**: Typos suggest carelessness
7. **Follow Instructions**: Exactly as specified
8. **Be Realistic**: Don't overpromise
9. **Show Passion**: Enthusiasm is contagious
10. **Learn from Process**: This is a skill you'll use throughout your career
