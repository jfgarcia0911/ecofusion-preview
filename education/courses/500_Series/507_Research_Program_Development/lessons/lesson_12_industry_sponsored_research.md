# Lesson 12: Industry-Sponsored Research Programs

## Learning Objectives
1. Structure effective industry-sponsored research partnerships
2. Negotiate contract terms that protect academic and industry interests
3. Manage IP and publication rights in sponsored research
4. Balance confidentiality with academic freedom
5. Build and maintain long-term industry relationships

## Introduction

Industry-sponsored research represents a critical funding source and pathway to real-world impact. Successful industry partnerships require careful structuring, clear agreements, and active relationship management to balance the interests of academic research with commercial objectives.

---

## 1. Types of Industry-Sponsored Research

### Sponsored Research Models

**Contract Research (Fee-for-Service)**:
- Defined scope and deliverables
- Company dictates research questions and methods
- Results belong to company
- Limited publication rights
- Typical: $50K-$200K; 6-18 months
- **Pros**: Straightforward; immediate funding
- **Cons**: Limited academic freedom; restricted publication

**Sponsored Research Agreement (SRA)**:
- Shared interest in research questions
- University maintains research independence
- Results typically public with brief delay
- IP terms negotiated
- Typical: $100K-$500K+; 1-3 years
- **Pros**: Academic freedom preserved; publishable; good for students
- **Cons**: Negotiation complex; may have publication delays

**Cooperative Research and Development Agreement (CRADA)**:
- Partnership with federal labs
- Shared resources and expertise
- IP shared based on contributions
- Typical: Variable; multi-year
- **Pros**: Access to federal facilities; shared resources
- **Cons**: Complex agreements; federal regulations

**Strategic Alliance**:
- Long-term partnership (5-10 years)
- Multiple projects over time
- Joint research planning
- Significant commitment from both parties
- Typical: $500K-$5M+/year
- **Pros**: Sustained funding; deep collaboration
- **Cons**: Significant commitment; complex governance

### Industry Partnership Continuum

```
Low Engagement ←→ High Engagement
Low Funding ←→ High Funding
Low Restriction ←→ High Restriction

Consulting → Contract Research → Sponsored Research → Strategic Alliance
```

**Choose Model Based On**:
- Research objectives (fundamental vs. product development)
- Funding needs
- Publication requirements
- Student involvement
- IP considerations
- Time commitment

## 2. Structuring Industry Partnerships

### Initial Engagement

**Discovery Phase**:
- Identify potential industry partners (conferences, existing relationships, cold outreach)
- Understand company's research needs and challenges
- Present your capabilities and relevant research
- Explore alignment and mutual interest
- Establish personal connections and trust

**Scoping Conversations**:
- Define research questions and objectives
- Discuss timelines and resources needed
- Explore IP and publication expectations
- Identify potential concerns or obstacles
- Outline next steps toward formal agreement

**Proposal Development**:
- Technical proposal describing research plan
- Timeline and milestones
- Budget and justification
- Team and qualifications
- Deliverables and reporting

### Partnership Structure Design

**Governance**:
- **Steering Committee**: Senior leaders from both parties
- **Technical Advisory**: Subject matter experts providing guidance
- **Project Team**: Day-to-day research execution
- **Regular Meetings**: Monthly technical updates; quarterly strategic reviews

**Communication Protocols**:
- Points of contact for each party
- Meeting frequency and format
- Reporting requirements (quarterly reports typical)
- Issue escalation procedures
- Decision-making authorities

**Resource Commitments**:
- **University**: Personnel, facilities, equipment, expertise
- **Industry**: Funding, materials, equipment access, technical guidance
- **In-Kind Contributions**: Valued and documented
- **Cost-Sharing**: If required by other funders

### Budget and Pricing

**Cost Components**:
- **Personnel**: Salaries and benefits for researchers
- **Materials and Supplies**: Consumables for experiments
- **Equipment**: If needed and agreed upon
- **Travel**: Conference presentations; site visits
- **Indirect Costs (F&A)**: Institutional overhead

**Pricing Strategies**:

**Full Cost Recovery**:
- All direct costs plus full indirect rate (50-60% typically)
- Appropriate for most sponsored research
- Example: $100K direct costs + $55K F&A (55%) = $155K total

**Negotiated Indirect Rate**:
- Lower than full rate (e.g., 25-30%)
- Industry often requests; institutions may accept to secure relationship
- Must follow institutional policies
- Example: $100K direct costs + $26K F&A (26% negotiated) = $126K total

**Fixed Price**:
- Total project price regardless of actual costs
- Risk to university if costs exceed price
- Appropriate for well-defined scope
- Example: $150K fixed price for defined deliverables

**Milestone-Based Payments**:
- Payment tied to achievement of milestones
- Aligns incentives; manages company risk
- Example: $200K total = $50K start + $75K upon Phase 1 completion + $75K upon final report

## 3. Contract Negotiation

### Key Agreement Terms

**Statement of Work (SOW)**:
- Research objectives and scope
- Methods and approach
- Deliverables (reports, data, prototypes)
- Timelines and milestones
- Responsibilities of each party

**Financial Terms**:
- Total funding amount
- Payment schedule
- Allowable costs
- Invoicing procedures
- Budget modifications
- Cost overruns (who bears?)

**Intellectual Property Rights**:

**Background IP**:
- IP brought into project by each party
- Rights to use background IP for project
- No transfer of ownership

**Foreground IP (Created During Project)**:

*Option 1: University Owns, Company Option to License*:
- University retains ownership
- Company has first option to negotiate exclusive or non-exclusive license
- Option period (e.g., 90 days)
- Terms negotiated if option exercised
- **Most common for sponsored research**

*Option 2: Joint Ownership*:
- Both parties own IP jointly
- Rights to use and license typically negotiated
- Can complicate commercialization
- Less common; use carefully

*Option 3: Company Owns*:
- Company acquires full ownership
- University may retain research use rights
- Typically requires premium pricing
- **More common for contract research than sponsored research**

**Publication and Confidentiality**:

**Academic Preference**:
- Right to publish all results
- Brief review period (30 days)
- No indefinite delays

**Industry Preference**:
- Longer review period (60-90 days)
- Right to delay for patent filing (additional 90 days)
- Ability to redact confidential information

**Typical Compromise**:
- 30-60 day review period for company feedback
- Additional 60-90 days delay (one time) if company filing patent
- Redaction of company confidential information (not research results)
- Commitment to publish within reasonable timeframe (e.g., 12-24 months after project)

**Confidentiality Terms**:
- Definition of confidential information
- Obligations to protect
- Exceptions (public information, independently developed)
- Duration (typically 3-5 years)
- Return or destruction at end

**Term and Termination**:
- Agreement duration
- Termination for cause (breach, misconduct)
- Termination for convenience (either party with notice, e.g., 90 days)
- Wind-down procedures
- Surviving obligations (confidentiality, publication rights)

**Liability and Indemnification**:
- Each party limits liability
- Indemnification for third-party claims
- Insurance requirements
- No warranties on research results (important for universities)

**Compliance and Ethics**:
- Compliance with applicable laws and regulations
- IRB/IACUC approval if needed
- Export control compliance
- Research integrity standards

### Negotiation Process

**Preparation**:
- Know your institution's non-negotiable terms
- Understand company's priorities and constraints
- Identify your priorities (funding, publication, IP)
- Determine acceptable trade-offs

**Engagement of Stakeholders**:
- Technology Transfer Office (TTO) / Office of Sponsored Programs
- Legal counsel
- Research administration
- Department chair or dean (for large agreements)

**Negotiation Strategies**:
- Start from institutional template when possible
- Understand reasons behind each party's positions
- Look for creative win-win solutions
- Be flexible on lower-priority terms
- Firm on critical institutional policies
- Document all agreed terms in writing

**Common Sticking Points**:

**Indirect Costs**:
- Company: Wants to pay 0% or low rate
- University: Needs to recover actual costs
- *Compromise*: Negotiate fixed rate (25-30%); or additional direct costs in lieu of F&A

**IP Ownership**:
- Company: Wants to own or have exclusive rights
- University: Wants to own; available to others
- *Compromise*: University owns; company has option to exclusive license in field of use

**Publication Delays**:
- Company: Wants long delays to protect IP
- University: Wants timely publication for academic progress
- *Compromise*: 60-day review + one-time 90-day delay for patent filing

**Timeline**:
- Negotiation can take 3-12 months for complex agreements
- Simple SRAs: 1-3 months
- Strategic alliances: 6-12+ months
- Plan accordingly; start early

## 4. Managing Industry Partnerships

### Project Execution

**Kick-Off Meeting**:
- Review agreement terms and expectations
- Introduce all team members
- Clarify roles and responsibilities
- Establish communication protocols
- Discuss confidentiality and IP procedures
- Set meeting schedules
- Identify potential risks and mitigation

**Regular Communication**:
- **Monthly Technical Meetings**: Progress updates, data review, troubleshooting
- **Quarterly Business Reviews**: Overall progress, milestones, budget, strategic direction
- **Annual Strategic Reviews**: Assess partnership, renew or adjust

**Progress Reporting**:
- Quarterly written reports typical
- Include: work performed, results, challenges, next steps, publications
- Budget status and spending rate
- Transparency about problems; no surprises

**Budget Management**:
- Track spending carefully
- Invoice promptly according to agreement
- Communicate early if overruns anticipated
- Request budget modifications in writing

### Relationship Management

**Building Trust**:
- Deliver on commitments
- Communicate proactively
- Be honest about challenges and timelines
- Respect confidential information
- Professional interactions

**Managing Expectations**:
- Research is uncertain; not all experiments succeed
- Timelines may slip due to unforeseen issues
- Results may not be what company hopes for
- Academic process (publication, students) takes time

**Boundary Management**:
- Students are learners, not employees
- Research independence maintained
- No undue influence on results or conclusions
- Reasonable access for company but not micromanagement

**Conflict Resolution**:
- Address issues early and directly
- Escalate to steering committee if needed
- Refer to agreement terms
- Mediation if serious disputes
- Maintain professionalism throughout

### Student and Postdoc Involvement

**Benefits**:
- Relevant research experience
- Exposure to industry perspectives
- Networking and career connections
- Practical skills development
- Potential job opportunities

**Considerations**:
- Students must be able to publish (requirement for degrees)
- Appropriate topics for theses/dissertations
- No undue pressure from industry partner
- Clear expectations about confidentiality
- Company interactions appropriate and professional

**Best Practices**:
- Include publication rights in agreement
- Student presents at conferences
- Thesis research is publishable
- Company feedback is advisory, not directive
- PI maintains mentoring relationship and protects student interests

## 5. Intellectual Property in Industry Partnerships

### IP Policies and Practices

**University Policies**:
- Inventions created using university resources belong to university (Bayh-Dole Act)
- University has obligation to commercialize
- Revenue sharing with inventors (typically 30-40%)
- TTO manages IP

**Industry Expectations**:
- Access to or ownership of IP
- Exclusive rights in relevant fields
- Ability to commercialize without restrictions
- Reasonable licensing terms

**Balancing Interests**:
- University: Maximize societal impact; revenue generation; reputation
- Company: Competitive advantage; return on investment; freedom to operate
- Researchers: Academic recognition; publications; career advancement

### IP Strategies for Sponsored Research

**Option 1: University Owns, Company Option to License** (Most Common):
- All foreground IP owned by university
- Company has first option to negotiate license (exclusive or non-exclusive)
- Option period: 60-90 days after disclosure
- If option exercised, license negotiated (can include specific terms in SRA)
- If option declined, university free to license to others

**Advantages**:
- Clear ownership
- University can ensure dissemination
- Company has opportunity to commercialize
- Enables publication without company permission

**Option 2: Joint Ownership with Commercialization Lead**:
- IP jointly owned
- One party designated as commercialization lead
- Lead handles licensing negotiations
- Revenue shared according to contributions
- Publication by mutual agreement

**Advantages**:
- Reflects joint contributions
- Clear commercialization path
- Both parties have stake

**Challenges**:
- More complex
- Can complicate future licensing
- Potential for disagreement

### Patent Filing and Prosecution

**Sponsored Research Typical Practice**:
1. University evaluates invention disclosure
2. If patentable and company interested, university files provisional application
3. Company decides whether to support non-provisional filing
4. If company supports, company may cover patent costs (or pay through licensing)
5. If company declines, university decides whether to proceed independently

**Cost Sharing**:
- Patent costs $10K-$30K for US patent
- International patents much more expensive
- Company may pay patent costs in exchange for exclusive license
- Alternatively, deducted from future royalties

## 6. Building Long-Term Industry Relationships

### From Project to Partnership

**Successful Project Outcomes**:
- Results useful to company (even if not expected outcomes)
- Professional execution and communication
- Publications demonstrating quality
- On-time, on-budget delivery

**Expanding Relationships**:
- Additional projects in related areas
- Broader research program
- Strategic alliance or center
- Industry consortium membership
- Ongoing consulting or advisory roles

### Research Consortia with Industry

**Structure**:
- Multiple industry members fund shared research
- Pre-competitive topics
- Results shared among members
- Non-exclusive IP rights for members

**Benefits**:
- Diversified funding
- Reduced dependence on single company
- Broader impact potential
- Less restrictive IP and publication

**Management**:
- Formal governance with industry advisory board
- Clear IP policies
- Regular member meetings and updates
- Coordinating office or center

**Example**: A university CEA research center with 10 industry members each contributing $50K-$100K annually for shared research on topics like energy optimization, crop variety screening, and automation technologies.

### Industry Relations Best Practices

**Proactive Engagement**:
- Attend industry conferences
- Present at industry events
- Publish in trade journals
- Serve on industry committees
- Facility tours for industry visitors

**Communication**:
- Newsletter or updates to industry partners
- Annual industry day or showcase
- Highlight research results relevant to industry
- Respond promptly to inquiries

**Value Delivery**:
- Conduct high-quality, relevant research
- Disseminate findings effectively
- Train students who join industry
- Provide technical consultation
- Facilitate technology transfer

**Relationship Development**:
- Understand industry challenges and needs
- Build personal relationships with key contacts
- Long-term perspective (not transactional)
- Mutual respect and benefit

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Strategic partnerships**: Industry-sponsored research provides funding and impact pathways; choose models aligned with objectives

2. **Clear agreements essential**: Negotiate terms protecting academic freedom, publication rights, and appropriate IP arrangements

3. **Active management required**: Successful partnerships require communication, trust, and meeting commitments

4. **Balance interests**: Find win-win solutions balancing academic goals with commercial objectives

5. **Student considerations**: Ensure industry partnerships support educational missions and student career development

6. **Long-term thinking**: Build relationships over time through delivering value and professional engagement
