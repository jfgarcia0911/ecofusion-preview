# Research Program Development Glossary

**Course 507: Research Program Development**

---

## A

**Abstract**: Brief summary of research proposal or publication (typically 200-500 words) covering problem, approach, and expected outcomes.

**AFRI (Agriculture and Food Research Initiative)**: USDA's flagship competitive grants program supporting agricultural research.

**Altmetrics**: Alternative metrics measuring online attention to research (social media, news mentions, downloads) beyond traditional citations.

**APC (Article Processing Charge)**: Fee charged by open access journals for publishing articles, typically $1,500-$5,000.

**Applied Research**: Research directed toward practical application and solving specific problems, typically with 1-3 year timeline to implementation.

**ARS (Agricultural Research Service)**: USDA's internal research agency operating federal laboratories.

**AUTM (Association of University Technology Managers)**: Professional organization for technology transfer professionals.

---

## B

**Basic Research**: Fundamental research advancing scientific knowledge without specific application in mind, typically with 5-10+ year timeline.

**BCR (Benefit-Cost Ratio)**: Ratio of total benefits to total costs, used in economic impact analysis.

**Belmont Report**: Foundational document defining ethical principles for research (respect, beneficence, justice).

**Biosketches**: Brief professional biographies required in grant applications (typically 2-3 pages), summarizing education, experience, and publications.

**Broader Impacts**: NSF criterion evaluating research benefits beyond scientific advancement, including societal benefits, training, and outreach.

---

## C

**Calibration**: Process of verifying and adjusting measurement equipment against known standards to ensure accuracy.

**Co-Authorship**: Shared authorship on publications; all authors must meet authorship criteria (substantial contribution, drafting, approval, accountability).

**COI (Conflict of Interest)**: Financial or personal interest that could influence research conduct, requiring disclosure and management.

**Commercialization**: Process of bringing research discoveries to market through licensing, startups, or other mechanisms.

**Consortium**: Multi-partner research collaboration, often with multiple universities or companies funding shared research.

**Corresponding Author**: Author responsible for manuscript submission and communication with journal (often senior author).

**CRADA (Cooperative Research and Development Agreement)**: Partnership between federal labs and external organizations for collaborative research.

**Current and Pending Support**: Required documentation in grant applications listing all active and pending research funding for personnel.

---

## D

**Data Management Plan (DMP)**: Required component of grant proposals describing how data will be collected, stored, shared, and archived.

**Deliverables**: Specific outputs promised in research agreements (reports, prototypes, data, publications).

**DOE (Department of Energy)**: Federal agency funding energy-related research, including bioenergy from agriculture.

**DOI (Digital Object Identifier)**: Unique persistent identifier for publications and datasets (e.g., 10.1000/xyz123).

---

## E

**EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)**: Federal agency regulating environmental aspects; offers research grants through STAR program.

**Exclusive License**: Intellectual property license granted to single licensee, preventing licensor from licensing to others.

**Export Control**: Regulations restricting sharing of certain technologies internationally, requiring compliance in research with foreign nationals.

---

## F

**F&A (Facilities and Administrative Costs)**: Indirect costs charged on grants to cover institutional overhead (also called IDC); typically 25-60% of direct costs.

**Fabrication**: Making up data or results; form of research misconduct.

**FAIR Principles**: Data should be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.

**Falsification**: Manipulating research data or methods to misrepresent results; form of research misconduct.

**Field of Use**: Limitation in license agreement restricting applications (e.g., exclusive for food crops, non-exclusive for ornamentals).

**First Author**: Primary author who led research and writing (typically student or postdoc); receives primary credit.

**FTO (Freedom to Operate)**: Analysis ensuring practicing an invention won't infringe others' patents.

**FWCI (Field-Weighted Citation Impact)**: Citation metric normalized to field average; FWCI > 1.0 means above average citations.

---

## G

**Gantt Chart**: Visual timeline showing project activities, milestones, and dependencies over time.

**Grant**: Financial award for research, typically competitive and based on peer review.

**Green Open Access**: Self-archiving published articles in institutional repositories, often after embargo period.

**Gold Open Access**: Publishing in fully open access journals where all articles are immediately freely available.

---

## H

**h-index**: Metric where researcher has h papers with at least h citations each (e.g., h=20 means 20 papers with 20+ citations).

**Horizon**: Time category for research projects (Horizon 1: 0-2 years; Horizon 2: 2-5 years; Horizon 3: 5-10+ years).

**Hypothesis**: Testable prediction about research outcomes, typically in format "If [condition], then [outcome], because [mechanism]."

---

## I

**IBC (Institutional Biosafety Committee)**: Committee overseeing research with biohazardous materials or recombinant DNA.

**IACUC (Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee)**: Committee overseeing ethical treatment of animals in research.

**ICMJE (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors)**: Organization establishing authorship guidelines widely adopted beyond medical research.

**IDC (Indirect Costs)**: See F&A (Facilities and Administrative Costs).

**IDP (Individual Development Plan)**: Personalized plan for researcher development, including career goals, skills assessment, and action plan.

**IF (Impact Factor)**: Journal metric; average citations per paper over 2-year period (e.g., IF = 5.2 means average of 5.2 citations per paper).

**Intellectual Merit**: NSF criterion evaluating scientific quality, innovation, and qualifications of research team.

**IP (Intellectual Property)**: Creations of mind protectable by law, including patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secrets.

**IRB (Institutional Review Board)**: Committee reviewing research involving human subjects to ensure ethical conduct.

---

## K

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**: Measurable value demonstrating effectiveness in achieving objectives.

**KRI (Key Risk Indicator)**: Metric providing early warning of increasing risk levels.

---

## L

**Last Author**: Senior author providing oversight and resources (typically PI); often corresponding author.

**License**: Legal agreement granting rights to use intellectual property.

**Logic Model**: Visual representation showing progression from inputs through activities, outputs, outcomes to impacts.

---

## M

**Milestone**: Specific, measurable achievement marking progress toward goals, often tied to payments in research agreements.

**Mission Statement**: Statement of research program's purpose, activities, and primary beneficiaries.

**MOU (Memorandum of Understanding)**: Non-binding agreement expressing intent to collaborate; precedes formal binding agreements.

**MRL (Market Readiness Level)**: Assessment scale (1-9) for commercial viability of technology.

**MTA (Material Transfer Agreement)**: Agreement governing transfer of research materials between institutions.

---

## N

**NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**: Legal contract protecting confidential information; required before sharing sensitive research details.

**NIFA (National Institute of Food and Agriculture)**: USDA agency administering competitive grant programs for agricultural research.

**NIH (National Institutes of Health)**: Federal agency funding biomedical and health research.

**Non-Exclusive License**: IP license allowing multiple licensees; licensor retains rights to license others.

**NSF (National Science Foundation)**: Federal agency funding fundamental research across science and engineering.

---

## O

**Open Access (OA)**: Free, immediate online availability of research articles to all readers.

**ORCID**: Persistent digital identifier for researchers (e.g., 0000-0001-2345-6789) distinguishing individuals and linking research outputs.

**Overhead**: See F&A (Facilities and Administrative Costs).

---

## P

**Patent**: Legal protection for inventions, granting exclusive rights for 20 years from filing date.

**Peer Review**: Evaluation of research by independent experts in the field, used for publications and grant proposals.

**PI (Principal Investigator)**: Lead researcher responsible for research project or grant; provides overall direction.

**Plagiarism**: Using others' ideas, processes, results, or words without proper credit; form of research misconduct.

**Plant Patent**: Patent protecting new plant varieties reproduced asexually; 20-year duration.

**Portfolio**: Collection of research projects managed strategically to balance risk, timeline, and objectives.

**Postdoc (Postdoctoral Researcher)**: PhD holder conducting research training (typically 2-5 years) before independent position.

**PVP (Plant Variety Protection)**: Protection similar to patent but for sexually reproduced plant varieties; administered by USDA.

---

## Q

**Q1 Journal**: Journal in top 25% (first quartile) by impact factor or citations within field; indicates high-quality publication venue.

---

## R

**RCR (Responsible Conduct of Research)**: Ethical principles and professional standards governing research practice.

**RFP (Request for Proposals)**: Formal solicitation by funding agency specifying research topics and application requirements.

**Royalty**: Payment to licensor based on licensee's sales or revenues, typically 2-10% for agricultural technologies.

**ROI (Return on Investment)**: Measure of benefit relative to cost; ROI = (Benefits - Investment) / Investment × 100%.

---

## S

**SAB (Scientific Advisory Board)**: Group of experts providing strategic guidance to research programs or companies.

**SARE (Sustainable Agriculture Research and Education)**: USDA program supporting sustainable agriculture research, education, and outreach.

**SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact)**: Feedback framework describing situation, specific behavior, and its impact.

**SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)**: Federal program providing grants to small businesses for research and commercialization.

**SCRI (Specialty Crop Research Initiative)**: USDA program funding research benefiting specialty crops (fruits, vegetables, nuts, etc.).

**SDG (Sustainable Development Goals)**: UN framework of 17 global goals; research impacts often aligned with specific SDGs.

**SDS (Safety Data Sheet)**: Document providing hazard information for chemicals; required for all chemicals in research labs.

**SMART Objectives**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound objectives.

**SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)**: Written instructions for routine research procedures ensuring consistency.

**SOW (Statement of Work)**: Document detailing specific work to be performed, deliverables, and timeline in research agreements.

**SRA (Sponsored Research Agreement)**: Contract between university and external sponsor defining research to be performed and terms.

**Stage-Gate Process**: Project management approach with stages of work separated by review gates determining whether to proceed.

**STTR (Small Business Technology Transfer)**: Federal program similar to SBIR but requiring university partnership (≥30%).

**Subaward**: Funding provided to collaborating institution under prime award; subrecipient performs substantive work.

---

## T

**Tech Transfer (Technology Transfer)**: Process of moving research discoveries from laboratory to practical application through licensing or other mechanisms.

**Thought Leadership**: Recognition as expert whose ideas influence thinking and practice in field.

**Trade Secret**: Confidential information providing competitive advantage; protected indefinitely if secrecy maintained.

**Translational Research**: Research bridging basic science to practical applications; "Pasteur's Quadrant" with both fundamental understanding and practical relevance.

**TRL (Technology Readiness Level)**: Scale (1-9) assessing technology maturity from basic research (TRL 1-3) to deployment (TRL 9).

**TTO (Technology Transfer Office)**: University office managing intellectual property, patents, and technology licensing.

---

## U

**USDA (United States Department of Agriculture)**: Federal department overseeing agriculture policy and primary funder of agricultural research.

**Utility Patent**: Most common patent type protecting new and useful inventions; 20-year duration from filing.

---

## V

**Valley of Death**: Gap between early research results (TRL 3-4) and commercial readiness (TRL 7-8) where funding is scarce.

**Vision Statement**: Aspirational description of what research program aims to achieve long-term (5-10 years).

---

## W

**White Space**: Research questions or areas not adequately addressed by existing programs; opportunities for differentiation.

---

## Numbers and Acronyms

**3-2-1 Rule (Data Backup)**: Best practice of maintaining 3 copies of data on 2 different media types with 1 copy offsite.

**3Rs (Animal Research)**: Replacement, Reduction, Refinement - principles for ethical animal research.

---

## Additional Abbreviations

**CEA**: Controlled Environment Agriculture
**CGIAR**: Consultative Group on International Agricultural Research
**CV**: Curriculum Vitae (academic resume)
**FTE**: Full-Time Equivalent (1.0 FTE = 100% time commitment)
**GDPR**: General Data Protection Regulation (European data privacy law)
**GHS**: Globally Harmonized System (chemical hazard classification)
**i10-index**: Number of publications with ≥10 citations
**LED**: Light-Emitting Diode
**NEPA**: National Environmental Policy Act
**OSHA**: Occupational Safety and Health Administration
**PPE**: Personal Protective Equipment
**UBMTA**: Uniform Biological Material Transfer Agreement
**VC**: Venture Capital

---

## Key Metrics and Standards

**Typical Grant Success Rates**:
- USDA AFRI: 15-25%
- NSF: 15-25%
- Industry: 30-50%

**Typical Royalty Rates** (agriculture):
- 2-10% of net sales

**Typical Publication Timeline**:
- Submission to first decision: 2-8 weeks
- Revision and resubmission: 2-3 months
- Acceptance to publication: 1-4 months

**Typical Data Retention**:
- 3-7 years after publication (varies by funder)

**Patent Duration**:
- 20 years from filing date (utility and plant patents)

---

## Notes on Usage

- Terminology may vary by institution, discipline, and country
- Some terms have multiple meanings depending on context
- Consult institutional policies for specific definitions at your organization
- Regulations and standards evolve; verify current requirements
