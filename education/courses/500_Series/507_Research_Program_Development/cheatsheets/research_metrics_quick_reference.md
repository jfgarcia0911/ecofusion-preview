# Research Metrics Quick Reference

**Course 507: Research Program Development**

## Publication and Citation Metrics

### Key Metrics Defined

**Publications Count**
- Total number of peer-reviewed papers
- Simple but doesn't account for quality

**Journal Impact Factor (IF)**
- Average citations per paper in journal over 2 years
- Field-specific; varies widely (0.5 to 60+)
- Agricultural journals: typically 1-7; top journals 7-15+

**h-index**
- Researcher has h papers with at least h citations each
- Example: h=20 means 20 papers with 20+ citations
- Cumulative; increases throughout career

**i10-index**
- Number of publications with at least 10 citations
- Simpler alternative to h-index

**Citations per Paper**
- Total citations / number of papers
- Accounts for productivity differences

**Field-Weighted Citation Impact (FWCI)**
- Citations relative to field average
- FWCI = 1.0 is average; >1.0 above average

## Research Program Metrics

### Input Metrics
- Total research funding per year
- Funding per FTE researcher
- Diversity of funding sources
- Equipment and facility value

### Activity Metrics
- Active projects
- Experiments/trials conducted
- Samples analyzed
- Collaborative partnerships
- Student credit hours taught

### Output Metrics
- **Publications**: Papers/year, average IF
- **Presentations**: Conferences, invited talks
- **Grants**: Proposals submitted, success rate
- **IP**: Patents filed, disclosures
- **People**: Students graduated, postdocs trained
- **Outreach**: Extension publications, media

### Outcome Metrics
- **Citations**: H-index, total citations, citations/paper
- **Funding**: Dollars awarded, ROI
- **Recognition**: Awards, honors, invitations
- **Adoption**: Technologies licensed, practices adopted
- **Influence**: Committee service, advisory roles

### Impact Metrics
- **Economic**: GDP impact, jobs created, cost savings
- **Social**: Food security, livelihoods, health
- **Environmental**: Resource savings, emissions reduced
- **Policy**: Regulations influenced, standards set
- **Capacity**: Infrastructure built, institutions strengthened

## Target Metrics (Research Program, $3M-$5M annual budget)

### Annual Targets
- **Publications**: 10-15 peer-reviewed papers
  - 40-50% in Q1 journals (top quartile)
  - Average IF: 4-6
- **Presentations**: 15-25 total (conference, invited)
- **Grant Submissions**: 8-12 proposals
- **Grant Success Rate**: 25-35%
- **New Funding**: $2M-$4M awarded
- **Students Trained**: 8-12 active; 2-4 graduated/year
- **IP**: 2-4 invention disclosures; 1-2 patents filed
- **Industry Partnerships**: 5-10 active projects
- **Outreach**: 3-5 extension publications; 2-3 media features

### Multi-Year Targets (5 years)
- **Total Publications**: 50-75 papers
- **Average Citations/Paper**: 15-25
- **H-index Growth**: +8-12 points
- **Total Funding**: $15M-$25M over 5 years
- **Students**: 15-20 graduated
- **Technology Transfers**: 2-5 licenses or startups
- **Industry Partners**: $3M-$5M in sponsored research
- **Recognition**: 3-5 major awards or honors

## Portfolio Balance Metrics

### Research Horizon Distribution
- **Horizon 1 (0-2 years)**: 30-40% of portfolio
- **Horizon 2 (2-5 years)**: 40-50% of portfolio
- **Horizon 3 (5+ years)**: 20-30% of portfolio

### Basic vs. Applied Research
- **Basic Research**: 20-40%
- **Translational**: 40-50%
- **Applied/Development**: 20-40%

### Risk Profile
- **Low-Medium Risk**: 60% of portfolio
- **Medium-High Risk**: 30% of portfolio
- **High Risk/High Reward**: 10% of portfolio

### Funding Diversification
- **Federal Grants**: 40-60%
- **Industry**: 20-30%
- **State/Foundations**: 10-20%
- **Other**: 5-10%

## Economic Impact Calculations

### Return on Investment (ROI)
```
ROI = (Economic Benefits - Research Investment) / Research Investment × 100%

Example:
Research: $5M investment
Benefits: $50M economic impact
ROI = ($50M - $5M) / $5M = 900%
```

### Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)
```
BCR = Total Benefits / Total Costs

Example:
BCR = $50M / $5M = 10:1
```

### Impact Per Dollar Invested
```
$Value Created / $Research Invested

Example:
$50M / $5M = $10 per $1 invested
```

## Technology Transfer Metrics

### TTO Performance
- **Invention Disclosures**: 10-15/year (for $5M program)
- **Patents Filed**: 3-5/year
- **Licenses Executed**: 2-4/year
- **Startups Formed**: 0-2/year
- **Licensing Revenue**: $50K-$500K+/year

### Technology Readiness Level (TRL)
- **TRL 1-3**: Basic research, proof of concept
- **TRL 4-6**: Technology development, prototyping
- **TRL 7-9**: Demonstration, production, deployment

**Target**: Move technologies from TRL 3-4 to TRL 6-7

## Using Metrics Strategically

### For Grant Applications
- Demonstrate productivity and impact
- Show trajectory and growth
- Benchmark against peers
- Highlight best metrics

### For Promotion/Tenure
- Document cumulative achievements
- Show sustained productivity
- Demonstrate impact beyond publications
- Compare to standards in field

### For Program Management
- Track progress toward goals
- Identify trends and issues
- Allocate resources strategically
- Communicate value to stakeholders

### Cautions
- Metrics are tools, not goals in themselves
- Quality matters more than quantity
- Context matters (field, career stage, resources)
- No single metric tells full story
- Beware of gaming metrics at expense of real impact

---

**Remember**: Use metrics to inform decisions and demonstrate value, but don't let metrics alone drive research direction. The ultimate goal is impactful research that advances knowledge and benefits society.
