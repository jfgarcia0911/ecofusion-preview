# Quiz 4: Grant Writing Excellence

**Course**: 507 Research Program Development
**Module**: 4 - Grant Writing Excellence
**Total Questions**: 10
**Passing Score**: 80%

---

## Questions

### Question 1
What is the MOST important principle in grant proposal writing?

A) Using complex technical jargon
B) Making the proposal as long as possible
C) Addressing the funder's priorities and review criteria directly
D) Focusing only on your research interests

**Correct Answer**: C

---

### Question 2
In a typical NIH or USDA grant proposal, which section is most critical for reviewers?

A) Budget justification
B) Significance and Innovation - explaining why the research matters and what's novel
C) References cited
D) Facilities and equipment

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 3
What is the recommended approach for developing a competitive grant budget?

A) Maximize all line items to get the most funding
B) Minimize costs to appear efficient
C) Justify each cost element clearly showing necessity for project success
D) Copy budgets from other proposals

**Correct Answer**: C

---

### Question 4
Which writing technique is most effective for grant proposal narratives?

A) Passive voice and complex sentences
B) Active voice with clear, concise writing organized around reviewer questions
C) Stream of consciousness style
D) Highly technical writing for all audiences

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 5
What is a "broader impacts" section in NSF proposals?

A) Description of laboratory equipment
B) Explanation of how research will benefit society beyond scientific knowledge
C) Budget details
D) Literature review

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 6
When should you start developing a grant proposal for a deadline?

A) The day before it's due
B) One week before deadline
C) 6-12 weeks before deadline for major proposals
D) Proposals cannot be started early

**Correct Answer**: C

---

### Question 7
What is the purpose of preliminary data in a research proposal?

A) To show the research is already complete
B) To demonstrate feasibility and researcher capability
C) To make the proposal longer
D) Preliminary data is never needed

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 8
Which common error most frequently leads to proposal rejection?

A) Too much preliminary data
B) Not directly addressing review criteria and funder priorities
C) Using too many references
D) Budget is too detailed

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 9
What is the recommended strategy if your proposal receives a "good but not funded" decision?

A) Never submit to that funder again
B) Revise based on reviewer feedback and resubmit
C) Submit identical proposal to different funder without changes
D) Give up on the research

**Correct Answer**: B

---

### Question 10
In collaborative proposals with multiple institutions, who typically serves as the lead PI?

A) The newest researcher
B) The person with least experience
C) The researcher with strongest track record and primary project responsibility
D) Always the largest institution

**Correct Answer**: C

---

## Answer Key

1. C - Addressing the funder's priorities and review criteria directly
2. B - Significance and Innovation - explaining why the research matters and what's novel
3. C - Justify each cost element clearly showing necessity for project success
4. B - Active voice with clear, concise writing organized around reviewer questions
5. B - Explanation of how research will benefit society beyond scientific knowledge
6. C - 6-12 weeks before deadline for major proposals
7. B - To demonstrate feasibility and researcher capability
8. B - Not directly addressing review criteria and funder priorities
9. B - Revise based on reviewer feedback and resubmit
10. C - The researcher with strongest track record and primary project responsibility
