# CEA Systems Architecture Glossary

## A

**ADM (Architecture Development Method)**
The core of TOGAF, providing a step-by-step approach to developing enterprise architecture.

**ADR (Architecture Decision Record)**
A document that captures an important architectural decision made along with its context and consequences.

**API (Application Programming Interface)**
A set of definitions and protocols for building and integrating application software.

**API Gateway**
A server that acts as an API front-end, receiving API requests, enforcing throttling and security policies, passing requests to the back-end service, and passing the response back to the requester.

**Application Architecture**
One of the four EA domains, defining the blueprint for individual application systems and their interactions.

**ARB (Architecture Review Board)**
A governance body that reviews and approves architecture proposals and ensures alignment with enterprise standards.

**ArchiMate**
An open and independent enterprise architecture modeling language supported by The Open Group.

**Auto-Scaling**
Automatic adjustment of computational resources based on actual demand.

---

## B

**Batch Processing**
Processing large volumes of data where a group of transactions is collected over a period before processing.

**Blue-Green Deployment**
A deployment strategy that reduces downtime by running two identical production environments, only one of which serves live traffic at any time.

**Business Architecture**
One of the four EA domains, defining the business strategy, governance, organization, and key business processes.

**Business Continuity**
The capability of an organization to continue delivery of products or services at acceptable predefined levels following a disruptive incident.

---

## C

**C4 Model**
A lean graphical notation technique for modeling software architecture through Context, Containers, Components, and Code.

**Cache**
Hardware or software component that stores data so future requests for that data can be served faster.

**Canary Deployment**
A deployment strategy where a new version is gradually rolled out to a small subset of users before full deployment.

**CDN (Content Delivery Network)**
A geographically distributed network of proxy servers and their data centers, providing high availability and performance.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Agricultural technology where environmental parameters are controlled for optimal plant growth (e.g., vertical farms, greenhouses).

**Cloud-Native**
An approach to building and running applications that exploits the advantages of cloud computing delivery model.

**Container**
A standard unit of software that packages code and dependencies so the application runs quickly and reliably across computing environments.

---

## D

**Data Architecture**
One of the four EA domains, describing the structure of an organization's logical and physical data assets.

**Data Governance**
The overall management of the availability, usability, integrity, and security of data in an organization.

**Data Lake**
A centralized repository that allows storage of all structured and unstructured data at any scale.

**Data Warehouse**
A system used for reporting and data analysis, serving as a central repository of integrated data from one or more disparate sources.

**Digital Twin**
A virtual representation of a physical object or system, updated from real-time data to mirror its physical counterpart.

**Disaster Recovery (DR)**
The process of maintaining or reestablishing vital infrastructure and systems following a natural or human-induced disaster.

**DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology)**
A consensus of replicated, shared, and synchronized digital data geographically spread across multiple sites (includes blockchain).

---

## E

**Edge Computing**
A distributed computing paradigm that brings computation and data storage closer to the location where it is needed.

**ELT (Extract, Load, Transform)**
A data pipeline pattern where data is extracted from sources, loaded into a target system, then transformed.

**Enterprise Architecture (EA)**
The organizing logic for business processes and IT infrastructure, reflecting integration and standardization requirements.

**ESB (Enterprise Service Bus)**
An architecture pattern for integrating various applications together over a bus-like infrastructure.

**ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)**
A data pipeline pattern where data is extracted from sources, transformed, then loaded into a target system.

**Event-Driven Architecture**
A software architecture paradigm promoting the production, detection, consumption of, and reaction to events.

---

## F

**Fact Table**
In a data warehouse, the central table in a star schema containing the measurements, metrics, or facts of a business process.

**Failover**
Switching to a redundant or standby system upon the failure or abnormal termination of the previously active system.

**FinOps**
The practice of bringing financial accountability to the variable spend model of cloud computing.

---

## G

**Gap Analysis**
The comparison of actual performance with potential or desired performance in enterprise architecture.

---

## H

**High Availability (HA)**
Systems designed to operate continuously without failure for a long period of time (typically 99.9%+ uptime).

**Horizontal Scaling (Scale Out)**
Adding more machines to a pool of resources to handle increased load.

**Hybrid Cloud**
A computing environment that combines on-premises infrastructure with public cloud services.

**Hypertable**
In TimescaleDB, an abstraction of a single continuous table across all space and time intervals, partitioned into chunks.

---

## I

**IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service)**
A cloud computing model providing virtualized computing resources over the internet.

**IAM (Identity and Access Management)**
Framework of policies and technologies ensuring that the right users have appropriate access to resources.

**Integration Architecture**
The design and strategy for connecting disparate systems and enabling them to work together.

**IoT (Internet of Things)**
The network of physical devices embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity enabling them to exchange data.

**IT/OT Convergence**
The integration of Information Technology (business systems) with Operational Technology (industrial control systems).

---

## K

**Kubernetes (K8s)**
An open-source container orchestration platform for automating deployment, scaling, and management of containerized applications.

---

## L

**Lambda Architecture**
A data-processing architecture designed to handle massive quantities of data by combining batch and stream-processing methods.

**Load Balancer**
A device that distributes network or application traffic across a cluster of servers.

---

## M

**Master Data Management (MDM)**
The discipline of defining and maintaining consistent and accurate representations of an organization's core business entities.

**MES (Manufacturing Execution System)**
Control system for managing and monitoring work-in-process on a factory floor.

**Microservices**
An architectural style that structures an application as a collection of loosely coupled, independently deployable services.

**MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport)**
A lightweight messaging protocol for small sensors and mobile devices, optimized for high-latency or unreliable networks.

**MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)**
The predicted elapsed time between inherent failures of a system during operation.

**MTTR (Mean Time To Repair)**
The average time required to repair a failed component or device.

**Multi-Tenancy**
A software architecture where a single instance serves multiple customers (tenants), each with isolated data.

---

## O

**OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)**
An approach to answer multi-dimensional analytical queries swiftly, used for data mining, analytics, and business intelligence.

**OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)**
A class of systems that facilitate and manage transaction-oriented applications, typically for data entry and retrieval.

**OPC-UA (OPC Unified Architecture)**
A machine-to-machine communication protocol for industrial automation, enabling data exchange from sensors to cloud applications.

**OT (Operational Technology)**
Hardware and software that detects or causes change through direct monitoring and control of physical devices and processes.

---

## P

**PaaS (Platform as a Service)**
A cloud computing model providing a platform allowing customers to develop, run, and manage applications without infrastructure complexity.

**PLC (Programmable Logic Controller)**
An industrial digital computer adapted for control of manufacturing processes.

**Pub/Sub (Publish-Subscribe)**
A messaging pattern where senders (publishers) categorize messages into classes without knowledge of subscribers.

---

## R

**RBAC (Role-Based Access Control)**
An approach to restricting system access to authorized users based on their role within an organization.

**Refactoring**
The process of restructuring existing code without changing its external behavior, improving nonfunctional attributes.

**REST (Representational State Transfer)**
An architectural style for distributed hypermedia systems, commonly used for web APIs.

**RTO (Recovery Time Objective)**
The targeted duration of time within which a business process must be restored after a disaster.

**RPO (Recovery Point Objective)**
The maximum targeted period in which data might be lost from an IT service due to a major incident.

---

## S

**SaaS (Software as a Service)**
A software licensing and delivery model where software is centrally hosted and licensed on a subscription basis.

**SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)**
A control system architecture comprising computers, networked data communications, and graphical user interfaces for high-level supervision of machines and processes.

**Serverless Computing**
A cloud computing execution model where the cloud provider dynamically manages server allocation and provisioning.

**Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)**
An architectural pattern where software components provide services to other components via a communications protocol over a network.

**Sharding**
A database partitioning pattern that separates very large databases into smaller, faster, more easily managed parts called shards.

**SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)**
An approach to security management combining security information management and security event management functions.

**SLA (Service Level Agreement)**
A commitment between a service provider and a client defining expected service level, availability, and responsibilities.

**SOC 2 (Service Organization Control 2)**
An auditing procedure ensuring service providers securely manage data to protect client interests.

**Stakeholder**
An individual, team, or organization with interests in or concerns relative to a system.

**Star Schema**
A database schema with a central fact table and a set of dimension tables, resembling a star shape.

**Stream Processing**
A computational model for processing data records continuously, one record at a time or in micro-batches.

**Strangler Pattern**
An incremental modernization approach where legacy system functionality is gradually replaced by new functionality.

---

## T

**TCO (Total Cost of Ownership)**
A financial estimate designed to help buyers and owners determine direct and indirect costs of a product or system.

**Technology Architecture**
One of the four EA domains, describing the logical software and hardware capabilities required to support deployment of services.

**Time-Series Database**
A database optimized for time-stamped or time-series data (measurements indexed by time).

**TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework)**
An enterprise architecture methodology providing an approach for designing, planning, implementing, and governing enterprise information architecture.

**Twelve-Factor App**
A methodology for building software-as-a-service applications with modern best practices.

---

## V

**Vertical Scaling (Scale Up)**
Adding more power (CPU, RAM) to an existing machine to handle increased load.

**VPC (Virtual Private Cloud)**
An on-demand configurable pool of shared computing resources allocated within a public cloud environment, providing isolation.

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)**
The difference between the amount of moisture in the air and how much moisture the air can hold when saturated, critical in CEA.

---

## W

**WebSocket**
A communications protocol providing full-duplex communication channels over a single TCP connection.

---

## Z

**Zachman Framework**
An enterprise architecture framework providing a formal and structured way of viewing and defining an enterprise.

**Zero-Trust Architecture**
A security concept centered on the belief that organizations should not automatically trust anything inside or outside its perimeters.

---

## CEA-Specific Terms

**Growing Batch**
A cohort of plants seeded together and grown under the same conditions, tracked as a unit through harvest.

**Growing Recipe**
A defined set of environmental parameters (temperature, humidity, light, nutrients) for optimal crop production.

**Yield per Square Foot**
A key CEA metric measuring production efficiency (typically kg harvested per sq ft of growing area).

**Photoperiod**
The duration of light exposure per day, critical for plant growth cycles.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**
The amount of photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) reaching plant canopy, measured in μmol/m²/s.

**DLI (Daily Light Integral)**
The total amount of photosynthetically active radiation received per day, critical for CEA lighting design.

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)**
A measure of nutrient concentration in hydroponic solutions.
