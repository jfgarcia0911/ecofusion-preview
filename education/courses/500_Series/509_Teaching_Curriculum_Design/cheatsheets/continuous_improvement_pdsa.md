# Continuous Improvement & PDSA Cycle Guide

## CEA-509 Cheatsheet #4

---

## PDSA Cycle Overview

**Plan-Do-Study-Act**
Systematic approach to testing and implementing improvements

```
    PLAN
     ↓
    DO ←→ Iterate
     ↓     if needed
   STUDY
     ↓
    ACT
     ↓
  (Return to PLAN for next cycle)
```

---

## PLAN Phase

**Purpose:** Identify problem and develop solution

### Steps:
1. **Identify the problem/opportunity**
   - What's not working?
   - What could be better?
   - Data supporting need for change?

2. **Analyze root causes**
   - Why is this happening?
   - 5 Whys technique
   - Fishbone diagram

3. **Develop solution hypothesis**
   - What might fix it?
   - Theory of change
   - Evidence from research/practice

4. **Set measurable goals**
   - What will success look like?
   - How will we measure it?
   - Specific, quantifiable targets

5. **Create action plan**
   - What steps?
   - Who responsible?
   - What resources?
   - Timeline

### CEA Example:
**Problem:** Only 55% of students can interpret VPD charts
**Root Cause:** Concept too abstract; formula-first approach
**Solution:** Start with analogy (sweating), use visual chart before formula
**Goal:** 80% correctly interpret VPD chart
**Plan:** Redesign lesson; pilot with next cohort (15 students)

---

## DO Phase

**Purpose:** Implement on small scale

### Steps:
1. **Start small (pilot)**
   - One class, not all
   - One module, not whole course
   - Controlled test

2. **Document everything**
   - What actually happened?
   - Deviations from plan?
   - Unexpected observations?

3. **Collect data**
   - Quantitative (scores, time, numbers)
   - Qualitative (feedback, observations)
   - Process and outcome data

### CEA Example:
- Taught revised VPD lesson to one section (15 students)
- Students practiced with visual chart
- Took assessment
- Collected: test scores, time, student feedback, observations

---

## STUDY Phase

**Purpose:** Analyze results

### Steps:
1. **Analyze data**
   - Compare to predictions
   - What worked? What didn't?
   - Expected vs. actual results

2. **Look for patterns**
   - Consistent findings?
   - Outliers?
   - Unexpected outcomes?

3. **Identify learning**
   - What did we learn?
   - Why did it work/not work?
   - What surprised us?

### CEA Example:
**Results:**
- 87% correctly interpret chart (vs. 55% before)
- Exceeded 80% goal
- Student feedback: "Much clearer!" "Sweating analogy helped"
- Time: 10 min longer but worthwhile
- Observation: Still struggle with calculating VPD from temp/RH

---

## ACT Phase

**Purpose:** Decide next steps

### Three Options:

**1. ADOPT**
- It worked! Implement fully
- Standardize the change
- Document in materials
- Train others
- Monitor sustainability

**2. ADAPT**
- Partial success; needs adjustment
- Modify and test again
- Refine based on learning

**3. ABANDON**
- Didn't work
- Try different approach
- Document learning
- Start new PDSA

### CEA Example:
**Decision:** ADOPT (it worked!)
- Keep analogy and chart approach
- Implement in all sections
- Update lesson plan
- **NEXT PDSA:** Improve calculation instruction (identified gap)

## Continuous Improvement Principles

### 1. KAIZEN
**Small, incremental improvements**
- Better to make many small improvements than wait for perfect solution
- Continuous, never-ending
- Everyone participates

### 2. DATA-DRIVEN
**Decisions based on evidence**
- Collect data systematically
- Analyze objectively
- Let data guide decisions

### 3. SYSTEMS THINKING
**See the whole picture**
- How do parts interact?
- Unintended consequences?
- Ripple effects

### 4. STUDENT-CENTERED
**Focus on learning outcomes**
- What's best for students?
- Improve their success
- Listen to student voice

## Quick Improvement Tools

### 5 WHYS
Identify root cause by asking "Why?" five times

**Example:**
1. Why are students failing lab practicals? → Poor technique
2. Why poor technique? → Didn't practice enough
3. Why didn't practice? → Only one lab session
4. Why only one? → Limited equipment
5. Why limited? → Budget constraints

**Root Cause:** Budget for equipment
**Solution:** Seek donations, build low-cost alternatives, rotate stations

---

### FISHBONE DIAGRAM
Organize potential causes by category

```
Methods    Materials    Equipment
    \         |          /
     \        |         /
      ----PROBLEM-----
     /        |         \
    /         |          \
People    Environment   Management
```

## Program Review Cycle

**Every 3-5 Years: Comprehensive Review**

### Components:
1. **Curriculum** - Current? Aligned?
2. **Instruction** - Effective methods?
3. **Assessment** - Valid? Reliable?
4. **Resources** - Adequate facilities/equipment?
5. **Outcomes** - Students succeeding?
6. **Impact** - Meeting industry needs?

### Process:
- Form review committee
- Collect data (6-12 months)
- Analyze findings
- Develop recommendations
- Create action plan
- Implement improvements

## Common Improvement Mistakes

**MISTAKE:** Changing too much at once
**FIX:** One variable at a time; small tests

**MISTAKE:** No data collection
**FIX:** Plan measurement before implementing

**MISTAKE:** Implementing without testing
**FIX:** Always pilot first

**MISTAKE:** Ignoring failures
**FIX:** Learn from what doesn't work

**MISTAKE:** Improvements based on opinion
**FIX:** Use data to guide decisions

**MISTAKE:** No documentation
**FIX:** Record process and results

## Quick Decision Matrix

**High Impact + High Feasibility = DO NOW**
**High Impact + Low Feasibility = PLAN CAREFULLY**
**Low Impact + High Feasibility = QUICK WIN**
**Low Impact + Low Feasibility = DON'T DO**
