# Glossary of Educational Terms

## CEA-509: Teaching & Curriculum Design for CEA

**Comprehensive Reference Guide**

---

## A

**ABCD Format:** Framework for writing learning objectives including Audience, Behavior, Condition, and Degree.

**Academic Integrity:** Ethical principles governing honest scholarly work; avoiding plagiarism, cheating, and other forms of dishonesty.

**Accessibility:** Design of learning experiences that can be accessed and used by all learners, including those with disabilities.

**Accommodation:** Modification or support provided to enable students with disabilities to participate fully in learning.

**Accreditation:** Official recognition by an authorizing body that an educational program meets established quality standards.

**ADDIE Model:** Systematic instructional design framework consisting of Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation phases.

**Advisory Committee:** Group of industry professionals and stakeholders who provide guidance and feedback on educational programs.

**Affective Domain:** Category of learning outcomes related to attitudes, values, emotions, and dispositions (Krathwohl's Taxonomy).

**Alignment:** Consistency and coherence among learning objectives, assessments, and instructional activities.

**Andragogy:** Theory and practice of adult education (contrast with pedagogy for children).

**Analytic Rubric:** Scoring guide that provides separate scores for multiple criteria of performance.

**Assessment:** Process of gathering evidence of student learning; measuring achievement of objectives.

**Asynchronous Learning:** Instruction where students engage with materials on their own schedule, not in real-time.

**Authentic Assessment:** Evaluation tasks that mirror real-world professional work and contexts.

---

## B

**Backward Design:** Curriculum planning approach that starts with desired outcomes, then determines assessment, then plans instruction (Understanding by Design).

**Benchmarking:** Comparing program performance to similar programs or established standards to identify improvement opportunities.

**Blended Learning:** See Hybrid Learning.

**Bloom's Taxonomy:** Classification of cognitive learning objectives into hierarchical levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.

**Blueprint (Test):** Specification table showing content coverage and cognitive levels for an examination.

---

## C

**Capstone:** Culminating project or experience that integrates learning from an entire program.

**Case-Based Learning:** Instructional method using realistic scenarios or cases for analysis and problem-solving.

**Certificate Program:** Educational program resulting in credential based on completion of coursework (distinct from certification).

**Certification:** Credential awarded based on demonstration of competence through assessment (distinct from certificate).

**Coaching:** One-on-one support and guidance to improve performance.

**Cognitive Domain:** Category of learning outcomes related to knowledge and intellectual skills (Bloom's Taxonomy).

**Cognitive Load Theory:** Principle that working memory is limited; instructional design should minimize extraneous load and optimize germane load.

**Collaborative Learning:** Instructional approach where students work together to achieve learning goals.

**Competency:** Specific knowledge, skill, or ability that can be demonstrated and measured.

**Competency-Based Education (CBE):** Approach where learning is organized around demonstrable competencies with flexible pacing.

**Concept Map:** Visual representation showing relationships among concepts and ideas.

**Constructivism:** Learning theory positing that learners actively construct knowledge through experience and reflection.

**Content Validity:** Extent to which an assessment covers the important content it's intended to measure.

**Continuous Improvement:** Ongoing, systematic process of making incremental enhancements based on data.

**Cooperative Learning:** Structured group work with individual accountability and positive interdependence.

**Course Management System:** See Learning Management System (LMS).

**Criterion-Referenced Assessment:** Evaluation comparing student performance to established standards (not to other students).

**Critical Thinking:** Higher-order thinking involving analysis, evaluation, and synthesis of information.

**Curriculum:** Planned educational experiences including content, learning activities, and assessments.

**Curriculum Map:** Visual representation showing courses, content coverage, and how competencies are addressed across a program.

---

## D

**DACUM (Developing A Curriculum):** Structured process for job/task analysis involving subject matter experts to define competencies.

**Differentiation:** Tailoring instruction to meet diverse learning needs, styles, and abilities.

**Direct Instruction:** Teacher-led presentation of content through lecture, demonstration, or modeling.

**Discussion Forum:** Online space for asynchronous text-based dialogue and exchange of ideas.

**Distance Education:** Learning that occurs when student and instructor are geographically separated.

---

## E

**E-Learning:** Learning delivered electronically, typically via internet-based platforms.

**Elaboration Theory:** Instructional sequencing principle suggesting teaching simplest version first, then elaborating with complexity.

**Enduring Understanding:** Core concepts and principles students should retain long-term (backward design).

**Essential Question:** Thought-provoking, open-ended question that frames inquiry and exploration.

**Evaluation:** Making judgments about quality or value of educational programs (distinct from assessment of student learning).

**Experiential Learning:** Learning through reflection on direct experience (Kolb's cycle).

**Extrinsic Motivation:** Drive to engage in activity due to external rewards or consequences.

---

## F

**Facilitation:** Guiding and supporting learning rather than directing through lecture.

**Feedback:** Information provided to learners about their performance to guide improvement.

**Field Experience:** Learning occurring in authentic work or community settings (internships, practicums, site visits).

**Fishbone Diagram:** Tool for organizing and analyzing potential causes of a problem by category.

**Flipped Classroom:** Model where students watch lectures at home and engage in active learning during class.

**Formative Assessment:** Ongoing evaluation during instruction to monitor learning and provide feedback.

**Framework:** Organizing structure or model that guides practice.

---

## G

**Gamification:** Application of game elements (points, badges, levels) to educational contexts.

**Goal:** Broad, general statement of educational purpose (less specific than objectives).

**Gradual Release of Responsibility:** Instructional model progressing from "I do" to "we do" to "you do."

**Grading Rubric:** See Rubric.

**Guest Speaker:** External expert invited to share industry perspectives and knowledge.

---

## H

**Hands-On Learning:** Experiential instruction involving physical manipulation and practice.

**Holistic Rubric:** Scoring guide providing single overall score based on general impression of quality.

**Hybrid Learning (Blended Learning):** Combination of in-person and online instruction.

**HyFlex:** Instructional model allowing students to choose in-person, synchronous online, or asynchronous participation.

---

## I

**Implementation:** Delivery of instruction; putting designed learning experiences into practice.

**Inquiry-Based Learning:** Approach where students learn through questioning, investigating, and discovering.

**Instructional Design:** Systematic process of creating effective and efficient learning experiences.

**Instructional Strategy:** Specific method or approach for teaching (lecture, discussion, lab, etc.).

**Internship:** Supervised work experience integrating academic learning with professional practice.

**Intrinsic Motivation:** Internal drive to engage in activity for inherent satisfaction.

**Item Analysis:** Statistical examination of test questions to assess difficulty and discrimination.

---

## J

**Job Analysis:** Systematic study of work to identify required knowledge, skills, and abilities.

**Just-in-Time Training:** Instruction provided exactly when needed, immediately applicable.

---

## K

**Kaizen:** Japanese philosophy of continuous improvement through small, incremental changes.

**Kemp Design Model:** Circular, non-linear instructional design framework with nine components.

**Key Performance Indicator (KPI):** Metric used to evaluate program success and quality.

**Kirkpatrick's Four Levels:** Evaluation framework measuring Reaction, Learning, Behavior, and Results.

**Knowledge:** Facts, information, concepts, and principles that learners should know.

**Knowles, Malcolm:** Pioneer of adult learning theory; developed principles of andragogy.

**Kolb's Learning Cycle:** Model of experiential learning: Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualization → Active Experimentation.

**Krathwohl's Affective Taxonomy:** Classification of learning outcomes related to attitudes and values.

---

## L

**Laboratory:** Controlled environment for hands-on skill development and experimentation.

**Learning Community:** Group of learners engaged together in sustained educational experience.

**Learning Management System (LMS):** Software platform for delivering, managing, and tracking online learning (Canvas, Moodle, Blackboard).

**Learning Objective:** Specific, measurable statement of what students will be able to do.

**Learning Outcome:** What students actually achieve (often used interchangeably with objective).

**Learning Progression:** Pathway from novice to expert understanding through sequential steps.

**Learning Style:** Preferred mode of receiving and processing information (visual, auditory, kinesthetic).

**Lecture:** Oral presentation of information by instructor to students.

**Lesson Plan:** Detailed outline of instructional activities, timing, and resources for a single lesson.

---

## M

**Mastery Learning:** Approach where students must demonstrate proficiency before advancing.

**Metacognition:** Awareness and understanding of one's own thinking and learning processes.

**Microlearning:** Brief, focused learning experiences (typically 3-10 minutes).

**Modality:** Mode of instruction delivery (in-person, online synchronous, online asynchronous).

**Module:** Self-contained unit of instruction focused on specific topic or set of objectives.

**Multiple Choice Question:** Assessment item with one question stem and multiple answer options.

**Multimodal:** Using multiple sensory modes (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) for learning.

---

## N

**NCCA (National Commission for Certifying Agencies):** Accrediting body for certification programs.

**Needs Assessment:** Systematic process of identifying gaps between current and desired performance.

**Norm-Referenced Assessment:** Evaluation comparing student performance to peers (contrast with criterion-referenced).

---

## O

**Objective:** See Learning Objective.

**Open Educational Resources (OER):** Freely accessible educational materials.

**Online Learning:** Education delivered via internet without face-to-face meetings.

---

## P

**Pedagogy:** Theory and practice of education (originally for children; contrast with andragogy).

**Peer Assessment:** Students evaluating each other's work using established criteria.

**Peer Review:** Structured feedback process where students review and comment on each other's work.

**Performance Assessment:** Evaluation requiring demonstration of actual skills rather than written tests.

**Performance Task:** Authentic, complex challenge requiring application of knowledge and skills.

**PDSA Cycle:** Plan-Do-Study-Act; framework for continuous improvement through systematic testing.

**Pilot Test:** Small-scale trial of instruction or assessment before full implementation.

**Portfolio:** Collection of student work demonstrating learning and growth over time.

**Practical Examination:** Assessment requiring hands-on demonstration of skills.

**Practice Analysis:** Systematic study of professional work to identify competencies required.

**Prerequisite:** Required prior knowledge or skills needed before beginning new learning.

**Problem-Based Learning (PBL):** Instructional approach organized around solving authentic, complex problems.

**Professional Development:** Ongoing learning and skill development for educators and professionals.

**Program Review:** Comprehensive, periodic evaluation of entire educational program.

**Project-Based Learning:** Extended investigation of authentic question or challenge resulting in public product.

**Psychomotor Domain:** Category of learning outcomes related to physical skills (Dave's Taxonomy).

---

## Q

**Quality Assurance:** Systematic processes ensuring educational programs meet standards.

**Quiz:** Short assessment, typically formative, covering limited content.

---

## R

**Readiness to Learn:** Principle that adults learn when they need knowledge for real situations (Knowles).

**Recertification:** Process of renewing credential through continuing education or re-examination.

**Reflection:** Thoughtful consideration of experience to derive meaning and learning.

**Reliability:** Consistency of assessment results; extent to which assessment produces similar scores under similar conditions.

**Return on Investment (ROI):** Calculation comparing benefits to costs of training or program.

**Rubric:** Scoring guide articulating criteria and describing levels of performance quality.

---

## S

**SAM (Successive Approximation Model):** Agile, iterative instructional design approach with rapid prototyping.

**Scaffolding:** Temporary support structures that help learners achieve beyond current ability; gradually removed.

**Scope:** Breadth and depth of content coverage in curriculum.

**Scope and Sequence:** Document showing what content is covered (scope) and in what order (sequence).

**Self-Assessment:** Students evaluating their own work against criteria.

**Self-Directed Learning:** Learners taking initiative and responsibility for their own learning.

**Sequence:** Order in which content and learning experiences are arranged.

**Simulation:** Representation of real system or process for learning and practice.

**SMART Criteria:** Framework for objectives: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

**Spiral Curriculum:** Returning to concepts multiple times with increasing depth and complexity.

**Stakeholder:** Individual or group with interest in or affected by educational program.

**Standard Setting:** Process of determining passing score or performance level for assessment.

**Standards:** Established criteria or benchmarks for quality or achievement.

**Subject Matter Expert (SME):** Individual with deep knowledge and expertise in specific content area.

**Summative Assessment:** Evaluation of learning at end of instruction; typically graded.

**Syllabus:** Document outlining course objectives, content, schedule, policies, and requirements.

**Synchronous Learning:** Instruction occurring in real-time with simultaneous participation.

---

## T

**Task Analysis:** Breaking down complex skill or job into component tasks and sub-tasks.

**Taxonomy:** Classification system organizing concepts hierarchically (e.g., Bloom's Taxonomy).

**Think-Pair-Share:** Active learning technique where students think individually, discuss in pairs, then share with class.

**Transfer of Learning:** Application of knowledge and skills learned in one context to different situations.

**Transformative Learning:** Deep learning that changes how individuals understand themselves and the world.

---

## U

**Understanding by Design (UbD):** Backward design framework developed by Wiggins and McTighe.

**Universal Design for Learning (UDL):** Framework providing multiple means of representation, engagement, and expression to support diverse learners.

**Unpacking Standards:** Process of analyzing standards to identify specific knowledge and skills embedded within.

---

## V

**Validity:** Extent to which assessment measures what it's intended to measure.

**VARK Model:** Classification of learning preferences: Visual, Auditory, Reading/Writing, Kinesthetic.

**Virtual Reality (VR):** Computer-generated simulation providing immersive experience.

**Virtual Lab:** Online simulation of laboratory experiments and procedures.

---

## W

**WHERETO:** Framework for planning learning experiences in backward design (Where/Why, Hook/Hold, Explore/Equip, Reflect/Rethink, Evaluate, Tailored, Organized).

**Workshop:** Intensive, short-term learning experience focused on skill development.

---

## Z

**Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD):** Range between what learner can do independently and what they can achieve with support (Vygotsky).

## Additional CEA-Specific Educational Terms

**CEA Competency:** Specific knowledge or skill required for controlled environment agriculture professional work.

**Facility-Based Learning:** Instruction occurring in actual CEA operations (greenhouses, vertical farms, aquaponics facilities).

**Hands-On CEA Labs:** Practical sessions developing psychomotor skills in plant production, system operation, and facility management.

**Industry-Aligned Curriculum:** Educational program designed to meet actual CEA employer needs and industry standards.

**Technical Agriculture Education:** Instruction focused on practical skills and science-based knowledge for agricultural careers.
