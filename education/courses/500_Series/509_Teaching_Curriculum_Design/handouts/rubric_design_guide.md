# Rubric Design Guide

## CEA-509 Reference Handout #3

---

## What is a Rubric?

A scoring guide that articulates criteria and describes levels of performance quality.

**Purpose:**
- Clarify expectations for students
- Ensure consistent, fair grading
- Provide specific feedback
- Guide improvement

---

## Types of Rubrics

### Analytic Rubric
Separate scores for each criterion

**Use when:** Need detailed feedback; diagnostic information important

**Advantages:**
- Specific feedback on each dimension
- Identifies strengths and weaknesses
- More reliable scoring

**Disadvantages:**
- Takes longer to score
- May miss gestalt/overall quality

### Holistic Rubric
Single overall score

**Use when:** Need quick scoring; overall quality judgment sufficient

**Advantages:**
- Faster to score
- Captures overall quality
- Good for summative evaluation

**Disadvantages:**
- Less specific feedback
- Harder to identify specific improvements

## Steps to Design a Rubric

### Step 1: Identify Criteria
*What dimensions of quality matter for this task?*

**Questions to Ask:**
- What makes this excellent vs. poor?
- What will I look for when grading?
- What matters most?

**CEA Example - System Design Project:**
- Technical accuracy
- Completeness
- Cost-effectiveness
- Justification/rationale
- Professional presentation

**Tip:** Typically 3-6 criteria; too many becomes unwieldy

---

### Step 2: Define Performance Levels
*How many levels of quality?*

**Common Scales:**
- 4-point: Exemplary, Proficient, Developing, Beginning
- 5-point: Excellent, Good, Satisfactory, Needs Improvement, Unsatisfactory
- 3-point: Exceeds, Meets, Does Not Meet
- 6-point: Allows more discrimination

**Recommendation:** 4-point scale (no middle "average" to default to)

---

### Step 3: Write Descriptors
*Describe performance at each level for each criterion*

**Guidelines:**
- Be specific and observable
- Avoid vague language ("good," "adequate")
- Describe work, not effort or student
- Parallel structure across levels
- Focus on quality, not quantity (unless quantity is the criterion)

**Poor Descriptor:** "Good understanding of VPD"
**Better Descriptor:** "Accurately interprets VPD charts and explains how temperature and humidity interact to affect plant transpiration"

---

### Step 4: Assign Weights (if applicable)
*Which criteria are more important?*

**Example:**
- Technical accuracy: 40%
- Completeness: 25%
- Justification: 25%
- Presentation: 10%

---

### Step 5: Test and Refine
*Use rubric with sample work; revise as needed*

- Score sample student work
- Are descriptors clear and distinct?
- Can you reliably assign scores?
- Do students understand it?
- Revise unclear language

## Example CEA Rubrics

### Example 1: Hydroponic System Design (Analytic)

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Technical Accuracy (40%)** | All calculations correct; components properly sized for specifications; design fully functional | Minor calculation errors (90%+ correct); components generally appropriate; design mostly functional | Multiple errors (70-89% correct); some inappropriate component choices; questionable functionality | Major errors (<70% correct); inappropriate components; non-functional design |
| **Completeness (25%)** | All required components included; detailed specifications for each; comprehensive materials list | Most components included (90%+); adequate specifications; complete materials list | Some missing components (70-89%); vague specifications; incomplete materials list | Many missing components (<70%); minimal specifications; no materials list |
| **Cost-Effectiveness (20%)** | Optimized for budget; creative cost-saving; meets or under budget without compromising quality | Reasonable costs; standard solutions; within budget | Over budget by <20% OR excessive compromises to meet budget | Significantly over budget (>20%) OR severe quality compromises |
| **Justification (10%)** | Thorough rationale for all choices; supported by research/data; alternatives considered | Adequate justification; some supporting evidence; limited alternatives | Minimal justification; opinion-based; no alternatives | Little to no justification provided |
| **Presentation (5%)** | Publication-quality; clear professional diagrams; well-organized; no errors | Professional appearance; clear diagrams; organized; minor errors | Adequate presentation; diagrams unclear; some disorganization; several errors | Poor presentation; confusing or missing diagrams; disorganized; many errors |

**Total Points:** 100 (weight percentages applied to 4-point scale)

### Example 2: Pest Identification Practical Exam (Holistic)

**4 - Exemplary (90-100%):**
- Correctly identifies 9-10 pests
- Accurately describes damage for all
- Provides appropriate management recommendations
- Uses proper terminology throughout
- Completed efficiently within time limit

**3 - Proficient (80-89%):**
- Correctly identifies 7-8 pests
- Accurately describes damage for most
- Provides generally appropriate recommendations
- Uses terminology correctly most of the time
- Completed within time limit

**2 - Developing (70-79%):**
- Correctly identifies 5-6 pests
- Describes damage with some accuracy
- Recommendations partially appropriate
- Some terminology errors
- May exceed time limit slightly

**1 - Beginning (<70%):**
- Correctly identifies fewer than 5 pests
- Damage descriptions inaccurate
- Inappropriate or no recommendations
- Frequent terminology errors
- Significantly exceeds time or incomplete

## Common Rubric Mistakes

### MISTAKE: Vague Language
**Poor:** "Shows good understanding"
**Better:** "Explains three key concepts accurately with supporting examples"

### MISTAKE: Focus on Process, Not Product
**Poor:** "Student tried hard"
**Better:** "Submission includes all required components with accurate content"

### MISTAKE: Quantity Instead of Quality
**Poor:** "Includes 5 examples"
**Better:** "Includes relevant examples that effectively illustrate the concept"

### MISTAKE: Not Parallel Across Levels
**Poor:**
- 4: "Excellent technical accuracy"
- 3: "Uses appropriate resources"
- 2: "Tries to apply concepts"

**Better:**
- 4: "All technical content is accurate (95-100%)"
- 3: "Most technical content is accurate (85-94%)"
- 2: "Some technical content is accurate (70-84%)"

### MISTAKE: Overlapping Descriptors
Make levels clearly distinct; avoid overlap

### MISTAKE: Too Many Criteria
**Problem:** 15 criteria = overwhelming
**Fix:** Combine related criteria; focus on most important

## Tips for Using Rubrics

### Share Before Assignment
- Give rubric when assigning work
- Review criteria and levels
- Answer questions
- Let it guide their work

### Involve Students
- Co-create rubrics
- Review samples together
- Self-assessment using rubric
- Peer assessment

### Provide Feedback
- Check boxes/levels
- Add specific comments
- Highlight strengths
- Suggest improvements
- Connect to learning objectives

### Use for Revision
- Score draft
- Provide feedback
- Allow revision
- Re-score

---

## Rubric Development Checklist

- [ ] Clear criteria identified
- [ ] Appropriate number of performance levels
- [ ] Descriptors are specific and observable
- [ ] Language is clear and understandable
- [ ] Parallel structure across levels
- [ ] Levels are clearly distinct (no overlap)
- [ ] Weights assigned (if using weighted criteria)
- [ ] Tested with sample work
- [ ] Students will receive it before working
- [ ] Aligned with learning objectives
