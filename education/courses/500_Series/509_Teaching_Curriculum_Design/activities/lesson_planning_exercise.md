# Activity 2: Lesson Planning Exercise

## CEA-509 Practical Application

**Type:** Skills Development Activity
**Duration:** 2-3 hours per lesson; 5 lessons total over semester
**Points:** 20% of course grade
**Format:** Individual work with peer feedback

---

## Overview

Develop five complete, high-quality lesson plans for CEA topics using instructional design principles learned in this course. Each lesson should be classroom-ready and demonstrate mastery of different instructional approaches.

---

## Requirements: Five Diverse Lesson Plans

### Lesson 1: Lecture-Based Learning (Due Week 6)
**Focus:** Direct instruction for introducing new concepts

**Topic Examples:**
- Introduction to VPD and climate management
- Fundamentals of plant nutrition
- Overview of hydroponic system types
- Basics of integrated pest management

**Required Elements:**
- 60-90 minutes duration
- Clear learning objectives (3-5, using Bloom's)
- Engaging introduction/hook
- Organized content delivery
- Visual aids (slides, diagrams)
- Active learning elements (think-pair-share, polls, questions)
- Formative assessment
- Summary and closure

---

### Lesson 2: Hands-On Laboratory (Due Week 8)
**Focus:** Psychomotor skill development

**Topic Examples:**
- Mixing nutrient solutions
- Transplanting seedlings
- Water quality testing
- System assembly

**Required Elements:**
- 90-120 minutes duration
- Safety protocols
- Pre-lab introduction
- Clear step-by-step procedures
- Student lab handout
- Observation checklist for assessment
- Debrief/reflection activity
- Connection to theory

---

### Lesson 3: Online Asynchronous Module (Due Week 10)
**Focus:** Self-paced digital learning

**Topic Examples:**
- Reading and interpreting VPD charts
- Calculating crop cycles and scheduling
- Identifying nutrient deficiencies
- Understanding grow light specifications

**Required Elements:**
- 60-90 minutes student time
- Video content (script or storyboard)
- Interactive elements (quizzes, clickable graphics)
- Discussion forum prompt
- Self-assessment
- Resources/links
- LMS organization structure

---

### Lesson 4: Problem-Based Learning (Due Week 12)
**Focus:** Critical thinking and application

**Topic Examples:**
- Diagnosing crop production problems
- Designing system for specific constraints
- Troubleshooting equipment failures
- Optimizing production for profitability

**Required Elements:**
- 90-120 minutes (may extend to 2-3 sessions)
- Authentic problem scenario
- Resources for research
- Guiding questions (not answers)
- Group work structure
- Presentation/reporting requirements
- Rubric for assessment
- Facilitator notes

---

### Lesson 5: Assessment-Focused Lesson (Due Week 14)
**Focus:** Measuring learning effectively

**Topic Examples:**
- Review and prepare for any major concept
- Skills demonstration day
- Portfolio presentations
- Peer teaching sessions

**Required Elements:**
- Assessment as the lesson
- Clear criteria/rubric
- Preparation guidance for students
- Formative feedback opportunities
- Summative evaluation method
- Reflection component
- Connection to course objectives

## Lesson Plan Template

Use this structure for each lesson:

### Header Section
- Lesson title
- Duration
- Target audience (course level, background)
- Instructional mode (lecture, lab, online, PBL, assessment)

### Learning Objectives
- 3-5 specific, measurable objectives
- Action verbs from appropriate Bloom's level
- Aligned with broader course goals

### Standards Alignment (if applicable)
- Industry competencies addressed
- Academic standards met

### Materials and Resources
- Instructor materials
- Student materials/handouts
- Technology/equipment needed
- Facilities requirements

### Pre-requisite Knowledge
- What students should already know
- How you'll check for prerequisite understanding

### Instructional Sequence

**Introduction (10-15 min)**
- Hook/engagement activity
- Activate prior knowledge
- State objectives and relevance

**Learning Activities (Main instruction, 30-90 min)**
- Content delivery
- Practice activities
- Formative assessment checkpoints
- Scaffolding and differentiation notes

**Closure (10 min)**
- Summary of key points
- Connection to next lesson
- Assignment of homework/preparation

### Assessment
- Formative (during lesson)
- Summative (if applicable)
- Rubric or criteria

### Differentiation
- Support for struggling learners
- Extension for advanced learners
- Accommodations

### Reflection (Complete after teaching)
- What worked well?
- What would you change?
- Student performance on objectives
- Timing accuracy

## Grading Rubric (Per Lesson Plan)

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Learning Objectives** | Clear, measurable, appropriately leveled, complete | Clear and measurable with minor issues | Vague or not fully measurable | Missing or poorly written |
| **Instructional Design** | Excellent sequencing, engagement, alignment | Good structure with minor gaps | Some organization issues | Poorly organized |
| **Assessment** | Perfectly aligned, valid, well-designed | Aligned with minor issues | Alignment concerns | Poor or missing |
| **Materials** | Complete, professional, ready to use | Adequate with minor gaps | Incomplete or needs work | Missing or inadequate |
| **Differentiation** | Multiple strategies, thoughtful | Some strategies included | Minimal differentiation | Missing |

## Peer Review Process

**Schedule:**
- Week 7: Exchange Lesson 1 with partner
- Week 9: Exchange Lesson 2 with partner
- Week 11: Exchange Lesson 3 with partner
- Week 13: Exchange Lesson 4 with partner

**Process:**
1. Review partner's lesson using rubric
2. Provide constructive written feedback (1 page)
3. Discuss together (optional but encouraged)
4. Revise your lesson based on feedback received
5. Submit final version with peer feedback attached

**Benefit:** Improves quality; learns from others' approaches

## Example Lesson Plan (Abbreviated)

**Lesson:** Understanding Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)
**Duration:** 75 minutes
**Audience:** Intro to CEA course students

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Explain the relationship between temperature, humidity, and VPD (Understand)
2. Interpret VPD charts to determine optimal growing conditions (Apply)
3. Adjust environmental controls to achieve target VPD ranges (Apply)

**Materials:**
- PPT with VPD charts, photos, diagrams
- VPD chart handouts
- Practice scenarios
- Quiz (formative)

**Sequence:**

**Introduction (10 min):**
- Ask: "Has anyone ever felt uncomfortable even though temperature was fine?"
- Discuss: humidity makes us feel hotter/colder
- Analogy: Plants "sweat" too! VPD measures their comfort

**Learning Activity 1 (15 min):**
- Explain: temperature, humidity, plant transpiration
- Show: VPD chart with color zones
- Demonstrate: reading chart (temp + RH → VPD)

**Practice 1 (10 min):**
- Pairs: practice reading VPD from chart
- 5 scenarios: "What's the VPD?"
- Circulate and check

**Learning Activity 2 (15 min):**
- Explain: Optimal VPD ranges for different growth stages
- Show: real facility examples (photos of good/bad VPD effects)
- Discuss: Why VPD matters (disease, growth rate, quality)

**Practice 2 (15 min):**
- Individuals: "Given current conditions, what would you adjust?"
- Share solutions; discuss trade-offs

**Assessment (10 min):**
- Quiz: 5 questions (interpret chart, recommend adjustments)
- Exit ticket: "One thing you learned; one question you still have"

**Closure (5 min):**
- Summary: VPD is temp + humidity's effect on plants
- Next class: Calculating VPD mathematically
- Homework: Monitor VPD in your space (app or calculator)

## Tips for Success

**1. Start with objectives**
- What do you want students to be able to DO?
- Everything else supports these

**2. Backward design**
- Objectives → Assessment → Instruction
- Don't plan activities without purpose

**3. Engage actively**
- Adults learn by doing
- Limit lecture to 10-15 min chunks
- Intersperse with activity

**4. Check for understanding**
- Frequent formative assessment
- Adjust pacing based on understanding
- Don't assume they got it

**5. Real-world connection**
- Why does this matter?
- When will they use it?
- How does it apply?

**6. Professional quality**
- Proofread carefully
- Format consistently
- Include all components
- Ready to use in real classroom

**7. Be realistic**
- Can you actually fit all this in the time?
- Do you have the resources?
- Is it appropriate for audience?
