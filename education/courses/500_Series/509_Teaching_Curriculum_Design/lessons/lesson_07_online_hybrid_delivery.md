## Key Concepts

### 1. Modalities of Course Delivery

**Fully Online (Asynchronous):**
- Students work independently on their schedule
- Pre-recorded lectures, readings, activities
- Discussion forums for interaction
- Best for: Working professionals; geographically dispersed; self-directed learners

**Fully Online (Synchronous):**
- Live virtual meetings at scheduled times
- Video conferencing (Zoom, Teams, WebEx)
- Real-time interaction
- Best for: Building community; complex discussions; immediate feedback

**Hybrid/Blended:**
- Combination of online and in-person
- Flipped classroom model
- Online theory + in-person hands-on
- Best for: Maximizing flexibility while maintaining practical work

**HyFlex:**
- Students choose format per session
- Attend in-person OR online synchronously OR asynchronously
- Maximum flexibility
- Best for: Diverse student needs; uncertain circumstances

**CEA Applications:**
- **Theory content:** Well-suited for online asynchronous
- **Discussions and analysis:** Online synchronous or asynchronous forums
- **Hands-on skills:** Require in-person OR creative virtual alternatives
- **Optimal for CEA:** Hybrid model with online theory and in-person labs

### 2. Learning Management Systems (LMS)

**Common Platforms:**
- Canvas
- Moodle
- Blackboard
- Google Classroom
- Schoology

**Core LMS Functions:**

**Content Organization:**
- Modules/units
- Files and resources
- Pages and text
- External links

**Assignments:**
- Create and collect submissions
- Online grading
- Rubrics
- Return feedback

**Assessments:**
- Quizzes and exams
- Question banks
- Timed tests
- Immediate feedback options

**Communication:**
- Announcements
- Discussion forums
- Messaging
- Email integration

**Gradebook:**
- Automatic calculation
- Weighted categories
- Student view of progress

**Analytics:**
- Time on task
- Resource access
- Participation metrics
- At-risk student identification

**Best Practices for LMS Design:**

1. **Consistent Structure**
   - Each module follows same pattern
   - Predictable organization
   - Easy navigation

2. **Clear Instructions**
   - Explicit directions for all tasks
   - Due dates prominent
   - Points/grading criteria clear

3. **Chunked Content**
   - Break into digestible pieces
   - 10-15 minute videos
   - Short readings with activities

4. **Multiple Formats**
   - Text, video, graphics, audio
   - Accommodate learning preferences
   - Redundancy in key information

5. **Regular Communication**
   - Weekly announcements
   - Presence and engagement
   - Quick response to questions

**Example CEA Course Module Structure:**

```
Module 3: Plant Nutrition in CEA
├── Overview and Learning Objectives
├── Pre-Module: Self-Assessment Quiz
├── Lesson 3.1: Essential Nutrients (Video + Notes)
├── Lesson 3.2: Nutrient Uptake (Reading + Diagram)
├── Lesson 3.3: Formulating Solutions (Video Tutorial)
├── Activity: Nutrient Calculation Practice
├── Discussion: Troubleshooting Deficiencies (Forum)
├── Lab Assignment: Mix and Test Solutions (Submit Report)
├── Module 3 Quiz
└── Reflection: What was most challenging?
```

### 3. Creating Effective Instructional Videos

**Types of Videos:**

**Lecture Capture:**
- Record traditional lecture
- Use: Presenting information, explanations

**Screencast:**
- Record screen with narration
- Use: Software tutorials, demonstrating calculations, showing data analysis

**Demonstration:**
- Record hands-on procedures
- Use: Laboratory techniques, equipment operation, facility tours

**Interview:**
- Conversation with expert
- Use: Industry insights, career advice, case studies

**Animation:**
- Animated explanations
- Use: Complex processes, systems visualization

**Best Practices:**

**Length:**
- 5-10 minutes ideal
- Maximum 15 minutes
- Break longer content into segments

**Quality:**
- Good audio critical (clear, no background noise)
- Adequate lighting
- Stable camera
- Clear visuals

**Engagement:**
- Enthusiastic delivery
- Direct address to camera
- Vary pacing and tone
- Visual interest (not just talking head)

**Accessibility:**
- Captions/subtitles
- Transcripts
- Clear audio
- Described visuals

**Production Tips:**

**Equipment:**
- Smartphone or webcam adequate
- Lapel microphone recommended
- Ring light or natural lighting
- Tripod or stable surface

**Editing:**
- Trim errors and pauses
- Add text overlays for key points
- Insert graphics/diagrams
- Simple transitions
- Software: iMovie, Camtasia, Adobe Premiere

**Hosting:**
- YouTube (unlisted or private)
- Vimeo
- LMS built-in video
- Panopto or Kaltura

**CEA Video Examples:**

1. **"Transplanting Seedlings"** (7 min demonstration)
2. **"Understanding VPD Charts"** (10 min screencast)
3. **"Virtual Facility Tour"** (12 min walkthrough with narration)
4. **"Interview with Vertical Farm Manager"** (15 min Q&A)
5. **"How Nutrients Move in Plants"** (6 min animation)

### 4. Facilitating Online Discussions

**Purpose of Discussions:**
- Deepen understanding through dialogue
- Multiple perspectives
- Critical thinking
- Build community
- Practice communication

**Discussion Prompts:**

**Poor Prompt:** "What did you think of the reading?"
- Too vague; surface-level responses

**Better Prompt:** "The article argues that vertical farming cannot achieve price parity with field agriculture. Do you agree or disagree? Support your position with evidence from course materials and at least one outside source."

**Types of Prompts:**

**Analysis:** Examine case study; identify key issues
**Debate:** Take and defend a position
**Application:** How would you apply concept in your context?
**Problem-Solving:** Propose solutions to scenario
**Reflection:** What changed in your thinking?

**Facilitation Strategies:**

**Initial Post:**
- Specific prompt
- Clear expectations (length, evidence required)
- Due date for initial post

**Peer Responses:**
- Require 2+ substantive responses
- Criteria for quality response
- Later due date than initial post

**Instructor Role:**
- Monitor but don't dominate
- Ask probing questions
- Connect ideas across posts
- Summarize key themes
- Clarify misconceptions

**Grading:**
- Use rubric for consistency
- Participation + quality
- Give feedback

**Example Discussion Rubric:**

| Criterion | Points | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Initial Post | 5 | Thorough response; evidence-based; minimum 250 words; posted by deadline |
| Peer Responses | 3 | Two+ substantive responses; extends conversation; respectful |
| Quality | 2 | Clear writing; correct citations; professional tone |
| **Total** | **10** | |

### 5. Adapting Hands-On Content for Online

**Challenge:** CEA requires significant hands-on practice; how to teach virtually?

**Strategies:**

**Virtual Labs:**
- Simulations and virtual environments
- Example: Climate control simulation software

**Video Demonstrations:**
- Record procedures clearly
- Multiple angles
- Close-ups of details
- Slow motion for complex steps

**At-Home Kits:**
- Ship materials to students
- Simple systems (Kratky method)
- Testing supplies (pH/EC meters)
- Seeds and nutrients

**Synchronous Virtual Labs:**
- Instructor performs live via video
- Students follow along with their kits
- Real-time Q&A

**Local Partnerships:**
- Students visit facilities in their area
- Structured observation guides
- Report back to class

**Virtual Reality:**
- VR facility tours
- Equipment operation simulations
- Emerging technology

**Asynchronous Video Tours:**
- Comprehensive facility walkthroughs
- Pause for student observations
- Guided questions

**CEA Example - Online Hydroponics Lab:**

**Option 1: At-Home Kit**
- Ship: Container, nutrients, rockwool, seeds, pH/EC meter
- Students build Kratky jar system
- Grow for 4 weeks
- Weekly observations and data
- Photo documentation

**Option 2: Virtual Simulation**
- Use online hydroponic simulator
- Adjust variables (pH, EC, nutrients)
- Observe plant responses
- Experiment without real-world costs

**Option 3: Synchronous Demo**
- Instructor sets up system via video call
- Students watch and take notes
- Q&A throughout
- Submit design drawings and calculations

### 7. Hybrid/Blended Learning Models

**Flipped Classroom:**
- **At home:** Watch lectures, read materials
- **In class:** Discussion, problem-solving, hands-on work

**Benefits:**
- Maximizes face-to-face time for high-value activities
- Students can review content at own pace
- Instructor available during challenging work

**CEA Application:**
- **Online:** Lectures on theory, equipment operation videos
- **In-person:** Lab work, troubleshooting practice, facility tours

**Rotation Model:**
- Students rotate between online and in-person
- **Example:** Week 1 online, Week 2 in-person, repeat

**Lab Hybrid:**
- Lecture/content online
- Lab sessions in-person
- Common in CEA programs

**Enrichment Model:**
- Core content same for all
- Optional online modules for enrichment
- Differentiation for advanced students

### 8. Accessibility and Inclusion in Online Learning

**Universal Design for Learning (UDL) Online:**

**Multiple Means of Representation:**
- Text + audio + video
- Transcripts and captions
- Visual descriptions
- Text alternatives for images

**Multiple Means of Engagement:**
- Variety of activities
- Choice in assignments
- Relevance to diverse contexts
- Low-stakes practice

**Multiple Means of Expression:**
- Video OR written submissions
- Individual OR group work
- Different formats accepted

**Technical Accessibility:**
- Mobile-friendly design
- Screen reader compatible
- Keyboard navigation
- Sufficient color contrast
- Captions on videos

**Accommodations:**
- Extended time on assessments
- Alternative formats
- Flexible deadlines (with documentation)
- Note-taking assistance

**Digital Divide Considerations:**
- Internet access limitations
- Device availability
- Technology skills
- Provide alternatives when possible

## Case Study: Hybrid CEA Course Design

**Course:** Commercial Hydroponic Production (12 weeks)

**Structure:**
- 8 weeks online (asynchronous with optional synchronous sessions)
- 4 intensive weekends in-person at teaching facility

**Online Components:**
- Weekly modules with video lectures (2-3 per week, 10-15 min each)
- Reading assignments from textbook and articles
- Discussion forums (1 per module)
- Online quizzes (formative)
- Virtual facility tours (recorded)
- Guest speaker webinars (live, recorded for later viewing)

**In-Person Components:**
- Weekend 1 (Week 3): System design and construction
- Weekend 2 (Week 6): Crop management and monitoring
- Weekend 3 (Week 9): Troubleshooting and optimization
- Weekend 4 (Week 12): Final project presentations and skills assessment

**Rationale:**
- Theory learned flexibly online
- Hands-on skills require in-person
- Concentrated in-person time accommodates travel
- Scaffolded: each weekend builds on previous

**Student Feedback:**
- Appreciate flexibility of online
- Value intensive hands-on weekends
- Request more synchronous sessions for real-time questions
- Suggest shipping small at-home growing kits

## Key Takeaways

1. **Hybrid/blended models combine strengths of online and in-person learning**
2. **Effective online design requires intentional structure, engagement, and interaction**
3. **Video content should be short, high-quality, and accessible**
4. **Discussion forums enable asynchronous dialogue and critical thinking**
5. **Hands-on CEA content requires creative adaptation for online delivery**
6. **Building community online requires deliberate effort and strategies**
7. **Accessibility and universal design benefit all learners**
