# Lesson 6: Assessment Design and Evaluation Methods

## Module 6: Creating Valid and Reliable Assessments for CEA

**Duration:** Week 2 (6 hours total)

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Distinguish between formative and summative assessment
2. Design valid and reliable assessment instruments
3. Create rubrics for complex performance tasks
4. Develop authentic assessments aligned with industry needs
5. Write effective test questions across Bloom's levels
6. Implement alternative assessment methods
7. Use assessment data to improve instruction

---

## Key Concepts

### 1. Assessment Fundamentals

**Assessment vs. Evaluation:**
- **Assessment:** Gathering evidence of learning
- **Evaluation:** Making judgments about quality or value

**Purpose of Assessment:**
- Measure student achievement
- Provide feedback to learners
- Inform instructional decisions
- Certify competence
- Program evaluation

**Validity:** Does it measure what it's supposed to measure?
**Reliability:** Does it produce consistent results?

---

### 2. Formative vs. Summative Assessment

**Formative Assessment (Assessment FOR Learning)**

**Purpose:** Monitor learning during instruction; provide feedback

**Characteristics:**
- Frequent and ongoing
- Low-stakes or no grades
- Immediate feedback
- Informs next instructional steps

**CEA Examples:**
- Exit tickets: "What's still confusing about VPD?"
- Quick polls: "Which nutrient causes this symptom?"
- Practice problems with immediate feedback
- Observation during lab activities
- Draft submissions with feedback
- Self-assessment checklists

**Summative Assessment (Assessment OF Learning)**

**Purpose:** Evaluate achievement at end of instruction

**Characteristics:**
- End of unit/course
- High-stakes; graded
- Comprehensive
- Certifies mastery

**CEA Examples:**
- Final exams
- Capstone projects
- Skills demonstrations
- Certification exams
- Portfolio assessment

**Best Practice:** Use both; formative to guide learning, summative to certify achievement

---

### 3. Traditional Assessment Methods

**Multiple Choice Questions:**

**Strengths:** Efficient to score; objective; can test breadth of knowledge
**Weaknesses:** Hard to test higher-order thinking; guessing possible

**Writing Effective MC Questions:**

**Poor:** "Which is true about hydroponics?"
- Vague; unclear what aspect of hydroponics

**Better:** "Which factor most directly affects nutrient availability in hydroponic solutions?"
A) Air temperature
B) Solution pH
C) Light intensity
D) CO2 concentration

**Answer:** B

**Guidelines:**
- Test important concepts, not trivia
- Use clear, concise language
- Make all distractors plausible
- Avoid "all of the above" or "none of the above"
- Only one clearly correct answer

**True/False:**

**Use sparingly:** 50% guessing rate; encourage memorization

**When appropriate:** Identifying misconceptions

**Example:**
T/F: "Organic fertilizers cannot be used in hydroponic systems." (False - can use organic hydroponic inputs)

**Short Answer:**

**Strengths:** Reduces guessing; tests recall and understanding

**Example:** "List three symptoms of nitrogen deficiency in plants."

**Essay Questions:**

**Strengths:** Tests higher-order thinking; communication skills

**Weaknesses:** Time-consuming to grade; subjectivity possible

**Example:** "Analyze the water quality report and recommend appropriate treatment before use in hydroponics. Justify your recommendations."

**Use Rubric:** Ensures consistent grading

---

### 4. Performance-Based Assessment

**Definition:** Students demonstrate skills in authentic contexts

**Types:**

**Skills Demonstrations:**
- Perform specific task
- Observed and scored
- Example: "Calibrate pH meter following standard procedure"

**Practical Exams:**
- Complete series of tasks
- Often timed
- Example: "Set up functional DWC system in 2 hours"

**Projects:**
- Extended work producing deliverable
- Example: "Design complete facility environmental control system"

**Presentations:**
- Oral communication of learning
- Example: "Present crop trial results to industry panel"

**Portfolios:**
- Collection of work showing growth
- Example: Learning portfolio with reflections, projects, assessments

**Work Samples:**
- Products from authentic tasks
- Example: Standard operating procedures written for real facility

---

### 5. Rubric Design

**Types of Rubrics:**

**Analytic Rubric:**
- Separate scores for each criterion
- Detailed feedback
- More time to score

**Holistic Rubric:**
- Single overall score
- Faster to score
- Less detailed feedback

**CEA Example - Analytic Rubric for System Design:**

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Technical Accuracy** | All calculations correct; components properly sized | Minor errors; generally appropriate sizing | Multiple errors; some inappropriate choices | Major technical errors; non-functional design |
| **Completeness** | All required components; detailed specifications | Most components; adequate specs | Missing key components; vague specs | Incomplete; insufficient detail |
| **Justification** | Thorough rationale with citations; considers alternatives | Adequate reasoning; some supporting evidence | Limited justification; opinion-based | Little to no justification |
| **Cost Efficiency** | Optimized for budget; creative solutions | Reasonable costs; standard solutions | Over budget or excessive compromises | No cost consideration |
| **Professional Quality** | Publication-ready; clear graphics; organized | Professional appearance; generally clear | Adequate presentation; some clarity issues | Poor presentation; difficult to follow |

**Rubric Development Process:**

1. Identify criteria (what matters?)
2. Define performance levels
3. Write descriptors for each cell
4. Test with sample work
5. Revise for clarity
6. Share with students before assignment

---

### 6. Authentic Assessment

**Definition:** Tasks that mirror real-world professional work

**Characteristics:**
- Realistic context
- Complex and open-ended
- Multiple possible approaches
- Integrates multiple skills
- Has meaning beyond the classroom

**CEA Authentic Assessment Examples:**

**1. Facility Audit:**
Students conduct actual operational audit of CEA facility; produce report with findings and recommendations; present to facility manager

**2. Client Consultation:**
Respond to real inquiry from prospective grower; provide needs assessment, system recommendation, and implementation plan

**3. Training Development:**
Create and deliver actual training session for facility staff on specific topic; assess effectiveness

**4. Troubleshooting Case:**
Given real production problem from industry, diagnose cause and propose solution; compare to what actually worked

**5. Research Project:**
Conduct original research on CEA question; present at student research conference; submit to industry publication

**Design Process:**
1. Identify authentic task from profession
2. Define what competent performance looks like
3. Create scenario and context
4. Develop assessment criteria
5. Provide necessary resources
6. Score using rubric
7. Provide feedback and reflection opportunity

---

### 7. Alternative Assessment Methods

**Self-Assessment:**
Students evaluate own work against criteria

**Benefits:** Metacognition; ownership; realistic self-perception
**Tools:** Rubrics; checklists; reflection prompts

**Peer Assessment:**
Students evaluate each other's work

**Benefits:** Learn from others; practice giving feedback; multiple perspectives
**Protocols:** Clear criteria; structured feedback forms; training in constructive criticism

**Concept Maps:**
Visual representation of knowledge structures

**Assessing:** Accuracy of concepts; correctness of relationships; complexity

**Journals/Reflections:**
Written reflection on learning experiences

**Prompts:**
- What did I learn?
- How does this connect to previous knowledge?
- What questions do I still have?
- How will I apply this?

**Demonstrations:**
Show rather than tell what was learned

**Examples:** Video tutorials; teaching sessions; facility tours they lead

**Digital Portfolios:**
Online collection of work with reflections

**Platforms:** Google Sites, WordPress, portfolio software
**Contents:** Best work; reflections; evidence of growth

### 8. Test Construction Principles

**Planning the Test:**

**Blueprint/Table of Specifications:**

| Topic | Remember | Understand | Apply | Analyze | Total |
|-------|----------|------------|-------|---------|-------|
| Plant Nutrition | 5 items | 3 items | 2 items | - | 10 items |
| Lighting | 3 items | 3 items | 2 items | 2 items | 10 items |
| Climate Control | 4 items | 3 items | 2 items | 1 item | 10 items |

**Ensures:** Appropriate content coverage; balance of cognitive levels; alignment with objectives

**Writing Clear Questions:**

**Guidelines:**
- Use clear, simple language
- Avoid trick questions
- One correct answer only
- Avoid negatives ("Which is NOT...")
- Test important concepts
- Avoid textbook language verbatim

**Test Layout and Administration:**

**Layout:**
- Clear instructions
- Grouped by question type
- Adequate space for answers
- Organized and professional

**Administration:**
- Quiet environment
- Adequate time
- Clear start/stop times
- Academic integrity measures
- Accommodations as needed

---

### 9. Grading and Feedback

**Grading Principles:**

**Criterion-Referenced:** Compare to standards, not other students
**Transparency:** Clear criteria shared in advance
**Consistency:** Use rubrics for consistent application
**Timeliness:** Return promptly while still relevant

**Effective Feedback:**

**Characteristics:**
- Specific, not vague
- Actionable
- Focused on work, not person
- Balanced (strengths and areas for growth)
- Timely

**Poor Feedback:** "Good job!"
**Better Feedback:** "Your system design correctly sized the pump for flow requirements. To improve, consider energy efficiency in pump selection and include maintenance access in your layout."

**Feedback Methods:**
- Written comments on work
- Rubrics with checkmarks and notes
- One-on-one conferences
- Video or audio feedback
- Peer feedback structured by protocol

---

### 10. Using Assessment Data

**Formative Use:**
- Identify students who need help
- Adjust pacing and instruction
- Re-teach if needed
- Provide additional practice

**Summative Use:**
- Assign grades
- Determine who achieved competency
- Award credentials
- Track student progress

**Program Evaluation:**
- Are students achieving outcomes?
- Which objectives are challenging?
- Is curriculum effective?
- What needs revision?

**Data Analysis:**

**Item Analysis:**
- Which questions did most students miss?
- Were questions too easy or too hard?
- Are there patterns?

**Example:** If 80% miss a question about VPD calculation, need to improve instruction on that topic

**Reliability Check:**
- Do results make sense?
- Are scores consistent with other evidence?
- Any anomalies to investigate?

## Case Study: Comprehensive Assessment Plan

**Course:** CEA Crop Production (8 weeks)

**Assessment Strategy:**

**Formative Assessments:**
- Weekly quizzes (10 questions, online, unlimited attempts)
- Lab observation checklists (completed during hands-on work)
- Draft project submissions with feedback (Weeks 3, 5)
- Peer review of growing protocols (Week 6)
- Self-assessment of skills (Week 4, 8)

**Summative Assessments:**
- Midterm exam (Week 4): 50 questions, multiple choice and short answer
- Crop production project (Week 8): Design and justify complete growing protocol
- Skills demonstration (Week 7): Perform 5 essential tasks, scored with rubric
- Final exam (Week 8): Comprehensive, 75 questions

**Grading Breakdown:**
- Formative work: 20%
- Midterm: 15%
- Project: 30%
- Skills demo: 20%
- Final: 15%

**Alignment Check:**
All assessments map to course learning objectives; multiple measures of each objective

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Use formative assessment to guide learning; summative to certify achievement**
2. **Assessments must be valid (measure what matters) and reliable (consistent)**
3. **Rubrics provide clear criteria and consistent scoring for complex tasks**
4. **Authentic assessments mirror real-world professional work**
5. **Effective feedback is specific, actionable, and timely**
6. **Multiple assessment methods provide comprehensive picture of learning**
7. **Assessment data should drive instructional improvement**
