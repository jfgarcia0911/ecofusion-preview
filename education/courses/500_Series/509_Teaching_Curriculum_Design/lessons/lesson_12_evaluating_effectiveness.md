## Key Concepts

### 1. Why Evaluate?

**Purposes of Evaluation:**
- Measure achievement of learning objectives
- Assess program effectiveness
- Identify areas for improvement
- Demonstrate accountability
- Justify continued funding
- Make data-driven decisions
- Celebrate successes

**Evaluation vs. Assessment:**
- **Assessment:** Measuring student learning
- **Evaluation:** Judging program quality and impact

**Both are essential for educational excellence**

---

### 2. Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Evaluation

**Developed by Donald Kirkpatrick (1959), refined over decades**

**Level 1: Reaction**
- What participants thought and felt about training
- "Did they like it?"

**Level 2: Learning**
- Knowledge, skills, attitudes acquired
- "Did they learn?"

**Level 3: Behavior**
- Application of learning on the job
- "Did they use it?"

**Level 4: Results**
- Business or organizational impact
- "Did it matter?"

**Pyramid Model:**
```
    Results (Impact)
         ↑
    Behavior (Transfer)
         ↑
    Learning (Acquisition)
         ↑
    Reaction (Satisfaction)
```

**Each level builds on previous; all are important**

---

### 3. Level 1: Reaction Evaluation

**Purpose:** Measure participant satisfaction and perceived value

**Why It Matters:**
- Dissatisfied learners won't engage
- Identifies immediate issues
- Provides quick feedback for improvement
- Predicts completion rates

**What to Measure:**

**Content Relevance:**
- Was content relevant to your work?
- Will you use this information?

**Instructor Effectiveness:**
- Was instructor knowledgeable?
- Did instructor facilitate learning?

**Instructional Methods:**
- Were activities engaging?
- Were hands-on components valuable?

**Materials and Resources:**
- Were materials helpful?
- Was technology effective?

**Logistics:**
- Were facilities adequate?
- Was timing appropriate?

**Overall Satisfaction:**
- Would you recommend this training?
- Overall rating

**Methods:**

**End-of-Session Surveys:**
- Immediately after training
- Short (5-10 minutes)
- Mix rating scales and open-ended

**Example Questions:**

Rating (1-5):
- "The content was relevant to my job responsibilities"
- "The instructor was knowledgeable and engaging"
- "The hands-on activities enhanced my learning"
- "Overall, this training met my expectations"

Open-Ended:
- "What was most valuable about this training?"
- "What suggestions do you have for improvement?"
- "What additional topics should be covered?"

**Digital Tools:**
- Google Forms
- SurveyMonkey
- LMS built-in surveys
- QR codes to mobile surveys

**Analysis:**

**Quantitative:**
- Calculate means and percentages
- Identify low-scoring items
- Track trends over time
- Compare across sessions

**Qualitative:**
- Code open-ended responses
- Identify themes
- Note specific suggestions
- Highlight testimonials

**CEA Example Results:**

Hydroponic Production Workshop
- Overall satisfaction: 4.3/5
- Content relevance: 4.6/5
- Instructor: 4.5/5
- Hands-on activities: 4.8/5
- Facilities: 3.9/5

**Themes from Comments:**
- Loved building systems hands-on
- Want more time for practice
- Greenhouses too hot in afternoon
- Request follow-up webinars

**Actions:**
- Add 30 minutes practice time
- Schedule afternoon break during hot months
- Develop webinar series
- Maintain high-value hands-on approach

**Limitations of Level 1:**
- Measures satisfaction, not learning
- "Happy sheets" can be misleading
- Popularity ≠ effectiveness
- Must evaluate deeper levels

### 4. Level 2: Learning Evaluation

**Purpose:** Measure knowledge, skills, and attitude changes

**Methods:**

**Pre/Post Tests:**
- Administer test before and after training
- Compare scores to measure gain
- Shows individual and group learning

**Example:**
- Pre-test average: 62%
- Post-test average: 87%
- Learning gain: 25 percentage points

**Knowledge Tests:**
- Multiple choice, true/false, short answer
- Aligned with learning objectives
- Cover key concepts

**Skills Demonstrations:**
- Performance-based assessment
- Rubric scoring
- Can they do it?

**CEA Example:**
After nutrient management training:
- Written test: Identify deficiencies, calculate formulas
- Practical: Mix solution to specifications, test and adjust

**Self-Assessment:**
- Confidence ratings before and after
- Perceived competence
- Supplement objective measures

**Example:**
"Rate your confidence in diagnosing pest problems (1-10)"
- Before: 4.2 average
- After: 7.8 average

**Portfolio Evidence:**
- Work samples
- Projects completed
- Application of concepts

**Best Practices:**

**Alignment:**
- Test what was taught
- Match objectives
- Appropriate difficulty

**Timing:**
- Pre-test at start
- Post-test immediately after
- Delayed post-test (retention check)

**Validity:**
- Actually measures learning
- Not just memorization

**Analysis:**
- Individual gains
- Group averages
- Item analysis (which concepts mastered?)
- Pass rates

**CEA Example:**

**Course:** IPM for CEA
**Assessment:** 50-question exam + practical demonstration

**Results:**
- 28 participants
- Average score: 84% (range 68-98%)
- 26 passed (93% pass rate)
- 2 need remediation
- Lowest scores on: biological control options, threshold levels
- All demonstrated competent scouting technique

**Actions:**
- Enhance biological control content
- Add more practice with thresholds
- Offer remediation session
- Update question bank

### 5. Level 3: Behavior Evaluation

**Purpose:** Measure application of learning in the workplace

**The Critical Question:** Are they using what they learned?

**Why It Matters:**
- Training is wasted if not applied
- Identifies barriers to transfer
- True measure of training utility

**Challenges:**
- Requires follow-up weeks/months later
- Multiple factors affect behavior
- Hard to isolate training effect

**Methods:**

**Observation:**
- Watch employees perform
- Compare to before training
- Use checklist or rubric

**Supervisor Surveys:**
- Ask supervisors about behavior changes
- Specific examples
- Frequency of new behaviors

**Example Questions:**
- "Is [employee] applying IPM principles learned in training?"
- "Have you observed improved pest scouting practices?"
- "What new behaviors have you noticed?"

**Self-Report:**
- Employees report own application
- Specific behaviors

**360-Degree Feedback:**
- Multiple perspectives (supervisor, peers, self, subordinates)
- Comprehensive picture

**Performance Data:**
- Metrics related to training
- Before and after comparison

**CEA Examples:**
- Crop loss rates
- System uptime
- Quality control pass rates
- Safety incidents
- Production efficiency

**Interviews/Focus Groups:**
- Qualitative insights
- Barriers and enablers
- Success stories

**Timing:**
- 1-3 months post-training (allow time for application)
- 6-12 months (sustainability check)

**Barriers to Transfer:**

**Lack of Opportunity:**
- No equipment to practice on
- Different systems at their facility
- Not their job responsibility

**Solutions:** Ensure training matches job; verify resources available

**Lack of Support:**
- Supervisor doesn't value training
- No time to apply new methods
- Organizational resistance

**Solutions:** Engage supervisors; communicate benefits; provide implementation time

**Lack of Reinforcement:**
- No feedback on application
- No recognition for using new skills
- Consequences for old behavior removed

**Solutions:** Coaching; feedback systems; rewards for application

**CEA Case Study:**

**Training:** Automated Climate Control Systems (2-day workshop)

**3-Month Follow-Up Evaluation:**

**Method:** Phone interviews with 20 participants

**Findings:**
- 85% are using automated systems (up from 40% before)
- 60% made data-driven adjustments in past month
- Barriers: Cost of systems (3 facilities); lack of IT support (2)
- Enablers: Management support; ongoing vendor support
- Unexpected benefit: Energy savings averaging 22%

**Actions:**
- Connect non-adopters with financing options
- Develop IT support guide
- Share energy savings data to encourage adoption
- Create user community for ongoing support

### 6. Level 4: Results Evaluation

**Purpose:** Measure organizational impact and ROI

**The Ultimate Question:** Did training achieve business results?

**Types of Results:**

**Productivity:**
- Increased output
- Faster processes
- Higher efficiency

**Quality:**
- Reduced defects
- Improved consistency
- Higher grades/ratings

**Financial:**
- Increased revenue
- Reduced costs
- Better profitability

**Safety:**
- Fewer accidents
- Reduced injuries
- Lower insurance costs

**Customer Satisfaction:**
- Higher ratings
- Reduced complaints
- Increased retention

**Employee Outcomes:**
- Reduced turnover
- Higher engagement
- Improved morale

**CEA-Specific Metrics:**
- Crop yields
- Quality (shelf life, appearance, nutrition)
- Resource efficiency (water, energy, nutrients)
- Crop loss rates
- Labor productivity
- Food safety compliance

**Challenges:**

**Attribution:**
- Hard to isolate training effect
- Many variables affect results
- Long time lag

**Measurement:**
- Requires baseline data
- Need business metrics
- Access to data

**Methodology:**

**Control Group Design:**
- Compare trained vs. untrained groups
- Best for attribution
- Often impractical

**Pre/Post Comparison:**
- Metrics before and after training
- Easier but less rigorous
- Account for other changes

**ROI Calculation:**

**Formula:**
```
ROI = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs × 100%
```

**Example:**

**Costs:**
- Training development: $5,000
- Instructor: $2,000
- Facilities: $500
- Materials: $500
- Participant time (20 people × 8 hours × $25/hr): $4,000
- **Total Costs: $12,000**

**Benefits (First Year):**
- Reduced crop losses: $25,000
- Energy savings: $8,000
- Reduced overtime: $3,000
- **Total Benefits: $36,000**

**ROI:**
(36,000 - 12,000) / 12,000 × 100% = **200% ROI**

**For every $1 invested, returned $2 in benefits**

**CEA Example:**

**Training:** Nutrient Management Certification (20 growers)

**18-Month Results:**

**Productivity:**
- Average yield increase: 12%
- Reduced crop cycles by 3 days

**Quality:**
- Shelf life improved 2 days
- Visual quality scores up 15%

**Financial:**
- Nutrient costs reduced 18% through optimization
- Revenue increase: $180,000 (yield × quality)
- Cost savings: $22,000 (nutrients, reduced losses)

**Environmental:**
- 25% reduction in nutrient discharge

**ROI:**
- Costs: $25,000 (training + certification)
- Benefits: $202,000
- ROI: 708%

**Intangible Benefits:**
- Grower confidence and satisfaction
- Industry recognition
- Competitive advantage

### 7. Designing Comprehensive Evaluation Plans

**Start with Objectives:**

**Training Objective:** "Participants will implement integrated pest management protocols"

**Evaluation Plan:**

| Level | What to Measure | Method | Timing | Target |
|-------|-----------------|--------|--------|--------|
| 1. Reaction | Satisfaction; relevance | Survey | End of training | 4.0/5.0 average |
| 2. Learning | IPM knowledge; ID pests | Test; practical demo | End of training | 80% pass rate |
| 3. Behavior | Weekly scouting; use of bio controls | Observation; supervisor survey | 2 months post | 75% applying |
| 4. Results | Pest-related crop losses | Production data | 6 months post | 50% reduction |

**Data Collection Tools:**
- Level 1: 10-question satisfaction survey
- Level 2: 30-question test + pest ID practical
- Level 3: Supervisor checklist; interview protocol
- Level 4: Monthly crop loss reports

**Responsibility:**
- Instructor: Levels 1 & 2
- HR/Training: Level 3 coordination
- Operations: Level 4 data

**Budget:** $2,000 for evaluation (8% of training budget)

### 8. Using Evaluation Data for Improvement

**Continuous Improvement Cycle:**

```
Plan → Implement → Evaluate → Improve → Plan...
```

**Data-Driven Decisions:**

**Low Level 1 Scores:**
- Revise content or delivery
- Change instructor or methods
- Improve logistics

**High Level 1, Low Level 2:**
- Content not leading to learning
- Assessments misaligned
- Need more practice/depth

**High Level 2, Low Level 3:**
- Transfer problem
- Workplace barriers
- Need follow-up support

**High Level 3, Low Level 4:**
- Wrong training focus
- Outcomes take longer
- Other factors limiting results

**Action Planning:**
1. Review data
2. Identify patterns
3. Diagnose root causes
4. Develop solutions
5. Implement changes
6. Re-evaluate

**Example:**

**Finding:** Satisfaction high (4.5/5) but only 60% applying skills

**Root Cause Analysis:**
- Interviews reveal lack of equipment at some facilities
- Different systems than training covered
- Supervisor resistance to change

**Solutions:**
- Offer multiple tracks for different systems
- Pre-training equipment audit
- Supervisor orientation session
- Loaner equipment program

**Re-Evaluation:** Application rate increases to 85%

## Case Study: Comprehensive Evaluation of CEA Education Program

**Program:** Associate Degree in Controlled Environment Agriculture (2-year program, 45 students enrolled)

**Evaluation Plan:**

**Level 1 - Reaction (Each Course):**
- End-of-course surveys (avg 4.2/5 across all courses)
- Highest rated: Hands-on labs (4.7/5)
- Lowest rated: General education requirements (3.6/5)

**Level 2 - Learning (Each Course + Program Exit):**
- Course assessments (avg 83%)
- Capstone project (avg 87%)
- Comprehensive exit exam (78% pass first attempt, 96% after retake)
- Skills demonstrations (92% competent)

**Level 3 - Behavior (6 months post-graduation):**
- Graduate survey: 89% report using skills daily
- Employer survey: 85% rate graduates as competent or excellent
- Job placement: 91% employed in CEA within 6 months
- Avg starting salary: $42,500 (vs. $35,000 regional avg for similar ed)

**Level 4 - Results:**

**Program Impact:**
- 67 graduates over 5 years
- Filled critical workforce needs in regional industry
- 3 graduates started own CEA businesses
- Regional CEA employment increased 24%

**Employer Impact:**
- Reduced training costs (employer estimate $8,000 saved per hire)
- Decreased turnover (75% retention after 1 year vs. 50% for non-graduates)
- Increased productivity (employer ratings)

**Student Impact:**
- Career change opportunities
- Wage premium
- Reported high job satisfaction

**Community Impact:**
- Increased local food production
- Economic development
- Educational access

**ROI (Institutional):**
- Annual program cost: $250,000
- Revenue (tuition + grants): $320,000
- Enrollment growing; sustainable model

**Improvements Based on Evaluation:**
1. Added more business/entrepreneurship content (student request)
2. Expanded internship program (employer feedback)
3. Updated equipment (industry trends)
4. Created alumni network (graduate request)
5. Developed online certificate option (market demand)

**Results After Improvements:**
- Satisfaction increased to 4.5/5
- Enrollment increased 35%
- Job placement improved to 96%
- Starting salaries up 12%

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels provide comprehensive evaluation framework**
2. **Reaction measures satisfaction; learning measures acquisition; behavior measures application; results measure impact**
3. **All levels are important and interconnected**
4. **Evaluation should be planned from the start, not afterthought**
5. **Data must be collected, analyzed, and used for improvement**
6. **ROI demonstrates value and justifies investment**
7. **Continuous evaluation drives continuous improvement**
