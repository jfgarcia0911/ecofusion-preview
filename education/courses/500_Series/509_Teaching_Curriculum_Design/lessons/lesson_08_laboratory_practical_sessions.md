# Lesson 8: Laboratory and Practical Training Sessions

## Module 8: Managing Safe and Effective Hands-On Learning

**Duration:** Week 2 (6 hours total)

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Design safe and compliant laboratory learning environments
2. Develop standard operating procedures for teaching labs
3. Manage laboratory logistics and resources effectively
4. Implement safety protocols and emergency procedures
5. Facilitate effective hands-on skill development
6. Assess practical skills using performance criteria
7. Maintain teaching facilities and equipment

---

## Key Concepts

### 1. Laboratory Safety and Compliance

**Regulatory Requirements:**

**OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration):**
- Hazard communication
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)
- Chemical safety
- Emergency procedures
- Record keeping

**EPA (Environmental Protection Agency):**
- Chemical storage and disposal
- Wastewater management
- Pesticide application (if applicable)

**State and Local:**
- Building codes
- Fire safety
- Biosafety requirements

**Institutional:**
- Safety committees
- Insurance requirements
- Risk management policies

**Safety Culture:**
- Safety is non-negotiable priority
- Model safe behavior always
- Immediately correct unsafe practices
- No shortcuts or exceptions

**Required Safety Elements:**

**1. Safety Training:**
- Initial orientation for all students
- Topic-specific training (chemicals, equipment)
- Refresher training annually
- Documentation of training

**2. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**
- Safety glasses/goggles
- Gloves (appropriate for hazard)
- Lab coats or aprons
- Closed-toe shoes
- Respiratory protection (when needed)

**3. Safety Equipment:**
- Eyewash stations (accessible within 10 seconds)
- Safety showers
- Fire extinguishers (inspected annually)
- First aid kits (stocked and accessible)
- Spill kits
- Emergency exits (clear and marked)

**4. Chemical Safety:**
- Safety Data Sheets (SDS) accessible
- Proper labeling
- Compatible storage
- Secondary containment
- Fume hoods for volatile chemicals
- Proper disposal procedures

**5. Biological Safety:**
- Proper handling of plant materials
- Soil and media sterilization
- Pest and pathogen containment
- Waste disposal

**6. Equipment Safety:**
- Guards and safety features functional
- Lockout/tagout for maintenance
- Training before use
- Regular inspections

**Emergency Procedures:**

**Emergency Action Plan:**
- Fire: evacuation routes and assembly points
- Chemical spill: containment and cleanup procedures
- Injury: first aid and emergency contact
- Power failure: backup systems and protocols

**Communication:**
- Posted emergency numbers
- Emergency contact list
- Clear chain of command
- Incident reporting procedures

---

### 2. Laboratory Design and Setup

**Space Requirements:**

**Teaching Greenhouses:**
- Benches at comfortable working height
- Adequate aisle space (3+ feet)
- Good lighting
- Temperature control
- Water source and drainage
- Storage for materials

**Wet Laboratory:**
- Sinks and water supply
- Chemical storage cabinets
- Fume hood (if using volatile chemicals)
- Work benches
- Equipment storage
- Safety equipment

**Preparation Area:**
- Storage for equipment and materials
- Work space for setup
- Refrigeration if needed
- Computer for records

**Design Principles:**

**1. Accessibility:**
- Wheelchair accessible
- Left-handed and right-handed accommodation
- Adjustable height workstations

**2. Visibility:**
- Instructor can see all students
- Students can see demonstrations
- Clear sightlines

**3. Organization:**
- Designated locations for everything
- Labeled storage
- Logical workflow

**4. Safety:**
- Emergency equipment accessible
- Clear egress
- Non-slip flooring
- Proper ventilation

**Equipment and Materials Management:**

**Inventory System:**
- List of all equipment
- Quantity on hand
- Condition
- Maintenance schedule
- Replacement date

**Checkout System:**
- Students sign out equipment
- Check condition upon return
- Accountability for loss/damage

**Maintenance:**
- Regular inspection schedule
- Calibration of instruments
- Cleaning protocols
- Repair or replacement procedures

**Materials Planning:**
- Consumables needed per session
- Lead time for ordering
- Backup supplies
- Storage requirements

### 3. Laboratory Session Planning

**Preparation:**

**1-2 Weeks Before:**
- Finalize procedure
- Order materials
- Reserve facilities
- Prepare handouts
- Create data sheets

**1-3 Days Before:**
- Test procedure
- Set up equipment
- Prepare solutions
- Plant materials ready
- Check safety equipment

**Day Of:**
- Arrive early
- Final safety check
- Materials organized at stations
- Visual aids ready
- Backup plans ready

**Lab Session Structure:**

**Introduction (10-15 min):**
- Review learning objectives
- Safety briefing
- Overview of procedure
- Demonstrate technique
- Q&A

**Activity (60-90 min):**
- Students perform procedure
- Instructor circulates
- Observe and assist
- Ask guiding questions
- Monitor safety

**Cleanup (10-15 min):**
- Proper waste disposal
- Equipment cleaning
- Return materials
- Workspace cleanup
- Safety check

**Debrief (10-15 min):**
- What did we observe?
- Expected vs. actual results
- Challenges encountered
- Connection to concepts
- Next steps

**Facilitation Techniques:**

**Demonstrations:**
- Clear visibility for all
- Explain while doing
- Emphasize key points
- Show common errors
- Invite questions

**Circulating:**
- Visit each student/group
- Observe technique
- Ask questions to assess understanding
- Provide coaching
- Correct errors immediately

**Questioning:**
- "What do you observe?"
- "Why did that happen?"
- "What would happen if...?"
- "How does this connect to...?"

**Scaffolding:**
- More guidance initially
- Reduce support as competence grows
- Encourage independence

### 4. Managing Group Dynamics in Lab

**Group Formation:**
- Assign groups strategically (mix skills/backgrounds)
- Rotate groups periodically
- Consider group size (2-4 ideal for most CEA labs)

**Role Assignment:**
- Ensure all participate
- Rotate roles
- Examples: Equipment manager, recorder, analyst, presenter

**Individual Accountability:**
- Each student submits own report
- Individual quizzes on procedure
- Observation of each student's performance
- Peer evaluation

**Addressing Issues:**
- Freeloading: Individual accountability measures
- Dominance: Assign roles; facilitate equitable participation
- Conflict: Mediate; teach collaboration skills

### 5. Teaching Specific CEA Skills

**Propagation Skills:**

**Seed Starting:**
- Media preparation
- Seed placement depth and spacing
- Moisture management
- Environmental conditions

**Teaching Approach:**
- Demonstrate with several seed types
- Students practice with different crops
- Compare germination rates
- Troubleshoot failures

**Transplanting:**
- Timing (when seedling is ready)
- Root handling
- Planting depth
- Minimizing stress

**Teaching Approach:**
- Show common mistakes
- Practice on multiple seedlings
- Assess survival rates
- Time for efficiency development

**Water Quality Testing:**

**Skills:**
- pH measurement and calibration
- EC/TDS measurement
- Sample collection
- Data recording

**Teaching Approach:**
- Demonstrate calibration procedure
- Students test multiple samples
- Compare results (quality control)
- Interpret results

**Nutrient Solution Preparation:**

**Skills:**
- Weighing/measuring
- Mixing order
- Achieving target EC and pH
- Adjusting concentrations

**Teaching Approach:**
- Calculations first (on paper)
- Demonstrate safe handling
- Students mix solutions
- Test and adjust
- Assess accuracy

**Environmental Monitoring:**

**Skills:**
- Using sensors (temp, RH, light, CO2)
- Data logging
- Chart reading (VPD)
- Identifying optimal ranges

**Teaching Approach:**
- Tour facility with instruments
- Measure at different locations
- Plot data
- Analyze patterns
- Make recommendations

**Equipment Operation:**

**Skills:**
- Climate control systems
- Irrigation timers and controllers
- Lighting systems
- Monitoring systems

**Teaching Approach:**
- Explain system components
- Demonstrate operation
- Supervised practice
- Troubleshooting scenarios
- Independent operation

**System Maintenance:**

**Skills:**
- Routine cleaning
- Filter changes
- Pump maintenance
- Calibration
- Troubleshooting

**Teaching Approach:**
- Follow manufacturer procedures
- Hands-on practice
- Scheduled maintenance planning
- Document procedures

### 6. Assessment in Laboratory Settings

**Formative Assessment:**

**Observation:**
- Circulate during lab
- Checklist of key techniques
- Immediate feedback

**Questions:**
- Ask students to explain what they're doing
- Check understanding
- Identify misconceptions

**Practice:**
- Allow multiple attempts
- Provide coaching
- Build to independence

**Summative Assessment:**

**Skills Demonstration:**
- Perform specific task
- Scored with rubric
- Pass/fail or graded

**Example:** Transplant 10 seedlings in 10 minutes with 90% survival

**Practical Exam:**
- Series of stations
- Complete tasks at each
- Timed
- Scored on accuracy and technique

**Lab Reports:**
- Document procedure
- Present data
- Analyze results
- Discuss conclusions

**Portfolio:**
- Photos of work
- Data sheets
- Reflections
- Evidence of growth

**Assessment Rubrics:**

**Example: Nutrient Solution Mixing Rubric**

| Criterion | Exceeds (4) | Meets (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| Accuracy | EC within ±0.05; pH within ±0.1 | EC within ±0.1; pH within ±0.2 | EC within ±0.3; pH within ±0.5 | Outside target ranges |
| Technique | Proper PPE; efficient workflow; clean | Generally appropriate; minor issues | Safety concerns; disorganized | Unsafe or incorrect procedure |
| Documentation | Complete, organized, accurate records | Adequate documentation | Incomplete records | Poor or no documentation |

### 7. Troubleshooting Common Lab Challenges

**Issue: Students finish at different rates**
**Solution:** Extension activities for fast finishers; checkpoint assistance for slower students

**Issue: Equipment failures during lab**
**Solution:** Backup equipment; partner with another group; alternative activity prepared

**Issue: Results don't match expectations**
**Solution:** Teaching opportunity; troubleshoot together; learn from failures

**Issue: Students not following safety procedures**
**Solution:** Immediate correction; review expectations; consequence if repeated

**Issue: Limited equipment for class size**
**Solution:** Rotation stations; demonstrations; shared equipment with scheduling

**Issue: Student breaks expensive equipment**
**Solution:** Assess intent (accident vs. negligence); teachable moment; repair/replacement plan

## Case Study: Designing a Hydroponics Lab Sequence

**Course:** Introduction to Hydroponic Production (8 weeks)

**Lab Sequence:**

**Week 1: Lab Safety and Equipment Orientation**
- Tour facilities
- Safety training
- Identify equipment
- Practice donning PPE

**Week 2: Water Quality Testing**
- Calibrate pH and EC meters
- Test samples
- Interpret results
- Calculate adjustments

**Week 3: Building Small-Scale Systems**
- Construct DWC units in pairs
- Install air pumps
- Check for leaks

**Week 4: Nutrient Solution Preparation**
- Calculate formulas
- Mix stock solutions
- Achieve target EC and pH
- Fill Week 3 systems

**Week 5: Seeding and Transplanting**
- Start seeds in rockwool
- Transplant to Week 3 systems
- Monitor environment

**Week 6: System Monitoring and Maintenance**
- Weekly observations
- Water quality checks
- Adjust pH and EC
- Record data

**Week 7: Harvesting and Evaluation**
- Harvest crops
- Measure yields
- Evaluate quality
- Clean systems

**Week 8: Practical Exam**
- Demonstrate all skills
- Performance-based assessment
- Written troubleshooting scenarios

**Progression:** Skills build cumulatively; integrated final project

## Key Takeaways

1. **Safety is paramount in all laboratory activities**
2. **Thorough preparation prevents most lab problems**
3. **Clear procedures and demonstrations set students up for success**
4. **Hands-on practice with feedback develops competence**
5. **Performance-based assessment measures actual skill mastery**
6. **Laboratory management requires organization and systems**
7. **Troubleshooting and flexibility are essential for effective lab teaching**
