### 3. Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) Cycle

**Origin:** W. Edwards Deming (quality management)

**The Cycle:**

```
PLAN → DO → STUDY → ACT → PLAN...
```

**Plan:**
- Identify problem or opportunity
- Analyze root causes
- Develop solution hypothesis
- Set measurable goals
- Create action plan

**Do:**
- Implement on small scale (pilot)
- Document what happens
- Collect data

**Study:**
- Analyze results
- Compare to predictions
- What worked? What didn't?
- Unexpected outcomes?

**Act:**
- Adopt, adapt, or abandon
- Standardize if successful
- Scale up
- Document learning
- Start next cycle

**CEA Education Example:**

**PLAN:**
- **Problem:** Students struggle with VPD concept
- **Root cause:** Abstract concept; formula intimidating
- **Solution hypothesis:** Use analogy to sweating; visual VPD chart before formula
- **Goal:** 80% of students can correctly interpret VPD chart
- **Plan:** Redesign one lesson; pilot with next cohort (15 students)

**DO:**
- Teach lesson with new approach
- Students practice reading chart
- Take assessment

**STUDY:**
- Results: 87% correctly interpret chart (up from 55%)
- Student feedback: "Much clearer!" "Sweating analogy helpful"
- Time: 10 minutes longer but worth it
- Observation: Still struggle with calculating VPD from temp/RH

**ACT:**
- Adopt new approach (keep analogy and chart)
- Add practice calculating from temp/RH
- Implement in all sections
- Document in lesson plan
- Next PDSA: Improve calculation instruction

### 4. Systematic Program Review

**Purpose:** Periodic comprehensive evaluation of entire program

**Typical Cycle:** Every 3-5 years (or as required by accreditation)

**Review Components:**

**1. Program Description:**
- Mission and goals
- Target audience
- Curriculum structure
- Delivery methods
- Resources and facilities

**2. Student Learning Outcomes:**
- Are outcomes clearly defined?
- Are they being measured?
- Are students achieving them?
- Are they aligned with industry needs?

**3. Curriculum:**
- Is content current and relevant?
- Is sequencing logical?
- Are prerequisites appropriate?
- Are there gaps or redundancies?
- Does it align with standards?

**4. Instruction:**
- Are instructional methods effective?
- Are instructors qualified?
- Is professional development provided?
- Are diverse learning needs met?

**5. Assessment:**
- Are assessments valid and reliable?
- Are they aligned with objectives?
- Is grading fair and consistent?
- Are results used for improvement?

**6. Resources:**
- Are facilities adequate?
- Is equipment current?
- Are materials available?
- Is budget sufficient?
- Is staffing appropriate?

**7. Student Support:**
- What support services exist?
- Are they accessible?
- Are they effective?
- What gaps exist?

**8. Outcomes and Impact:**
- Enrollment and completion trends
- Job placement and career outcomes
- Employer and graduate satisfaction
- Industry impact
- ROI

**9. Strengths and Challenges:**
- What's working well?
- What needs improvement?
- Opportunities for growth
- Threats to success

**10. Recommendations:**
- Short-term improvements
- Long-term strategic changes
- Resource needs
- Action plan

**Review Process:**

**Phase 1: Planning (1-2 months)**
- Form review committee
- Define scope and timeline
- Assign responsibilities
- Develop data collection plan

**Phase 2: Data Collection (2-3 months)**
- Gather quantitative data
- Conduct surveys and interviews
- Review documents
- Benchmark against others

**Phase 3: Analysis (1-2 months)**
- Analyze data
- Identify patterns and themes
- Compare to standards
- Develop findings

**Phase 4: Recommendations (1 month)**
- Draft recommendations
- Prioritize actions
- Develop implementation plan
- Estimate resources

**Phase 5: Reporting (1 month)**
- Write program review report
- Present to stakeholders
- Get feedback and approval
- Communicate widely

**Phase 6: Implementation (Ongoing)**
- Execute action plan
- Monitor progress
- Adjust as needed
- Report on implementation

## Case Studies: Continuous Improvement in Action

### Case Study 1: Improving Hands-On Lab Retention

**Context:** CEA program noticed students passing written exams but struggling with practical skills six months later.

**PDSA Cycle 1:**

**PLAN:**
- Hypothesis: Skills not retained without ongoing practice
- Solution: Add "Skills Bootcamp" at program midpoint
- Goal: 90% demonstrate competence 6 months post-training

**DO:**
- Pilot with one cohort (18 students)
- Half-day bootcamp reviewing all lab skills

**STUDY:**
- Results: 72% competent (up from 55%, but below 90% goal)
- Feedback: Helpful but too brief
- Observation: Struggled most with infrequently-used skills

**ACT:**
- Partial success; need more
- Try different approach

**PDSA Cycle 2:**

**PLAN:**
- New hypothesis: Need distributed practice, not massed
- Solution: Weekly 15-min skill practice in ongoing courses
- Goal: 90% competence

**DO:**
- Implement in all courses
- Rotate through skills weekly

**STUDY:**
- Results: 91% competent at 6 months!
- Time cost: Minimal (already meeting)
- Student feedback: "Helpful to keep practicing"

**ACT:**
- Adopt permanently
- Build into all course schedules
- Document in curriculum
- Share with other programs

### Case Study 2: Addressing Low Enrollment

**Context:** New CEA certificate program struggling with low enrollment (12 students vs. 30 capacity).

**Program Review Findings:**
- Marketing limited to website
- Schedule inflexible (daytime only)
- No financial aid information
- Unclear career pathways

**Improvement Plan:**

**Actions Taken:**
1. Market research: Surveyed potential students
2. Added evening and online options
3. Created financial aid guide
4. Developed career pathway map
5. Social media campaign
6. Partnership with workforce development
7. Employer endorsements featured

**Results After One Year:**
- Enrollment: 34 (113% of capacity)
- Demographics: More diverse (working adults, career changers)
- Satisfaction: Increased (flexibility appreciated)
- Challenge: Needed to add section

**Ongoing Improvement:**
- Monitor enrollment trends
- Continue marketing
- Assess quality with growth
- Plan capacity expansion

## Key Takeaways

1. **Continuous improvement is ongoing, incremental, data-driven process**
2. **PDSA cycle provides systematic framework for testing and implementing improvements**
3. **Program review offers comprehensive evaluation every 3-5 years**
4. **Quality metrics (KPIs) track program health and guide decisions**
5. **Culture of improvement requires leadership support, systems, and mindset**
6. **Documentation captures learning and enables sharing**
7. **Improvement should focus on student success and learning outcomes**
