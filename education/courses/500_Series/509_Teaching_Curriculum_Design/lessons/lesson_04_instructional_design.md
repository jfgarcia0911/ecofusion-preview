# Lesson 4: Instructional Design for Technical CEA Content

## Module 4: Applying Instructional Design Models to CEA Education

**Duration:** Week 1 (6 hours total)
- Lecture/Discussion: 2 hours
- Asynchronous Content: 2 hours
- Applied Practice: 2 hours

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Apply the ADDIE model to systematically design CEA instruction
2. Compare instructional design models and select appropriate frameworks
3. Design effective instruction for complex technical concepts
4. Select and sequence instructional methods based on learning objectives
5. Create scaffolded learning experiences that build expertise
6. Develop multimodal instructional materials for diverse learners
7. Integrate technology effectively into CEA instruction

## Key Concepts

### 1. The ADDIE Model

**ADDIE:** Analysis → Design → Development → Implementation → Evaluation

#### Phase 1: Analysis

**Purpose:** Understand the instructional problem and context

**Key Questions:**
- Who are the learners? (background, experience, motivation)
- What do they need to learn? (gap between current and desired performance)
- Why do they need to learn it? (business case, relevance)
- What constraints exist? (time, budget, resources)
- What is the learning environment? (online, in-person, hybrid)

**CEA Example - Designing IPM Training:**

**Learner Analysis:**
- Facility technicians, ages 22-45
- Mix of agriculture and non-agriculture backgrounds
- Some fear of insects/chemicals
- Motivated by job requirements and crop protection

**Need Analysis:**
- Current pest outbreaks causing 15% crop losses
- Staff can't identify pests or choose appropriate controls
- Need to recognize 10 common pests and implement IPM protocols

**Context Analysis:**
- Training must occur during work hours
- Access to greenhouses for hands-on practice
- Limited budget for materials
- Must comply with organic certification standards

#### Phase 2: Design

**Purpose:** Create blueprint for instruction

**Design Components:**

1. **Learning Objectives** (from Lesson 3)
   - What learners will be able to do

2. **Assessment Strategy**
   - How you'll measure achievement
   - Types: formative, summative, performance

3. **Instructional Strategy**
   - Teaching methods and sequence
   - Activities and materials needed
   - Media and technology

4. **Content Outline**
   - Topics and subtopics
   - Logical sequence
   - Time allocations

**CEA Example - IPM Training Design:**

**Objectives:**
- Identify 10 common CEA pests by sight
- Implement weekly scouting protocols
- Select appropriate biological and chemical controls

**Assessment:**
- Pest identification test (photos)
- Demonstration of scouting procedures
- Written IPM protocol for facility

**Instructional Strategy:**
- Lecture: pest biology and damage
- Hands-on: scouting in facility
- Case studies: IPM decisions
- Practice: using identification keys

**Content Outline:**
1. Introduction to IPM philosophy (30 min)
2. Common CEA pests (2 hours)
3. Monitoring and scouting (1 hour)
4. Control options (2 hours)
5. Developing protocols (1 hour)

#### Phase 3: Development

**Purpose:** Create instructional materials

**Deliverables:**
- Lesson plans
- Presentation slides
- Handouts and job aids
- Activities and exercises
- Assessments and rubrics
- Media (videos, animations, graphics)

**Best Practices:**
- Use professional design principles
- Ensure accessibility
- Test materials with sample audience
- Get expert review
- Iterate based on feedback

**CEA Example - IPM Materials:**
- PowerPoint with pest photos and life cycles
- Laminated pest identification cards
- Scouting data sheets
- Video demonstrating scouting technique
- IPM protocol template
- Quiz with photos of pest damage

#### Phase 4: Implementation

**Purpose:** Deliver the instruction

**Preparation:**
- Train instructors
- Prepare facilities and equipment
- Register/enroll students
- Communicate expectations
- Set up technology

**Delivery:**
- Facilitate learning (not just present)
- Monitor engagement and understanding
- Adjust pacing as needed
- Provide feedback
- Troubleshoot issues

**CEA Example - IPM Implementation:**
- Train lead grower to co-facilitate
- Prepare pest specimens and magnifiers
- Schedule during slower production period
- Email pre-reading materials
- Conduct training over two half-days
- Follow up with on-the-job coaching

#### Phase 5: Evaluation

**Purpose:** Assess effectiveness and improve

**Kirkpatrick's Four Levels:**

**Level 1 - Reaction:**
- Did learners like it?
- Was it relevant and useful?
- Tool: End-of-training survey

**Level 2 - Learning:**
- Did they achieve objectives?
- What did they learn?
- Tool: Tests, demonstrations, assessments

**Level 3 - Behavior:**
- Are they applying it on the job?
- Has performance changed?
- Tool: Observation, supervisor surveys, 360 feedback

**Level 4 - Results:**
- Did it achieve business goals?
- What's the ROI?
- Tool: Production data, cost savings, quality metrics

**CEA Example - IPM Evaluation:**

**Level 1:** Survey shows 4.5/5 satisfaction; want more hands-on time
**Level 2:** 90% pass pest ID test; all complete protocols
**Level 3:** Observations show consistent weekly scouting 3 months later
**Level 4:** Crop losses drop from 15% to 5%; $45,000 savings

**Continuous Improvement:**
- Add more hands-on scouting time
- Update with new pest pressures
- Create refresher training
- Develop advanced IPM course

### 2. Other Instructional Design Models

#### SAM (Successive Approximation Model)

**Philosophy:** Iterative, agile approach with rapid prototyping

**Process:**
1. **Savvy Start:** Brainstorm and gather information
2. **Iterative Design:** Create prototype, review, refine
3. **Iterative Development:** Develop, test, refine, repeat

**When to Use:**
- Projects with tight timelines
- E-learning development
- When requirements are unclear
- Need stakeholder buy-in early

**CEA Application:**
Rapid development of online modules for new equipment or procedures

#### Dick and Carey Model

**Philosophy:** Systems approach emphasizing relationships between components

**Key Feature:** Emphasis on analysis of instructional goals and subordinate skills

**When to Use:**
- Academic courses
- Comprehensive curriculum design
- When thorough analysis is needed

#### Kemp Design Model

**Philosophy:** Circular, non-linear approach allowing entry at any point

**When to Use:**
- Revision of existing courses
- Flexible development timeline
- Multiple concurrent projects

### 3. Instructional Strategies and Methods

#### Direct Instruction

**Description:** Teacher-led presentation of content

**Methods:**
- Lectures
- Demonstrations
- Worked examples
- Modeling

**Best For:**
- Introducing new concepts
- Presenting organized information
- Expert modeling of procedures

**CEA Examples:**
- Lecture on plant nutrition principles
- Demonstration of transplanting technique
- Worked example of DLI calculation

**Design Tips:**
- Keep lectures under 15-20 minutes
- Use visuals and examples
- Include engagement activities (think-pair-share)
- Provide note-taking guides

#### Experiential Learning

**Description:** Learning through reflection on doing

**Methods:**
- Hands-on practice
- Laboratory exercises
- Field experiences
- Simulations

**Best For:**
- Developing psychomotor skills
- Applying knowledge
- Problem-solving in context

**CEA Examples:**
- Building small hydroponic systems
- Conducting water quality tests
- Visiting commercial facilities
- Using climate control simulators

**Design Tips:**
- Provide clear objectives and procedures
- Allow time for exploration
- Debrief experiences (reflect)
- Connect to theory

#### Problem-Based Learning (PBL)

**Description:** Learning organized around solving authentic problems

**Process:**
1. Present problem scenario
2. Students identify what they need to learn
3. Research and gather information
4. Develop and test solutions
5. Present findings

**Best For:**
- Developing critical thinking
- Integrating multiple concepts
- Preparing for real-world challenges

**CEA Examples:**
- Diagnose why a crop is failing
- Design system for specific site and crop
- Solve profitability challenge

**Design Tips:**
- Use realistic, complex problems
- Provide appropriate resources
- Facilitate, don't tell answers
- Assess both process and product

#### Collaborative Learning

**Description:** Students working together to achieve learning goals

**Methods:**
- Group projects
- Peer instruction
- Jigsaw activities
- Discussion forums

**Best For:**
- Developing communication skills
- Leveraging diverse perspectives
- Complex problem-solving
- Building learning community

**CEA Examples:**
- Group design of facility layout
- Peer review of growing protocols
- Case study discussions
- Collaborative troubleshooting

**Design Tips:**
- Structure groups intentionally
- Assign roles and responsibilities
- Include individual accountability
- Teach collaboration skills

#### Case-Based Learning

**Description:** Analysis of real or realistic scenarios

**Process:**
1. Present case
2. Analyze situation
3. Identify key issues
4. Propose solutions
5. Justify recommendations

**Best For:**
- Developing analytical skills
- Learning from others' experiences
- Ethical reasoning
- Decision-making

**CEA Examples:**
- Analysis of facility failures (bankruptcies)
- Successful operation case studies
- Food safety incident investigations
- Sustainability challenges

**Design Tips:**
- Use authentic cases with complexity
- Provide sufficient information
- Include discussion questions
- Connect to concepts and principles

### 4. Scaffolding and Progressive Skill Development

#### Scaffolding Principles

**Definition:** Temporary support structures that help learners achieve beyond their current ability

**Types of Scaffolding:**

1. **Procedural:** Step-by-step guides, checklists
2. **Conceptual:** Organizers, models, frameworks
3. **Strategic:** Prompts for thinking processes
4. **Metacognitive:** Reflection guides, self-assessment

**Gradual Release of Responsibility:**

```
I Do (Modeling) →
We Do (Guided Practice) →
You Do Together (Collaborative) →
You Do Alone (Independent)
```

**CEA Example - Learning to Mix Nutrient Solutions:**

**Phase 1 - I Do:**
- Instructor demonstrates process
- Explains each step and why
- Shows calculations and measurements

**Phase 2 - We Do:**
- Instructor and students mix together
- Students suggest next steps
- Instructor guides and corrects

**Phase 3 - You Do Together:**
- Pairs mix solutions
- Use job aid with steps
- Instructor circulates and coaches

**Phase 4 - You Do Alone:**
- Individual students mix solutions
- No job aid (memorized process)
- Instructor observes and assesses

**Fading the Scaffold:**
Gradually remove supports as competence develops

#### Cognitive Load Theory

**Principle:** Working memory is limited; avoid overload

**Types of Cognitive Load:**

1. **Intrinsic Load:** Inherent complexity of material
2. **Extraneous Load:** Poor design that adds unnecessary difficulty
3. **Germane Load:** Effort to build schemas and understanding

**Strategies to Manage Load:**

**Reduce Extraneous Load:**
- Clear, simple visuals
- Eliminate distractions
- Well-organized content
- Redundancy in key information

**Manage Intrinsic Load:**
- Break complex topics into chunks
- Provide worked examples
- Use analogies and familiar contexts
- Sequence from simple to complex

**Optimize Germane Load:**
- Encourage elaboration and connection
- Use varied examples
- Provide practice and feedback
- Facilitate reflection

**CEA Example - Teaching VPD:**

**High Cognitive Load (Poor Design):**
- Complex formulas introduced first
- Multiple variables explained simultaneously
- Technical jargon without definition
- No visual aids

**Managed Cognitive Load (Good Design):**
- Start with familiar concept (sweating)
- Introduce one variable at a time (temp, then humidity)
- Use VPD chart (visual)
- Practice reading chart before calculating
- Gradually introduce formula

### 5. Designing for Different Learning Modalities

#### Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

**Principle 1: Multiple Means of Representation**
Present information in different ways

**CEA Applications:**
- Text descriptions + diagrams + videos
- Written procedures + demonstrations
- Verbal explanations + written notes
- Real examples + simulations

**Principle 2: Multiple Means of Action and Expression**
Allow different ways to demonstrate learning

**CEA Applications:**
- Written reports OR oral presentations
- Individual projects OR group projects
- Traditional tests OR portfolios
- Design drawings OR 3D models

**Principle 3: Multiple Means of Engagement**
Tap into different interests and motivation

**CEA Applications:**
- Choice of crop to focus on
- Real facility problems OR hypothetical scenarios
- Competition OR collaboration
- Self-paced OR cohort-based

#### Addressing Different Learning Preferences

**Visual Learners:**
- Diagrams, charts, graphs
- Color-coding
- Videos and animations
- Mind maps and organizers

**Auditory Learners:**
- Lectures and discussions
- Podcasts and audio recordings
- Verbal instructions
- Group dialogue

**Kinesthetic Learners:**
- Hands-on activities
- Field trips
- Role-playing
- Building and making

**Reading/Writing Learners:**
- Textbooks and articles
- Note-taking
- Written assignments
- Research and reports

**Best Practice:** Incorporate all modalities for multimodal learning

### 6. Integrating Technology in CEA Education

#### Technology Selection Criteria

**Match to Objective:**
Technology should support, not drive, learning objectives

**Example:**
- **Objective:** Analyze climate data to optimize settings
- **Appropriate Tech:** Spreadsheet software, data visualization tools
- **Inappropriate Tech:** VR simulation (overkill for this objective)

**Accessibility:**
Ensure all students can access technology

**Reliability:**
Have backup plans when technology fails

**Value-Add:**
Technology should enhance learning, not just digitize existing methods

**Cost-Effectiveness:**
Consider total cost vs. benefit

#### Educational Technologies for CEA

**Learning Management Systems (LMS):**
- Canvas, Moodle, Blackboard
- Organize content, assignments, grades
- Discussion forums
- Track progress

**Video and Multimedia:**
- Record demonstrations
- Flip classroom (watch lecture at home)
- Virtual facility tours
- Time-lapse plant growth

**Simulation and Modeling:**
- Climate control simulators
- Crop growth models
- Financial pro formas
- System design software

**Data Collection and Analysis:**
- Environmental sensors
- Spreadsheets and databases
- Statistical software
- Data visualization tools

**Communication and Collaboration:**
- Video conferencing (Zoom, Teams)
- Collaborative documents (Google Docs)
- Project management tools (Trello, Asana)
- Discussion platforms (Slack, Discord)

**Assessment Tools:**
- Online quizzes (Quizlet, Kahoot)
- E-portfolios
- Rubric tools
- Peer review systems

**Augmented/Virtual Reality:**
- Virtual facility tours
- Equipment operation training
- Spatial design visualization
- Remote troubleshooting

#### Flipped Classroom Model

**Traditional:** Lecture in class, homework at home
**Flipped:** Video lecture at home, active learning in class

**Benefits:**
- More class time for hands-on work
- Students can pause/rewind lectures
- Instructor available during challenging work
- Differentiated pacing

**CEA Application:**

**At Home:**
- Watch 15-min video on nutrient deficiencies
- Complete online quiz on symptoms
- Read short case study

**In Class:**
- Examine real plants with deficiencies
- Practice diagnosis in small groups
- Develop treatment plans
- Get immediate instructor feedback

### 7. Sequencing Instruction

#### Elaboration Theory

**Principle:** Teach simplest version first, then elaborate

**Zoom Lens Metaphor:**
1. Start with wide-angle view (overview)
2. Zoom in on one topic (detail)
3. Zoom back out (see relationships)
4. Zoom in on next topic
5. Continue pattern

**CEA Example - Hydroponic Systems Course:**

**Week 1:** Overview of all system types (wide angle)
**Week 2:** Deep dive into DWC (zoom in)
**Week 3:** Connect DWC to overview, compare to others (zoom out)
**Week 4:** Deep dive into NFT (zoom in)
**Week 5:** Compare all systems learned (zoom out)

#### Part-Whole Sequencing

**Part-to-Whole:**
- Learn components first
- Then integrate into whole

**Example:** Learn individual environmental factors (light, temp, humidity, CO2) before learning integrated climate management

**Whole-to-Part:**
- Start with big picture
- Then break into components

**Example:** Start with tour of functioning facility (whole), then study each subsystem (parts)

**CEA Best Practice:** Combine approaches
- Whole (facility tour)
- Part (study lighting)
- Part (study climate)
- Whole (integrated facility design)

## Practical Application

### Designing a Lesson Using ADDIE

**Scenario:** Design a 2-hour lesson on "Calculating Crop Cycles and Production Planning"

**Analysis:**
- **Learners:** New facility managers, varied math skills
- **Need:** Must plan production to meet weekly sales commitments
- **Prerequisite:** Basic crop knowledge, spreadsheet skills
- **Context:** In-person classroom with computers

**Design:**

**Objectives:**
1. Calculate days to harvest for different crops and growth methods
2. Determine planting frequency to achieve continuous weekly harvests
3. Create a production schedule showing planting and harvest dates

**Assessment:**
- Practice problems during lesson (formative)
- Create 12-week production schedule for assigned crops (summative)

**Instructional Sequence:**
1. Hook: Show example of running out of product or overproduction (5 min)
2. Direct instruction: Introduce concept and formula (15 min)
3. Worked example: Instructor calculates lettuce cycle (10 min)
4. Guided practice: Class calculates together for basil (15 min)
5. Independent practice: Students calculate for 3 crops (30 min)
6. Collaborative application: Pairs create integrated schedule (30 min)
7. Share and discuss: Present schedules, troubleshoot (10 min)
8. Summary and connection: Review concepts, next steps (5 min)

**Materials:**
- PowerPoint with formulas and examples
- Crop data sheet (days to maturity)
- Excel template for scheduling
- Practice problem worksheet
- Rubric for production schedule

**Development:**
[Create all materials listed above]

**Implementation:**
- Set up computer lab
- Test Excel template
- Prepare handouts
- Arrange seating for pair work

**Evaluation:**
- Observe during practice (Are they getting it?)
- Review completed schedules (Quality of work?)
- Exit ticket: "What's still confusing?" (Gaps to address?)
- Follow-up survey 2 weeks later (Are they using this?)

## Key Takeaways

1. **ADDIE provides systematic framework for instructional design**
2. **Analysis phase is critical - understand learners and context before designing**
3. **Multiple instructional methods exist; select based on objectives and content**
4. **Scaffold learning by gradually releasing responsibility**
5. **Manage cognitive load by chunking, sequencing, and clear design**
6. **Technology should enhance, not replace, good instruction**
7. **Evaluation drives continuous improvement of instruction**
