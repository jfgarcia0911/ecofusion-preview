# Activity: Comprehensive Feasibility Study Project

## Overview

This capstone project requires you to develop a complete feasibility study for a CEA project, applying all methodologies and frameworks learned in the course. This is your opportunity to demonstrate mastery of consulting skills through a realistic engagement simulation.

**Time Commitment:** 40-60 hours
**Format:** Individual or team (2-3 people)
**Deliverables:** Full feasibility study package
**Weight:** 25% of final course grade

---

## PROJECT SCENARIO

### Client Background

**Client:** GreenVenture Capital Partners
**Contact:** Maria Chen, Investment Director

**Situation:**
GreenVenture is evaluating an investment in a vertical farming operation to serve the Seattle metropolitan area. They have identified a potential site and initial business concept but need comprehensive feasibility analysis before proceeding with a $12M investment.

**Your Role:**
Lead consultant conducting 8-week feasibility study

**Budget:** $85,000 (your engagement fee - for simulation purposes)

---

## PROJECT SPECIFICATIONS

### Proposed Project Details

**Facility:**
- Location: Seattle, Washington
- Site: 40,000 sq ft industrial building available for lease
- Technology: Multi-layer vertical farming (leafy greens focus)
- Target Production: 120,000 kg/year (steady state)
- Crops: Baby lettuce mix, arugula, spinach, herbs

**Investment:**
- Total Capital Budget: $12M
- Investor seeking: 20%+ IRR, <7 year payback
- Debt financing: Up to 40% of capital
- Timeline: Decision in 3 months, operational in 18 months if proceed

**Market Context:**
- Seattle population: 750,000 (metro: 4M)
- High-income, food-conscious demographic
- Strong restaurant and retail sectors
- Existing local farms: mostly conventional, some greenhouses
- Limited existing vertical farms (1-2 small operations)
- Strong sustainability values
- Premium pricing potential

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q: Can I work in a team?**
A: Yes, teams of 2-3 allowed. Clearly define roles and contributions.

**Q: How realistic does the scenario need to be?**
A: As realistic as possible. Use actual data where available, reasonable assumptions where not. Document all assumptions.

**Q: What if I can't conduct actual interviews?**
A: You may simulate interviews based on research, use publicly available information, or conduct informational interviews with industry contacts if accessible.

**Q: How detailed should the financial model be?**
A: Comprehensive enough to support decision-making. Include all major cost categories, multiple scenarios, sensitivity analysis.

**Q: Can I choose a different project scenario?**
A: With instructor approval, yes. Must be comparable in scope and complexity.

**Q: What's most important for grading?**
A: Quality of analysis and recommendations. Rigorous methodology, data-driven conclusions, actionable insights, professional presentation.
