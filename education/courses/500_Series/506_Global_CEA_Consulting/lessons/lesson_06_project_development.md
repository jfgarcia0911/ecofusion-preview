# Lesson 6: Project Development Consulting

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Guide clients through complete CEA project development lifecycle
- Manage multi-disciplinary teams and stakeholder coordination
- Navigate procurement, vendor management, and contractor relationships
- Oversee construction, installation, and commissioning processes
- Identify and mitigate project risks and issues
- Ensure successful project delivery on time and budget

## Introduction

Project development consulting involves guiding clients through the complex process of developing CEA facilities from concept through operation. This requires coordinating architects, engineers, contractors, equipment vendors, and other specialists while managing budgets, schedules, and stakeholder expectations. Successful project development consultants bring deep technical knowledge, project management expertise, and the ability to navigate the challenges inherent in complex capital projects.

## 1. Project Development Lifecycle

### Phase 1: Concept and Planning (2-4 months)

**Activities:**
- Business case development and validation
- Preliminary design and space programming
- Site identification and evaluation
- Budget and schedule development
- Stakeholder alignment
- Funding and financing strategy

**Deliverables:**
- Project charter and business case
- Conceptual design
- Preliminary budget ($-20%/+30% accuracy)
- High-level schedule
- Risk register
- Go/no-go decision point

**Consultant Role:**
- Facilitate planning process
- Technical expertise and input
- Market and industry insights
- Team assembly recommendations
- Risk identification
- Decision support

### Phase 2: Design Development (3-6 months)

**Activities:**
- Detailed facility design
- Engineering (MEP, structural, civil)

- Permitting and regulatory approvals
- Detailed cost estimation
- Procurement strategy development

**Deliverables:**
- Design development drawings
- Technical specifications
- Equipment selection
- Permit applications
- Detailed budget ($-10%/+15% accuracy)
- Procurement plan

**Consultant Role:**
- Design review and optimization
- Technology evaluation and selection
- Value engineering
- Constructability review
- Cost validation
- Permit support

### Phase 3: Procurement (2-4 months)

**Activities:**
- RFP/bid package development
- Contractor and vendor selection
- Contract negotiation
- Vendor qualification
- Long-lead item procurement
- Owner's representative selection

**Deliverables:**
- Bid packages
- Vendor and contractor selections
- Executed contracts
- Procurement schedule
- Vendor qualification reports

**Consultant Role:**
- RFP development support
- Bid evaluation
- Vendor technical qualification

## 2. Stakeholder Management

### Key Stakeholders

**Internal Stakeholders:**
- **Project Owner/Investor**: Ultimate decision maker, funding source
- **Executive Leadership**: Strategic direction, resource allocation
- **Project Manager**: Day-to-day project coordination
- **Operations Team**: End users, operational requirements
- **Finance/Accounting**: Budget, cash flow, financial reporting
- **Legal/Compliance**: Contracts, permits, regulatory compliance

**External Stakeholders:**
- **Architect**: Building design and aesthetics
- **Engineers**: MEP, structural, civil engineering
- **General Contractor**: Construction management and coordination
- **Equipment Vendors**: Growing systems, HVAC, lighting, controls
- **Technology Providers**: Software, automation, specialty systems
- **Permitting Authorities**: Building, health, environmental agencies
- **Lenders/Investors**: Funding sources, oversight requirements
- **Neighbors/Community**: Local impact, community relations

### Stakeholder Engagement Strategy

**Stakeholder Analysis Matrix:**

| Stakeholder | Interest | Influence | Strategy |
|-------------|----------|-----------|----------|
| Project Owner | Very High | Very High | Manage Closely |
| Lenders | High | High | Keep Satisfied |
| General Contractor | High | High | Manage Closely |
| Equipment Vendors | High | Medium | Keep Informed |
| Permitting Authorities | Medium | High | Keep Satisfied |
| Community | Low-Medium | Low-Medium | Monitor |

**Engagement Tactics:**
- **Manage Closely**: Regular meetings, detailed reporting, collaborative decision-making
- **Keep Satisfied**: Regular updates, responsive to concerns, involve in key decisions
- **Keep Informed**: Periodic updates, transparency on progress and issues
- **Monitor**: Awareness of concerns, responsive when issues arise

### Communication Management

**Communication Plan:**

**Weekly Project Team Meeting:**
- Attendees: Owner, PM, consultants, key vendors
- Duration: 1-2 hours
- Agenda: Progress update, upcoming activities, issues/risks, decisions needed
- Format: In-person or video conference
- Documentation: Meeting minutes, action items

**Monthly Steering Committee:**
- Attendees: Owner exec team, PM, lead consultant, major contractors
- Duration: 2-3 hours
- Agenda: Progress vs. plan, budget status, major issues, key decisions
- Format: In-person presentation
- Documentation: Executive summary, dashboard

**Progress Reports:**
- Frequency: Weekly or bi-weekly
- Distribution: All stakeholders
- Content: Schedule status, budget status, accomplishments, upcoming activities, issues
- Format: Standardized template, dashboard format

**Issue Escalation:**
- Level 1 (Normal): PM resolves, documented in weekly report
- Level 2 (Significant): PM escalates to steering committee, resolution plan developed
- Level 3 (Critical): Immediate escalation to owner, consultant support for resolution

## 3. Design Review and Optimization

### Design Review Process

**Conceptual Design Review:**

**Focus Areas:**
- Alignment with business objectives
- Space program and functional requirements
- Production capacity validation
- Budget order of magnitude
- Schedule feasibility
- Major risk identification

**Review Checklist:**
- Production goals achievable with design?
- Layout supports efficient workflow?
- Expansion provisions adequate?
- Site constraints addressed?
- Budget realistic for concept?
- Major risks identified and manageable?

**Design Development Review:**

**Technical Review:**
- Growing systems design and capacity
- Environmental control systems sizing
- Electrical loads and service capacity
- Water systems and treatment
- Structural adequacy
- Building envelope performance

**Operational Review:**
- Workflow and material flow
- Ergonomics and safety
- Maintenance access
- Hygiene zones and food safety
- Flexibility and adaptability

**Constructability Review:**
- Construction sequence and staging
- Access for installation
- Long-lead items identified
- Code compliance
- Coordination between disciplines

**Value Engineering:**

**Methodology:**
- Identify high-cost elements
- Develop alternatives maintaining function
- Evaluate cost vs. benefit trade-offs
- Recommend optimizations
- Document cost savings

**Example VE Opportunities:**

```
VE Item #1: Structural System
Current Design: Steel frame with concrete block
Alternative: Pre-engineered metal building
Cost Savings: $280,000 (22%)
Performance Impact: Equivalent structure, faster construction
Risk: Limited architectural flexibility
Recommendation: ACCEPT

VE Item #2: Growing System
Current Design: Custom stainless steel racking
Alternative: Standard industrial racking with food-grade coating
Cost Savings: $150,000 (35%)
Performance Impact: Equivalent functionality, proven in industry
Risk: May require more frequent replacement
Recommendation: ACCEPT with enhanced maintenance plan

VE Item #3: HVAC Controls
Current Design: Proprietary integrated system
Alternative: Standard BMS with custom programming
Cost Savings: $45,000 (30%)
Performance Impact: Equal capability, more flexibility
Risk: Integration complexity
Recommendation: ACCEPT with expert systems integrator

Total VE Savings: $475,000 (18% of construction budget)
```

## 4. Procurement and Vendor Management

### Procurement Strategy

**Delivery Methods:**

**Design-Bid-Build (Traditional):**
- Owner contracts separately with designer and contractor
- Sequential process: complete design, then bid, then build
- Clear scope definition, competitive bidding
- Longer timeline, less contractor input in design

**Design-Build:**
- Single contract for design and construction
- Contractor involved earlier, integrated team
- Faster timeline, better cost certainty
- Less owner control, requires strong contract

**Construction Manager at Risk:**
- CM provides input during design, guaranteed maximum price
- Balance of owner control and contractor collaboration
- Good for complex projects
- Requires experienced CM

**Turnkey/EPCM:**
- Single vendor for complete facility
- Simplicity, single point of responsibility
- Common for container farms and packaged systems
- Less flexibility, potentially higher cost

**Procurement Approach Selection:**

| Factor | Design-Bid-Build | Design-Build | CM at Risk | Turnkey |
|--------|------------------|--------------|------------|---------|
| Owner Control | High | Medium | Medium-High | Low |
| Cost Certainty | Medium | High | High | Highest |
| Schedule | Longer | Shorter | Medium | Shortest |
| Quality Control | High | Medium | High | Variable |
| Complexity Handling | Good | Good | Best | Limited |
| Owner Risk | Higher | Lower | Medium | Lowest |

## 5. Construction Oversight

### Quality Assurance

**QA Program Elements:**

**Inspection and Testing:**
- Material testing and verification
- Installation inspection
- System testing and commissioning
- Documentation and reporting

**Non-Conformance Management:**

- Verification of correction

**Documentation:**
- Inspection reports and logs
- Test results and certificates
- Non-conformance reports
- As-built drawings
- O&M manuals and warranties

### Progress Monitoring

**Schedule Tracking:**

**Critical Path Method (CPM):**
- Identify critical activities
- Monitor progress vs. baseline
- Analyze delays and impacts
- Forecast completion dates
- Develop recovery plans if needed

**Earned Value Management:**
- Track planned vs. actual progress
- Identify schedule and cost variances
- Calculate performance indices
- Forecast completion cost and date
- Early warning of problems

**Progress Reporting:**


### Change Management

**Change Order Process:**

**Step 1: Change Identification**
- Scope change request
- Design change
- Field condition
- Owner requested addition/modification

**Step 2: Impact Assessment**
- Cost impact analysis
- Schedule impact analysis
- Technical impact review
- Cascading effects evaluation

**Step 3: Review and Approval**
- Consultant technical review
- Owner budget review
- Negotiation if needed
- Approval or rejection decision

**Step 4: Implementation**

- Revise schedule if needed

## 6. Commissioning and Performance Validation

### Commissioning Process

**Pre-Functional Testing:**
- Equipment installation verification
- Control point checkout
- Safety system testing
- Sequence of operations verification

**Functional Performance Testing:**
- Equipment performance at design conditions
- System integration and control
- Capacity and efficiency verification
- Safety and alarm testing

**Integrated Systems Testing:**
- Full system operation under various conditions
- Interaction between systems
- Control system optimization
- Environmental uniformity verification

**Performance Validation:**

**Test Plan Example - HVAC System:**

```
TEST #1: Cooling Capacity
Objective: Verify cooling capacity meets design (500 kW)
Conditions: Full lighting load, outside design conditions
Acceptance: Maintain 22°C ± 1°C in all zones
Procedure:
1. Establish full heat load conditions
2. Monitor temperatures at 20 sensor locations
3. Verify airflow rates
4. Measure power consumption
5. Calculate delivered cooling capacity
Success Criteria:
- All zones within 22°C ± 1°C
- Cooling capacity ≥ 500 kW
- Energy efficiency within 10% of specification

TEST #2: Humidity Control
Objective: Verify dehumidification capacity (4,000 L/day)
Conditions: Full irrigation, crop transpiration load
Acceptance: Maintain 70% RH ± 5%
[Detailed procedure...]

TEST #3: Temperature Uniformity
Objective: Verify temperature uniformity across growing area
Conditions: Normal operating conditions
Acceptance: ≤ 2°C variation across zones
[Detailed procedure...]

[Additional tests for lighting, irrigation, controls, etc.]
```

## 7. Risk Management

### Project Risk Categories

**Technical Risks:**
- Design errors or omissions
- Technology performance issues
- Integration challenges
- Geotechnical surprises
- Utility capacity limitations

**Schedule Risks:**
- Permitting delays
- Weather delays
- Material delivery delays
- Labor availability
- Vendor performance issues

**Budget Risks:**
- Cost estimation errors
- Change orders and scope creep
- Market price escalation
- Hidden conditions
- Performance penalties

**External Risks:**
- Regulatory changes
- Economic conditions
- Supply chain disruptions
- Neighbor or community opposition
- Force majeure events

### Risk Response Strategies

**Risk Response Matrix:**

```
RISK REGISTER - TOP 10 RISKS

RISK | PROBABILITY | IMPACT | SCORE | RESPONSE | OWNER
Equipment delivery delay | High | High | 20 | Mitigate: Order early, expedite | PM
Permit approval delay | Medium | High | 15 | Mitigate: Early engagement, expert help | Owner
Construction cost overrun | Medium | High | 15 | Mitigate: Contingency, VE, monitoring | Consultant
HVAC performance shortfall | Low | Critical | 15 | Transfer: Performance guarantee | PM
Skilled labor shortage | High | Medium | 12 | Mitigate: Early contractor selection | GC
Soil conditions | Low | High | 10 | Accept: Contingency budget | Owner
Technology integration issues | Medium | Medium | 9 | Mitigate: Early testing, expert support | Consultant
Weather delays | Medium | Medium | 9 | Accept: Float in schedule | GC
Code interpretation issues | Low | Medium | 6 | Mitigate: Early discussions with AHJ | Architect
Community opposition | Low | Medium | 6 | Mitigate: Community engagement plan | Owner

Risk Response Strategies:
- Avoid: Eliminate risk by changing approach
- Mitigate: Reduce probability or impact
- Transfer: Shift risk (insurance, guarantees, contracts)
- Accept: Acknowledge and plan for contingency
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Project development lifecycle** encompasses concept through commissioning with consultant roles evolving from advisor to technical expert to owner advocate

2. **Stakeholder management** requires identifying interests and influence, developing engagement strategies, and maintaining effective communication throughout the project

3. **Design review and optimization** ensures technical adequacy, operational efficiency, and cost-effectiveness through systematic review and value engineering

4. **Procurement strategy** selection (design-bid-build, design-build, CM at risk, turnkey) depends on project characteristics, owner capabilities, and risk tolerance

5. **Vendor selection** uses structured evaluation of technical capability, financial strength, project approach, commercial terms, and references

6. **Construction oversight** involves quality assurance, progress monitoring, change management, and contractor coordination to ensure successful delivery

7. **Commissioning and validation** systematically tests and verifies system performance against design criteria and identifies deficiencies for resolution

8. **Risk management** identifies, assesses, and develops response strategies for technical, schedule, budget, and external risks throughout the project
