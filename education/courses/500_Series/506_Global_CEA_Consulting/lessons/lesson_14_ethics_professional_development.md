## 1. Ethical Frameworks

### Core Ethical Principles

**1. Client Interest First**
- Prioritize client benefit over personal gain
- Provide honest, objective advice even when contrary to self-interest
- Disclose conflicts and limitations
- Decline engagements where you can't deliver value
- Act as fiduciary for client's best interests

**2. Competence and Quality**
- Only accept work within your competence
- Maintain high standards of quality
- Stay current with knowledge and skills
- Seek help when needed
- Continuous professional development

**3. Integrity and Honesty**
- Truthful in all representations
- Don't overpromise or guarantee outcomes
- Acknowledge limitations and mistakes
- Transparent fees and terms
- Honor all commitments

**4. Independence and Objectivity**
- Provide unbiased recommendations
- Disclose relationships influencing advice
- Avoid conflicts of interest
- Don't accept vendor compensation (without disclosure)
- Maintain professional skepticism

**5. Confidentiality**
- Protect client proprietary information
- Secure handling of sensitive data
- Don't share client information without permission
- Respect NDAs and confidentiality agreements
- Maintain confidentiality after engagement ends

**6. Professional Conduct**
- Respectful treatment of all parties
- Honest competition with other consultants
- Comply with laws and regulations
- Professional appearance and behavior
- Contribute positively to profession

### Ethical Decision-Making Framework

**When Facing Ethical Dilemma:**

**Step 1: Recognize the Ethical Issue**
- Identify the stakeholders affected
- Understand competing interests
- Recognize principles in conflict
- Acknowledge personal biases

**Step 2: Get the Facts**
- What do you know for certain?
- What do you need to know?
- Who should be involved in decision?
- What policies or guidelines apply?

**Step 3: Evaluate Alternative Actions**
- What are the options?
- How do they align with ethical principles?
- What are consequences of each?
- Which stakeholders benefit or harmed?
- What would respected mentors advise?

**Step 4: Make a Decision and Test It**
- Which option best aligns with principles?
- Apply tests:
  - **Transparency Test**: Would you be comfortable if this became public?
  - **Role Reversal Test**: Would you accept this if roles were reversed?
  - **Colleague Test**: Would you be comfortable explaining this to colleagues?
  - **Gut Test**: Does this feel right?

**Step 5: Act and Reflect**
- Implement decision confidently
- Document reasoning if appropriate
- Monitor outcomes
- Reflect on lessons learned

## 2. Common Ethical Dilemmas

### Dilemma 1: Competing Clients

**Situation:**
You've been engaged by Company A to assess market opportunity in City X. Company B, a competitor of Company A, approaches you about similar work in the same market.

**Ethical Issues:**
- Conflict of interest
- Confidential information
- Client loyalty
- Business opportunity cost

**Analysis:**
- **Transparency**: Must disclose to both parties
- **Information Barriers**: Can you truly separate knowledge?
- **Client Benefit**: Does engagement with B harm A?

**Options:**
1. Decline Company B, citing conflict
2. Disclose to both, get permission from both to proceed with safeguards
3. Delay Company B until A engagement complete (if timing allows)

**Recommended Approach:**
Most conservative: Decline B citing conflict. Alternative: Full disclosure to both, strict information barriers, obtain explicit consent. Never: Accept B without disclosure to A.

### Dilemma 2: Vendor Relationships

**Situation:**
Technology vendor you've recommended to clients offers you 10% commission on sales resulting from your recommendations.

**Ethical Issues:**
- Conflict of interest
- Independence and objectivity
- Client trust
- Transparency

**Analysis:**
- Does compensation influence recommendations?
- Would clients feel misled if they knew?
- Does this undermine your independence?

**Options:**
1. Decline vendor compensation
2. Accept but disclose fully to all clients before recommendations
3. Accept and don't disclose (NEVER acceptable)
4. Accept modest compensation (e.g., nominal gift) with disclosure

**Recommended Approach:**
Best practice: Decline vendor compensation. If accepting: Full disclosure in writing before making recommendations, allow client to adjust your compensation if concerned.

### Dilemma 3: Confidential Information

**Situation:**
While working with Client A, you learn they're about to make a major acquisition that would significantly impact Client B (no competitive conflict). Client B would benefit from knowing this information for their planning.

**Ethical Issues:**
- Confidentiality obligations
- Conflicting client interests
- Use of confidential information
- Professional boundaries

**Analysis:**
- Client A's information is confidential
- Sharing would violate confidentiality
- Client B's interests don't override confidentiality
- General market knowledge vs. specific client information

**Recommended Approach:**
Maintain strict confidentiality. Do not share Client A's information with Client B. You may provide general market insights ("M&A activity in sector is increasing") without revealing specific confidential information. When in doubt, err on side of confidentiality.

### Dilemma 4: Beyond Your Expertise

**Situation:**
Long-standing client asks for advice on complex legal/regulatory matter outside your expertise. You could research and provide general guidance, or refer to specialist (client may not hire specialist due to cost).

**Ethical Issues:**
- Competence boundaries
- Client relationship and trust
- Value delivery
- Potential harm from inadequate advice

**Analysis:**
- Legal/regulatory advice requires specialized expertise
- Risk of inadequate or incorrect guidance
- Duty to client for competent advice
- Relationship value vs. professional standards

**Options:**
1. Decline and refer to specialist
2. Provide general educational information with strong caveats
3. Partner with specialist to provide integrated support
4. Research and provide advice (risky)

**Recommended Approach:**
Refer to specialist while offering to help client find right expert or work collaboratively with specialist if appropriate. Maintain relationship through appropriate role, not by exceeding competence.

### Dilemma 5: Unfavorable Findings

**Situation:**
Feasibility study reveals project is unlikely to succeed (poor returns, high risks). Client is emotionally invested and pressures you to present more favorable conclusions.

**Ethical Issues:**
- Objectivity and honesty
- Client relationship and satisfaction
- Professional integrity
- Potential client harm from biased advice

**Analysis:**
- Professional duty to provide honest assessment
- Client may be disappointed but needs truth
- Your credibility and reputation at stake
- Client's financial interests require honest advice

**Recommended Approach:**
Maintain professional objectivity. Present findings honestly with clear supporting rationale. Acknowledge client's disappointment but explain duty to provide objective assessment. Explore if any adjustments or alternatives could improve viability without compromising integrity. If client insists on altered conclusions, decline and document situation.

## 3. Professional Standards

### Institute of Management Consultants Code of Ethics

**Clients:**
1. Serve with integrity, competence, and objectivity
2. Establish realistic expectations and avoid misrepresentation
3. Perform services on agreed terms and protect confidential information
4. Avoid conflicts of interest and disclose unavoidable conflicts

**Engagements:**
5. Accept only engagements qualified to perform
6. Assign qualified staff to engagements
7. Ensure work is properly planned, executed, and supervised
8. Provide meaningful, actionable recommendations

**Fees:**
9. Charge reasonable fees commensurate with services and value
10. Disclose basis for fees and any third-party compensation
11. Avoid contingency fees that compromise objectivity
12. Don't pay or receive referral fees without disclosure

**Profession:**
13. Support fellow consultants in professional development
14. Respect intellectual property of others
15. Don't engage in advertising that is false or misleading
16. Report consultant misconduct to appropriate bodies

## 4. Professional Liability and Risk Management

### Understanding Professional Liability

**Professional Liability Exposures:**
- Errors or omissions in advice
- Breach of confidentiality
- Conflicts of interest
- Scope or budget overruns
- Missed deadlines or commitments
- Misrepresentation of capabilities
- Intellectual property issues

**Risk Management Strategies:**

**1. Professional Liability Insurance**
- Coverage: Errors and omissions (E&O)
- Typical coverage: $1-5M depending on practice size
- Annual cost: $2,000-10,000+ based on revenue and risk
- Essential for: Any consulting practice
- Key features: Prior acts coverage, defense costs, aggregate limits

**2. Clear Contracts and Agreements**
- Scope clearly defined with exclusions
- Deliverables and acceptance criteria
- Assumptions documented
- Limitation of liability clauses
- Disclaimer language where appropriate
- Payment terms and conditions
- Dispute resolution procedures

**3. Documentation and Communication**
- Document advice and rationale
- Confirm key conversations in writing
- Maintain project records
- Communication trails (emails, minutes)
- Client sign-offs on key decisions
- File retention policies

**4. Quality Control**
- Peer review of deliverables
- Fact checking and verification
- Multiple eyes on critical work
- Client review and feedback
- Continuous improvement

**5. Professional Conduct**
- Stay within competence boundaries
- Honest about limitations
- Proper credentials and representations
- Ethical decision-making
- Professional continuing education

### When Things Go Wrong

**Client Dissatisfaction:**
- Address promptly and professionally
- Listen without defensiveness
- Understand their perspective
- Develop corrective action plan
- Make it right when possible
- Document situation and resolution

**Errors or Mistakes:**
- Acknowledge promptly and honestly
- Assess impact and severity
- Notify professional liability insurer if significant
- Work with client on remediation
- Learn and improve processes
- Document lessons learned

**Disputes:**
- Attempt direct resolution first
- Mediation before litigation if possible
- Document all communications
- Consult legal counsel if appropriate
- Involve insurance if applicable
- Maintain professionalism throughout
