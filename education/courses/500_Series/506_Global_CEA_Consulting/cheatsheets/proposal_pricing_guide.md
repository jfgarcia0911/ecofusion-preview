# Cheatsheet: Proposal & Pricing Quick Guide

## PROPOSAL STRUCTURE

### Standard Proposal Outline (10-20 pages)

**1. Executive Summary (1-2 pages)**
- Client situation and needs
- Your solution overview
- Key qualifications
- Investment and timeline

**2. Understanding (2-3 pages)**
- Demonstrates you "get it"
- Client's challenges and goals
- Context and constraints
- Success criteria

**3. Approach (3-5 pages)**
- Methodology and framework
- Phases and activities
- Timeline and milestones
- Deliverables

**4. Team & Qualifications (2-3 pages)**
- Project team and roles
- Relevant experience
- Case studies
- Why choose us

**5. Investment (1-2 pages)**
- Fee structure
- Payment terms
- Expenses and assumptions
- Contract terms

## PRICING METHODOLOGIES

### 1. Hourly/Daily Rate
```
Formula: Hours × Rate = Fee

Pros: Simple, flexible, transparent
Cons: Caps value, commoditizes expertise
Use: Ongoing advisory, uncertain scope

Rates:
Principal: $400-500/hr or $3,000-4,000/day
Senior: $250-350/hr or $2,000-2,800/day
Consultant: $175-250/hr or $1,400-2,000/day
Associate: $100-175/hr or $800-1,400/day
```

### 2. Project Fixed Fee
```
Formula:
Estimated Hours × Blended Rate + Contingency + Profit = Fee

Example:
200 hrs × $200/hr = $40,000
Contingency (15%) = $6,000
Profit (30%) = $13,800
Total Fee = $60,000

Pros: Clear investment, incentivizes efficiency
Cons: Risk of underestimating
Use: Most consulting engagements
```

### 3. Value-Based Pricing
```
Formula: % of Value Delivered to Client

Example:
Client savings from optimization: $500,000/year
Fee: 20% of first-year savings = $100,000

Pros: Aligns with client value, higher fees
Cons: Hard to quantify, requires agreement
Use: High-impact engagements with measurable value
```

### 4. Retainer
```
Formula: Monthly Fee for Defined Availability

Example:
$8,000/month includes:
- Monthly strategy meeting (4 hrs)
- Ad-hoc support (up to 6 hrs/month)
- Document review
- Email/phone access

Pros: Recurring revenue, relationship depth
Cons: Scope definition challenging
Use: Ongoing advisory relationships
```

### 5. Success-Based
```
Formula: Base Fee + Performance Bonus

Example:
Base: $40,000
Bonus: 20% of realized savings
Target savings: $400,000
Potential bonus: $80,000
Total potential: $120,000

Pros: Aligns interests, reduces client risk
Cons: Outcome depends on many factors
Use: Performance improvement engagements
```

## RATE CALCULATION

### Solo Consultant
```
Target Income: $200,000
Benefits/Taxes (35%): $70,000
Overhead (25%): $50,000
Profit (20%): $40,000
Total Required: $360,000

Billable Hours: 1,200 (60% of 2,000)
Required Rate: $300/hour
Or: $2,400/day

Check: Does this align with market?
```

### Typical Engagement Fees
- Feasibility Study: $50,000-150,000
- Due Diligence: $40,000-100,000
- Market Assessment: $30,000-75,000
- Operational Improvement: $50,000-200,000
- Monthly Retainer: $5,000-15,000

## PRICING DECISIONS

### Increase Price For:
- High client value/ROI
- Specialized expertise required
- Urgent timeline
- Difficult/risky work
- High-demand period
- Strategic/complex work
- Small projects (overhead)

### Decrease Price For:
- Strategic relationship building
- Portfolio case study value
- Learning opportunity
- Large multi-project volume
- Off-peak utilization
- Strong existing relationship

## COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION

### When NOT Lowest Price

**Key Message:**
"Our fee reflects superior value through:"

1. **Deeper Expertise**
- 15 CEA projects vs. 2 for alternatives
- Specialized knowledge in [specific area]
- Proven methodologies

2. **Better Team**
- Led by [impressive credentials]
- vs. junior staff on competitor teams

3. **More Comprehensive**
- Includes [additional scope]
- More thorough analysis
- Better deliverables

4. **Lower Risk**
- Higher accuracy (95% vs. 70%)
- Proven track record
- Performance guarantees

5. **Better Outcomes**
- Clients proceed with confidence
- ROI far exceeds fee difference

## NEGOTIATION RESPONSES

### "Your price is too high"

**Response Template:**
"Help me understand - is it outside your budget, or is the concern about value for the price?

[If Budget]
Let's look at scope options:
1. Reduce scope to [reduced version] = $X
2. Phased approach starting with [Phase 1] = $X
3. [Alternative] with trade-offs in [areas] = $X

[If Value]
Here's the value you're getting:
- [Specific benefit 1 worth $X]
- [Specific benefit 2 worth $Y]
- [Specific benefit 3 worth $Z]

Based on our experience, this typically delivers [10x, 20x] the fee through [specific mechanism].

What would make you confident in the value?"

### "Can you include [more scope]?"

**Response Template:**
"I'd be happy to include [additional scope]. Let me assess what that involves...

That would add approximately [X hours/effort] because [reasoning].

Options:
1. Add to scope: $[amount], [timeline impact]
2. Trade-off: Include [addition] but reduce [something] to stay in budget
3. Separate phase: Address in follow-on project

What works best for your priorities?"

### "Competitor quoted less"

**Response Template:**
"Thanks for sharing that. Without seeing their proposal, let me ask:
- Are the scopes similar?
- Is team composition comparable?
- Are deliverables the same?

[Compare side-by-side]

I'm confident in our value. If they're truly offering comparable service at lower cost, that may be right for you. But often differences justify the price differential.

Let's review what each offers so you can make an informed decision."

## PAYMENT TERMS

### Standard Structure
```
Milestone payments:
30% - Contract execution (deposit)
40% - Midpoint milestone
30% - Final deliverable

Or:

25% - Contract execution
25% - Phase 1 completion
25% - Phase 2 completion
25% - Final delivery
```

### Terms and Conditions
- Payment due: Net 30 days
- Late fees: 1.5% per month
- Expenses: Reimbursed at cost or with markup
- Changes: Formal change order process
- IP: Client owns deliverables, you retain methodologies
- Liability: Limit to fees paid
- Termination: Notice period, payment for work completed
