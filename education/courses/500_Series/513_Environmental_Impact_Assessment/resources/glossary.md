# Environmental Impact Assessment Glossary

**Course:** 513 - Environmental Impact Assessment
**Purpose:** Comprehensive terminology reference for environmental assessment, LCA, and sustainability

---

## A

**Abiotic Depletion:** The consumption of non-renewable resources such as minerals and fossil fuels.

**Acidification Potential (AP):** A measure of the potential contribution of substances to acid rain and soil/water acidification, typically expressed in kg SO₂ equivalents.

**Adaptive Management:** A structured approach to managing resources under uncertainty by implementing actions as experiments and adjusting based on results.

**Allocation:** The partitioning of input or output flows of a process or product system between the product system under study and one or more other product systems (ISO 14044).

**Anthropogenic:** Originating from human activity.

**Assessment Boundary:** See System Boundary.

---

## B

**BAT (Best Available Techniques):** The most effective and advanced stage in the development of activities and their methods of operation which indicates the practical suitability of particular techniques for preventing or reducing emissions and impacts on the environment.

**Baseline:** The existing environmental, social, and economic conditions against which project impacts are assessed.

**Biodiversity:** The variability among living organisms from all sources including terrestrial, marine, and other aquatic ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; includes diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems.

**Biogeochemical Cycle:** The cyclical movement of chemical elements through the biosphere, atmosphere, lithosphere, and hydrosphere.

**Blue Water:** Surface and groundwater that is consumed through evaporation, incorporation into products, or taken out of one drainage basin and returned to another or the sea.

---

## C

**Carbon Footprint:** The total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly and indirectly by an activity or accumulated over the life cycle of a product, expressed in kg CO₂ equivalents.

**Carbon Neutrality:** Achieving net zero carbon dioxide emissions by balancing emissions with removal or elimination.

**Carrying Capacity:** The maximum population size that an environment can sustain indefinitely given available resources.

**Characterization:** Calculation of the magnitude of the contribution of each classified input/output to their respective impact categories, and aggregation of contributions within each category (ISO 14044).

**Characterization Factor:** A factor derived from a characterization model which is applied to convert an assigned life cycle inventory result to the common unit of the category indicator (ISO 14044).

**Classification:** Assignment of inventory results to impact categories (ISO 14044).

**Cradle-to-Gate:** An assessment of a partial product life cycle from resource extraction (cradle) to the factory gate.

**Cradle-to-Grave:** An assessment of a full product life cycle from resource extraction (cradle) to use phase and disposal (grave).

**Critical Habitat:** Areas with high biodiversity value, including habitat of significant importance to threatened species, ecosystems with restricted ranges, critically threatened ecosystems, or areas with key biodiversity importance.

**Cumulative Effects:** Environmental changes that result from the incremental impact of the action when added to other past, present, and reasonably foreseeable future actions.

**Cut-off Criteria:** Specification of the amount of material or energy flow or the level of environmental significance associated with unit processes or product system to be excluded from a study (ISO 14044).

---

## D

**Data Quality:** Characteristics of data that relate to their ability to satisfy stated requirements (ISO 14044). Typically assessed using pedigree matrix: reliability, completeness, temporal correlation, geographical correlation, technological correlation.

**Decommissioning:** The process of closing and decontaminating a facility at the end of its operational life.

**Direct Emissions:** Emissions from sources that are owned or controlled by the reporting entity (Scope 1 in GHG Protocol).

**Disability-Adjusted Life Year (DALY):** A measure of overall disease burden, expressed as the number of years lost due to ill-health, disability, or early death.

---

## E

**Ecotoxicity:** The potential for biological, chemical, or physical stressors to affect ecosystems.

**Ecosystem Services:** Benefits that humans obtain from ecosystems, including provisioning services (food, water), regulating services (climate, flood control), cultural services (recreation, aesthetic), and supporting services (nutrient cycling).

**Embodied Carbon:** The total greenhouse gas emissions associated with materials and processes throughout the life cycle of a product or structure, often used for infrastructure and buildings.

**Emission Factor:** A representative value that relates the quantity of a pollutant released to the atmosphere with an activity associated with the release of that pollutant.

**Environmental Aspect:** Element of an organization's activities or products or services that interacts or can interact with the environment (ISO 14001).

**Environmental Impact:** Any change to the environment, whether adverse or beneficial, resulting from an organization's environmental aspects (ISO 14001).

**Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA):** A systematic process to identify, predict, evaluate, and mitigate the biophysical, social, and other relevant effects of development proposals prior to major decisions being taken.

**Environmental Impact Statement (EIS):** A document required by NEPA for certain actions "significantly affecting the quality of the human environment." Comprehensive analysis of potential environmental impacts.

**Environmental Management System (EMS):** Part of the management system used to manage environmental aspects, fulfill compliance obligations, and address risks and opportunities (ISO 14001).

**Equator Principles:** A risk management framework adopted by financial institutions for determining, assessing, and managing environmental and social risk in project finance.

**Eutrophication:** Enrichment of water or soil with nutrients (particularly nitrogen and phosphorus) causing excessive algal growth and oxygen depletion.

**Exergy:** The maximum useful work that can be obtained from a system as it comes to equilibrium with its environment; a measure of energy quality.

---

## F

**Foreground System:** Processes directly affected by decisions analyzed in the LCA study (e.g., operations of the facility being assessed).

**Fugitive Emissions:** Emissions that are not released through a stack, vent, or other functionally equivalent opening (e.g., refrigerant leaks, natural gas leaks).

**Functional Unit:** Quantified performance of a product system for use as a reference unit in an LCA study (ISO 14044). Provides a reference to which inputs and outputs are related.

---

## G

**Gate-to-Gate:** System boundary that includes only processes occurring within the facility under study.

**GHG (Greenhouse Gas):** A gas that absorbs and emits radiant energy within the thermal infrared range, causing the greenhouse effect. Primary GHGs in Earth's atmosphere are water vapor, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and ozone.

**GHG Protocol:** The most widely used international accounting tool for quantifying and managing greenhouse gas emissions. Includes Corporate Standard and Product Standard.

**Global Warming Potential (GWP):** A factor describing the radiative forcing impact of one unit of a given GHG relative to one unit of CO₂ over a specified time period (typically 100 years).

**Green Water:** Precipitation stored in the soil and evaporated, transpired, or incorporated by plants.

**Greenwashing:** The practice of making unsubstantiated or misleading claims about the environmental benefits of a product, service, or company practice.

**Grey Water:** The volume of freshwater required to assimilate pollutants to meet specific water quality standards.

---

## H

**Habitat:** The natural environment in which an organism, species, or community lives.

**Hotspot:** In LCA, a process or life cycle stage that contributes disproportionately to an impact category.

**Human Toxicity:** The potential for chemical substances to cause adverse effects in humans through environmental exposure.

---

## I

**IFC Performance Standards:** Environmental and social standards developed by the International Finance Corporation, widely adopted in project finance.

**Impact Assessment:** Phase of LCA aimed at understanding and evaluating the magnitude and significance of potential environmental impacts (ISO 14044).

**Impact Category:** Class representing environmental issues of concern to which LCI results may be assigned (ISO 14044).

**Indirect Emissions:** Emissions that are a consequence of the activities of the reporting entity but occur at sources owned or controlled by another entity (Scopes 2 and 3 in GHG Protocol).

**Inventory Analysis:** Phase of LCA involving compilation and quantification of inputs and outputs for a product system throughout its life cycle (ISO 14044).

**ISO 14001:** International standard specifying requirements for an environmental management system.

**ISO 14040:** International standard providing principles and framework for life cycle assessment.

**ISO 14044:** International standard providing requirements and guidelines for life cycle assessment.

**ISO 14046:** International standard for water footprint assessment.

**ISO 14064:** International standard for greenhouse gas accounting and verification.

---

## L

**Land Use:** The human use of land for various purposes, and in LCA context, the occupation and transformation of land.

**LCA (Life Cycle Assessment):** Compilation and evaluation of the inputs, outputs, and potential environmental impacts of a product system throughout its life cycle (ISO 14040).

**LCI (Life Cycle Inventory):** Phase of LCA involving compilation and quantification of inputs and outputs for a product system throughout its life cycle.

**LCIA (Life Cycle Impact Assessment):** Phase of LCA aimed at understanding and evaluating the magnitude and significance of potential environmental impacts.

**LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design):** Green building certification program used worldwide.

**Leopold Matrix:** A qualitative EIA method using a matrix to identify potential impacts by intersecting project activities with environmental components.

---

## M

**Marginal Abatement Cost Curve (MACC):** A graph showing the cost-effectiveness of emissions reduction measures, typically with cost per tonne CO₂ on vertical axis and abatement potential on horizontal axis.

**Materiality:** The principle of defining relevant and significant topics for inclusion in a sustainability report based on their importance to the organization and stakeholders.

**Midpoint Indicator:** Impact category indicator at a middle position in the cause-effect chain between LCI results and endpoint category indicators (e.g., kg CO₂ eq for climate change).

**Mitigation:** Measures taken to reduce adverse impacts. The mitigation hierarchy prioritizes: avoid, minimize, restore, offset.

**Mitigation Hierarchy:** A sequence for managing impacts: avoidance, minimization, restoration/rehabilitation, offset/compensation.

**Monte Carlo Simulation:** A computational technique that uses random sampling to obtain numerical results, often used for uncertainty analysis in LCA.

---

## N

**NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act):** U.S. law requiring federal agencies to assess the environmental effects of proposed major federal actions prior to making decisions.

**Net Zero:** Achieving a balance between greenhouse gas emissions produced and emissions removed from the atmosphere.

**Normalization:** Calculation of the magnitude of category indicator results relative to reference information to better understand the relative magnitude (ISO 14044).

**NPDES (National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System):** U.S. permit program controlling water pollution by regulating point sources that discharge pollutants into waters.

---

## O

**Offset:** A compensatory measure that counterbalances an impact, typically used as a last resort in the mitigation hierarchy. Examples include biodiversity offsets and carbon offsets.

**Organizational Boundary:** Definition of which operations are included in a GHG inventory based on control (operational or financial) or equity share approach.

---

## P

**Particulate Matter (PM):** Solid or liquid particles suspended in air. PM₂.₅ (particles <2.5 μm) and PM₁₀ (particles <10 μm) are of health concern.

**PDCA Cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act):** An iterative four-step management method used for continuous improvement.

**Pedigree Matrix:** A data quality assessment framework evaluating reliability, completeness, temporal correlation, geographical correlation, and technological correlation.

**Photochemical Ozone Creation Potential (POCP):** The potential of volatile organic compounds to contribute to ground-level ozone (smog) formation.

**Precautionary Principle:** The principle that if an action or policy has a suspected risk of causing harm, in the absence of scientific consensus, the burden of proof falls on those taking the action.

---

## R

**RCRA (Resource Conservation and Recovery Act):** U.S. law governing disposal of solid and hazardous waste.

**ReCiPe:** A life cycle impact assessment method providing both midpoint and endpoint indicators.

**Recirculation Rate:** The percentage of water that is recycled and reused within a system.

**Reporting Boundary:** The scope of activities, operations, or life cycle stages included in a carbon footprint or environmental report.

**Resilience:** The capacity of a system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change, still retaining essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks.

---

## S

**Scope 1 Emissions:** Direct GHG emissions from sources owned or controlled by the reporting entity (GHG Protocol).

**Scope 2 Emissions:** Indirect GHG emissions from the generation of purchased electricity, steam, heating, and cooling consumed by the reporting entity (GHG Protocol).

**Scope 3 Emissions:** All other indirect GHG emissions that occur in the value chain, upstream and downstream of the reporting entity (GHG Protocol). 15 categories defined.

**Scoping:** The process of determining the spatial and temporal boundaries of an EIA, the impacts to be considered, and the depth of assessment required.

**Screening:** The process of determining whether a proposed project requires an EIA and, if so, at what level of detail.

**Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematic procedures for estimating the effects of the choices made regarding methods and data on the outcome of a study (ISO 14044).

**Significant Aspect:** An environmental aspect that has or can have a significant environmental impact (ISO 14001).

**Stakeholder:** Individual or group that has an interest in any decision or activity of an organization (ISO 14001).

**Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA):** Assessment of environmental effects of policies, plans, and programs.

**Sustainability:** Meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs (Brundtland Commission).

**System Boundary:** Set of criteria specifying which unit processes are part of a product system (ISO 14044).

**System Dynamics:** A methodology for studying and managing complex systems that change over time, using stocks, flows, and feedback loops.

---

## T

**TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures):** Framework for companies to disclose climate-related risks and opportunities to investors and stakeholders.

**Threshold:** A point or level at which a sudden or significant change occurs; in environmental science, often refers to tipping points in ecosystems.

**TRACI (Tool for Reduction and Assessment of Chemicals and Other Environmental Impacts):** Life cycle impact assessment method developed by U.S. EPA, particularly suitable for North American context.

**Transparency:** Open, comprehensive, and understandable presentation of information.

---

## U

**Uncertainty:** Parameter that characterizes the dispersion of values that could reasonably be attributed to a quantity (ISO 14044).

**Unit Process:** Smallest element considered in LCA for which input and output data are quantified (ISO 14044).

**USEtox:** Consensus model for characterizing human and ecotoxicological impacts in LCA.

---

## V

**Valued Ecosystem Component (VEC):** An environmental or social feature considered to be of scientific, social, cultural, economic, historical, archaeological, or aesthetic importance in cumulative effects assessment.

**Verification:** Confirmation through provision of objective evidence that specified requirements have been fulfilled (ISO 14064).

**Virtual Water:** The volume of water used to produce a product or service, "embedded" in the product.

---

## W

**Water Footprint:** The total volume of freshwater used to produce goods and services consumed by an individual, community, or business, or produced within a defined geographic area.

**Water Scarcity:** Condition in which demand for water exceeds available supply in a specific region and time period.

**Water Stress Index (WSI):** A ratio of water use to water availability, indicating the level of competition for water resources.

**Weighting:** Converting and possibly aggregating indicator results across impact categories using numerical factors based on value choices (ISO 14044). Optional in LCA; prohibited for public comparative assertions.

---

## Z

**Zero Discharge:** A facility design that eliminates all wastewater discharge through complete recirculation and treatment.

**Zero Waste:** A goal that encourages redesign of resource life cycles so that all products are reused and no trash is sent to landfills or incinerators.

## Units and Conversions

### Energy
- 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ = 0.00341 mmBtu
- 1 mmBtu = 1,000,000 Btu = 293.1 kWh = 1,055 MJ
- 1 therm = 100,000 Btu = 0.1 mmBtu = 29.31 kWh
- 1 joule (J) = 1 watt-second

### Mass
- 1 tonne = 1,000 kg = 1 metric ton = 2,204.6 lbs
- 1 kg = 1,000 g = 2.2046 lbs
- 1 lb = 0.4536 kg

### Volume
- 1 m³ = 1,000 L = 264.2 gallons (US)
- 1 L = 0.001 m³ = 0.2642 gallons (US)
- 1 gallon (US) = 3.785 L

### Concentration
- 1 ppm (by volume in air) ≈ 1 mg/L (by mass in water)
- 1% = 10,000 ppm = 10 g/L (for aqueous solutions)
