# Lesson 7: Water Stewardship Programs

## Learning Objectives
- Conduct water footprint analysis for CEA operations
- Implement source water protection strategies
- Design comprehensive water quality management systems
- Develop community water stewardship initiatives

## Introduction

Water stewardship extends beyond efficiency to encompass water security, watershed health, and equitable access. For CEA operations, implementing comprehensive water stewardship demonstrates environmental leadership and builds resilience against water scarcity and regulatory changes.

## 1. Water Stewardship Framework

```

```

### 1.2 Water Footprint Components

```
WATER FOOTPRINT CALCULATION
============================

BLUE WATER FOOTPRINT:
- Surface and groundwater consumed
- Not returned to source
Calculation: Volume withdrawn - Volume returned

GREEN WATER FOOTPRINT:
- Rainwater consumed (soil moisture)
- Minimal in CEA, significant in field agriculture

GREY WATER FOOTPRINT:
- Theoretical water needed to dilute pollution
Calculation: (Pollutant load) / (Max concentration - Background concentration)

CEA EXAMPLE (per kg lettuce):
Blue water: 3-8 L (direct use)
Green water: 0 L (controlled environment)
Grey water: 0.5-2 L (nutrient discharge)
TOTAL: 3.5-10 L/kg

Comparison to field: 150-300 L/kg
Water productivity: 15-85x improvement
```

## 2. Water Source Management

### 2.1 Source Water Assessment

```
WATER SOURCE EVALUATION
========================

MUNICIPAL WATER:
Advantages:
- Reliable supply
- Treated quality
- Infrastructure exists

Disadvantages:
- High cost ($2-8/m³)
- Supply restrictions possible
- Chemical treatment residues

Risk assessment:
- Supply interruption probability
- Price escalation trends
- Regulatory changes

GROUNDWATER:
Advantages:
- Lower cost
- Consistent temperature
- Mineral content

Disadvantages:
- Permitting requirements
- Sustainability concerns
- Quality variability

Risk assessment:
- Aquifer depletion rate
- Competing users
- Contamination vulnerability

SURFACE WATER:
Advantages:
- Potentially abundant
- Lower cost
- Multiple sources possible

Disadvantages:
- Seasonal variability
- Treatment requirements
- Rights and regulations

Risk assessment:
- Drought vulnerability
- Upstream impacts
- Ecosystem flow needs

RAINWATER:
Advantages:
- Free source
- Reduces stormwater runoff
- High purity

Disadvantages:
- Variable supply
- Storage required
- Collection infrastructure

Calculation:
Annual harvest = Roof area × Annual rainfall × Collection efficiency
Example: 1,000 m² × 800 mm × 0.85 = 680 m³/year
```

## 3. Water Quality Management

### 3.1 Quality Parameters for CEA

```
WATER QUALITY STANDARDS
========================

PARAMETER          ACCEPTABLE       OPTIMAL
--------------------------------------------
pH                 5.5-7.5          5.8-6.5
EC (mS/cm)         <0.75            <0.3
TDS (mg/L)         <480             <200
Alkalinity (ppm)   <100             <60
Hardness (ppm)     <150             <60

NUTRIENTS (should be absent in source water):
Nitrate-N          <10 mg/L         <2 mg/L
Phosphate-P        <1 mg/L          <0.1 mg/L
Ammonium-N         <1 mg/L          <0.1 mg/L

CONTAMINANTS:
Sodium             <50 mg/L         <20 mg/L
Chloride           <100 mg/L        <50 mg/L
Iron               <0.3 mg/L        <0.1 mg/L
Manganese          <0.1 mg/L        <0.05 mg/L
Boron              <0.5 mg/L        <0.1 mg/L

BIOLOGICAL:
Total coliform     0 CFU/100mL      0
E. coli            0 CFU/100mL      0
Pythium/Phytophthora   Absent       Absent

TESTING FREQUENCY:
Source water: Quarterly
Recirculated: Weekly
Final product: Before sale
```

## 6. Drought and Water Scarcity Resilience

### 6.1 Water Risk Assessment

```
WATER RISK EVALUATION (WRI Aqueduct Tool)
==========================================

PHYSICAL RISKS:
Baseline water stress: Water demand/supply ratio
  Low: <10%
  Medium: 10-40%
  High: 40-80%
  Extremely high: >80%

Interannual variability: Year-to-year variation
Seasonal variability: Within-year variation
Flood occurrence: Historical flood risk
Drought severity: Multi-year drought probability

REGULATORY RISKS:
Enabling environment: Governance quality
Media coverage: Public attention to water
Access to water: Equity of distribution

REPUTATIONAL RISKS:
Conflict with other users
Sensitive watershed location

CEA EXAMPLE:
Location: Phoenix, Arizona
Baseline stress: Extremely high (>80%)
Drought severity: High
Regulatory risk: Medium (strong rights, but contested)
Overall risk: HIGH --> Requires mitigation
```

### 6.2 Drought Contingency Planning

```
DROUGHT RESPONSE PLAN
======================

TRIGGER LEVELS:

LEVEL 1: WATER WATCH (10% below normal)
Actions:
- Increase monitoring frequency
- Review conservation practices
- Check equipment efficiency
- Communicate with water provider

LEVEL 2: WATER WARNING (20% below normal)
Actions:
- Implement voluntary conservation (10% reduction target)
- Fix all leaks within 48 hours
- Optimize irrigation schedules
- Delay non-essential water uses
- Increase recycling rates

LEVEL 3: WATER EMERGENCY (30% below normal)
Actions:
- Mandatory conservation (25% reduction target)
- Suspend outdoor irrigation
- Maximize recirculation
- Reduce production if necessary
- Activate alternative water sources

LEVEL 4: WATER CRISIS (>40% below normal)
Actions:
- Maximum conservation (40%+ reduction)
- Production curtailment
- Full alternative source activation
- Emergency supplies (trucked water)

CONSERVATION MEASURES BY LEVEL:

Level 1 (10% reduction):
- Eliminate all leaks
- Optimize nutrient solution concentration
- Adjust growing schedules

Level 2 (25% reduction):
- Increase recycling 90% --> 95%
- Switch to lower-water crops
- Reduce new plantings

Level 3 (40% reduction):
- Maximum recycling (98%)
- Rainwater/condensate only new input
- Reduce production 20-30%

ALTERNATIVE SOURCES:
- Rainwater: 500 m³ storage
- Condensate: 80% capture implemented
- Greywater: Treatment plant operational
- Trucked water: Emergency contracts
- Municipal reclaimed: Connection available
```

## 7. Water Stewardship Metrics and Reporting

### 7.1 Performance Indicators

```
KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
===========================

WATER EFFICIENCY:
1. Total water use intensity
   Formula: L water / kg product
   Target: <10 L/kg (leafy greens)
   Best: <5 L/kg

2. Water recycling rate
   Formula: (Recycled / Total use) × 100%
   Target: >90%
   Best: >95%

3. Water productivity
   Formula: $ revenue / m³ water
   Target: >$500/m³
   Best: >$1,000/m³

WATER QUALITY:
4. Discharge quality
   Target: Better than receiving water
   Measure: All parameters below limits

5. Treatment effectiveness
   Target: >99.9% pathogen removal
   Measure: Regular testing

STEWARDSHIP:
6. Watershed investment
   Target: >0.5% of revenue
   Measure: $ invested in watershed projects

   Target: Active participation

8. Stakeholder engagement
   Target: Quarterly interactions

RISK MANAGEMENT:
```

## Practical Exercise

**Exercise: Water Stewardship Strategy Development**

Develop a comprehensive water stewardship plan:

1. **Baseline Assessment:**
   - Calculate current water footprint
   - Identify all water sources
   - Map water flows through facility
   - Assess current recycling rate

2. **Risk Analysis:**
   - Use Aqueduct tool for location
   - Identify quantity and quality risks
   - Assess regulatory environment
   - Evaluate drought vulnerability

3. **Target Setting:**
   - Set 1-year, 3-year, 5-year targets
   - Include efficiency and stewardship goals
   - Align with AWS or similar standard
   - Define success metrics

4. **Strategy Development:**
   - Prioritize efficiency measures
   - Design recycling improvements
   - Plan watershed initiatives
   - Create stakeholder engagement approach

5. **Implementation Plan:**
   - Timeline and milestones
   - Budget and resources
   - Responsibilities
   - Monitoring and reporting

## Key Takeaways

1. **Water stewardship extends beyond facility boundaries** to encompass watershed health and shared water challenges

2. **Closed-loop systems achieve 90-95% recycling** dramatically reducing water footprint

3. **Condensate recovery provides high-quality water** at minimal cost

4. **Collective action multiplies impact** through watershed partnerships

5. **Drought resilience requires planning** with tiered response protocols
