# Lesson 12: Sustainability Reporting Frameworks

## Learning Objectives
- Implement GRI Standards for sustainability reporting
- Apply SASB and integrated reporting frameworks
- Conduct materiality assessments
- Develop comprehensive sustainability reports

## Introduction

Sustainability reporting has evolved from voluntary public relations to strategic business practice and, increasingly, regulatory requirement. This lesson covers major reporting frameworks, enabling CEA operations to communicate performance transparently and align with investor, customer, and stakeholder expectations.

## 1. Reporting Framework Landscape

### 1.1 Major Frameworks Overview

```
SUSTAINABILITY REPORTING ECOSYSTEM
===================================

COMPREHENSIVE FRAMEWORKS:
├── GRI (Global Reporting Initiative)
│   ├── Most widely used globally
│   ├── Stakeholder-focused
│   ├── Multi-dimensional sustainability
│   └── Applicability: All organizations
│
├── Integrated Reporting (<IR>)
│   ├── Value creation focus
│   ├── Financial + non-financial
│   ├── Six capitals model
│   └── Applicability: Investor-oriented
│
└── CDP (Carbon Disclosure Project)
    ├── Environmental focus
    ├── Climate, water, forests
    ├── Investor and supply chain programs
    └── Applicability: All organizations

SECTOR-SPECIFIC:
├── SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board)
│   ├── Industry-specific standards
│   ├── Financially material topics
│   ├── Investor-focused
│   └── Agricultural Products sector relevant
│
└── TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures)
    ├── Climate risk and opportunity
    ├── Governance, strategy, risk, metrics
    ├── Increasingly mandatory
    └── Applicability: All with climate impact

CONVERGENCE:
International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)
- Consolidating SASB, TCFD, others
- Creating global baseline
- Expected mandatory in many jurisdictions by 2025-2027
```

### 2.2 Materiality Assessment

```
MATERIALITY PROCESS (GRI 3)
===========================

STEP 1: UNDERSTAND CONTEXT
[Organization] --> Activities, relationships, impacts
                   |
                   v
         Sustainability context
         (climate change, water scarcity, etc.)

STEP 2: IDENTIFY IMPACTS
Positive and negative
Actual and potential
Across value chain

Methods:
- Value chain mapping
- Media analysis
- Peer benchmarking
- Expert consultation
- Stakeholder input

Example Impacts (CEA):
ENVIRONMENTAL:
+ Water conservation (positive, actual)
+ Renewable energy use (positive, actual)
- Electricity consumption (negative, actual)
- Embodied emissions in materials (negative, actual)

SOCIAL:
+ Job creation (positive, actual)
+ Skills development (positive, actual)
- Potential labor issues in supply chain (negative, potential)

ECONOMIC:
+ Local economic development (positive, actual)
+ Innovation in agriculture (positive, actual)
- Market disruption (negative, potential)

STEP 3: ASSESS SIGNIFICANCE
Dimensions:
- Scale (severity of impact)
- Scope (extent of impact)
- Likelihood (probability)
- Irremediability (difficulty to reverse)

Scoring (1-5 scale):
Energy use:
  Scale: 4 (high consumption, high emissions)
  Scope: 3 (facility + supply chain)
  Likelihood: 5 (ongoing)
  Irremediability: 2 (reversible)
  SCORE: 14/20 (MATERIAL)

Water use:
  Scale: 5 (scarce resource, watershed impact)
  Scope: 4 (facility, community, watershed)
  Likelihood: 5 (ongoing)
  Irremediability: 3 (ecosystem impacts)
  SCORE: 17/20 (HIGHLY MATERIAL)

STEP 4: PRIORITIZATION

MATERIALITY MATRIX:
        ↑ Significance to stakeholders
        │
   High │     ● Water
        │   ● Energy
        │ ● Waste    ● Labor practices
        │ ● Local economic impact
        │   ● Climate
  Medium│ ● Biodiversity
        │             ● Diversity
        │ ● Materials ● Packaging
        │
    Low │ ● Noise
        └───────────────────────────→
            Significance of impacts

MATERIAL TOPICS (report on these):
1. Energy management
2. Water stewardship
3. Climate change / GHG emissions
4. Waste and circularity
5. Labor practices and working conditions
6. Local economic impact
7. Food safety and quality
8. Sustainable supply chain

STEP 5: VALIDATION
- Senior management review
- Board approval
- Stakeholder feedback
- Public disclosure

UPDATE FREQUENCY:
- Full process: Every 3 years
- Annual review: Check for changes
- Ad-hoc: If significant events occur
```

## 6. Assurance and Verification

### 6.1 Assurance Standards

```
THIRD-PARTY ASSURANCE
======================

STANDARDS:

ISAE 3000: Assurance Engagements Other than Audits
- General standard for non-financial assurance
- Used by major accounting firms
- Reasonable or limited assurance

AA1000AS: Accountability Assurance Standard
- Sustainability-specific
- Evaluates adherence to AA1000 principles
- Type 1: Adherence to principles
- Type 2: + Performance information

ASSURANCE LEVELS:

LIMITED ASSURANCE:
- Procedures: Inquiry, analytical review
- Conclusion: "Nothing has come to our attention..."
- Confidence: Moderate
- Cost: Lower
- Typical for: Initial assurance, less material topics

REASONABLE ASSURANCE:
- Procedures: + Testing, validation, site visits
- Conclusion: "In our opinion..."
- Confidence: High
- Cost: Higher
- Typical for: Mature reporting, material topics

SCOPE DECISIONS:

BEGINNER:
- Limited assurance
- Key metrics only (GHG, water, safety)
- Engages: Sustainability consultant

INTERMEDIATE:
- Limited assurance
- Expanded scope (all GRI material topics)
- Engages: Accounting firm sustainability practice

ADVANCED:
- Reasonable assurance (GHG, water, safety)
- Limited assurance (other topics)
- Engages: Big 4 accounting firm

WORLD-CLASS:
- Reasonable assurance (all material topics)
- Integrated with financial audit
- Engages: Same firm as financial audit

TYPICAL COSTS:
Limited (key metrics): $15,000-30,000
Limited (comprehensive): $30,000-60,000
Reasonable (key metrics): $40,000-80,000
Reasonable (comprehensive): $80,000-150,000
```

## Practical Exercise

**Exercise: Sustainability Report Development**

Create a sustainability report for your organization:

1. **Framework Selection:**
   - Choose primary framework (recommend GRI)
   - Identify supplemental frameworks (SASB, CDP)
   - Justify selection based on stakeholders

2. **Materiality Assessment:**
   - Identify potential topics (20-30)
   - Assess significance of impacts
   - Map stakeholder importance
   - Create materiality matrix
   - Determine material topics (8-12)

3. **Content Development:**
   - For each material topic:
     * Management approach
     * Key metrics (3-year trend)
     * Targets and performance
     * Future commitments
   - Use GRI disclosure templates

4. **Assurance Planning:**
   - Determine assurance level and scope
   - Identify assurance provider
   - Estimate costs and timeline

5. **Report Production:**
   - Create executive summary (2 pages)
   - Develop full report (20-40 pages)
   - Design for readability
   - Plan dissemination strategy

## Key Takeaways

1. **Reporting frameworks provide structure** for comprehensive sustainability communication

2. **Materiality assessment focuses** on topics that matter most to business and stakeholders

3. **GRI remains the global standard** for comprehensive sustainability reporting

4. **Investor-focused frameworks** (SASB, TCFD) are increasingly important

5. **Third-party assurance enhances credibility** and identifies improvement opportunities
