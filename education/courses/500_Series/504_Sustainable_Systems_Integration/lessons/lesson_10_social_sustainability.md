# Lesson 10: Social Sustainability Metrics

## Learning Objectives
- Develop stakeholder engagement frameworks
- Calculate social return on investment (SROI)
- Implement fair labor practices and standards
- Design community development indicators

## Introduction

Social sustainability encompasses the people dimension of sustainable development - ensuring equitable, inclusive, and beneficial relationships with employees, communities, and stakeholders. This lesson provides frameworks and metrics for measuring and enhancing social performance in CEA operations.

## 3. Labor Practices and Standards

### 3.1 Fair Compensation

```
LIVING WAGE FRAMEWORK
======================

CALCULATION:

Living Wage = Basic Needs + Discretionary Income

Basic Needs:
- Food: Nutritious diet for family
- Housing: Decent quality
- Healthcare: Adequate coverage
- Education: Children's schooling
- Transportation: Commute + occasional personal
- Communications: Phone/internet
- Other: Clothing, household items

Example Calculation (location-specific):
Family size: 4 (2 adults, 2 children)
1.5 wage earners

Annual basic needs: $45,000
Discretionary (10%): $4,500
Total family need: $49,500
Per wage earner: $33,000/year
Hourly (2080 hours): $15.87/hour

IMPLEMENTATION:

Minimum wage (local): $12.00/hour
Living wage (calculated): $15.87/hour
COMPANY COMMITMENT: $16.50/hour (4% above living wage)

Benefits:
- Health insurance (100% coverage)
- Retirement matching (4%)
- Paid time off (15 days + 10 holidays)
- Parental leave (12 weeks paid)
- Education assistance ($3,000/year)

Total compensation value: $20-22/hour equivalent

MONITORING:
- Annual living wage recalculation
- Benchmark against local costs
- Employee feedback on adequacy
- Third-party verification
```

### 3.2 Health and Safety

```
OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH & SAFETY
=============================

HAZARD CATEGORIES IN CEA:

PHYSICAL:
- Slips, trips, falls (wet floors)
- Ergonomic (repetitive motion)
- Temperature extremes
- Noise (HVAC equipment)
- Machinery (processing)

CHEMICAL:
- Pesticides/treatments
- Cleaning products
- Nutrient solutions
- CO2 enrichment

BIOLOGICAL:
- Molds and fungi
- Plant allergens
- Pathogens (in water)
- Insect stings

PSYCHOSOCIAL:
- Work stress
- Fatigue
- Monotonous tasks
- Isolation

CONTROL HIERARCHY:

ELIMINATION:
- Redesign to remove hazard
- Example: Automation of repetitive tasks

SUBSTITUTION:
- Replace with safer alternative
- Example: Biopesticides vs. synthetic

ENGINEERING CONTROLS:
- Physical barriers
- Example: Machine guards, ventilation

ADMINISTRATIVE:
- Policies and training
- Example: Job rotation, breaks

PPE (LAST RESORT):
- Personal protective equipment
- Example: Gloves, safety glasses

METRICS:

Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR):
Formula: (Incidents × 200,000) / Total hours worked
Target: <2.0 (World-class: <1.0)

Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR):
Formula: (Lost time injuries × 1,000,000) / Total hours worked
Target: <0.5

Near-miss reporting:
Target: >20 near-misses per incident (proactive culture)

Training hours:
Target: >8 hours/employee/year on safety

SAFETY CULTURE:
- Leadership commitment
- Employee participation
- Open reporting (no blame)
- Continuous improvement
- Regular audits
```

## 4. Social Return on Investment (SROI)

### 4.1 SROI Methodology

```
SROI FRAMEWORK
==============

PROCESS:

STEP 1: STAKEHOLDER IDENTIFICATION
Who experiences change from activities?

STEP 2: OUTCOMES MAPPING
What changes occur? (positive and negative)

STEP 3: VALUATION
Assign monetary value to outcomes

STEP 4: IMPACT CALCULATION
Account for deadweight, attribution, drop-off

STEP 5: SROI CALCULATION
Ratio = Net Present Value / Investment

STEP 6: REPORTING
Document and communicate findings

EXAMPLE: JOB TRAINING PROGRAM

Investment: $150,000/year

Outcomes:

1. EMPLOYEE SKILL INCREASE
   Outcome: 50 employees trained
   Indicator: Wage increase
   Proxy: $2/hour average increase
   Financial value: 50 × $2 × 2,080 = $208,000/year

2. REDUCED TURNOVER
   Outcome: Turnover decreases 20% to 10%
   Indicator: Retention improvement
   Cost saved: 10 positions × $8,000 replacement cost = $80,000

3. INCREASED PRODUCTIVITY
   Outcome: 15% productivity increase
   Indicator: Output per hour
   Value: $120,000/year additional production

4. COMMUNITY ECONOMIC IMPACT
   Outcome: Income spent locally
   Indicator: Local multiplier (1.5)
   Value: $208,000 × 0.5 = $104,000 community benefit

Total Benefits: $512,000/year

ADJUSTMENTS:

Deadweight (would have occurred anyway): 20%
Attribution (others contributed): 30%
Drop-off (benefits decrease over time): 10%

Adjusted benefits:
$512,000 × (1-0.20) × (1-0.30) × (1-0.10) = $258,048

SROI RATIO:
$258,048 / $150,000 = 1.72:1

Interpretation: Every $1 invested generates $1.72 in social value
```

## Practical Exercise

**Exercise: Social Impact Assessment**

Conduct a comprehensive social sustainability assessment:

1. **Stakeholder Analysis:**
   - Identify all stakeholder groups
   - Prioritize using influence/interest matrix
   - Design engagement strategy for each

2. **Baseline Measurement:**
   - Employment metrics (jobs, wages, benefits)
   - Community impact (spending, programs)
   - Diversity and inclusion data
   - Health and safety statistics

3. **SROI Calculation:**
   - Select 2-3 key programs
   - Map outcomes and value
   - Calculate SROI ratios
   - Interpret and communicate results

4. **Gap Analysis:**
   - Compare performance to benchmarks
   - Identify areas for improvement
   - Assess certification readiness (B Corp)
   - Prioritize actions

5. **Action Plan:**
   - Set 1-year and 3-year goals
   - Design new programs
   - Budget requirements
   - Monitoring and reporting plan

## Key Takeaways

1. **Social sustainability is measurable** using frameworks like SROI and SDG indicators

2. **Stakeholder engagement builds trust** and creates shared value

3. **Fair labor practices attract and retain talent** improving business performance

4. **Community investment generates returns** through loyalty, reputation, and resilience

5. **Certification and reporting demonstrate commitment** to social responsibility
