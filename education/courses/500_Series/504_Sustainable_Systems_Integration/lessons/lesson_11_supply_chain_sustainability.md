# Lesson 11: Supply Chain Sustainability

## Learning Objectives
- Map and analyze supply chain sustainability risks
- Implement supplier sustainability assessment programs
- Develop sustainable procurement strategies
- Reduce Scope 3 emissions across the value chain

## Introduction

Supply chains often represent 80-90% of an organization's environmental and social impact. For CEA operations, achieving comprehensive sustainability requires extending practices beyond facility boundaries to engage suppliers, distributors, and partners throughout the value chain.

## 3. Sustainable Procurement

### 3.1 Procurement Policy

```
SUSTAINABLE PROCUREMENT FRAMEWORK
==================================

POLICY PRINCIPLES:

1. PREFERENCE HIERARCHY
   First: Local and sustainable
   Second: Sustainable (non-local)
   Third: Local (working toward sustainability)
   Fourth: Conventional (with improvement plan)

2. TOTAL COST OF OWNERSHIP
   Consider:
   - Purchase price
   - Operating costs
   - Environmental costs
   - Social costs
   - End-of-life costs

3. MINIMUM STANDARDS
   All suppliers must:
   - Comply with laws
   - No child or forced labor
   - Meet environmental regulations
   - Provide safe working conditions
   - Sign code of conduct

4. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
   Suppliers must:
   - Set annual improvement targets
   - Report progress
   - Engage in collaborative projects
   - Participate in assessments

CATEGORY-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS:

NUTRIENTS:
- Preference for organic-certified
- Minimal packaging
- Take-back program for containers
- Local production when possible
- Carbon footprint disclosure

GROWING MEDIA:
- Peat-free alternatives prioritized
- Recycled content >30%
- Reusable or recyclable
- Certified sustainable forestry (if wood-based)
- End-of-life plan

PACKAGING:
- Reusable systems preferred
- Recyclable or compostable materials
- Recycled content >50%
- Minimal design (lightweighting)
- Local production <500 miles

ENERGY:
- 100% renewable electricity
- Green-e or equivalent certified
- New renewable projects supported
- Community benefits

SEEDS/TRANSPLANTS:
- Organic certified
- Non-GMO
- Fair trade where applicable
- Diverse varieties (heirloom support)
- Regional production preferred
```

## Practical Exercise

**Exercise: Supply Chain Sustainability Program Design**

Create a comprehensive supply chain sustainability program:

1. **Supply Chain Mapping:**
   - Categorize spend by category
   - Identify top 20 suppliers (80/20 rule)
   - Map to Tier 2 where possible
   - Geographic analysis

2. **Risk Assessment:**
   - Environmental risks by category
   - Social risks by supplier location
   - Economic/operational risks
   - Prioritization matrix

3. **Scorecard Development:**
   - Adapt provided template
   - Weight categories for your context
   - Define data collection process
   - Set scoring thresholds

4. **Engagement Strategy:**
   - Segment suppliers (strategic, leverage, bottleneck, routine)
   - Design engagement approach for each
   - Create supplier development program
   - Plan for capacity building

5. **Implementation Roadmap:**
   - Year 1: Assessment and quick wins
   - Year 2: Systematic engagement
   - Year 3: Optimization and innovation
   - Budget and resource plan
   - Success metrics

## Key Takeaways

1. **Supply chains often represent the majority** of environmental and social impact

2. **Mapping and assessment** are essential first steps to understand risks and opportunities

3. **Collaboration creates more value** than purely transactional compliance

4. **Scope 3 emissions can be significantly reduced** through strategic supply chain management

5. **Transparency builds trust** with customers and stakeholders
