# Sustainability Terminology Glossary

## A

**Additionality** - The principle that carbon offsets or environmental projects would not have occurred without offset funding; essential for credible carbon credits.

**Alliance for Water Stewardship (AWS)** - International standard for responsible water use that addresses water challenges in a catchment context.

**Anaerobic Digestion** - Biological process where microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas and digestate fertilizer.

## B

**B Corporation** - Certification for businesses that meet high standards of social and environmental performance, accountability, and transparency.

**Balancing Loop** - Feedback mechanism that counteracts change and maintains system stability (negative feedback).

**Baseline** - Reference point for measuring progress; initial state before interventions.

**Biochar** - Charcoal produced from biomass pyrolysis; used as soil amendment and for permanent carbon sequestration.

**Biodiversity Net Gain** - Development that leaves biodiversity in a measurably better state than before.

**Biomimicry** - Design approach that emulates nature's patterns and strategies to solve human challenges.

**Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL)** - Insect larvae used to process organic waste into protein and frass fertilizer.

## C

**Carbon Dioxide Equivalent (CO2e)** - Universal unit for measuring greenhouse gases, accounting for global warming potential of different gases.

**Carbon Footprint** - Total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an individual, organization, event, or product.

**Carbon Negative** - Removing more carbon from the atmosphere than emitted.

**Carbon Neutral** - Net-zero carbon dioxide emissions achieved through reduction and offsetting.

**Causal Loop Diagram (CLD)** - Visual representation of feedback relationships in a system.

**Circular Economy** - Economic system aimed at eliminating waste through continual cycling of resources.

**Closed-Loop System** - System where outputs become inputs, minimizing external resource needs and waste.

**Cradle-to-Cradle (C2C)** - Design framework that eliminates waste by designing all materials for biological or technical cycles.

## D

**Decarbonization** - Process of reducing carbon dioxide emissions through energy efficiency and renewable energy.

**Dehumidification Condensate** - Water recovered from air conditioning/dehumidification systems; high-quality water source for CEA.

**Diversion Rate** - Percentage of waste diverted from landfill through recycling, composting, or reuse.

## E

**Ecosystem Services** - Benefits people obtain from ecosystems (provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting).

**Embodied Carbon** - Total greenhouse gas emissions associated with producing a material or product.

**Emergent Property** - System characteristic that arises from component interactions but cannot be predicted from components alone.

**Energy Return on Investment (EROI)** - Ratio of energy delivered to energy required to obtain that energy.

**Energy Use Intensity (EUI)** - Energy consumed per unit of production or floor area.

**Environmental, Social, Governance (ESG)** - Three factors used to measure sustainability and ethical impact of investments.

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** - Policy making producers responsible for end-of-life management of products.

## F

**Feedback Loop** - Circular causal pathway where output influences input.

**Functional Unit** - Reference unit in life cycle assessment (e.g., "1 kg of lettuce").

## G

**Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)** - Most widely used framework for sustainability reporting.

**Global Warming Potential (GWP)** - Measure of how much heat a greenhouse gas traps compared to CO2 over 100 years.

**Green Building** - Structure designed, built, and operated to minimize environmental impact.

**Greenwashing** - Misleading claims about environmental benefits of products or practices.

**Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol** - Comprehensive global standardized frameworks to measure and manage greenhouse gas emissions.

## H

**Hydroponic** - Method of growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions in water.

## I

**Industrial Symbiosis** - Collaboration where waste or by-products of one industry become raw materials for another.

**Integrated Design Process (IDP)** - Collaborative approach to building design that integrates all systems from the start.

**Integrated Reporting** - Approach that communicates how strategy, governance, performance, and prospects create value across six capitals.

**International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)** - Body developing global baseline of sustainability disclosure standards.

## L

**LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)** - Green building certification program.

**Leverage Point** - Place in a system where small change can produce large effects.

**Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)** - Methodology for assessing environmental impacts throughout a product's life cycle.

**Living Wage** - Income level that covers basic needs for worker and family, beyond minimum wage.

## M

**Material Flow Analysis (MFA)** - Systematic assessment of flows and stocks of materials within a system.

**Materiality Assessment** - Process to identify and prioritize most significant sustainability topics.

**Microgrid** - Localized energy grid that can operate independently from the main grid.

## N

**Natural Capital** - World's stocks of natural assets (geology, soil, air, water, living organisms).

**Net-Zero** - Achieving balance between greenhouse gases emitted and removed from atmosphere.

**Nexus** - Interconnection between energy, water, and food systems.

## O

**Offset** - Reduction in emissions made to compensate for emissions elsewhere.

## P

**Pareto Optimization** - State where improvement in one objective requires sacrifice in another.

**Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)** - Contract to purchase electricity from a renewable energy project.

**Precautionary Principle** - When activity raises threats of harm, precautionary measures should be taken.

## R

**Regenerative Agriculture** - Farming practices that restore and enhance ecosystems rather than just sustaining them.

**Reinforcing Loop** - Feedback mechanism that amplifies change (positive feedback).

**Renewable Energy** - Energy from sources that are naturally replenishing (solar, wind, hydro, biomass).

**Resilience** - Capacity to recover from difficulties and adapt to change.

**ReSOLVE Framework** - Circular economy framework: Regenerate, Share, Optimize, Loop, Virtualize, Exchange.

## S

**Sankey Diagram** - Flow diagram where arrow width represents flow quantity.

**SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board)** - Industry-specific sustainability accounting standards.

**Science-Based Targets (SBT)** - Greenhouse gas reduction targets aligned with climate science (Paris Agreement).

**Scope 1 Emissions** - Direct greenhouse gas emissions from owned or controlled sources.

**Scope 2 Emissions** - Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, heat, or steam.

**Scope 3 Emissions** - All other indirect emissions in value chain.

**Social Return on Investment (SROI)** - Methodology measuring social value created relative to investment.

**Stakeholder** - Individual or group with interest in or affected by organization's activities.

**Sustainability** - Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.

**Systems Thinking** - Approach to problem-solving that views problems as parts of overall system.

## T

**Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)** - Framework for climate risk disclosure.

**Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR)** - Safety metric: recordable work injuries per 200,000 hours.

**Traceability** - Ability to track product's journey through supply chain.

**Triple Bottom Line** - Framework considering people, planet, profit equally.

**TRUE (Total Resource Use and Efficiency)** - Zero waste certification program.

## V

**Valorization** - Process of converting waste materials into more valuable products.

**Vermicomposting** - Composting using worms to break down organic waste.

**Vertical Farm** - Practice of growing crops in vertically stacked layers.

## W

**Water Footprint** - Total volume of freshwater used to produce goods and services.

**Water Stewardship** - Use of water that is socially equitable, environmentally sustainable, and economically beneficial.

**Watershed** - Land area that drains water to a common outlet.

## Z

**Zero Waste** - Philosophy eliminating waste through resource recovery and product redesign.

**Zero Waste Hierarchy** - Priority order: Rethink, Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Recover (avoid disposal).
