### Question 1
What is a key characteristic of complex systems?
A) Linear cause-and-effect relationships
B) Predictable behavior from individual components
C) Emergent properties not predictable from components alone
D) Simple, direct solutions to problems

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Complex systems exhibit emergent properties that arise from interactions between components but cannot be predicted by analyzing components in isolation.*

---

### Question 2
In a reinforcing feedback loop, what happens to changes in the system?
A) They are dampened and stabilized
B) They are amplified and grow exponentially
C) They remain constant over time
D) They reverse direction automatically

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Reinforcing (positive) feedback loops amplify changes, creating exponential growth or decline in the system.*

---

### Question 3
According to Donella Meadows' leverage points hierarchy, which intervention point has the HIGHEST leverage for system change?
A) Changing numerical parameters like taxes or subsidies
B) Adding or strengthening balancing feedback loops
C) Changing the mindset or paradigm from which the system arises
D) Increasing material stocks and flows

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Changing paradigms (the mindset or belief system underlying the system) is the highest leverage point because it fundamentally transforms how the system operates.*

---

### Question 4
What is the primary purpose of creating a causal loop diagram?
A) To calculate precise numerical outcomes
B) To visualize feedback relationships and system structure
C) To create a project timeline
D) To assign blame for system failures

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Causal loop diagrams help visualize the causal relationships and feedback loops in a system, making complex dynamics easier to understand.*

---

### Question 5
In a CEA facility, which of the following represents a balancing feedback loop?
A) Increased profits leading to more investment, leading to higher profits
B) Plant stress triggering quality testing, leading to treatment, restoring plant health
C) Positive customer reviews leading to more customers, leading to more reviews
D) Energy efficiency reducing costs, freeing capital for more efficiency improvements

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: This is a balancing loop because it works to restore the system to a desired state (healthy plants) when deviation occurs.*

---

### Question 6
What is "policy resistance" in systems thinking?
A) Government opposition to new regulations
B) Systems resisting external interventions and returning to previous patterns
C) Employee resistance to new policies
D) Legal challenges to organizational decisions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Policy resistance occurs when systems resist interventions that don't address underlying structure, often returning to previous states despite efforts to change.*

---

### Question 7
Which of the following is an example of optimizing the whole system rather than individual parts?
A) Maximizing LED efficiency without considering heat recovery
B) Minimizing water use without considering nutrient management
C) Integrating LED waste heat into the facility heating system
D) Reducing labor costs without considering quality impacts

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Integrating LED waste heat considers the system as a whole, creating synergies between lighting and heating rather than optimizing them separately.*

---

### Question 8
What does the "iceberg model" in systems thinking emphasize?
A) Most important information is visible at the surface
B) Events are more important than underlying patterns and structures
C) Looking beneath events to patterns, structures, and mental models
D) Focusing only on measurable data

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: The iceberg model shows that events (visible) are driven by underlying patterns, systemic structures, and mental models (below the surface)—the deeper you go, the higher the leverage for change.*

---

### Question 9
In the case study example of the vertical farm integrating LED heat recovery, what percentage reduction in energy costs was achieved?
A) 30%
B) 45%
C) 60%
D) 75%

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: The case study showed a 60% energy cost reduction through the integrated systems approach combining heat recovery, solar PV, and waste valorization.*

### Question 10
What is a common pitfall when applying systems thinking?
A) Considering too many variables at once
B) Reductionism—breaking problems into isolated parts without seeing connections
C) Taking too long to make decisions
D) Over-relying on computer models

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Reductionism—analyzing components in isolation without understanding their interactions—is a common pitfall that systems thinking specifically aims to overcome.*
