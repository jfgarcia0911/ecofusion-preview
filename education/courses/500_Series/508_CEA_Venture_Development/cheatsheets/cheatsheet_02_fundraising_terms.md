## Funding Stages and Typical Amounts

### Pre-Seed
- **Amount:** $50K-$500K
- **Sources:** Friends/family, angels, grants, accelerators
- **Stage:** Idea to prototype
- **Use:** Product development, initial validation

### Seed
- **Amount:** $500K-$3M
- **Sources:** Angel investors, seed VCs, strategic angels
- **Stage:** Prototype to pilot
- **Use:** Product-market fit, initial customers

### Series A
- **Amount:** $3M-$15M
- **Sources:** VCs, strategic investors
- **Stage:** Product-market fit proven
- **Use:** Scale go-to-market, expand team

### Series B
- **Amount:** $15M-$50M
- **Sources:** VCs, growth equity
- **Stage:** Scaling operations
- **Use:** Geographic expansion, market dominance

### Series C+
- **Amount:** $50M-$200M+
- **Sources:** Late-stage VCs, PE, strategic investors
- **Stage:** Mature operations
- **Use:** Major expansion, acquisitions, pre-IPO

## Valuation Methods

### 1. Comparable Transactions
**Method:** Look at recent similar company valuations
**Formula:** Your value = Comparable co. value × (Your metric / Their metric)
**Best for:** Companies with revenue or clear comparables

**Example:**
- Similar CEA company: $50M valuation, $10M revenue (5x multiple)
- Your company: $5M revenue
- Your valuation: $5M × 5 = $25M

### 2. Scorecard Method
**Method:** Compare to typical startup, adjust for factors
**Base:** Average angel valuation in region/sector
**Adjustments:**
- Strength of team: 0-30%
- Size of opportunity: 0-25%
- Product/technology: 0-15%
- Competitive environment: 0-10%
- Marketing/sales: 0-10%
- Need for additional investment: 0-5%
- Other: 0-5%

### 3. Venture Capital Method
**Method:** Discount expected exit value to present
**Formula:**
```
Post-money valuation = Terminal value / ROI multiple
Pre-money valuation = Post-money - Investment amount
```

**Example:**
- Expected exit in 7 years: $150M
- VC target ROI: 10x
- Post-money valuation: $150M / 10 = $15M
- Investment: $5M
- Pre-money valuation: $15M - $5M = $10M

### 4. Revenue Multiple (for revenue-stage companies)
**Method:** Apply industry multiple to revenue
**Formula:** Valuation = Annual Revenue × Multiple

**CEA Multiples (varies by market conditions):**
- Early-stage production: 2-4x revenue
- Growth-stage production: 4-8x revenue
- Technology/SaaS: 8-15x revenue
- High-growth market leaders: 10-20x+ revenue

## Key Term Sheet Components

### Economic Terms

#### 1. Valuation
**Pre-Money Valuation:** Company value before investment
**Post-Money Valuation:** Company value after investment
**Formula:** Post-Money = Pre-Money + Investment

**Example:**
- Pre-money: $8M
- Investment: $2M
- Post-money: $10M
- Investor ownership: $2M / $10M = 20%

#### 2. Liquidation Preference
**Definition:** How proceeds are distributed in exit

**Types:**
- **1x Non-Participating:** Get investment back OR pro-rata share (whichever is greater)
- **1x Participating:** Get investment back AND pro-rata share of remaining
- **Multiple (e.g., 2x):** Get 2x investment back before others

**Example (1x Non-Participating):**
- Investment: $2M for 20% ownership
- Exit at $15M:
  - Option 1: $2M (1x preference)
  - Option 2: $3M (20% of $15M)
  - Investor takes: $3M (greater amount)
- Exit at $5M:
  - Option 1: $2M (1x preference)
  - Option 2: $1M (20% of $5M)
  - Investor takes: $2M (greater amount)

#### 3. Anti-Dilution Protection
**Purpose:** Protect investors if future rounds are at lower valuation

**Types:**
- **Full Ratchet:** Investor price adjusts to new lower price (harsh for founders)
- **Weighted Average:** Proportional adjustment based on amount raised
- **None:** No protection (favorable for founders)

**Typical:** Weighted average broad-based

#### 4. Option Pool
**Definition:** Shares reserved for employee stock options
**Typical Size:** 10-20% of post-money, fully diluted
**Created:** Usually before investment (dilutes founders, not investors)

### Control Terms

#### 5. Board Composition
**Typical Structure:**
- 1-2 founder seats
- 1-2 investor seats
- 1 independent seat

**Example for 5-person board:**
- 2 founders
- 2 lead investors
- 1 independent (mutually agreed)

#### 6. Protective Provisions
**Definition:** Actions requiring investor approval

**Common Items:**
- Issuing new securities
- Selling the company
- Changing articles of incorporation
- Declaring dividends
- Borrowing money above threshold
- Hiring/firing CEO

#### 7. Drag-Along Rights
**Definition:** Majority shareholders can force minority to sell in acquisition
**Purpose:** Prevent small shareholders from blocking exit

#### 8. Right of First Refusal (ROFR)
**Definition:** Company/investors can buy shares before founders sell to third parties
**Purpose:** Control who becomes shareholder

### Other Important Terms

#### 9. Vesting
**Founder Vesting:** Typically 4 years, 1-year cliff
**Purpose:** Ensure continued commitment

**Example:**
- Founder has 1M shares
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff
- After 1 year: 250K shares vest
- Months 13-48: ~20,833 shares vest per month
- If leaves at 18 months: Keeps 375K, company repurchases 625K

#### 10. No-Shop / Exclusivity
**Definition:** Company can't solicit other investors during negotiation
**Typical Duration:** 30-60 days

## Valuation Calculator

### Quick Ownership Calculation
```
Post-Money Valuation = Pre-Money + Investment
Investor % = Investment / Post-Money
Founder % (post) = (Founder shares / Total shares) × (1 - Investor %)
```

### Example Calculation:
```
Pre-money valuation: $10M
Investment: $2.5M
Post-money valuation: $12.5M
Investor ownership: $2.5M / $12.5M = 20%
```

### Cap Table Evolution Example

**Founding:**
- Founder A: 50% (5M shares)
- Founder B: 50% (5M shares)
- Total: 10M shares

**Post-Seed ($2M at $8M pre):**
- Pre-money: $8M
- Option pool created: 15% (1.76M shares)
- Diluted founder ownership: 85%
- Investment: $2M at $8M pre = 20% post
- Total shares: 12.5M

**Cap Table:**
- Founder A: 42.5% (5M / 11.76M)
- Founder B: 42.5% (5M / 11.76M)
- Option Pool: 15% (1.76M shares)
- Seed investors: 20% (2.5M shares)
- Total: 100% (12.5M shares fully diluted)

## Negotiation Strategy

### Preparing for Negotiation

**Know Your BATNA** (Best Alternative To Negotiated Agreement)
- Other investor interest
- Alternative funding sources
- Ability to bootstrap longer
- Strategic partnerships

**Prioritize Terms**
1. **Critical:** Valuation, liquidation preference structure
2. **Important:** Board seats, protective provisions
3. **Nice to have:** Information rights, pro-rata rights

**Understand Investor Motivations**
- Return expectations (IRR, multiple)
- Risk tolerance
- Portfolio strategy
- Value-add they provide

### Red Flags in Term Sheets

❌ Multiple liquidation preference (2x, 3x)
❌ Participating preferred with no cap
❌ Full ratchet anti-dilution
❌ Excessive protective provisions
❌ Onerous vesting (>4 years, harsh acceleration triggers)
❌ Unreasonable board control
❌ Pay-to-play provisions (punitive)
❌ Overly restrictive exclusivity periods

### Green Flags

✓ 1x non-participating liquidation preference
✓ Weighted average anti-dilution
✓ Balanced board composition
✓ Standard protective provisions
✓ Reasonable information rights
✓ Pro-rata rights for investors
✓ Standard founder vesting (4 years, 1-year cliff)

## Fundraising Timeline

### Typical Process: 3-6 Months

**Month 1: Preparation**
- Update pitch deck and materials
- Build target investor list
- Get warm introductions
- Clean up cap table and docs

**Month 2: Initial Meetings**
- First meetings with 15-20 investors
- Refine pitch based on feedback
- Narrow to 5-10 interested investors

**Month 3: Deep Dives**
- Detailed due diligence
- Product demos and facility tours
- Customer reference calls
- Negotiation of key terms

**Month 4: Term Sheet to Closing**
- Receive and negotiate term sheet
- Complete due diligence
- Draft and negotiate definitive documents
- Close and receive funds

## Key Metrics to Track

### For Production Operations
- Revenue and revenue growth rate
- Gross margin and contribution margin
- Production yield (kg/sq ft/year)
- Revenue per square foot
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Customer lifetime value (LTV)
- LTV:CAC ratio
- Cash burn rate and runway
- Months to profitability

### For Technology Companies
- Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)
- Annual recurring revenue (ARR)
- MRR growth rate
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- LTV:CAC ratio
- Gross margin (typically 70-85% for SaaS)
- Net revenue retention
- CAC payback period
- Rule of 40 (Growth % + Profit Margin %)

## Quick Reference: VC Fund Economics

### Fund Structure
- Fund size: $50M-$1B+
- Investment period: 3-5 years
- Fund life: 10 years
- Management fee: 2% of committed capital
- Carried interest: 20% of profits above hurdle

### Return Expectations
- Target fund return: 3-5x (net to LPs)
- Individual investment target: 10x+ for early-stage
- Portfolio construction: 20-30 companies per fund
- Expected outcome distribution:
  - 0-1x return: 40-50% of investments
  - 1-3x return: 20-30% of investments
  - 3-10x return: 15-25% of investments
  - 10x+ return: 5-10% of investments (drive fund returns)

### Investment Criteria
- Large market (TAM >$1B)
- 10x+ return potential
- Strong team
- Differentiated product/technology
- Scalable business model
- Clear exit path (acquisition or IPO)
- Reasonable capital efficiency

## Funding Stage Decision Tree

**Should you raise now?**

```
Do you have 18+ months runway?
├─ Yes → Focus on growth, build relationships
└─ No ↓

Have you achieved meaningful milestones since last raise?
├─ Yes ↓
└─ No → Delay if possible, focus on execution

Are investors showing strong interest?
├─ Yes ↓
└─ No → More validation needed

Can you articulate clear use of funds and ROI?
├─ Yes → PROCEED WITH RAISE
└─ No → Refine strategy first
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Raising too little** - Run out of runway before next milestones
2. **Raising too much** - Excessive dilution, high valuation pressure
3. **Wrong investors** - Misaligned expectations or value-add
4. **Poor negotiation** - Accepting bad terms out of desperation
5. **Overoptimistic projections** - Lose credibility
6. **Inadequate preparation** - Waste investor time, miss opportunities
7. **No buffer** - Fail to account for delays and setbacks
8. **Ignoring strategic fit** - Take money from wrong investors

---

**Remember:** Fundraising is about partnership, not just capital. Choose investors who add value, share your vision, and will support you through challenges.
