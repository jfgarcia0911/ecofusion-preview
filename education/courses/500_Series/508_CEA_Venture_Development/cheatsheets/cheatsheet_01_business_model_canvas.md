## Key Formulas

### Gross Margin
```
Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100%
Target for CEA Production: 40%+
```

### Contribution Margin
```
Contribution Margin = (Revenue - Variable Costs) / Revenue × 100%
Must be positive to cover fixed costs
```

### Break-Even Volume
```
Break-Even = Fixed Costs / (Price - Variable Cost per Unit)
```

### Unit Economics Example
```
Revenue per lb: $4.50
Variable cost per lb: $2.25
Contribution margin: $2.25 (50%)
Annual fixed costs: $1,000,000
Break-even: 444,444 lbs/year (8,547 lbs/week)
```

## Competitive Advantage Sources

**Rate Your Business (0-10):**

1. ☐ **Cost Advantage** - Lower operating costs
2. ☐ **Differentiation** - Unique value customers pay for
3. ☐ **Network Effects** - Value increases with users
4. ☐ **Switching Costs** - Difficult for customers to leave
5. ☐ **IP Protection** - Patents, trade secrets, trademarks
6. ☐ **Brand** - Recognition and loyalty
7. ☐ **Regulatory** - Compliance creates barriers
8. ☐ **Scale** - Economies of scale advantages

**Total Score:**
- 0-20: Weak moat
- 21-40: Modest moat
- 41-60: Strong moat
- 61-80: Exceptional moat

## Business Model Patterns in CEA

### 1. Vertically Integrated
- Control multiple value chain stages
- Example: AeroFarms (tech + operations + brand)
- Pros: Value capture, quality control
- Cons: Capital intensive, complexity

### 2. Platform Model
- Connect multiple parties
- Example: Farm management software, marketplaces
- Pros: Asset-light, scalable, network effects
- Cons: Chicken-and-egg, lower margins

### 3. Franchise/Licensing
- License brand/tech to operators
- Example: Freight Farms, Agricool
- Pros: Capital efficient, rapid expansion
- Cons: Quality control, support needs

### 4. Technology-Enabled Service
- Scale expertise through technology
- Example: Remote agronomy with AI
- Pros: Higher margins, recurring revenue
- Cons: Tech development costs, acquisition

## Validation Checklist

**Before Committing Resources:**

☐ Customer validation (20-30 interviews per segment)
☐ Value proposition testing (customers understand and want it)
☐ Willingness to pay validation (pricing confirmed)
☐ Channel validation (can reach customers efficiently)
☐ Unit economics positive (contribution margin >0)
☐ Partner commitments (key partners on board)
☐ Resource access (can acquire needed resources)
☐ Financial model realistic (conservative assumptions)

## Quick Decision Framework

**Should you pursue this business model?**

1. **Market Opportunity**
   - TAM >$500M ✓
   - Growing market ✓
   - Accessible ✓

2. **Competitive Position**
   - Differentiated ✓
   - Defensible advantages ✓
   - Can scale ✓

3. **Economic Viability**
   - Positive unit economics ✓
   - Path to profitability ✓
   - Reasonable capital needs ✓

4. **Team Fit**
   - Aligned with strengths ✓
   - Passionate about mission ✓
   - Can execute ✓

**Result:** If 10/12 checkmarks, strong candidate. If <8, reconsider or iterate.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ Copying competitors without differentiation
❌ Ignoring unit economics until too late
❌ Building without customer validation
❌ Underestimating capital requirements
❌ Overcomplicating the model
❌ Neglecting competitive advantage
❌ Scaling before product-market fit
❌ Poor alignment between model elements
