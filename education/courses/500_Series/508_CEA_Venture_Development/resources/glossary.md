# Glossary: CEA Venture Development Terms

**Course:** CEA-508 CEA Venture Development
**Comprehensive Reference Guide**

---

## A

**Accredited Investor**
An individual or entity meeting SEC requirements for investing in private securities. Generally requires $1M+ net worth (excluding primary residence) or $200K+ annual income.

**Acqui-hire**
An acquisition where the primary motivation is to acquire the team/talent rather than the product or business itself.

**ACV (Annual Contract Value)**
Total value of a contract normalized to one year. For multi-year contracts, divide total value by number of years.

**Angel Investor**
High-net-worth individual who invests personal funds in early-stage startups, typically $25K-$500K per investment.

**Anti-dilution Protection**
Contractual provision protecting investors from future down rounds by adjusting their effective purchase price. Types: full ratchet (harsh) or weighted average (common).

**ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)**
Total value of recurring revenue contracts normalized to one year. Key metric for SaaS and subscription businesses.

**Asset-Light Business Model**
Business model requiring minimal physical assets or capital investment. Examples: software, consulting, franchise licensing.

---

## B

**Balance Sheet**
Financial statement showing assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time. Must balance: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

**BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement)**
Your best option if current negotiation fails. Knowing your BATNA strengthens negotiating position.

**Beachhead Market**
Initial target market segment where company focuses all resources to establish market position before expanding.

**Board of Directors**
Group of individuals elected to represent shareholders and oversee company management. Typically includes founders, investors, and independent members.

**Bootstrap**
Building a company using personal funds and revenue rather than external investment. Maintains founder control but limits growth speed.

**Break-even Point**
Level of sales or production where total revenue equals total costs. Neither profit nor loss.

**Bridge Round**
Short-term financing between major rounds to extend runway until next milestone or funding round.

**Burn Rate**
Rate at which company spends cash, typically measured monthly. Gross burn = total monthly expenses. Net burn = monthly expenses minus revenue.

**Business Model Canvas**
Strategic tool for designing and analyzing business models using nine building blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, and cost structure.

---

## C

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**
Total sales and marketing costs divided by number of new customers acquired. Key metric for evaluating customer economics.

**Cap Table (Capitalization Table)**
Spreadsheet showing company ownership structure, including founders, investors, employees, and their respective equity percentages.

**Capital Efficiency**
Measure of how effectively company uses invested capital to generate returns. Revenue or value creation per dollar invested.

**Carried Interest (Carry)**
Share of investment profits (typically 20%) paid to venture capital general partners above agreed-upon hurdle rate.

**Cash Conversion Cycle**
Time between paying for inventory/inputs and collecting payment from customers. Shorter is better for cash flow.

**Cash Flow Statement**
Financial statement showing cash inflows and outflows from operating, investing, and financing activities over a period.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Agricultural production in structures where environmental factors (light, temperature, humidity, CO2, nutrients) are controlled. Includes greenhouses, vertical farms, and indoor farms.

**Chasm, The**
Gap in technology adoption curve between early adopters (13.5%) and early majority (34%). Many innovations fail to cross this chasm.

**Churn Rate**
Percentage of customers who stop using product or service in a given period. Annual churn = customers lost / total customers at start of period.

**Cliff**
Period before vesting begins. Typical founder vesting: 4 years with 1-year cliff (nothing vests until year 1, then 25% vests, then monthly thereafter).

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)**
Direct costs attributable to producing goods sold. For CEA: seeds, nutrients, media, packaging, direct labor. Excludes overhead and fixed costs.

**Concierge MVP**
Minimum viable product delivered manually before building automated solution. Tests willingness to pay and value proposition with minimal investment.

**Contribution Margin**
Revenue minus variable costs. Represents amount available to cover fixed costs and generate profit. Formula: (Revenue - Variable Costs) / Revenue × 100%

**Convertible Note**
Debt instrument that converts to equity at a future financing round, typically with discount (e.g., 20%) or valuation cap.

**Coopetition**
Cooperation between competitors. Strategic partnerships with competing companies for mutual benefit.

**Corporate Venture Capital (CVC)**
Investment arm of corporation making venture investments in startups, often strategically related to parent company's business.

---

## D

**Deal Flow**
Number and quality of investment opportunities a venture capital firm sees. Strong deal flow is competitive advantage.

**Depreciation**
Systematic allocation of asset cost over its useful life. Non-cash expense that reduces taxable income. Straight-line depreciation = cost / useful life.

**Dilution**
Reduction in ownership percentage when new shares are issued. Example: If company issues 20% to investors, founders' ownership is diluted by 20%.

**Down Round**
Funding round at lower valuation than previous round. Often includes punitive terms (anti-dilution, ratchets) and signals trouble.

**Drag-Along Rights**
Contractual right allowing majority shareholders to force minority shareholders to join in sale of company. Prevents small shareholders from blocking exit.

**Due Diligence**
Comprehensive investigation and analysis conducted before investment or acquisition. Covers financial, legal, technical, commercial, and team factors.

---

## E

**Early Adopters**
13.5% of market that adopts new innovations early. More risk-tolerant than early majority but less than innovators. Often opinion leaders.

**Early Majority**
34% of market that adopts after early adopters. Pragmatic buyers wanting proven solutions. Crossing chasm means reaching early majority.

**EBITDA**
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. Measures operating profitability independent of financing and accounting decisions.

**Economies of Scale**
Cost advantages from increased production volume. Spreading fixed costs over more units or bulk purchasing discounts.

**Economies of Scope**
Cost advantages from producing multiple products or serving multiple markets using shared resources.

**Equity**
Ownership interest in company. Total equity = assets minus liabilities. Divided into common stock (founders, employees) and preferred stock (investors).

**Exit**
Event where investors realize returns on investment: acquisition (M&A), initial public offering (IPO), or secondary sale.

**Exit Strategy**
Plan for how investors and founders will eventually realize value from company. Typical exits: acquisition or IPO.

---

## F

**Fixed Costs**
Costs that don't vary with production volume (within relevant range). Examples: rent, salaries, insurance. Total fixed costs remain constant.

**Founder Vesting**
Schedule over which founders earn their equity. Protects company if founder leaves early. Typical: 4 years, 1-year cliff, monthly vesting thereafter.

**Franchise Model**
Business model licensing brand, operations system, and support to independent operators who pay franchise fees and royalties.

**Freemium**
Business model offering basic product/service free and charging for premium features. Common in software. Success requires high conversion rate to paid.

**Full Ratchet**
Harsh anti-dilution protection where investor's effective price adjusts fully to new down round price. Heavily dilutes founders.

**Fully Diluted**
Total shares outstanding including all issued shares, options, warrants, and convertible securities. Used to calculate true ownership percentages.

---

## G

**General Partner (GP)**
Venture capital fund manager making investment decisions and managing fund operations. Receives management fees and carried interest.

**Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy**
Plan for reaching customers and delivering value proposition. Includes target customers, channels, sales strategy, and customer acquisition approach.

**Gross Margin**
(Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100%. Measures profitability before operating expenses. Benchmarks: CEA production 40%+, SaaS 70-85%.

**Growth Hacking**
Marketing approach focused on rapid experimentation across channels to identify most effective ways to grow business. Emphasizes data-driven decisions.

---

## H

**Hockey Stick Growth**
Growth pattern showing flat/slow growth followed by rapid exponential growth. Shaped like hockey stick. Goal of venture-backed companies.

**Hurdle Rate**
Minimum return VC fund must achieve before general partners receive carried interest. Typically 8%.

**Hypergrowth**
Extremely rapid growth, typically 50%+ annually, often 100%+ for venture-backed companies. Requires significant capital and scaling challenges.

---

## I

**Income Statement (P&L)**
Financial statement showing revenue, expenses, and profit/loss over a period. Structure: Revenue - COGS = Gross Profit - OpEx = EBITDA - D&A - Interest - Taxes = Net Income.

**Innovators**
First 2.5% of market to adopt new innovations. Highly risk-tolerant, technology enthusiasts. Important for initial validation but small market.

**Intellectual Property (IP)**
Creations of the mind protected by law: patents, trademarks, copyrights, trade secrets. Critical for defensibility.

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return)**
Annualized rate of return accounting for timing of cash flows. Standard metric for venture capital returns. Target: 3x+ fund return, 20%+ IRR.

---

## J

**J-Curve**
Typical venture capital fund return pattern: negative returns early (investments made), then positive returns later (exits occur). Shaped like letter J.

---

## K

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**
Metrics used to measure success in achieving business objectives. Should be specific, measurable, relevant. Examples: MRR growth, CAC payback, EBITDA margin.

**Key Resources**
Assets required to make business model work. Types: physical (equipment, facilities), intellectual (IP, data), human (expertise), financial (capital, credit).

---

## L

**Late Majority**
34% of market adopting after early majority. Conservative buyers, skeptical of innovation. Want proven, simple solutions.

**Lead Investor**
Primary investor in funding round, typically investing largest amount and negotiating terms. Often takes board seat and plays active role.

**Lean Startup**
Methodology emphasizing rapid experimentation, customer feedback, and iterative product development. Build-Measure-Learn cycle. Minimizes waste and risk.

**Letter of Intent (LOI)**
Non-binding preliminary agreement outlining key terms before definitive agreement. In M&A, precedes deep due diligence.

**Leverage**
Using debt or other means to amplify potential returns. Operating leverage: fixed costs amplify profitability impact of revenue changes.

**Limited Partner (LP)**
Investor in venture capital fund (e.g., pension fund, endowment, family office). Provides capital but doesn't manage fund. Receives returns minus fees and carry.

**Liquidation Preference**
Determines proceeds distribution in exit. "1x non-participating" means investor gets investment back OR pro-rata share (whichever is greater).

**LTV (Lifetime Value)**
Total gross profit expected from customer over their lifetime. Formula: (Average annual revenue per customer × Gross margin %) × Average customer lifetime.

**LTV:CAC Ratio**
Customer lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost. Target: 3:1 or higher. Key metric for unit economics viability.

---

## M

**Management Fee**
Annual fee paid to venture capital GPs for managing fund. Typically 2% of committed capital during investment period, then 2% of NAV.

**Market Penetration**
Strategy to increase market share of existing product in existing market. One of four Ansoff Matrix growth strategies.

**Market Segmentation**
Dividing market into distinct groups with similar characteristics, needs, or behaviors. Enables targeted marketing and product development.

**Milestone**
Significant achievement de-risking business and typically triggering next funding round. Examples: product launch, first customer, profitability.

**Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**
Version of product with minimum features needed to test key assumptions with early customers. Enables learning with minimal investment.

**Moat**
Competitive advantage protecting company from competition. Sources: cost advantage, differentiation, network effects, switching costs, IP, brand.

**MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)**
Total recurring revenue normalized to one month. Key metric for SaaS companies. New MRR + expansion MRR - churned MRR = net new MRR.

---

## N

**NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**
Legal contract prohibiting sharing of confidential information. Used when disclosing sensitive business information to potential partners, employees, or advisors.

**Net Income**
Bottom-line profit after all expenses, interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Also called "net profit" or "earnings."

**Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**
Measures revenue retention from existing customers including upgrades and downgrades. Formula: (Starting ARR + Expansions - Churn - Downgrades) / Starting ARR. Target: >100%.

**Network Effects**
Phenomenon where value of product/service increases as more users join. Strong competitive moat. Examples: marketplaces, social networks, platforms.

**No-Shop Clause**
Provision preventing company from soliciting other investors during negotiation period. Typical duration: 30-60 days.

---

## O

**Operating Expenses (OpEx)**
Costs to run business not directly tied to production. Categories: sales & marketing, R&D, general & administrative. Excludes COGS.

**Option Pool**
Shares reserved for employee stock options. Typically 10-20% of post-money fully diluted. Created before investment to avoid diluting investors.

**Organic Growth**
Growth from existing business operations rather than acquisitions. Sustainable but typically slower than inorganic (acquisition-driven) growth.

---

## P

**Participating Preferred**
Liquidation preference where investor receives investment back PLUS pro-rata share of remaining proceeds. More favorable to investors than non-participating.

**Patent**
Government-granted exclusive right to invention for limited time (typically 20 years). Types: utility (functional), design (ornamental), plant.

**Payback Period**
Time to recover initial investment. For equipment: cost / annual cash flow benefit. For CAC: CAC / (monthly revenue × gross margin). Target: <18 months.

**Pivot**
Fundamental change to business model, product, or strategy based on learning. Famous examples: Instagram (photo sharing), Slack (gaming to communication).

**Platform Business Model**
Business connecting multiple parties and facilitating interactions/transactions. Revenue from fees, commissions, or subscriptions. Network effects create moat.

**Porter's Five Forces**
Framework for analyzing industry competition: threat of new entrants, bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers, threat of substitutes, competitive rivalry.

**Post-Money Valuation**
Company valuation after investment. Formula: Pre-money valuation + Investment amount. Used to calculate investor ownership percentage.

**Pre-Money Valuation**
Company valuation before investment. Investor ownership % = Investment / Post-money valuation.

**Pre-Seed**
Earliest funding stage, typically $50K-$500K from friends, family, angels. Used for initial product development and validation.

**Preferred Stock**
Class of stock with preferential rights (liquidation preference, anti-dilution, board seats). Typically issued to investors.

**Product-Market Fit**
Degree to which product satisfies strong market demand. Indicated by: strong customer demand you can't meet, high retention, word-of-mouth growth, can't lose customers with price increases.

**Proof of Concept**
Demonstration that idea, method, or product is feasible. Used to validate key assumptions before full development.

**Pro-Rata Rights**
Investor right to participate in future rounds to maintain ownership percentage. Prevents dilution.

**Protective Provisions**
Company actions requiring investor approval: raising new capital, selling company, changing charter, taking on debt above threshold, hiring/firing CEO.

---

## Q

**Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS)**
IRS Section 1202 allows excluding up to 100% of capital gains from sale of stock in qualified small businesses. Requirements: C-corp, <$50M assets, held >5 years, active business.

---

## R

**Ratchet**
See Anti-dilution Protection. Full ratchet = harsh, weighted average ratchet = moderate protection.

**Recapitalization**
Restructuring of company's debt and equity. Often involves private equity investment providing liquidity to founders while company remains private.

**Revenue Multiple**
Valuation method: Company value = Revenue × Multiple. Multiples vary by industry, growth rate, profitability. CEA: 2-8x revenue typical, depending on stage and performance.

**ROFR (Right of First Refusal)**
Investor or company right to purchase shares before shareholder can sell to third party. Controls who becomes shareholder.

**ROI (Return on Investment)**
(Gain from Investment - Cost of Investment) / Cost of Investment × 100%. Measures investment efficiency.

**Rule of 40**
SaaS health metric: Revenue growth rate % + EBITDA margin % should exceed 40%. Example: 30% growth + 15% EBITDA margin = 45% (healthy).

**Runway**
Number of months until company runs out of cash. Formula: Cash balance / Monthly burn rate. Rule of thumb: raise next round with 6 months runway remaining.

---

## S

**SaaS (Software as a Service)**
Software delivery model where applications are hosted by provider and accessed by customers via internet on subscription basis.

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**
Portion of TAM your business model can serve, considering geographic, regulatory, or product limitations.

**Scenario Analysis**
Creating multiple potential future outcomes (downside, base case, upside) to understand range of possibilities and key sensitivities.

**Seed Round**
Early-stage funding round, typically $500K-$3M from angels and seed VCs. Used to achieve product-market fit and initial traction.

**Semi-Variable Costs**
Costs with both fixed and variable components. Example: utilities (base charge + usage), maintenance, some labor.

**Sensitivity Analysis**
Examining how changes in key variables impact outcomes. Shows which assumptions have greatest impact on results.

**Series A, B, C, etc.**
Subsequent rounds of VC financing. Series A typically $3M-$15M for scaling proven model. Later rounds for expansion and growth.

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**
Realistic market share you can capture in near term (3-5 years), considering competition and execution capabilities.

**SPAC (Special Purpose Acquisition Company)**
"Blank check" company that raises money via IPO to acquire private company. Alternative to traditional IPO.

**Strategic Investor**
Corporate or industry player investing for strategic reasons (access to technology, market intelligence, partnership potential) in addition to returns.

**Switching Costs**
Costs or friction customers face when changing from one product/service to another. High switching costs create competitive moat.

---

## T

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**
Total demand for product category if you achieved 100% market share. Calculated top-down (market research) or bottom-up (customer counting).

**Term Sheet**
Non-binding summary of key investment terms. Includes: valuation, investment amount, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, board composition, protective provisions, vesting.

**Trade Secret**
Confidential business information providing competitive advantage. Examples: formulas, processes, customer lists, algorithms. Protected by keeping secret, not by registration.

**Trademark**
Word, phrase, symbol, or design identifying source of goods/services. Protected by registration (®) or use (TM). Protects brand identity.

---

## U

**Unicorn**
Private company valued at $1 billion or more. Term coined 2013 by Aileen Lee. Rare but increasing in frequency.

**Unit Economics**
Revenue and costs at individual unit level (per customer, per pound, per facility). Must be positive for business to work at scale.

**Upside Scenario**
Optimistic but achievable future outcome. Used in scenario analysis alongside base case and downside.

---

## V

**Value Proposition**
Statement of functional, emotional, and self-expressive benefits delivered to customers. Formula: "We help [customer] who [has problem] by [solution], resulting in [benefit], unlike [alternatives]."

**Value Proposition Canvas**
Tool for designing and testing value propositions. Customer profile (jobs, pains, gains) matched with value map (products/services, pain relievers, gain creators).

**Variable Costs**
Costs that vary directly with production volume. Examples: raw materials, direct labor, packaging, shipping. Double production = double variable costs.

**Venture Capital (VC)**
Investment in high-growth potential startups in exchange for equity. Seeks 10x+ returns through exits (acquisition or IPO).

**Vertical Integration**
Controlling multiple stages of value chain (upstream suppliers, core production, downstream distribution). Increases control but also complexity and capital needs.

**Vesting Schedule**
Timeline over which equity is earned. Typical: 4 years with 1-year cliff. Protects against early departures. Can have acceleration triggers.

---

## W

**Waterfall**
Distribution order of exit proceeds. Typically: liquidation preferences first, then remaining proceeds pro-rata by ownership.

**Weighted Average Anti-Dilution**
Moderate anti-dilution protection adjusting investor price based on amount raised and new price. Broad-based (includes options) less dilutive than narrow-based.

**Working Capital**
Current assets minus current liabilities. Measures short-term financial health and operational efficiency. Positive working capital means company can pay short-term obligations.

---

## Y

**Yield**
In CEA: Production output per unit area or time. Example: 25 lbs per sq ft per year, 500,000 lbs per facility per year.

**Y Combinator (YC)**
Leading startup accelerator providing seed funding ($125K for 7%), mentorship, and network. Demo Day at end of program. Famous alumni: Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe, Reddit.

---

## Z

**Zero-Based Budgeting**
Budgeting approach requiring justification for every expense each period rather than using previous budget as baseline. Ensures efficient resource allocation.
