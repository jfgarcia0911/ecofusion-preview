# Activity 1: Pitch Deck Development

**Course:** CEA-508 CEA Venture Development
**Type:** Hands-on Exercise
**Duration:** 4-6 hours (can be completed over multiple sessions)
**Format:** Individual or team (2-3 people)

---

## Objective

Develop a professional, investor-ready pitch deck for your CEA venture concept that effectively communicates your opportunity, solution, market, business model, and funding requirements.

---

## Overview

A pitch deck is typically a 12-20 slide presentation used to raise funding from investors. It must tell a compelling story while providing sufficient detail for investors to understand your opportunity and make an investment decision.

This exercise will guide you through creating each section of a professional pitch deck following venture capital best practices.

---

## Pitch Deck Structure

Your deck should include the following slides:

1. **Cover Slide**
2. **Problem**
3. **Solution**
4. **Why Now**
5. **Market Opportunity**
6. **Product/Technology**
7. **Business Model**
8. **Traction**
9. **Competition**
10. **Go-to-Market Strategy**
11. **Team**
12. **Financial Projections**
13. **Funding Ask and Use of Funds**
14. **Closing/Contact**

---

## Detailed Slide Guidelines

### Slide 1: Cover Slide

**Purpose:** Make strong first impression, establish professionalism

**Elements to Include:**
- Company name and logo
- Tagline (one sentence describing what you do)
- Founder name(s) and title(s)
- Contact information (email, phone)
- Date
- Confidential/proprietary notice (optional)

**Example:**
```
VertiFresh Farms
"Ultra-Local, Zero-Pesticide Produce Delivered Fresh Daily"

Jane Smith, CEO & Co-Founder
jane@vertifresh.com | (555) 123-4567

May 2025 | Confidential
```

**Tips:**
- Use high-quality imagery
- Keep it clean and professional
- Establish visual branding you'll use throughout

---

### Slide 2: Problem

**Purpose:** Establish the pain point you're solving

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- What problem are you solving?
- Who experiences this problem?
- How significant is the problem?
- What are the consequences of not solving it?

**Structure:**
1. Problem statement (1-2 sentences)
2. Supporting evidence (data, customer quotes)
3. Current alternatives and their limitations
4. Why existing solutions are inadequate

**Example Content:**
```
Problem: Urban restaurants struggle to source consistent, high-quality greens year-round

- 73% of urban chefs cite produce consistency as top challenge
- Average shelf life: 3-5 days (often spoiled on arrival)
- 40% of leafy greens are wasted in supply chain
- Pest/pesticide concerns limit options
- California/Arizona supply vulnerable to climate, labor issues

"I spend 3 hours per day calling suppliers to find quality greens.
Half the time, I have to change my menu." - Chef Maria Rodriguez,
Top 50 Restaurant, NYC
```

**Tips:**
- Use compelling statistics
- Include customer quotes if possible
- Focus on problem, not solution yet
- Make it relatable and urgent

---

### Slide 3: Solution

**Purpose:** Introduce your product/service and how it solves the problem

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- What is your solution?
- How does it solve the problem?
- What makes it unique?
- Why is it better than alternatives?

**Structure:**
1. Solution statement (1-2 sentences)
2. Key features and benefits (3-5 bullet points)
3. Visual (product image, diagram, or mockup)
4. Value proposition

**Example Content:**
```
Solution: Hyper-local vertical farms delivering restaurant-quality
greens with 21-day shelf life within 12 hours of harvest

Key Benefits:
• Unmatched freshness: Harvest-to-delivery <12 hours vs. 5-7 days
• Zero pesticides: Controlled environment eliminates need
• Year-round consistency: No weather or seasonal variation
• 21-day shelf life: 4x longer than conventional
• Ultra-local: Grown within city limits, supports sustainability goals

[Image: Product in use, facility exterior/interior, or before/after comparison]
```

**Tips:**
- Focus on benefits, not just features
- Use visuals to illustrate
- Connect directly to problem stated earlier
- Keep it simple and understandable

---

### Slide 4: Why Now

**Purpose:** Explain market timing and urgency

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- Why is now the right time for this solution?
- What has changed recently?
- Why hasn't this been done before?
- What tailwinds support your success?

**Structure:**
1. Market trends driving demand
2. Technology enablers that make solution possible
3. Regulatory or policy support
4. Recent events or changes

**Example Content:**
```
Why Now: Convergence of consumer demand, technology maturity,
and sustainability imperative

Consumer Trends:
• 68% of consumers willing to pay premium for local, sustainable food
• "Farm-to-table" restaurant revenue growing 15% annually
• Supply chain vulnerabilities highlighted by pandemic

Technology Maturity:
• LED efficiency improved 300% in past decade, costs down 80%
• AI/ML enabling autonomous growing systems
• Modular facility designs reduce construction time and cost

Policy Support:
• Urban agriculture incentives in 50+ cities
• Carbon accounting standards favor local production
• Food safety regulations favoring controlled environments
```

**Tips:**
- Show you understand market dynamics
- Demonstrate this is not just a good idea, but the right time
- Use data and trends to support timing

---

### Slide 5: Market Opportunity

**Purpose:** Show size and attractiveness of market

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- How big is the market?
- How fast is it growing?
- What is your target segment?
- What revenue opportunity does this represent?

**Structure:**
1. TAM (Total Addressable Market)
2. SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
3. SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
4. Market growth rate
5. Key market drivers

**Example Content:**
```
Market Opportunity: $3B serviceable market growing 12% annually

Total Addressable Market (TAM):
• North American fresh vegetables: $25B
• CEA-suitable crops (greens, herbs): $8B

Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM):
• Urban markets with year-round demand: $3B
• Growing 12% annually (2x overall market)

Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM):
• Target: 2% market share in 5 years
• $60M annual revenue opportunity

[Visual: Market sizing pyramid or concentric circles showing TAM/SAM/SOM]
```

**Tips:**
- Use credible sources (cite them)
- Show your work on calculations
- Be conservative (investors will assume you're optimistic)
- Include growth rate

---

### Slide 6: Product/Technology

**Purpose:** Demonstrate your product and competitive advantages

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- What exactly is your product/service?
- How does it work?
- What technology or IP do you have?
- What makes it defensible?

**Structure:**
1. Product description with visuals
2. Key features and specifications
3. Technology or IP advantages
4. Development status
5. Roadmap (current/future)

**Example Content:**
```
Product: Proprietary modular vertical farm system with AI-powered
growing automation

Current Product:
• 20,000 sq ft modular facilities
• 500,000 lbs annual capacity
• 12 crop varieties (leafy greens, herbs)
• Full growing cycle automation
• Real-time monitoring and optimization

Technology Advantages:
• Patent-pending vertical airflow system (30% energy savings)
• Proprietary AI growing algorithms (15% yield improvement)
• Modular design (50% faster deployment vs. custom builds)

[Images: Facility photos, technology diagrams, product samples]

Development Roadmap:
✓ Pilot facility operational (Current)
→ Two additional facilities (12 months)
→ Franchise model launch (18 months)
→ Fruiting crops expansion (24 months)
```

**Tips:**
- Use high-quality visuals
- Highlight IP and defensibility
- Show what's built vs. planned
- Balance detail with clarity

### Slide 7: Business Model

**Purpose:** Explain how you make money

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- Who pays you?
- What do they pay for?
- How much do they pay?
- What are your revenue streams?
- What are your margins?

**Structure:**
1. Revenue model overview
2. Pricing strategy
3. Unit economics (if applicable)
4. Multiple revenue streams (if applicable)
5. Path to profitability

**Example Content:**
```
Business Model: Direct sales to restaurants and retailers with
subscription model for key accounts

Revenue Streams:
• Direct restaurant sales: 60% of revenue ($15/lb average)
• Retail partnerships: 30% of revenue ($10/lb average)
• Subscription programs: 10% of revenue ($5K/month per customer)

Unit Economics (per facility):
• Annual revenue: $2.5M at full capacity
• Gross margin: 45%
• Contribution margin: 60%
• Facility payback: 3.2 years
• EBITDA margin at scale: 18%

[Visual: Revenue breakdown pie chart or unit economics diagram]

Path to Profitability:
• Facility 1: Break-even Month 14
• Company-level profitability: 3-facility mark (Month 28)
```

**Tips:**
- Keep unit economics simple and clear
- Show margins and profitability path
- Explain pricing rationale
- Demonstrate scalability

---

### Slide 8: Traction

**Purpose:** Prove validation and momentum

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- What have you accomplished?
- What customer validation do you have?
- What are your key metrics?
- What milestones have you hit?

**Structure:**
1. Customer traction (sales, pilots, LOIs)
2. Product milestones (launches, features)
3. Team milestones (key hires)
4. Funding milestones (previous raises, grants)
5. Key partnerships or press

**Example Content:**
```
Traction: Pilot facility operational with $1.2M in signed contracts

Operational Milestones:
✓ Pilot facility launched (Dec 2024)
✓ 300,000 lbs produced year 1
✓ 95% uptime, 98% quality pass rate

Customer Validation:
✓ 15 restaurant customers (avg. $2K/month)
✓ 2 regional grocery chains (pilots in progress)
✓ $1.2M in LOIs for next 12 months
✓ 92% customer retention rate

Recognition:
✓ AgTech Innovation Award 2024
✓ Featured in Forbes, AgFunder News
✓ Selected for TechStars Accelerator

[Visual: Metric progression chart showing growth trajectory]
```

**Tips:**
- Focus on most impressive metrics
- Show momentum and growth trajectory
- Include customer testimonials if possible
- Be honest (investors will verify)

### Slide 9: Competition

**Purpose:** Show market understanding and competitive positioning

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- Who are your competitors?
- How are you different/better?
- What is your competitive advantage?
- Why can't competitors easily replicate you?

**Structure:**
1. Competitive landscape overview
2. Key competitors (direct and indirect)
3. Competitive matrix or positioning
4. Your differentiation and advantages
5. Barriers to entry you're building

**Example Content:**
```
Competition: Differentiated by hyperlocal model + proprietary
technology + premium quality focus

Competitive Landscape:
• Traditional agriculture: CA/AZ farms (5-7 day supply chain)
• Regional greenhouses: 2-3 day supply chain, weather dependent
• Vertical farm competitors: 5-10 players, mostly commodity focus
• Imports: 7-14 day supply chain, quality issues

[Visual: 2x2 matrix with axes: Price (low-high) vs. Freshness/Quality (low-high)]

Our Differentiation:
✓ <12 hour harvest-to-delivery (vs. 2-7 days)
✓ Proprietary growing algorithms (15% yield advantage)
✓ Premium restaurant focus (vs. commodity)
✓ 21-day shelf life (vs. 3-5 days conventional)
✓ Modular, capital-efficient model (50% lower capex per lb)

Competitive Moats:
• Technology IP (patents pending)
• Data advantage (proprietary growing database)
• Brand and customer relationships
• Operational excellence and unit economics
```

**Tips:**
- Be honest about competition
- Focus on your advantages
- Show you understand the landscape
- Explain defensibility

### Slide 10: Go-to-Market Strategy

**Purpose:** Explain how you'll acquire customers and grow

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- Who are your target customers?
- How will you reach them?
- What is your sales strategy?
- What are your customer acquisition costs?
- What channels will you use?

**Example Content:**
```
Go-to-Market: Direct sales to high-end restaurants, followed by
retail partnerships

Phase 1 (Months 1-12): Restaurant Direct
• Target: Top 200 restaurants in metro area
• Approach: Direct outreach + sampling program
• CAC: $500 per customer
• Sales cycle: 4-6 weeks
• Goal: 50 restaurant customers

Phase 2 (Months 12-24): Retail Expansion
• Target: Regional grocery chains (3-10 stores)
• Approach: Pilot programs with in-store demos
• CAC: $50K per chain (across multiple stores)
• Sales cycle: 3-6 months
• Goal: 3 regional chains

Phase 3 (Months 24+): Geographic Expansion
• Replicate model in new metros
• Franchise/licensing model
• Build brand recognition nationally

Customer Acquisition:
• Direct sales team (hiring 2 reps in Q2)
• Industry events and trade shows
• Chef partnerships and influencer marketing
• PR and media strategy
```

**Tips:**
- Show clear phases and sequencing
- Include specific tactics
- Demonstrate customer acquisition economics
- Show scalability

### Slide 12: Financial Projections

**Purpose:** Show the business opportunity and path to scale

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- What are your revenue projections?
- What is your path to profitability?
- What are your key assumptions?
- What are the unit economics at scale?

**Structure:**
1. 3-5 year revenue projection
2. Key financial metrics
3. Path to profitability
4. Key assumptions
5. Scenario analysis (optional but good)

**Example Content:**
```
Financial Projections: $25M revenue, $5M EBITDA by Year 5

Revenue Projection (in millions):
Year 1: $1.5M (1 facility at 60% ramp)
Year 2: $3.8M (2 facilities)
Year 3: $8.5M (4 facilities)
Year 4: $16M (7 facilities)
Year 5: $25M (10 facilities)

CAGR: 105%

Key Metrics (Year 5):
• 10 facilities operational
• 5M lbs annual production
• 200+ restaurant customers
• 10 retail chain partnerships
• Gross margin: 48%
• EBITDA: $5M (20% margin)

[Visual: Bar chart showing revenue growth and path to profitability]

Key Assumptions:
• Average facility revenue: $2.5M at full capacity
• 18-month ramp to full capacity per facility
• $4.50 average selling price per lb
• 45% gross margin, 60% contribution margin
• One new facility every 4-5 months after Year 2
```

**Tips:**
- Use charts/graphs for visual impact
- Show path to profitability clearly
- Be conservative (investors will stress-test)
- Include key assumptions
- Consider showing scenario analysis

### Slide 13: Funding Ask and Use of Funds

**Purpose:** Clearly state funding needs and how you'll use capital

**Key Questions to Answer:**
- How much are you raising?
- What will you use it for?
- What milestones will it enable?
- When will you need to raise again?

**Structure:**
1. Funding amount
2. Round details (Series A, Seed, etc.)
3. Use of funds breakdown
4. Milestones you'll achieve
5. Pathway to next round or profitability

**Example Content:**
```
Funding Ask: Raising $5M Series A to expand to 4 facilities and
achieve company-level profitability

Use of Funds:
• Facility expansion (3 new facilities): $3.0M (60%)
• Working capital: $0.8M (16%)
• Team expansion (10 hires): $0.7M (14%)
• Technology development: $0.3M (6%)
• Marketing & sales: $0.2M (4%)

[Visual: Pie chart showing use of funds breakdown]

Milestones with This Round:
✓ 4 total facilities operational
✓ $8.5M annual revenue run rate
✓ Company-level EBITDA positive
✓ 100+ restaurant customers
✓ 5 retail chain partnerships
✓ Team scaled to 35 people

Timeline to Profitability: Month 24 (company-level)
Timeline to Next Round: Month 30 (Series B to scale to 15 facilities)
```

**Tips:**
- Be specific on amount and use
- Show you've thought through capital needs
- Tie spending to clear milestones
- Demonstrate capital efficiency

## Design and Presentation Guidelines

### Visual Design Best Practices

**General Principles:**
- Clean, professional design
- Consistent branding and colors throughout
- High-quality images and graphics
- Minimal text per slide (use visuals)
- Easy-to-read fonts (min 24pt for body text)
- White space is good (don't cram slides)

**Color Scheme:**
- Choose 2-3 primary brand colors
- Use consistently throughout
- Ensure good contrast for readability
- Consider color-blind accessibility

**Typography:**
- Max 2-3 font families
- Sans-serif generally works best (Helvetica, Arial, Calibri)
- Consistent heading and body text sizes
- Bold for emphasis, not underlining

**Images:**
- High-resolution only
- Relevant to content
- Original photos of your product/facility when possible
- Avoid cheesy stock photos

**Charts and Graphs:**
- Simple and easy to understand
- Clear labels and legends
- Use to show trends and comparisons
- Bar charts for comparisons, line charts for trends

### Presentation Tips

**Preparation:**
- Practice your pitch many times
- Time yourself (typically 10-15 minutes for deck + 10-15 for Q&A)
- Anticipate questions and prepare answers
- Have backup slides for likely questions
- Bring handouts or leave-behinds

**Delivery:**
- Tell a story, don't just read slides
- Make eye contact
- Show enthusiasm and passion
- Speak slowly and clearly
- Pause for questions/discussion
- Know your numbers cold

**Handling Q&A:**
- Listen carefully to questions
- Answer directly and honestly
- If you don't know, say so and follow up
- Don't get defensive
- Use questions to provide additional detail
- Thank them for tough questions

## Deliverables

**Part 1: Complete Pitch Deck**
- 12-20 slides in professional format
- PDF version (final submission)
- PowerPoint/Keynote source file
- All content complete and polished
- High-quality visuals throughout

**Part 2: Pitch Practice Recording (Optional)**
- 10-15 minute recorded pitch
- Present as if to investors
- Include Q&A response examples

**Part 3: One-Page Executive Summary**
- Condensed version of deck in document format
- Can be sent before meeting or as follow-up
- Includes all key information

**Part 4: Appendix Slides**
- Detailed financials
- Additional competitive analysis
- Technology deep dive
- Team bios
- Customer references
- Any supporting data

## Evaluation Criteria

Your pitch deck will be assessed on:

**Content (50%):**
- Clear problem and solution articulation
- Compelling market opportunity
- Solid business model and unit economics
- Strong team
- Realistic financial projections
- Appropriate funding ask
- Completeness of information

**Design (25%):**
- Professional visual design
- Consistent branding
- Effective use of visuals
- Readability and clarity
- Appropriate level of detail

**Storytelling (25%):**
- Compelling narrative flow
- Logical progression
- Engaging and persuasive
- Clear value proposition
- Strong opening and closing
