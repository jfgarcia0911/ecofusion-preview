## Part II: Short Answer (5 questions × 4 points = 20 points)

**41.** Explain the difference between a patent and a trade secret, and provide one example of each that would be appropriate for a CEA company. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
A patent provides exclusive rights for 20 years but requires public disclosure, while a trade secret can last indefinitely but must be kept confidential. A CEA company might patent a novel LED array configuration (visible to competitors anyway) but keep proprietary growing recipes as trade secrets (difficult to reverse engineer). (Must mention duration, disclosure, and appropriate examples for full credit)

**42.** Describe three key components of an innovation culture and why each is important. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
(1) Psychological safety - allows people to take risks and share ideas without fear of punishment, enabling experimentation; (2) Collaboration - cross-functional teamwork brings diverse perspectives and breaks down silos, accelerating problem-solving; (3) Empowerment - delegating authority and resources enables teams to act quickly and take ownership of innovation. (Must provide 3 components with explanations for full credit)

**43.** What is the Innovation Value Chain and what are its three main phases? Provide a CEA example for one phase. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
The Innovation Value Chain is a framework showing three phases of innovation: (1) Idea Generation - sourcing ideas internally, cross-pollination, and external sourcing; (2) Idea Conversion - selection, development, and funding; (3) Idea Diffusion - spreading innovations across the organization. CEA Example for Generation: A vertical farm solicits ideas from employees, partners with universities for research, and engages equipment vendors for new technologies. (Must name framework, list 3 phases, and provide relevant example)

**44.** Explain what a stage-gate process is and list at least three benefits it provides to R&D management. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
Stage-gate is a structured project management approach dividing innovation projects into stages separated by decision gates where go/kill decisions are made. Benefits: (1) Disciplined decision-making with clear criteria; (2) Risk management through staged investment and early termination of weak projects; (3) Resource efficiency by focusing on best opportunities; (4) Consistent evaluation across projects; (5) Clear accountability and deliverables. (Must define concept and list 3 benefits)

**45.** What is Return on Innovation Investment (ROII) and why is it challenging to calculate? Provide at least two specific challenges. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
ROII measures the financial returns from innovation investments relative to costs, calculated as (Benefits - Costs) / Costs. Challenges include: (1) Long time horizons between investment and returns make current ROI difficult to assess; (2) Attribution problems - determining which benefits are specifically due to the innovation vs. other factors; (3) Intangible benefits like knowledge and capabilities are hard to quantify; (4) Uncertain outcomes due to innovation's inherent risks. (Must define ROII and provide 2 challenges)

## Part III: Case Analysis (1 case × 20 points = 20 points)

**Case Study: VertiFresh Automation Decision**

VertiFresh is a vertical farm operating three facilities producing leafy greens. They are considering implementing robotic harvesting technology developed by a startup, RoboHarvest. The technology is at TRL 7 (demonstrated in operational environment at another farm) but not yet proven at VertiFresh's scale and crop mix.

**Investment Required:**
- Equipment: $800K per facility
- Integration and installation: $150K per facility
- Training: $50K
- Total for all 3 facilities: $3M

**Projected Benefits (per facility, annually):**
- Labor savings: $250K/year
- Improved harvest timing (quality/yield): $50K/year
- Reduced waste: $20K/year
- Total: $320K/year per facility

**Risks:**
- Technology may not perform as expected in their environment
- Integration challenges with existing systems
- Employee resistance to automation
- Supplier (RoboHarvest) is early-stage company

**Questions (answer all for full credit):**

**46.** Should VertiFresh conduct a pilot before full deployment? Why or why not? If yes, describe what the pilot should include. (5 points)

**Sample Answer:**
Yes, VertiFresh should conduct a pilot. Reasons: (1) Technology at TRL 7 but not proven in their specific environment; (2) $3M is significant investment with risks; (3) Integration challenges likely; (4) Can validate ROI assumptions before full commitment. Pilot should include: (1) One facility or subset (e.g., one growing tower); (2) 6-12 month duration covering multiple crop cycles; (3) Rigorous data collection on performance, costs, and issues; (4) Defined success criteria (e.g., 80% of human speed, <3% damage rate, cost targets); (5) Go/no-go decision after pilot based on criteria.

**47.** Calculate the simple payback period and ROI for this investment (assume 5-year horizon and ignore time value of money for this calculation). Show your work. (5 points)

**Sample Answer:**
**Calculation:**
- Total Investment: $3M
- Total Annual Benefits: $320K/facility × 3 facilities = $960K/year
- Payback Period = Investment / Annual Benefits = $3M / $960K = 3.125 years
- 5-year total benefits = $960K × 5 = $4.8M
- 5-year ROI = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs = ($4.8M - $3M) / $3M = 60%

(Full credit requires correct calculations shown)

**48.** Identify and describe three key risks in this decision and propose a mitigation strategy for each. (6 points)

**Sample Answer:**
**Risk 1: Technology Performance Risk**
- Description: Robots may not achieve expected speed, quality, or reliability in VertiFresh environment
- Mitigation: Conduct thorough pilot with defined success criteria; negotiate performance guarantees with RoboHarvest; maintain manual harvesting capability as backup

**Risk 2: Supplier Risk**
- Description: RoboHarvest is early-stage startup that may fail or be unable to provide ongoing support
- Mitigation: Assess supplier financial stability; negotiate service level agreements; require source code/technical documentation escrow; develop internal technical capability

**Risk 3: Integration Risk**
- Description: Robots may not integrate smoothly with existing systems, causing operational disruptions
- Mitigation: Detailed integration planning with RoboHarvest and internal teams; phased implementation starting with pilot; maintain manual processes during transition; allocate buffer time and resources

(Each risk needs description and specific mitigation strategy for full credit)

**49.** Beyond financial ROI, what other factors should VertiFresh consider in making this decision? Name and briefly explain at least three. (4 points)

**Sample Answer:**
(1) **Strategic Positioning:** Automation may provide competitive advantage and position VertiFresh as technology leader; (2) **Scalability:** Automated solution may enable expansion to more facilities more easily than labor-dependent model; (3) **Labor Market:** Addresses potential labor shortages and reduces dependence on tight labor markets; (4) **Employee Impact:** Need to manage employee concerns and potential displacement, though may enable workers to move to higher-value roles; (5) **Learning and Capability:** Experience with automation builds organizational capabilities valuable for future innovations; (6) **Timing:** First-mover advantages vs. risks; ability to learn from others vs. competitive pressure to act

(Must provide 3 factors with explanations for full credit)
