# Glossary of Innovation & Technology Transfer Terms

**Course 503: Innovation & Technology Transfer | EcoFusion Academy**

---

## A

**Accelerator**
A fixed-term, cohort-based program providing startups with mentorship, resources, and often investment in exchange for equity. Typically lasts 3-6 months.

**Acqui-hire**
Acquisition of a company primarily to obtain its team and talent rather than its products or technology.

**Adoption Curve**
Model describing the stages of technology adoption: innovators (2.5%), early adopters (13.5%), early majority (34%), late majority (34%), and laggards (16%).

**Agile Innovation**
Iterative, flexible approach to innovation emphasizing rapid prototyping, customer feedback, and adaptation over rigid planning.

**Ambidextrous Organization**
Organization that balances exploitation (optimizing existing capabilities) with exploration (discovering new opportunities).

**Architectural Innovation**
Innovation that reconfigures existing technologies or components in new ways without fundamental technical breakthroughs.

---

## B

**Balanced Scorecard**
Performance measurement framework with multiple perspectives (typically financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth).

**BATNA**
Best Alternative To Negotiated Agreement - the best option if a negotiation fails, used to determine negotiating position.

**Biotechnology**
Use of biological systems, organisms, or derivatives to develop products or processes. In CEA: crop genetics, microbial products, biological pest control.

**Business Model Innovation**
Fundamental changes to how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value, beyond product or process changes.

---

## C

**Capability**
Organizational ability to perform specific activities or processes effectively. Can be technical, operational, or strategic capabilities.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**
Agricultural production in structures where environmental parameters are controlled, including vertical farms, greenhouses, and container farms.

**Co-creation**
Collaborative innovation process involving customers, partners, or other stakeholders in developing solutions.

**Commercialization**
Process of bringing an innovation to market and capturing economic value from it.

**Corporate Venture Capital (CVC)**
Investment by corporations in external startups, combining strategic and financial objectives.

**Crowdsourcing**
Obtaining ideas, services, or content from a large group of people, typically an online community.

---

## D

**Design Thinking**
Human-centered innovation approach emphasizing empathy, experimentation, and iteration. Phases: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test.

**Diffusion**
Spread of an innovation through a population or organization over time. See also Innovation Value Chain.

**Disruptive Innovation**
Innovation that creates new markets by serving overlooked segments, eventually displacing established competitors. Term coined by Clayton Christensen.

**Due Diligence**
Comprehensive assessment of a technology, company, or opportunity before making an investment or partnership decision.

---

## E

**Ecosystem**
Network of interdependent organizations, institutions, and individuals that collectively enable innovation in a domain or region.

**Ecosystem Orchestrator**
Organization or entity that shapes ecosystem evolution through convening, resource mobilization, and vision setting.

**Expected Value (EV)**
Probability-weighted value of an outcome, calculated as: Probability × Value if outcome occurs.

**Exploitation**
Activities that refine and optimize existing capabilities; associated with incremental innovation and efficiency.

**Exploration**
Activities that discover new opportunities; associated with radical innovation and experimentation.

---

## F

**Field of Use**
Specific application area or market segment in which licensed technology can be used. Example: vertical farming vs. greenhouse applications.

**Freedom-to-Operate (FTO)**
Analysis assessing whether commercializing a product or process would infringe third-party patents or other IP rights.

---

## G

**Gate**
Decision point in stage-gate process where project evaluation occurs and go/kill decisions are made.

**Grant-back**
Provision in license agreement where licensee grants licensor rights to improvements or new IP developed.

---

## H

**Horizon 1, 2, 3**
Three Horizons Framework categories: H1 (core business, 0-2 years), H2 (emerging opportunities, 2-5 years), H3 (future options, 5+ years).

**Hybrid Innovation Model**
Approach combining elements of multiple models, such as closed and open innovation, or centralized and decentralized R&D.

---

## I

**Incubator**
Program providing startups with resources, space, mentorship, and support over flexible timeframes (typically longer than accelerators).

**Incremental Innovation**
Small improvements or enhancements to existing products, services, or processes. Lower risk than radical innovation.

**Innovation**
Successful implementation of creative ideas that generate value. Requires novelty, implementation, and value creation.

**Innovation Culture**
Shared values, beliefs, behaviors, and norms that support and encourage innovation within an organization.

**Innovation Ecosystem**
See Ecosystem.

**Innovation Portfolio**
Collection of innovation projects and initiatives managed as a portfolio to balance risk, return, and strategic objectives.

**Innovation Value Chain**
Framework describing three phases: idea generation, idea conversion (selection and development), and idea diffusion (scaling).

**Intellectual Property (IP)**
Legal rights to inventions, creative works, and other intangible assets. Types include patents, trade secrets, trademarks, copyrights.

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return)**
Discount rate at which net present value of an investment equals zero; measure of investment attractiveness.

---

## J

**Joint Venture**
Business entity created by two or more parties, combining resources to achieve shared objectives.

---

## K

**Kaizen**
Japanese term for continuous improvement through small, incremental changes. Philosophy of ongoing improvement involving all employees.

**Key Performance Indicator (KPI)**
Measurable value demonstrating how effectively an organization achieves key objectives.

**Knowledge Transfer**
Process of transferring knowledge, skills, and expertise from one party to another, often from research to commercial application.

---

## L

**Lead User**
User or customer with needs ahead of the broader market, often developing innovations for their own use.

**Lean Startup**
Methodology emphasizing rapid experimentation, minimum viable products (MVP), validated learning, and iterative development.

**License**
Legal agreement granting rights to use intellectual property in exchange for consideration (typically royalties).

**Licensing**
Business model where IP owner (licensor) grants rights to another party (licensee) to use IP.

---

## M

**Make-Buy-Partner Decision**
Strategic choice between developing capability internally (make), purchasing or licensing from others (buy), or collaborating (partner).

**Manufacturing Readiness Level (MRL)**
Scale (1-10) assessing manufacturing maturity, parallel to TRL for production readiness.

**Market Readiness**
State where market is prepared to adopt an innovation; includes customer awareness, distribution channels, regulatory clearances.

**Milestone Payment**
Payment in licensing or development agreement triggered by achieving specific technical or commercial milestones.

**Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**
Version of product with minimum features needed to test with early customers and validate learning.

**MRL**
See Manufacturing Readiness Level.

---

## N

**Net Present Value (NPV)**
Present value of future cash flows minus initial investment, accounting for time value of money.

**Not Invented Here (NIH) Syndrome**
Organizational tendency to reject external ideas or technologies in favor of internal development.

---

## O

**Open Innovation**
Innovation model that leverages external ideas and paths to market alongside internal resources. Includes inbound, outbound, and coupled innovation.

**Open Source**
Development model where source code, designs, or data are publicly available for use, modification, and distribution.

**Orchestrator**
See Ecosystem Orchestrator.

---

## P

**Patent**
Exclusive right granted for an invention, typically lasting 20 years from filing date. Requires novelty, non-obviousness, and utility.

**Patent Landscape**
Analysis of patents in a technology domain to understand competitive position, white spaces, and trends.

**Pilot**
Test of innovation in relevant operational environment at intermediate scale, larger than proof of concept but smaller than full deployment.

**Platform**
Core technology or system upon which complementary innovations are built by the platform owner or third parties.

**Portfolio Management**
Systematic selection, prioritization, and management of projects to maximize strategic value within resource constraints.

**Proof of Concept (POC)**
Demonstration that a technology or approach is technically feasible, typically at small scale in controlled conditions.

**Psychological Safety**
Belief that one can take interpersonal risks (sharing ideas, asking questions, admitting mistakes) without negative consequences.

---

## R

**R&D (Research and Development)**
Activities directed toward innovation and introduction of new products, services, or processes.

**Radical Innovation**
Fundamental changes creating new-to-the-world products, processes, or business models. Higher risk and potential return than incremental innovation.

**Real Options**
Approach to valuing innovation projects based on option value and flexibility to abandon, expand, or delay.

**Relief from Royalty**
IP valuation method calculating value as present value of royalties saved by owning vs. licensing the IP.

**Return on Investment (ROI)**
Measure of investment profitability: (Benefits - Costs) / Costs × 100%.

**ROII (Return on Innovation Investment)**
ROI specifically for innovation investments, often calculated at portfolio level.

**Royalty**
Payment made by licensee to licensor based on sales or usage of licensed IP, typically percentage of revenues.

---

## S

**Scale-Out**
Growth strategy involving replication of standardized units (e.g., multiple facilities) rather than increasing size of individual units.

**Scale-Up**
Process of transitioning from small-scale pilot to full commercial-scale production or operation.

**Scenario Planning**
Strategic planning method examining multiple plausible futures to identify robust strategies across scenarios.

**Scouting (Technology)**
Systematic process of identifying and evaluating emerging technologies relevant to organization's strategy.

**Spin-off**
New company created from internal innovation or business unit, often with parent company retaining equity stake.

**Stage-Gate Process**
Structured project management approach dividing innovation projects into stages separated by decision gates.

**Strategic Alliance**
Partnership between organizations to pursue shared objectives while remaining independent.

**Sublicense**
License granted by licensee to third party; typically requires licensor approval and involves revenue sharing.

---

## T

**Technology Intelligence**
Systematic process of capturing, analyzing, and disseminating information about technological developments and trends.

**Technology Platform**
See Platform.

**Technology Readiness Level (TRL)**
Scale (1-9) assessing maturity of technology from basic research (TRL 1-3) through development (TRL 4-6) to deployment (TRL 7-9).

**Technology Roadmap**
Visual representation of planned evolution of products, technologies, and markets over time, showing linkages between layers.

**Technology Scouting**
See Scouting (Technology).

**Technology Transfer**
Process of transferring technology, knowledge, and skills from one organization to another, often from research to commercial application.

**Term Sheet**
Summary of key business terms for an investment or licensing deal, typically non-binding except for confidentiality and exclusivity.

**Three Horizons Framework**
Portfolio management model allocating resources across H1 (core, 70%), H2 (emerging, 20%), and H3 (future, 10%).

**Trade Secret**
Confidential business information providing competitive advantage. Protection lasts indefinitely if kept secret.

**TRL**
See Technology Readiness Level.

---

## U

**University Technology Transfer**
Process of commercializing research from universities through licensing, startups, or partnerships.

---

## V

**Valuation**
Process of determining economic value of technology, company, or opportunity using various methods (market, income, cost-based).

**Value Chain**
Set of activities an organization performs to create and deliver value to customers. See also Innovation Value Chain.

**Venture Capital (VC)**
Investment in early-stage, high-growth companies in exchange for equity, with goal of significant financial returns.

**Vertical Integration**
Ownership and control of multiple stages of value chain, from raw materials to final product and distribution.

---

## W

**White Space**
Unpatented areas in technology domain; opportunities for new IP creation without infringement concerns.

## CEA-Specific Terms

**Aeroponics**
Growing method where plant roots are suspended in air and misted with nutrient solution.

**DLI (Daily Light Integral)**
Total amount of photosynthetically active radiation received per day; key metric in CEA lighting.

**Growing Recipe**
Specific combination of environmental parameters (light spectrum/intensity, temperature, humidity, CO2, nutrients) optimized for a crop.

**Hydroponics**
Growing method where plants grow in nutrient solution without soil.

**LED Grow Lights**
Light-emitting diode lights designed for plant growth, offering spectrum control and energy efficiency.

**Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)**
Hydroponic method where thin film of nutrient solution flows over plant roots.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**
Measure of light intensity for plant growth, measured in μmol/m²/s.

**Substrate**
Growing medium supporting plants; examples include rockwool, coco coir, perlite.

**Vertical Farm**
Indoor farm using stacked layers to grow crops, typically with LED lighting and controlled environment.
