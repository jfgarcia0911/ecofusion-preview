# Lesson 11: Proof of Concept and Piloting

## Module Overview

This module covers the design and execution of proof of concept studies and pilot programs for CEA innovations. Students will learn rigorous approaches to testing innovations, collecting meaningful data, and making informed scale-up decisions.

**Duration:** 1 week
**Learning Objectives:**
- Design effective proof of concept studies
- Execute pilot programs with rigorous data collection
- Analyze pilot results and assess performance
- Make evidence-based scale-up decisions
- Manage stakeholder expectations during pilots

---

## 1. POC and Pilot Fundamentals

### 1.1 Definitions and Purposes

**Proof of Concept (POC):**
- Demonstrates technical feasibility
- Small-scale, controlled conditions
- Focus: Does it work at all?
- Lab or research setting typical
- Short duration (weeks to months)

**Pilot:**
- Tests in relevant operational environment
- Larger scale than POC
- Focus: Does it work in practice? At what performance?
- Real-world conditions
- Longer duration (months to year+)

**Demonstration:**
- Shows proven solution to stakeholders
- Marketing and adoption focus
- Full-scale or near-full-scale
- Showcase for customers, partners, investors

### 1.2 When to Conduct POC/Pilots

**POC Triggers:**
- Novel technology or approach
- Significant technical uncertainty
- Need to demonstrate basic feasibility
- Before major resource commitment

**Pilot Triggers:**
- Post-POC, before full deployment
- Significant capital investment pending
- Performance in real conditions uncertain
- Regulatory or customer validation needed
- Learning required for scale-up

**When to Skip:**
- Proven technology in new application (lower risk)
- Small-scale implementation anyway (entire deployment is pilot)
- Opportunity cost too high (market window)
- Reliable models and simulations available

### 1.3 Success Criteria Definition

**Characteristics of Good Success Criteria:**
- **Specific:** Clearly defined and measurable
- **Measurable:** Quantifiable with available methods
- **Achievable:** Realistic given constraints
- **Relevant:** Tied to business objectives
- **Time-bound:** Specific timeframe for assessment

**Examples for CEA:**

**LED Lighting POC:**
- Achieve 95% of HPS yield for lettuce
- 30% energy savings vs. HPS
- Equal or better quality (visual, nutritional)
- Demonstrated over 2 full crop cycles
- Cost within 10% of target

**Automated Harvesting Pilot:**
- 80% of human harvesting speed
- <2% damage rate
- 95% uptime
- $0.50/lb cost (vs. $0.75 manual)
- Demonstrated over 6 months

## 2. POC Design and Execution

### 2.1 POC Design Principles

**Control and Variables:**
- Identify key variables to test
- Control confounding factors
- Use appropriate controls/baselines
- Replicate for statistical validity

**Minimum Viable Test:**
- Essential features only
- Simplify where possible
- Focus on critical uncertainties
- Avoid over-engineering

**Measurement Plan:**
- What to measure (dependent variables)
- How to measure (methods, instruments)
- When to measure (frequency, timing)
- Data quality assurance

**Resource Efficiency:**
- Appropriate scale (small enough for efficiency, large enough for validity)
- Timeframe (long enough for confidence, short enough for speed)
- Reuse existing infrastructure where possible

### 2.2 Experimental Design

**Comparative Experiments:**
- Treatment vs. control
- Multiple treatments
- Randomization
- Replication
- Statistical analysis

**CEA Example: LED Spectrum Test**
- Control: Standard LED spectrum
- Treatments: 3 alternative spectra
- Randomized assignment to growing positions
- 4 replicates per treatment
- 3 crop cycles
- Measure: yield, quality, energy use
- Analyze: ANOVA, post-hoc tests

**Sequential Experiments:**
- Series of experiments
- Each informs next
- Iterative learning
- Optimize parameters

**Factorial Designs:**
- Multiple factors tested simultaneously
- Interactions assessed
- Efficient exploration
- Example: Light intensity × spectrum × photoperiod

### 2.3 Data Collection and Analysis

**Data Types:**
- **Quantitative:** Yield, energy use, costs, time
- **Qualitative:** Observations, issues, learnings
- **Operational:** Reliability, usability, maintenance

**Quality Assurance:**
- Calibrate instruments
- Standard protocols
- Training for personnel
- Data validation checks
- Documentation

**Analysis Methods:**
- Descriptive statistics (means, distributions)
- Comparative statistics (t-tests, ANOVA)
- Correlation and regression
- Cost-benefit analysis
- Uncertainty quantification

**Reporting:**
- Clear presentation of results
- Statistical significance indicated
- Limitations acknowledged
- Interpretation and implications
- Recommendations for next steps

## 3. Pilot Program Design

### 3.1 Pilot Scope Definition

**Scale Determination:**
- Large enough for realistic performance
- Small enough to limit risk
- Typical: 5-25% of full-scale
- CEA example: 1 growing rack out of 20, or 1 module

**Duration:**
- Long enough for multiple cycles
- Capture seasonal variations if relevant
- Observe reliability over time
- Typical: 6-18 months

**Location:**
- Representative environment
- Accessible for monitoring
- Supportive host organization
- Risk tolerance appropriate

**Scope Boundaries:**
- What's included vs. excluded
- System boundaries
- Interfaces with existing systems
- Support and resources

### 3.2 Pilot Planning

**Project Plan:**
- Objectives and success criteria
- Timeline and milestones
- Roles and responsibilities
- Budget and resources
- Risk assessment and mitigation

**Technical Plan:**
- Detailed design specifications
- Installation and commissioning
- Operating protocols
- Troubleshooting procedures
- Safety and contingencies

**Measurement Plan:**
- Comprehensive data collection
- Performance metrics
- Cost tracking
- Qualitative observations
- Baseline and comparison data

**Stakeholder Management:**
- Pilot host engagement
- Executive sponsorship
- Regular communication
- Expectation management
- Involvement and buy-in

### 3.3 Pilot Execution

**Phase 1: Installation and Commissioning**
- Equipment installation
- System integration
- Testing and debugging
- Training operators
- Documentation

**Phase 2: Shake down (First 1-2 months)**
- Initial operation
- Issue identification and resolution
- Process refinement
- Intensive monitoring
- Frequent adjustments

**Phase 3: Steady-State Operation**
- Routine operation
- Consistent data collection
- Periodic maintenance
- Performance monitoring
- Anomaly investigation

**Phase 4: Wrap-Up**
- Final data collection
- Equipment disposition
- Lessons learned documentation
- Reporting and presentation
- Transition planning

### 3.4 Risk Management

**Technical Risks:**
- Performance below expectations
- Reliability issues
- Integration problems
- Mitigation: Redundancy, fallbacks, support

**Operational Risks:**
- Disruption to host operations
- Resource constraints
- Personnel turnover
- Mitigation: Planning, training, buffers

**Business Risks:**
- Cost overruns
- Schedule delays
- Stakeholder dissatisfaction
- Mitigation: Contingencies, communication, flexibility

**Risk Monitoring:**
- Regular risk reviews
- Early warning indicators
- Escalation procedures
- Adaptive responses

## 4. Data Collection and Analysis

### 4.1 Comprehensive Data Strategy

**Performance Data:**
- Primary metrics (yield, quality, efficiency)
- Secondary metrics (time, usability)
- Baseline and target values
- Statistical rigor

**Cost Data:**
- Capital costs (equipment, installation)
- Operating costs (energy, labor, materials)
- Maintenance costs
- Opportunity costs

**Qualitative Data:**
- User experience and feedback
- Observations and issues
- Lessons learned
- Unexpected findings

**Contextual Data:**
- Environmental conditions
- Operating conditions
- External factors
- Timeline and events

### 4.2 Data Quality

**Validity:**
- Measures what intended
- Appropriate methods
- Proper calibration
- Elimination of biases

**Reliability:**
- Consistent measurements
- Trained personnel
- Standard procedures
- Quality control checks

**Completeness:**
- All required data collected
- Missing data minimized
- Documentation of gaps
- Imputation if appropriate

**Integrity:**
- Secure storage
- Version control
- Audit trail
- Protection from tampering

### 4.3 Analysis Approaches

**Performance Analysis:**
- Compare to success criteria
- Statistical testing
- Trend analysis over time
- Sensitivity analysis

**Economic Analysis:**
- Total cost of ownership
- Return on investment
- Payback period
- Break-even analysis
- Scenario analysis

**Benchmarking:**
- Compare to baseline
- Compare to alternatives
- Industry standards
- Best practices

**Root Cause Analysis:**
- Investigation of failures
- Identification of limiting factors
- Understanding of mechanisms
- Improvement opportunities

## 5. Scale-Up Decision Making

### 5.1 Scale-Up Readiness Assessment

**Technical Readiness:**
- Performance meets criteria
- Reliability demonstrated
- Understood operating envelope
- Scale-up challenges identified
- Mitigation plans developed

**Economic Viability:**
- Positive business case
- Acceptable ROI and payback
- Costs competitive
- Benefits realized
- Risks manageable

**Organizational Readiness:**
- Capabilities developed
- Resources available
- Support infrastructure
- Change management prepared
- Stakeholder buy-in

**Market Readiness:**
- Customer validation
- Competitive positioning
- Market timing
- Supply chain prepared
- Regulatory clearances

### 5.2 Go/No-Go Decision Framework

**Decision Options:**

**Go (Full Scale-Up):**
- All criteria met
- Clear business case
- Organizational support
- Proceed with implementation

**Go with Modifications:**
- Most criteria met
- Specific improvements needed
- Modified approach
- Proceed with adjustments

**Conditional Go:**
- Promising but uncertainties remain
- Phased or limited scale-up
- Continued learning
- Further validation

**Recycle (Additional Pilots):**
- Results promising but insufficient
- More data needed
- Modified pilot required
- Delay scale-up

**No-Go (Terminate):**
- Criteria not met
- Business case inadequate
- Technical barriers insurmountable
- Terminate or fundamentally rethink

**Decision Criteria:**
- Minimum thresholds on key metrics
- Risk assessment
- Opportunity cost considerations
- Strategic fit
- Resource availability

### 5.3 Scale-Up Planning

**Scaling Strategy:**
- Phased vs. immediate full-scale
- Geographic or system unit expansion
- Parallel implementation vs. sequential
- Timing and pacing

**Technology Transfer:**
- Documentation and procedures
- Training programs
- Support infrastructure
- Knowledge capture and sharing

**Supply Chain:**
- Supplier qualification and expansion
- Manufacturing scale-up
- Quality assurance
- Inventory and logistics

**Risk Mitigation:**
- Lessons learned integration
- Contingency planning
- Performance monitoring
- Continuous improvement

## 6. CEA POC and Pilot Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Vertical Harvest - Robotic Harvesting Pilot

**Context:**
Pilot of robotic harvesting system for leafy greens

**Pilot Design:**
- Location: 1 of 3 growing towers
- Duration: 6 months
- Success criteria: 70% of human speed, <5% damage, 90% uptime, cost-competitive

**Execution:**
- Month 1-2: Installation and shakedown (many issues)
- Month 3-4: Steady improvement (software updates, training)
- Month 5-6: Stable operation and data collection

**Results:**
- Speed: 65% of human (short of 70% target)
- Damage: 3% (met <5% target)
- Uptime: 92% (exceeded 90% target)
- Cost: On par with labor at current speed; better if speed improves

**Decision:**
- Conditional go: Proceed with expanded pilot (2 towers)
- Work with vendor on speed improvements
- Plan for full deployment in 12 months if speed target met

**Lessons:**
- Longer shakedown than expected (normal for robotics)
- Operator training critical for uptime
- Speed improvements realistic through software
- Cost model sensitive to speed and labor rates

### Case Study 2: AeroFarms - AI Climate Control POC

**Context:**
Proof of concept for AI-based climate control optimization

**POC Design:**
- Scale: Software simulation + 1 small growing area
- Duration: 3 months
- Success criteria: 10% yield improvement, 5% energy savings, stable operation

**Execution:**
- Month 1: Baseline data collection, algorithm training
- Month 2: AI control implementation, monitoring
- Month 3: Performance assessment, refinement

**Results:**
- Yield: 12% improvement (exceeded 10% target)
- Energy: 3% savings (short of 5% target)
- Stability: Generally stable, occasional interventions needed

**Decision:**
- Go to pilot: Scale to full growing room
- 6-month pilot to validate at scale
- Continue algorithm refinement for energy optimization

**Lessons:**
- Simulation valuable but real-world testing essential
- Yield improvements easier than energy savings (multiple factors)
- AI system required operator trust-building
- Data quality critical for AI performance

### Case Study 3: Freight Farms - Container System Pilot

**Context:**
Pilot of new container farm design with first customer

**Pilot Structure:**
- Customer as pilot partner
- 6-month pilot period
- Freight Farms support and monitoring
- Discounted pricing

**Data Collection:**
- Freight Farms: Technical performance data, remote monitoring
- Customer: Operational data, cost data, crop data, feedback

**Results:**
- Performance: Met technical specifications
- Issues: Several design issues identified and addressed
- Customer satisfaction: High after issue resolution
- Business model: Validated customer value proposition

**Decision:**
- Go: Launch to market
- Incorporate design improvements
- Use customer as reference and case study
- Scale manufacturing

**Lessons:**
- Customer pilots provide real-world validation
- Strong customer support critical during pilot
- Issues are opportunities for improvement
- Reference customers valuable for sales

## Key Takeaways

1. **POCs and pilots** de-risk innovation through progressive validation in increasingly realistic conditions
2. **Clear success criteria** defined upfront enable objective evaluation
3. **Rigorous experimental design** with controls, replication, and statistics provides confidence
4. **Comprehensive data collection** on performance, costs, and qualitative observations informs decisions
5. **Pilot programs** require careful planning, execution, and risk management
6. **Scale-up decisions** should be evidence-based using defined criteria and frameworks
7. **Stakeholder management** and communication throughout are critical
8. **CEA-specific considerations** include biological variability, long crop cycles, and environmental interactions
9. **Failing pilots** can be successes if learning is captured and applied
10. **Pilot-to-scale transition** requires technology transfer, training, and continued monitoring
