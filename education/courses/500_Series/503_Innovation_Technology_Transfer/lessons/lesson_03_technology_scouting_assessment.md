# Lesson 3: Technology Scouting and Assessment

## Module Overview

This module provides systematic approaches for identifying, evaluating, and acquiring emerging technologies relevant to CEA. Students will learn to build technology intelligence systems, conduct comprehensive technology assessments, and make informed decisions about technology adoption or development.

**Duration:** 1 week
**Learning Objectives:**
- Establish technology intelligence and scouting systems
- Apply systematic frameworks for technology assessment
- Conduct thorough technical and commercial due diligence
- Evaluate technology readiness and adoption risks
- Make informed technology sourcing decisions

---

## 1. Technology Intelligence Systems

### 1.1 What is Technology Intelligence?

**Technology Intelligence (TI)** is the systematic process of capturing, analyzing, and disseminating information about technological developments, trends, and opportunities relevant to an organization's strategic objectives.

**Purpose:**
- Identify emerging technologies early
- Monitor competitive technology developments
- Support strategic planning and R&D prioritization
- Enable proactive technology acquisition
- Reduce risk of technology disruption

### 1.2 Technology Intelligence Framework

**Components of TI System:**

**1. Scanning**
- Broad monitoring of technology landscape
- Multiple information sources
- Automated and manual processes
- Early warning of emerging trends

**2. Monitoring**
- Focused tracking of specific technologies
- Regular updates on development progress
- Competitive intelligence gathering
- Quantitative metrics and indicators

**3. Analysis**
- Assessment of technology potential
- Evaluation of strategic relevance
- Identification of threats and opportunities
- Scenario development

**4. Communication**
- Dissemination to stakeholders
- Decision support
- Knowledge management
- Strategic dialogue facilitation

**5. Action**
- Technology acquisition decisions
- R&D project initiation
- Partnership development
- Strategic planning input

**Patent and IP Sources**
- Patent databases (USPTO, EPO, WIPO)

**Industry and Market Sources**
- Trade publications and journals

**Digital and Social Sources**
- Technology blogs and websites

**Network and Expert Sources**
- Scientific advisory boards

- Consultant reports
- Peer companies and collaborators

## 2. Technology Scouting

### 2.1 Technology Scouting Approaches

**Passive Scouting**
- Broad environmental scanning
- Opportunistic identification
- Low resource requirements
- May miss important developments

**Active Scouting**
- Targeted search campaigns
- Dedicated scouting resources
- Systematic and comprehensive
- Higher cost but more complete

**Hybrid Approach**
- Continuous passive scanning
- Periodic active campaigns
- Balanced resource allocation
- Combines benefits of both

### 2.2 Scouting Methodologies

**Technology Trend Analysis**
- Identify major trends in relevant domains
- Analyze trajectory and pace of development
- Assess implications for CEA
- Prioritize trends for deeper investigation

**S-Curve Analysis**
- Map technologies on adoption/maturity curves
- Identify technologies approaching inflection points
- Assess timing of technology transitions
- Plan technology evolution strategy

**Technology Forecasting**
- Extrapolate from current developments
- Use expert panels (Delphi method)
- Scenario planning for multiple futures
- Roadmapping for desired futures

**Competitor Technology Intelligence**
- Monitor competitor technology activities
- Analyze competitor patents and publications
- Track competitor partnerships
- Benchmark technology capabilities

### 2.3 Scouting Networks

**Internal Scouts**
- R&D personnel with scanning responsibilities
- Business development team
- Strategic planning function
- Technical specialists

**External Scouts**
- Academic collaborators
- Industry consultants
- Technology brokers
- Regional innovation centers

**Organized Scouting Networks**
- Formal scout networks (e.g., P&G Connect + Develop)
- Technology hunting partnerships
- Scout posting system (scouts in key regions/institutions)
- Incentive systems for scouts

### 2.4 Technology Scouting Process

**Step 1: Define Search Scope**
- Specify technology domains of interest
- Set parameters (geography, maturity, performance)
- Identify priority needs and challenges
- Create search briefs

**Step 2: Execute Search**
- Query multiple sources
- Engage scouting network
- Attend relevant events
- Review captured technologies

**Step 3: Screen and Filter**
- Apply initial screening criteria
- Eliminate clearly unsuitable options
- Prioritize for deeper assessment
- Document rationale

**Step 4: Assess and Evaluate**
- Conduct detailed technology assessment
- Perform due diligence
- Estimate value and fit
- Make recommendations

**Step 5: Take Action**
- Decide on technology acquisition, development, or monitoring
- Initiate partnerships or licensing negotiations
- Plan integration or adoption
- Track outcomes

## 3. Technology Assessment Frameworks

### 3.1 Multi-Criteria Technology Assessment

**Assessment Dimensions:**

**1. Technical Performance**
- Functionality and capabilities
- Performance specifications
- Reliability and robustness
- Scalability
- Integration requirements

**2. Technology Maturity**
- Technology Readiness Level (TRL)
- Development stage and timeline
- Risk and uncertainty
- Proven applications
- Remaining development needs

**3. Strategic Fit**
- Alignment with strategy
- Competitive advantage potential
- Core vs. non-core technology
- Build vs. buy decision
- Partnership opportunities

**4. Economic Viability**
- Cost to acquire/develop
- Implementation costs
- Ongoing operating costs
- Expected benefits and ROI
- Break-even analysis

**5. Market and Competitive Context**
- Market readiness and acceptance
- Competitive technology landscape
- IP landscape and freedom to operate
- Standards and regulations
- Supplier ecosystem

**6. Organizational Fit**
- Required capabilities
- Cultural compatibility
- Change management needs
- Resource requirements
- Risk tolerance

### 3.2 Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)

**TRL Scale for CEA Technologies:**

**TRL 1-3: Research Phase**
- TRL 1: Basic principles observed and reported
- TRL 2: Technology concept formulated
- TRL 3: Experimental proof of concept
- **CEA Example:** Novel LED spectrum concept demonstrated in lab

**TRL 4-6: Development Phase**
- TRL 4: Technology validated in lab environment
- TRL 5: Technology validated in relevant environment (controlled)
- TRL 6: Technology demonstrated in relevant environment (trial)
- **CEA Example:** LED system tested in research greenhouse

**TRL 7-9: Deployment Phase**
- TRL 7: System prototype demonstration in operational environment
- TRL 8: System complete and qualified
- TRL 9: Actual system proven in operational environment
- **CEA Example:** LED system operating in commercial vertical farm

**Manufacturing Readiness Levels (MRL):**
- Assess production readiness
- Parallel to TRL for manufacturing capability
- Critical for technology scale-up

### 3.3 Technology Assessment Scoring

**Scoring Model Example:**

| Criterion | Weight | Score (1-10) | Weighted Score |
|-----------|--------|--------------|----------------|
| Technical Performance | 20% | 8 | 1.6 |
| Technology Maturity | 15% | 6 | 0.9 |
| Strategic Fit | 25% | 9 | 2.25 |
| Economic Viability | 20% | 7 | 1.4 |
| Market Context | 10% | 8 | 0.8 |
| Organizational Fit | 10% | 5 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **100%** | - | **7.45** |

**Decision Thresholds:**
- Score ≥ 8.0: Pursue aggressively
- Score 6.0-7.9: Pursue selectively
- Score 4.0-5.9: Monitor and reassess
- Score < 4.0: Pass

### 3.4 Technology Attractiveness-Feasibility Matrix

**Dimensions:**
- **Y-axis:** Technology attractiveness (strategic value, market potential)
- **X-axis:** Feasibility (technical maturity, organizational capability)

**Quadrants:**

**High Attractiveness, High Feasibility:**
- **Action:** Invest and implement
- **Priority:** Immediate
- **Approach:** Aggressive adoption

**High Attractiveness, Low Feasibility:**
- **Action:** Develop capabilities or partner
- **Priority:** High
- **Approach:** Capability building, joint development

**Low Attractiveness, High Feasibility:**
- **Action:** Consider for operational improvements
- **Priority:** Medium
- **Approach:** Selective adoption if low cost

**Low Attractiveness, Low Feasibility:**
- **Action:** Pass or monitor
- **Priority:** Low
- **Approach:** Periodic reassessment

## 5. Technology Adoption Decision Framework

### 5.1 Adoption Decision Criteria

**Threshold Criteria (Must-Meet):**
- Technical feasibility for intended application
- Economic viability (acceptable ROI)
- Strategic relevance
- Acceptable risk level
- Available resources for implementation

**Evaluation Criteria (Should-Meet):**
- Competitive advantage potential
- Degree of improvement over current state
- Speed to implementation
- Scalability and flexibility
- Supplier capability and reliability

### 5.2 Technology Sourcing Options

**Option 1: Internal Development**
- **When:** Core strategic technology, unique requirements, IP control desired
- **Advantages:** Full control, customization, IP ownership
- **Disadvantages:** Higher cost, longer time, technical risk
- **Example:** Proprietary crop growing protocols

**Option 2: License or Purchase**
- **When:** Mature technology, proven solution, non-core application
- **Advantages:** Faster, lower risk, proven performance
- **Disadvantages:** Dependence, licensing costs, limited customization
- **Example:** Commercial climate control system

**Option 3: Collaborative Development**
- **When:** Shared needs, complementary capabilities, shared risk
- **Advantages:** Shared costs, combined expertise, relationship building
- **Disadvantages:** Coordination complexity, shared IP, slower decisions
- **Example:** Joint research project with university

**Option 4: Acquisition**
- **When:** Strategic capability, strong startup, speed critical
- **Advantages:** Immediate capability, talent acquisition, IP control
- **Disadvantages:** High cost, integration challenges, cultural fit
- **Example:** Acquiring automation startup

**Option 5: Investment/Partnering**
- **When:** Early stage technology, strategic relationship, shared interests
- **Advantages:** Option value, relationship, insights, influence
- **Disadvantages:** Limited control, financial risk, time to maturity
- **Example:** Minority investment in sensor company

### 5.3 Make-Buy-Partner Decision Framework

**Decision Factors:**

**Make (Internal Development):**
- Core to competitive advantage
- Unique requirements
- Available internal capabilities
- IP control critical
- Time available
- Higher acceptable risk

**Buy (License/Purchase):**
- Non-core technology
- Proven mature solution
- Speed critical
- Lower risk tolerance
- Limited internal capability
- Standard requirements

**Partner (Collaborate):**
- Complementary capabilities needed
- Risk sharing beneficial
- Relationship strategically valuable
- Access to expertise/infrastructure
- Shared market opportunity
- Long-term capability building

**Decision Matrix:**

| Factor | Make | Buy | Partner |
|--------|------|-----|---------|
| Strategic importance | High | Low | Medium |
| Internal capability | High | Low | Medium |
| Time urgency | Low | High | Medium |
| Customization needs | High | Low | Medium |
| Risk tolerance | High | Low | Medium |
| IP control importance | High | Low | Medium |
| Cost constraints | Medium | Low | High |

### 5.4 Adoption Planning

**Phase 1: Preparation**
- Secure executive sponsorship
- Allocate resources and budget
- Assemble implementation team
- Develop detailed project plan
- Establish success criteria

**Phase 2: Pilot Implementation**
- Limited scope deployment
- Controlled environment testing
- Performance monitoring and validation
- Issue identification and resolution
- Lessons learned capture

**Phase 3: Scale-Up Decision**
- Evaluate pilot results vs. success criteria
- Assess readiness for broader deployment
- Refine implementation approach
- Update business case
- Make go/no-go decision

**Phase 4: Full Deployment**
- Phased rollout across organization
- Training and change management
- Performance monitoring
- Continuous improvement
- Value realization tracking

**Phase 5: Optimization**
- Fine-tune implementation
- Address remaining issues
- Maximize value capture
- Share best practices
- Plan next enhancements

## 6. Technology Risk Assessment

### 6.1 Types of Technology Risk

**Technical Risks**
- Performance below expectations
- Integration difficulties
- Scalability challenges
- Reliability issues
- Obsolescence

**Commercial Risks**
- Costs exceed projections
- Benefits lower than expected
- Market acceptance problems
- Competitive response
- Supplier failure

**Implementation Risks**
- Project delays
- Cost overruns
- Resource constraints
- Organizational resistance
- Capability gaps

**Strategic Risks**
- Strategic fit erosion
- Competitive advantage loss
- Technology disruption
- Opportunity costs
- Lock-in effects

### 6.2 Risk Assessment Methods

**Probability-Impact Matrix**

| Probability | Low Impact | Medium Impact | High Impact |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| High | Monitor | Mitigate | Avoid/Transfer |
| Medium | Accept | Mitigate | Mitigate |
| Low | Accept | Monitor | Monitor |

**Risk Scoring:**
```
Risk Score = Probability (0-1) × Impact ($) × Exposure Factor (0-1)
```

**Risk Categories:**
- Critical risks: Require immediate mitigation
- Significant risks: Require active management
- Moderate risks: Require monitoring
- Minor risks: Accept with awareness

### 6.3 Risk Mitigation Strategies

**Technical Risk Mitigation:**
- Proof of concept before full commitment
- Phased implementation with off-ramps
- Parallel development of alternatives
- Technology insurance (backup options)
- Expert review and validation

**Commercial Risk Mitigation:**
- Performance-based contracts
- Supplier financial assessment
- Multiple sourcing strategies
- Flexible contract terms
- Market validation studies

**Implementation Risk Mitigation:**
- Detailed project planning
- Resource buffers
- Change management programs
- Training and capability building
- Pilot testing before scale-up

**Strategic Risk Mitigation:**
- Regular strategy reviews
- Scenario planning
- Flexible architectures
- Option preservation
- Portfolio approach

## 7. CEA Technology Assessment Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Automated Harvesting Robots

**Technology:** Robotic systems for automated leafy green harvesting

**Assessment Process:**

**Scouting:**
- Identified through industry conference
- Multiple vendors evaluated
- University partnerships explored
- Patent landscape analyzed

**Technical Assessment:**
- Performance: 80% of human speed, improving
- TRL: 7 (prototype in operational environment)
- Integration: Requires facility modifications
- Reliability: 95% uptime in pilots

**Commercial Assessment:**
- Cost: $500K capital + $50K annual maintenance
- Benefits: Labor savings $200K/year
- Payback: 2.5 years
- Market: Growing vendor ecosystem

**Strategic Assessment:**
- Fit: High (labor cost reduction priority)
- Advantage: Enables cost competitiveness
- Timing: Early adopter opportunity
- Risk: Medium (proven concept, integration risk)

**Decision:**
- Pilot with one vendor
- 6-month evaluation period
- Scale-up decision post-pilot
- Negotiate volume pricing for future

**Outcomes:**
- Pilot successful
- Expanded to multiple facilities
- 30% labor cost reduction achieved
- Continuous improvement program established

### Case Study 2: Artificial Intelligence Crop Optimization

**Technology:** AI-powered climate and crop management platform

**Assessment Process:**

**Scouting:**
- Startup identified through accelerator program
- Technology demonstrated at research facility
- Customer references contacted
- Competitive alternatives reviewed

**Technical Assessment:**
- Performance: 10-15% yield improvement claimed
- TRL: 6 (demonstrated in relevant environment)
- Integration: API integration with existing systems
- Data requirements: Significant sensor and operational data

**Commercial Assessment:**
- Cost: $10K setup + $2K/month SaaS fee
- Benefits: Increased yield value $50K/year potential
- Payback: 6-8 months
- Market: Several competitors emerging

**Strategic Assessment:**
- Fit: High (data-driven optimization strategy)
- Advantage: Platform for continuous improvement
- Timing: Technology maturing rapidly
- Risk: Medium (performance variability, data needs)

**Decision:**
- Trial period with performance milestones
- Start with one crop/environment
- Evaluate performance rigorously
- Option to expand or exit

**Outcomes:**
- 8% yield improvement achieved (below claims)
- Valuable insights generated
- Data infrastructure requirements significant
- Renewed contract with adjusted expectations
- Expanded to additional crops

### Case Study 3: Novel Growing Substrate

**Technology:** Biodegradable growing substrate from agricultural waste

**Assessment Process:**

**Scouting:**
- University research project
- Published papers and presentations
- Patent filed
- Licensing opportunity identified

**Technical Assessment:**
- Performance: Comparable to rockwool in trials
- TRL: 4-5 (lab validation, limited field trials)
- Integration: Drop-in replacement
- Sustainability: Significant environmental benefits

**Commercial Assessment:**
- Cost: Currently higher than rockwool
- Benefits: Sustainability value, waste reduction
- Market: Increasing demand for sustainable inputs
- Scale-up: Manufacturing not yet established

**Strategic Assessment:**
- Fit: High (sustainability positioning)
- Advantage: Differentiation opportunity
- Timing: Early stage, development needed
- Risk: High (technical and commercial uncertainty)

**Decision:**
- Collaborative development partnership
- Co-fund scale-up research
- Exclusive trials in commercial setting
- Right of first refusal for commercial product

**Outcomes:**
- Technical challenges in scale-up
- Timeline extended 18 months
- Performance in commercial setting validated
- Supply partnership established
- Market differentiation value realized

## Key Takeaways

1. **Technology intelligence systems** enable proactive identification of opportunities and threats
2. **Systematic scouting** across multiple sources and networks improves technology discovery
3. **Multi-criteria assessment** evaluates technologies across technical, commercial, strategic, and organizational dimensions
4. **Technology readiness levels** provide common language for assessing maturity
5. **Thorough due diligence** reduces risk and informs better decisions
6. **Make-buy-partner framework** guides technology sourcing decisions
7. **Risk assessment and mitigation** are critical for technology adoption success
8. **Pilot testing** validates performance before full-scale commitment
9. **CEA-specific considerations** include biological complexity, system integration, and scale-up challenges
10. **Structured processes and tools** improve consistency and quality of technology decisions
