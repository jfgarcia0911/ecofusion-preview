# Lesson 10: Technology Roadmapping

## Module Overview

This module covers technology roadmapping—a strategic planning tool that aligns technology development with business objectives over time. Students will learn roadmapping methodologies, develop roadmaps, and integrate them with business strategy and innovation portfolio management.

**Duration:** 1 week
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand technology roadmapping purposes and benefits
- Apply roadmapping methodologies and formats
- Facilitate collaborative roadmapping workshops
- Integrate roadmaps with business and innovation strategy
- Implement and maintain technology roadmaps

---

## 1. Technology Roadmapping Fundamentals

### 1.1 What is a Technology Roadmap?

**Definition:** A visual representation of the planned evolution of products, technologies, and markets over time, showing relationships between technology developments and business objectives.

**Key Elements:**
- **Time horizon:** Typically 3-10 years
- **Multiple layers:** Markets, products, technologies, resources
- **Linkages:** Connections between layers
- **Milestones:** Key events and decision points
- **Strategic alignment:** Connection to business strategy

**Purpose:**
- Align technology development with business needs
- Communicate technology strategy
- Facilitate strategic planning discussions
- Prioritize R&D investments
- Identify gaps and dependencies

### 1.2 Types of Roadmaps

**Product-Technology Roadmap:**
- Links products to enabling technologies
- Shows technology development timeline
- Identifies technology gaps
- Most common format

**Technology-Capability Roadmap:**
- Focus on capability development
- Technologies and skills needed
- Resource planning emphasis

**Market-Technology Roadmap:**
- Links market needs to technologies
- Market pull perspective
- Customer-centric view

**Integration Roadmap:**
- System integration focus
- Multiple technology streams
- Interfaces and dependencies

**Industry Roadmap:**
- Sector or industry level
- Pre-competitive collaboration
- Shared vision and challenges

### 1.3 Benefits of Roadmapping

**Strategic Benefits:**
- Alignment of technology and business strategy
- Common vision across organization
- Long-term perspective
- Strategic decision support

**Planning Benefits:**
- R&D portfolio prioritization
- Resource allocation guidance
- Timeline and milestone planning
- Dependency identification

**Communication Benefits:**
- Visual and accessible
- Facilitates discussion
- Shared understanding
- Stakeholder alignment

**Learning Benefits:**
- Knowledge capture and sharing
- Scenario exploration
- Collective intelligence
- Continuous refinement

## 2. Roadmapping Methodology

### 2.1 Standard Roadmapping Process

**Phase 1: Preliminary Activities**
- Define scope and boundaries
- Assemble team and stakeholders
- Gather information and data
- Plan workshops

**Phase 2: Development Workshops**
- Workshop 1: Market and business drivers
- Workshop 2: Products and services
- Workshop 3: Technologies and capabilities
- Workshop 4: Integration and refinement

**Phase 3: Follow-Up Activities**
- Document roadmap
- Validate and refine
- Communicate to stakeholders
- Integrate with planning processes

**Phase 4: Implementation**
- Execute R&D and development activities
- Monitor progress and milestones
- Periodic reviews and updates
- Continuous improvement

### 2.2 Roadmap Structure

**Standard Format (Horizontal Timeline):**

```
TIME →          2024      2025      2026      2027      2028

MARKET/NEEDS    [Market trends and drivers]

PRODUCTS        [Product evolution and launches]

TECHNOLOGIES    [Technology development streams]

RESOURCES       [Capabilities and investments needed]
```

**Layer Details:**

**Market/Needs Layer:**
- Market trends and drivers
- Customer needs evolution
- Regulatory changes
- Competitive landscape

**Products Layer:**
- Current products
- Product enhancements
- New product launches
- Product retirement

**Technology Layer:**
- Current technologies
- Technology improvements
- New technology development
- Technology acquisition

**Resources Layer:**
- Capabilities needed
- Talent requirements
- Partnerships and collaborations
- Investments required

**Linkages:**
- Vertical arrows connecting layers
- Show dependencies
- Critical path highlighted
- Decision points marked

### 2.3 Information Gathering

**Market Intelligence:**
- Market research and trends
- Customer needs assessment
- Competitive analysis
- Regulatory forecasting

**Technology Intelligence:**
- Technology trend analysis
- Patent landscape review
- Research literature scan
- Expert consultations

**Internal Assessment:**
- Current capabilities audit
- Resource availability
- Strategic priorities
- Portfolio review

**External Input:**
- Customer interviews
- Supplier discussions
- Partner consultations
- Expert panels

## 3. Developing CEA Roadmaps

### 3.1 CEA Roadmap Example

**Vertical Farm Operator Roadmap (2024-2029):**

**Market Layer:**
- 2024: Local leafy greens market
- 2025: Regional expansion
- 2026: Specialty crops emerging
- 2027: National distribution
- 2028: Export markets

**Product Layer:**
- 2024: Lettuce and herbs (current)
- 2025: Spinach and microgreens added
- 2026: Strawberries pilot
- 2027: Strawberries commercial
- 2028: Tomatoes development

**Technology Layer:**
- 2024: LED optimization, automation v1
- 2025: Advanced sensors, automation v2
- 2026: AI climate control, robotics pilot
- 2027: Robotics v1, strawberry system
- 2028: Full automation, tomato system

**Resources Layer:**
- 2024: $2M R&D, 5 engineers
- 2025: $3M R&D, partnership with university
- 2026: $4M R&D, robotics partner, pilot facility
- 2027: $5M R&D, expanded team
- 2028: $6M R&D, new commercial facility

**Key Linkages:**
- LED optimization enables spinach (2025)
- AI climate control enables strawberries (2026-27)
- Full automation required for profitability at scale (2028)
- University partnership accelerates sensor development (2025-26)

**Decision Points:**
- 2025 Q4: Go/no-go on strawberries based on pilot
- 2026 Q2: Build vs. buy decision on robotics
- 2027 Q3: New facility investment decision

## 4. Roadmapping Workshops

### 4.1 Workshop Planning

**Pre-Workshop:**
- Define objectives and scope
- Select participants (8-20 typical)
- Gather background information
- Prepare materials and templates
- Schedule (typically 1-2 days)

**Participant Selection:**
- Cross-functional representation
- Technical and business perspectives
- Different organizational levels
- External stakeholders if appropriate (customers, partners)
- Facilitator (internal or external)

**Materials:**
- Large format roadmap template (wall chart)
- Post-it notes and markers
- Background data and trends
- Examples and references
- Documentation tools

### 4.2 Workshop Facilitation

**Opening:**
- Introductions and context
- Objectives and process explanation
- Ground rules
- Warm-up exercise

**Market and Business Drivers:**
- Identify key market trends
- Customer needs evolution
- Competitive dynamics
- Regulatory and policy changes
- Capture on roadmap

**Products and Services:**
- Current offering assessment
- Enhancement opportunities
- New product concepts
- Timeline and dependencies
- Link to market drivers

**Technologies:**
- Current technology base
- Improvement opportunities
- New technology needs
- Development timelines
- Link to products

**Resources and Capabilities:**
- Capability requirements
- Resource needs (people, funding, partners)
- Timing and availability
- Gaps and dependencies

**Integration:**
- Identify linkages across layers
- Highlight critical paths
- Identify decision points
- Assess feasibility and risks

**Next Steps:**
- Prioritize actions
- Assign ownership
- Define follow-up process
- Plan communication

### 4.3 Facilitating Effectively

**Best Practices:**
- Stay neutral as facilitator
- Encourage participation from all
- Manage dominant voices
- Probe for details and clarity
- Synthesize and summarize
- Maintain energy and pace
- Use visual aids and templates
- Capture ideas without immediate judgment
- Build on contributions
- Create psychological safety

**Common Challenges:**
- **Scope creep:** Keep focused on defined scope
- **Analysis paralysis:** Balance detail with progress
- **Disagreements:** Capture different perspectives, defer resolution if needed
- **Unrealistic expectations:** Reality-check and challenge assumptions
- **Lack of engagement:** Use interactive techniques, address concerns

## 5. Scenario Planning and Roadmapping

### 5.1 Incorporating Uncertainty

**Scenario Planning Integration:**
- Develop multiple future scenarios
- Create roadmaps for each scenario
- Identify common elements (robust strategies)
- Identify scenario-specific actions (contingent strategies)
- Monitor signposts and triggers

**Scenario Dimensions for CEA:**
- Market adoption rate (slow vs. fast)
- Technology breakthroughs (incremental vs. disruptive)
- Policy environment (supportive vs. neutral)
- Capital availability (abundant vs. constrained)

**Example Scenarios:**

**Scenario 1: Rapid Adoption**
- Strong policy support
- Technology breakthroughs
- Abundant capital
- Fast market growth
- → Aggressive expansion roadmap

**Scenario 2: Steady Growth**
- Moderate policy support
- Incremental technology progress
- Selective capital
- Steady market growth
- → Balanced roadmap (base case)

**Scenario 3: Slow Adoption**
- Limited policy support
- Technology challenges
- Capital constraints
- Niche markets only
- → Conservative roadmap

**Robust Strategies:**
- Core technology improvements needed in all scenarios
- Essential capabilities to build regardless
- Maintain flexibility and options

**Contingent Strategies:**
- Aggressive expansion if rapid adoption
- Partnership approach if capital constrained
- International focus if domestic policy unsupportive

### 5.2 Real Options Approach

**Technology Roadmap as Options:**
- Early investments create options
- Options can be exercised or abandoned
- Value of flexibility and learning
- Staged commitments

**Decision Points on Roadmap:**
- Major investment gates
- Technology selection decisions
- Market entry decisions
- Partnership vs. internal development

**Managing Options:**
- Keep multiple technology paths open initially
- Down-select as uncertainty resolves
- Invest enough to preserve credible options
- Exercise options at right time

## 6. Roadmap Implementation and Maintenance

### 6.1 From Roadmap to Action

**Action Planning:**
- Translate roadmap into projects
- Assign ownership and resources
- Set milestones and deliverables
- Integrate with R&D portfolio management
- Link to budgeting process

**Communication:**
- Tailor communication to audiences
- Executive summary for leadership
- Detailed version for R&D teams
- High-level version for broader organization
- External version for partners/customers (if appropriate)

**Integration:**
- Link to strategic planning
- Inform R&D portfolio decisions
- Guide partnership discussions
- Support investment cases

### 6.2 Monitoring and Updating

**Regular Reviews:**
- Quarterly: Track progress against milestones
- Annually: Major roadmap review and update
- Ad hoc: Respond to significant changes

**What to Monitor:**
- Progress on technology development
- Market and competitive changes
- Emergence of new technologies
- Resource and capability development
- External factors (policy, economy)

**Updating Process:**
- Assess changes since last version
- Update market, product, technology layers
- Adjust timelines and milestones
- Revise resource requirements
- Communicate changes

**Common Updates:**
- Timeline adjustments (delays or accelerations)
- New technologies or products added
- Discontinued efforts removed
- Reprioritization based on learning
- Response to external changes

### 6.3 Avoiding Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Too Much Detail**
- Problem: Overly complex, unmanageable
- Solution: Right level of abstraction, focus on key elements

**Pitfall 2: One-Time Exercise**
- Problem: Roadmap becomes outdated, unused
- Solution: Regular reviews and updates, integration with processes

**Pitfall 3: Top-Down Dictated**
- Problem: Lack of buy-in, unrealistic
- Solution: Collaborative development, bottom-up input

**Pitfall 4: Disconnected from Action**
- Problem: Roadmap sits on shelf
- Solution: Link to projects, decisions, and budgets

**Pitfall 5: Overcommitment**
- Problem: Too many initiatives, insufficient resources
- Solution: Realistic assessment, prioritization, saying no

**Pitfall 6: Lack of Flexibility**
- Problem: Rigid roadmap, unable to adapt
- Solution: Scenarios, options, regular updates

## 7. Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Signify GreenPower LED Roadmap

**Context:**
Multi-year roadmap for horticultural LED development

**Roadmap Elements:**
- Market: Adoption curve for LED in horticulture (greenhouse, indoor, vertical)
- Products: LED product families for different applications
- Technologies: LED efficiency, spectrum control, light recipes, system integration
- Resources: R&D investment, university partnerships, manufacturing capabilities

**Key Features:**
- 10-year horizon
- Multiple product streams for different crops and systems
- Technology platform approach (common core, application-specific)
- Partnerships for light recipe development
- Manufacturing scale-up planned

**Outcomes:**
- Guided R&D investment prioritization
- Facilitated partner discussions
- Communicated technology vision to market
- Achieved market leadership

**Lessons:**
- Long-term perspective necessary for platform technologies
- Multiple product streams from core technology
- Partnerships critical for application knowledge
- Regular updates as market evolved faster than expected

### Case Study 2: AeroFarms Innovation Roadmap

**Context:**
Roadmap for vertical farm operator to guide technology development

**Roadmap Elements:**
- Market: Crop expansion and geographic growth
- Products: Crop varieties and production systems
- Technologies: Growing methods, automation, data analytics
- Resources: R&D team, facilities, partnerships

**Approach:**
- Annual roadmapping workshop with cross-functional team
- 5-year horizon
- Integration with business planning
- Quarterly progress reviews

**Key Decisions Informed:**
- Prioritization of crop development
- Automation investment timing
- Partnership selection
- Facility design evolution

**Results:**
- Clear innovation priorities
- Aligned organization
- Successful crop launches
- Efficient resource allocation

**Lessons:**
- Annual update essential in fast-changing environment
- Integration with business planning increases impact
- Cross-functional participation improves quality
- Roadmap evolves significantly year-to-year

### Case Study 3: Dutch Greenhouse Industry Roadmap

**Context:**
Industry-level roadmap for greenhouse horticulture led by Wageningen UR and industry associations

**Roadmap Elements:**
- Market: Global competitiveness and sustainability requirements
- Technologies: Climate control, resource efficiency, automation, data
- Infrastructure: Energy systems, knowledge development
- Policy: Supportive regulations and incentives

**Development Process:**
- Multi-stakeholder workshops (growers, suppliers, researchers, government)
- Scenario planning for market and policy futures
- Collaborative definition of technology priorities
- Commitment to shared R&D agenda

**Outcomes:**
- Shared industry vision
- Coordinated R&D efforts
- Public funding alignment
- Technology development acceleration

**Lessons:**
- Industry roadmaps enable pre-competitive collaboration
- Government participation aligns public support
- Scenario planning essential for long-term industry roadmaps
- Regular updates maintain relevance (updated every 3-5 years)

## 8. Advanced Topics

**Dedicated Roadmapping Tools:**
- Aha! Roadmaps

- Cons: Cost, learning curve

**Selection Criteria:**

- Budget

### 8.2 Linking Roadmaps to Other Tools

**Strategic Planning:**
- Technology roadmap informs strategy
- Strategy drives roadmap focus
- Mutual alignment and iteration

**Portfolio Management:**
- Roadmap identifies project needs
- Portfolio projects feed roadmap progress
- Gaps trigger new projects

**Financial Planning:**
- Roadmap resource requirements inform budgets
- Budget constraints shape roadmap feasibility
- Multi-year planning alignment

**Partnership Strategy:**
- Roadmap identifies collaboration needs
- Partnerships enable roadmap execution
- Joint roadmapping with key partners

## Key Takeaways

1. **Technology roadmaps** align technology development with business strategy over time
2. **Standard format** includes market, product, technology, and resource layers with linkages
3. **Collaborative development** through workshops engages stakeholders and builds buy-in
4. **Scenario planning** integration addresses uncertainty and builds flexibility
5. **Regular updates** (annual reviews, quarterly monitoring) maintain relevance
6. **Action integration** links roadmaps to R&D portfolio, projects, and budgets
7. **Communication** of roadmaps facilitates alignment and decision-making
8. **CEA applications** span operators, equipment vendors, and industry-level roadmaps
9. **Facilitating effectively** requires skill in group process and synthesis
10. **Avoiding pitfalls** like excessive detail, one-time efforts, and disconnect from action is critical
