## Questions

### Question 1 (10 points)
In capital-intensive CEA businesses, what is typically the largest use of capital?

A) Marketing and sales
B) Research and development
C) Facility construction and equipment
D) Working capital for operations

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** CEA businesses are highly capital-intensive, with facility construction, growing systems, climate control equipment, and automation representing the largest capital investments, often $10-50M+ for commercial-scale operations.

---

### Question 2 (10 points)
What does "unit economics" refer to in CEA operations?

A) The cost per square foot of facility space
B) Revenue and costs associated with a single unit of production (e.g., per pound or per growing cycle)
C) Employee productivity metrics
D) Equipment depreciation schedules

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Unit economics analyzes revenue and all associated costs for a single unit of production (e.g., per pound of produce, per growing cycle) to determine profitability at the fundamental level before scaling.

---

### Question 3 (10 points)
Which funding source typically provides the highest ownership dilution?

A) Bank debt
B) Equipment financing
C) Venture capital equity
D) Revenue-based financing

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venture capital equity investment requires giving up ownership (equity) in exchange for capital, resulting in dilution. Debt financing options (bank loans, equipment financing) don't dilute ownership but require repayment with interest.

---

### Question 4 (10 points)
What is "cash runway" and why is it critical for CEA executives to monitor?

A) The facility's physical pathways
B) The number of months the company can operate with current cash reserves
C) Revenue growth rate
D) Production capacity utilization

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cash runway is the number of months a company can continue operations with its current cash balance before running out of money. Monitoring runway is critical for timing fundraising and avoiding cash crises.

---

### Question 5 (10 points)
What is the primary difference between CAPEX and OPEX?

A) CAPEX is for equipment; OPEX is for labor only
B) CAPEX is capital expenditures for long-term assets; OPEX is operating expenses for day-to-day operations
C) CAPEX is tax-deductible; OPEX is not
D) They are the same thing

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CAPEX (Capital Expenditures) are investments in long-term assets like facilities and equipment that provide value over multiple years. OPEX (Operating Expenses) are ongoing costs of running the business like labor, utilities, and supplies.

---

### Question 6 (10 points)
In CEA financial modeling, what is a typical targeted payback period for facility investments?

A) 1-2 years
B) 3-5 years
C) 5-7 years
D) 10-15 years

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** CEA facility investments typically target payback periods of 5-7 years due to high upfront capital costs and the time needed to optimize operations and achieve full production capacity and pricing.

---

### Question 7 (10 points)
What does "burn rate" measure?

A) Energy consumption per growing cycle
B) The rate at which a company spends cash (typically monthly)
C) Employee turnover rate
D) Crop failure percentage

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Burn rate measures how quickly a company is spending its cash reserves, typically expressed as monthly cash outflow. Understanding burn rate is essential for managing runway and planning fundraising timing.

---

### Question 8 (10 points)
Which financial metric is most important for demonstrating CEA business viability to investors?

A) Total revenue only
B) Contribution margin at mature facilities showing path to profitability
C) Number of SKUs produced
D) Social media followers

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Contribution margin (revenue minus variable costs) at mature facilities demonstrates the fundamental business model viability and path to profitability, showing whether the business model works at scale.

---

### Question 9 (10 points)
What is "investor relations" and why is it important for CEA executives?

A) Maintaining communication with investors to provide updates, build trust, and prepare for future funding
B) Only relevant when seeking new funding
C) Delegating all investor communication to CFO
D) Avoiding contact with investors unless problems arise

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Investor relations involves regular, transparent communication with current investors, providing performance updates, addressing concerns, building trust, and maintaining relationships essential for future funding rounds and strategic support.

---

### Question 10 (10 points)
In venture capital funding rounds, what typically follows a Series A round?

A) Seed round
B) Series B round
C) IPO (Initial Public Offering)
D) Pre-seed round

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Funding rounds typically progress: Pre-seed → Seed → Series A → Series B → Series C, etc., with each round raising larger amounts at higher valuations as the company demonstrates growth and reduces risk.
