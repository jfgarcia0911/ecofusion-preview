## Assignment Overview

Executive leaders in CEA must be prepared to respond to operational crises, market disruptions, and reputational challenges with decisive action and clear communication. This intensive exercise simulates a real-time crisis requiring immediate executive response, stakeholder management, and strategic decision-making.

---

## Crisis Scenario

**Company:** FreshLeaf Vertical Farms
- 5 facilities across 3 states
- 300 employees
- $45M annual revenue
- Supplies to 50+ grocery chains and restaurants
- Strong reputation for food safety and quality

**YOUR ROLE:** Chief Executive Officer

**THE CRISIS (Breaking at 6:00 AM Wednesday):**

Your COO calls with urgent news: The company's largest facility (representing 40% of production capacity) experienced a widespread crop contamination event overnight. Initial assessment shows:

- **Contamination Source:** Suspected bacterial contamination (possibly Listeria) in irrigation system
- **Affected Product:** ~200,000 lbs of leafy greens already harvested, 60% already shipped to customers
- **Timeline:** Products have 3-5 day shelf life; some may already be on store shelves
- **Customer Impact:** 30+ major retail customers affected
- **Regulatory:** State agriculture department will be notified per protocol; likely FDA involvement
- **Media:** Local news has somehow learned of the issue; reporters are calling
- **Financial:** Estimated immediate loss of $2-3M; potential liability exposure unknown
- **Employee Impact:** 120 employees at affected facility uncertain about next steps

**Additional Complications:**
- Board meeting scheduled for tomorrow (already planned)
- Major investor due diligence happening this week for next funding round
- Annual food safety audit scheduled for next week
- Competitor has excess capacity and may try to poach customers

**You have 4 hours to respond before the market opens and news breaks widely.**

## Assignment Tasks

### Part 1: Individual Crisis Response Plan (15 minutes)

Working independently, develop your immediate crisis response covering:

#### 1. Immediate Actions (First 60 minutes)
What are your first 3-5 actions as CEO? Prioritize and sequence them.

#### 2. Stakeholder Communication Strategy
Identify key stakeholders and draft communication approach for each:
- Customers (retailers/restaurants)
- Employees (affected facility and company-wide)
- Board of Directors
- Investors (current and prospective)
- Regulatory agencies
- Media
- Public/consumers

#### 3. Crisis Response Team
Who do you assemble for your crisis response team? What are their roles?

#### 4. Key Decisions
What major decisions must you make in the first 4 hours?
- Product recall scope?
- Facility operations (shut down, investigation, timeline)?
- Customer communications?
- Media strategy?
- Financial impacts and reserves?

#### 5. Risk Mitigation
What actions do you take to:
- Protect public health?
- Preserve customer relationships?
- Protect company reputation?
- Maintain business continuity?
- Manage legal/regulatory exposure?

**Deliverable:** Written crisis response plan (2-3 pages, bullet format acceptable)

### Part 2: Crisis Communication Simulation (15-20 minutes)

**Scenario Progression:** It's now 9:00 AM. News has broken. You must handle multiple stakeholder communications simultaneously.

**Activity Structure:**

Students will be divided into groups. Each CEO will face a series of role-played stakeholder interactions:

1. **Call with Major Retail Customer (3 minutes)**
   - Customer is angry, demanding answers
   - Threatens to delist all products
   - Wants immediate resolution

2. **Employee All-Hands Videoconference (3 minutes)**
   - 300 employees watching
   - Fear about job security
   - Questions about what went wrong
   - Looking for leadership and direction

3. **Media Interview Request (3 minutes)**
   - Local TV news wants on-camera interview
   - Decide whether to accept, and if so, deliver key messages
   - Manage reputational risk

4. **Board Chair Emergency Call (2 minutes)**
   - Board chair wants briefing before tomorrow's meeting
   - Concerned about liability and investor impact
   - Questioning company's food safety protocols

**Facilitators will play stakeholder roles and create realistic pressure.**

### Part 3: Post-Crisis Reflection (5-10 minutes)

After the simulation, individually reflect on:

1. **Decision Quality**
   - Were your immediate actions appropriate?
   - What would you change about your crisis response?
   - Did you make any critical mistakes?

2. **Communication Effectiveness**
   - How well did you communicate under pressure?
   - Were your messages clear and appropriate for each audience?
   - What could you improve?

3. **Leadership Performance**
   - Did you maintain composure and confidence?
   - How did you balance multiple competing priorities?
   - What leadership strengths/weaknesses emerged?

4. **Lessons Learned**
   - What did you learn about crisis leadership?
   - How would this change your company's crisis preparedness?
   - What would you do differently in a real crisis?

## Crisis Leadership Framework

### The First Hour (Golden Hour)
1. **Assess** - Understand the situation fully
2. **Assemble** - Gather your crisis team
3. **Analyze** - Determine severity and scope
4. **Act** - Take immediate protective actions
5. **Announce** - Begin stakeholder notifications

### The First Four Hours
1. **Contain** - Limit damage and spread
2. **Communicate** - Manage all stakeholder groups
3. **Coordinate** - Align all response efforts
4. **Comply** - Meet regulatory obligations
5. **Correct** - Begin fixing the root cause

### Crisis Communication Principles
- **Be first** - Control the narrative
- **Be right** - Accurate information only
- **Be credible** - Acknowledge what you don't know
- **Be empathetic** - Show genuine concern
- **Be actionable** - Explain what you're doing

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**DON'T:**
- Delay action hoping the problem will resolve itself
- Minimize the severity to stakeholders
- Blame others (suppliers, employees, etc.)
- Make promises you can't keep
- Hide from media or go "no comment"
- Make decisions without full crisis team input
- Ignore regulatory requirements or procedures
- Forget about employees in the crisis response

**DO:**
- Act quickly and decisively
- Be transparent about what you know and don't know
- Take responsibility and show leadership
- Prioritize public health and safety above all
- Communicate frequently with all stakeholders
- Involve appropriate experts (legal, technical, PR)
- Follow established protocols and regulations
- Support and communicate with affected employees
