## Porter's Five Forces

**Use:** Analyze competitive dynamics and industry attractiveness

**Forces:**
1. **Threat of New Entrants:** HIGH - Capital barriers exist but VCs enable entry
2. **Supplier Power:** MODERATE - Equipment/input suppliers concentrated but growing
3. **Buyer Power:** HIGH - Retail consolidation, commodity-like products
4. **Threat of Substitutes:** HIGH - Field ag, imports, alternative proteins
5. **Industry Rivalry:** MODERATE-HIGH - Capacity additions, limited differentiation

**Actions:** Build defensible advantages, focus on high willingness-to-pay markets, invest in R&D, consider vertical integration

## Blue Ocean Strategy (ERRC Framework)

**Use:** Create uncontested market space through differentiation

**Framework:**
- **Eliminate:** What factors can you remove that industry takes for granted?
- **Reduce:** What factors can you reduce below industry standard?
- **Raise:** What factors can you raise above industry standard?
- **Create:** What factors can you create that industry never offered?

**CEA Example:**
- Eliminate: Geographic/seasonal limitations, pesticides, supply uncertainty
- Reduce: Food miles, water usage, labor intensity
- Raise: Nutrient density, food safety, shelf life, flavor
- Create: Carbon-negative claims, transparent supply chain, personalized nutrition

## Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

**Use:** Understand customer needs beyond product features

**Customer Jobs:**
- **Functional:** Feed family, source ingredients, stock produce
- **Emotional:** Feel good about impact, trust food safety, support local
- **Social:** Demonstrate sophistication, align with values

**CEA Solutions:**
- Year-round availability → 365-day supply reliability
- Food safety assurance → Zero recalls, testing protocols
- Environmental impact → 95% water savings, carbon tracking
- Local food → <100 mile radius
- Premium quality → Optimized brix, flavor scores

## SWOT Analysis

**Use:** Assess internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats

**Template:**

**Strengths** (Internal, Positive):
- What do we do better?
- Unique resources/capabilities?

**Weaknesses** (Internal, Negative):
- What do competitors do better?
- Where do we lack resources?

**Opportunities** (External, Positive):
- Market trends favoring us?
- Unmet customer needs?

**Threats** (External, Negative):
- Competitive moves?
- Market/technology changes?

## OKR (Objectives and Key Results)

**Use:** Goal-setting and performance management

**Structure:**
- **Objective:** Qualitative, inspirational goal
- **Key Results:** 2-4 quantitative measures per objective
- **Limit:** 3-5 objectives max

**Example:**
- **Objective:** Achieve operational excellence
  - KR1: Increase utilization 75% → 85%
  - KR2: Reduce cost/unit by 20%
  - KR3: Achieve 95%+ yield consistency
  - KR4: Zero food safety incidents

**Cadence:** Set quarterly, review monthly, comprehensive review annually

## Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

**Use:** Lead organizational change initiatives

**Steps:**
1. **Create Urgency:** Make case for change
2. **Build Coalition:** Assemble aligned leaders
3. **Form Vision:** Articulate compelling future
4. **Enlist Army:** Engage employees broadly
5. **Enable Action:** Remove barriers
6. **Generate Wins:** Celebrate quick victories
7. **Sustain Acceleration:** Don't declare victory early
8. **Institute Change:** Embed in culture and systems

## Stage-Gate Innovation Process

**Use:** Manage R&D investment and risk

**Stages:**
1. **Ideation:** Generate and screen ideas
2. **Feasibility:** Proof of concept and business case
3. **Development:** Prototype and pilot
4. **Scale/Launch:** Full implementation

**Gates (Decision Points):**
- Review progress and results
- **Decision:** Kill, Hold/Recycle, or Advance
- Allocate resources for next stage

## Scenario Planning 2x2

**Use:** Prepare for multiple possible futures

**Process:**
1. Identify key uncertainties (6-8)
2. Select 2 most important/uncertain
3. Create 2x2 matrix (4 scenarios)
4. Develop narrative for each
5. Identify robust strategies (work across scenarios)

**Example CEA:**
- **Axis 1:** Energy costs (Low → High)
- **Axis 2:** Technology (Incremental → Breakthrough)
- **Scenarios:** Steady Growth, Tech Revolution, Struggling Industry, Efficiency Triumph

## McKinsey 3 Horizons

**Use:** Balance current business with future innovation

**Horizons:**
- **H1 (0-2 yrs):** Extend/defend core business (70% resources)
- **H2 (2-5 yrs):** Build emerging business (20% resources)
- **H3 (5-10 yrs):** Create transformational options (10% resources)

**Application:** Balance optimization of current operations with investment in future growth

## Competing Values Framework (Culture)

**Use:** Assess and design organizational culture

**Four Types:**
1. **Clan:** Collaborative, family-like, mentors, teamwork
2. **Adhocracy:** Entrepreneurial, innovative, risk-taking
3. **Market:** Results-oriented, competitive, hard-driving
4. **Hierarchy:** Structured, efficient, process-oriented

**CEA Recommended Mix:**
- 40% Adhocracy (innovation)
- 30% Market (commercial success)
- 20% Clan (collaboration)
- 10% Hierarchy (food safety, consistency)

## OODA Loop (Crisis Decision-Making)

**Use:** Make decisions in fast-moving, uncertain situations

**Loop:**
- **Observe:** Gather facts, multiple sources, verify
- **Orient:** Analyze situation, assess implications, evaluate options
- **Decide:** Choose course of action, communicate, allocate resources
- **Act:** Execute, monitor results, adapt

**Application:** Faster loops = better decisions in dynamic environments

## ESG Materiality Assessment

**Use:** Identify most important sustainability topics

**Process:**
1. List potential ESG topics (20-30)
2. Assess importance to **business** (financial impact, risk)
3. Assess importance to **stakeholders** (customers, investors, employees)
4. Plot on 2x2 matrix
5. Focus reporting on high/high topics (material)

**CEA High Priority Topics:**
- Energy use and carbon
- Water efficiency
- Food safety and quality
- Employee health and safety
- Diversity and inclusion
- Local economic impact

## Capital Allocation Framework

**Use:** Prioritize investment across competing uses

**Criteria:**
1. **Return on Investment:** IRR, NPV, payback
2. **Strategic Fit:** Alignment with strategy, competitive position
3. **Risk Profile:** Technology, market, execution risk
4. **Resource Constraints:** Capital, management bandwidth

**Competing Uses:**
- Organic growth (new facilities)
- Technology investment (R&D, automation)
- Working capital
- M&A
- Balance sheet (debt paydown, reserves)

## IP Strategy Matrix

**IP Type | Protection | Use Case**

- **Patents:** 20 years | Novel inventions (growing systems, automation)
- **Trade Secrets:** Indefinite | Confidential know-how (recipes, formulations)
- **Trademarks:** Renewable | Brand names and logos
- **Copyrights:** Automatic | Creative works (software, content)

**Strategy:** Patent defensible competitive advantages, protect trade secrets carefully, register key trademarks

## M&A Valuation Methods

**Method | Formula | CEA Application**

**Revenue Multiples:**
- EV = Revenue × Multiple
- CEA: 1-3x for private, growing companies

**EBITDA Multiples:**
- EV = EBITDA × Multiple
- CEA: 6-10x for profitable private companies

**DCF:**
- NPV of 10-year cash flows + terminal value
- Discount rate: 12-18% (venture) to 8-12% (mature)

**Replacement Value:**
- Facility cost × # facilities
- Floor value for distressed situations

## Crisis Communication Principles

**The 5 C's:**

1. **Compassion:** Put people first, show empathy
2. **Concern:** Demonstrate authentic care
3. **Control:** Show leadership and management of situation
4. **Competence:** Expertise and capability
5. **Commitment:** Long-term dedication to resolution

**Actions:**
- Speed (respond within hours if public)
- Transparency (honest about what happened)
- Responsibility (take accountability)
- Consistency (one voice, aligned messages)
