- Understand international trade agreements and their impact on agriculture

## 1. Foundations of Agricultural Trade Policy

### Why Trade Matters for Agriculture

**U.S. Agriculture Trade** (2024):
- Exports: $175+ billion annually
- Top exports: Soybeans, corn, beef, pork, poultry, wheat, dairy, fruits, nuts
- Top markets: China, Canada, Mexico, Japan, EU
- Trade surplus in agricultural products

**Benefits of Trade**:
- Market access for surplus production
- Higher prices for farmers
- Economic growth and rural prosperity
- Consumer access to diverse products year-round

**Trade Challenges**:
- Import competition
- Non-tariff barriers (regulations, standards)
- Currency fluctuations
- Political tensions affecting trade

### Trade Policy Framework

**Constitutional Authority**: Congress regulates foreign commerce

**Executive Branch Role**:
- USTR (U.S. Trade Representative): Negotiates trade agreements
- USDA Foreign Agricultural Service: Export promotion
- Commerce Department: Trade enforcement
- State Department: International relations

**Congressional Role**:
- Ratify trade agreements
- Set tariff levels
- Trade promotion authority (TPA)
- Oversight and appropriations

## 2. World Trade Organization (WTO)

### WTO Structure and Rules

**Purpose**: Liberalize international trade, reduce barriers

**Key Principles**:
- **Most Favored Nation (MFN)**: No discrimination among trading partners
- **National Treatment**: Imported products treated same as domestic
- **Transparency**: Trade rules published and predictable
- **Dispute Resolution**: Binding mechanism for trade disputes

**Agreements Affecting Agriculture**:
- Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)
- Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures (SPS Agreement)
- Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT Agreement)

### Agreement on Agriculture

**Three Pillars**:

**1. Market Access**:
- Reduce tariffs on agricultural products
- Convert non-tariff barriers to tariffs
- Tariff rate quotas (TRQs): Lower tariff up to quota, higher above

**2. Domestic Support**:
- Categorize agricultural subsidies by "boxes"
- **Green Box**: Minimal trade distortion (research, extension, conservation)
- **Blue Box**: Production-limiting programs
- **Amber Box**: Trade-distorting support (subject to limits)

**3. Export Competition**:
- Eliminate export subsidies
- Regulate export credits
- Food aid disciplines

**Doha Round Stalemate**:
- Negotiations launched 2001
- Developed vs. developing country positions
- Agricultural support and market access disagreements
- Minimal progress, essentially stalled

## 3. Regional and Bilateral Trade Agreements

### USMCA (U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement)

**Replaced NAFTA** (2020):
- Largest U.S. agricultural export markets
- Duty-free access for most agricultural products
- Regulatory cooperation

**Key Agricultural Provisions**:
- **Dairy**: Limited U.S. access to Canadian market
- **Wheat**: Canadian grading system access
- **Seasonal produce**: Dispute resolution mechanism (requested by U.S. vegetable growers)
- **Biotechnology**: Enhanced cooperation
- **Geographical indications**: Protections and limitations

**Advocacy Dynamics**:
- Farm groups generally supportive
- Regional differences (dairy producers opposed some provisions)
- Labor and environmental groups concerned
- Close vote in Congress

### Other Free Trade Agreements

**Active U.S. FTAs**:
- Australia, Bahrain, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic-Central America (CAFTA-DR), Jordan, Korea, Morocco, Oman, Panama, Peru, Singapore

**Typical Agricultural Provisions**:
- Eliminate or reduce tariffs
- Tariff rate quotas for sensitive products
- SPS cooperation
- Geographic indication protections

**Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP)**:
- Obama administration negotiated (2016)
- Trump administration withdrew (2017)
- Agricultural sector largely supportive
- Market access to Japan, Vietnam, others

## 4. Tariffs and Trade Barriers

### Tariff Types

**Ad Valorem Tariffs**: Percentage of value (e.g., 10% of product value)

**Specific Tariffs**: Fixed amount per unit (e.g., $5 per ton)

**Compound Tariffs**: Combination of ad valorem and specific

**Tariff Rate Quotas (TRQs)**: Two-tier system
- In-quota: Low or zero tariff up to quantity
- Over-quota: Higher tariff above quantity

**Example**: U.S. sugar TRQ
- In-quota: Minimal tariff
- Over-quota: Very high tariff (protects domestic sugar)

### Non-Tariff Barriers

**SPS Measures** (Sanitary and Phytosanitary):
- Food safety standards
- Plant health measures
- Animal health regulations
- Can be legitimate or disguised protectionism

**Example Issues**:
- EU ban on hormone-treated beef (U.S. opposes)
- Restrictions on biotechnology crops
- Maximum residue levels for pesticides

**TBT Measures** (Technical Barriers to Trade):
- Labeling requirements
- Packaging standards
- Quality and grading standards
- Certification requirements

**Other Barriers**:
- Import licensing
- State trading enterprises
- Customs procedures
- Geographic indications

## 5. Trade Policy Advocacy

### Export Promotion Advocacy

**USDA Programs**:
- **Market Access Program (MAP)**: Cost-share for overseas marketing
- **Foreign Market Development (FMD)**: Long-term market development
- **Export Credit Guarantee Program**: Financing for buyers
- **Food for Peace**: Food aid (also trade development)

**Advocacy Objectives**:
- Appropriations for trade promotion programs
- USDA trade missions and market development
- Trade agreement negotiations
- Removal of foreign trade barriers

### Import Competition Response

**Trade Remedy Laws**:
- **Antidumping**: Imports sold below cost
- **Countervailing Duties**: Subsidized imports
- **Safeguards**: Surge in imports causing injury

**Section 232**: National security-based tariffs

**Section 301**: Retaliation for unfair trade practices

**Advocacy Strategies**:
- Petition for trade remedies
- Support or oppose tariff actions
- Seek exclusions from tariffs
- Advocate for trade adjustment assistance

### Trade Agreement Advocacy

**During Negotiations**:
- Input to USTR through advisory committees
- Public comment opportunities
- Meetings with negotiators
- Coalition building

**Congressional Consideration**:
- Trade Promotion Authority (TPA): Fast track, yes/no vote
- Advocacy to support or oppose
- Implementation legislation
- Appropriations for programs in agreements

### Removing Foreign Barriers

**Advocacy Approaches**:
- Bilateral negotiations (USDA, USTR, State)
- WTO dispute settlement
- Retaliatory tariffs (political pressure)
- Technical assistance and capacity building

**SPS Barrier Removal**:
- Scientific evidence demonstrating safety
- Equivalency determinations
- Harmonization to international standards (Codex)
- Political pressure

- Future market opportunities

- Recent FTAs include enforceable provisions

**Carbon Border Adjustments**:

**Sustainable Agriculture Trade**:

- Eco-labeling in trade

**Food Sovereignty vs. Free Trade**:
- Some countries prioritize domestic food production
- Import barriers for food security
- Tension with trade liberalization

**Food Aid**:

- Cash-based aid more efficient (debate)

- WTO disciplines on food aid

## 8. Trade Policy Process and Stakeholders

### Trade Advisory Committees

**Agricultural Policy Advisory Committee (APAC)**:
- Senior-level agricultural stakeholders
- Advise USTR on trade policy
- Confidential access to negotiating texts

**Agricultural Technical Advisory Committees (ATACs)**:
- Commodity-specific (animals, plants, processed foods, sweeteners, tobacco)
- Technical expertise
- Review and comment on trade agreements

**Appointment Process**:
- USTR and USDA appoint members
- Balance of perspectives
- Time commitment (meetings, document review)

### Engaging in Trade Policy

**Public Comment Opportunities**:
- Federal Register notices on negotiations
- Section 301 investigations
- Trade remedy proceedings
- Regulatory harmonization

**Congressional Engagement**:
- Trade-related committees: Ways and Means, Finance, Agriculture
- TPA and trade agreement legislation
- Appropriations for trade programs
- Oversight hearings

**Coalition Building**:
- Agricultural Export Coalition
- Commodity-specific coalitions
- Broad coalitions with non-ag sectors

## 9. Case Studies

### Case Study 1: China Trade and Tariffs (2018-2020)

**Background**:
- Section 301 investigation into Chinese practices
- U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods
- China retaliatory tariffs on U.S. agricultural products
- Significant impact on soybeans, pork, other exports

**Agricultural Impacts**:
- Lost market access
- Falling commodity prices
- Farm income stress

**Policy Responses**:
- Market Facilitation Program (MFP): Direct payments to farmers
- $28 billion over 2018-2019
- Trade negotiations (Phase One deal)
- China purchase commitments

**Advocacy Dynamics**:
- Farm groups urged resolution
- Supported trade negotiations
- Appreciated MFP but preferred markets
- Bipartisan pressure for trade deal

**Lessons**:
- Agriculture vulnerable to trade retaliation
- Domestic support can mitigate but doesn't replace markets
- Long-term relationships damaged by trade disputes
- Political costs of agricultural pain

### Case Study 2: NAFTA Renegotiation to USMCA

**Agriculture Generally Supportive**:
- NAFTA beneficial for U.S. agriculture overall
- Canada and Mexico top export markets
- Integrated supply chains

**Sectoral Differences**:
- **Dairy**: Sought greater Canadian market access (achieved some)
- **Sugar**: Concerned about Mexican imports (complex issue)
- **Seasonal produce**: Florida and Georgia vegetable growers sought import protections (limited success)

**Advocacy Campaign**:
- Broad agricultural coalition support
- Regional divisions managed
- Testimony and congressional engagement
- Business and consumer coalition

**Outcome**:
- USMCA passed with bipartisan support
- Agricultural provisions largely maintained
- Some new access (Canadian dairy)
- Seasonal produce dispute mechanism

## Summary

International trade is vital to U.S. agriculture, providing markets for surplus production and economic benefits to farmers and rural communities. Trade policy advocacy requires understanding complex agreements, engaging in negotiations, and navigating political dynamics.

**Key Strategies**:
- Participate in trade advisory committees
- Engage in public comment processes
- Build coalitions across agricultural sectors
- Congressional advocacy for trade agreements and programs
- Bilateral engagement to remove barriers
- Balance export promotion with import sensitivity
