# Activity 3: Coalition Building Simulation

**Course**: AG-505 | **Time**: 2-3 hours in-class + prep | **Points**: 100

## Objective
Experience the dynamics of coalition building, negotiation, and consensus-building through role-playing diverse stakeholders working toward a common policy goal.

## Simulation Scenario

**Policy Goal**: Pass Farm Bill provision supporting "Agricultural Innovation and Urban Farming Research Initiative" with $50 million in mandatory funding.

**Challenge**: Diverse stakeholder groups must agree on program priorities, eligibility criteria, and governance to present unified position to Congress.

## Stakeholder Roles (Assign to teams of 2-3 students)

**1. Vertical Farming Association**
- Priority: CEA technology research
- Concerns: Inclusion of indoor operations, energy efficiency funding
- Non-negotiable: CEA explicitly eligible

**2. Organic Trade Association**
- Priority: Organic production research for all systems
- Concerns: Synthetic inputs in some systems
- Non-negotiable: Organic standards upheld

**3. National Sustainable Agriculture Coalition**
- Priority: Small and mid-size farmer access
- Concerns: Corporate domination, equity
- Non-negotiable: Small farmer set-aside

**4. Specialty Crop Growers**
- Priority: Crop diversity research
- Concerns: Commodity crop focus
- Non-negotiable: Fruits and vegetables included

**5. Land-Grant University Consortium**
- Priority: Academic research capacity
- Concerns: Industry commercialization focus
- Non-negotiable: Peer-reviewed competitive grants

**6. Community Food Security Coalition**
- Priority: Food access in underserved areas
- Concerns: Technology over community needs
- Non-negotiable: Community benefit requirement

**7. Agribusiness Technology Council**
- Priority: Public-private partnerships
- Concerns: Slow academic research timelines
- Non-negotiable: Industry participation

**8. Environmental Defense Fund**
- Priority: Sustainability metrics
- Concerns: Energy use, environmental impacts
- Non-negotiable: Environmental assessment required

## Program Design Questions to Negotiate

1. **Eligible Recipients**: Who can apply?
2. **Research Priorities**: What types of research funded?
3. **Funding Distribution**: How to allocate $50M?
4. **Governance**: Who decides on funded projects?
5. **Performance Metrics**: How to measure success?
6. **Geographic Distribution**: Regional requirements?
7. **Matching Requirements**: Cost-share needed?
8. **Special Provisions**: Set-asides, preferences?

## Simulation Process

### Phase 1: Preparation (30 minutes before class)
- Review role assignment
- Research stakeholder organization
- Develop priorities and negotiating position
- Identify potential allies and opponents

### Phase 2: Initial Coalition Meeting (30 minutes)
- Each stakeholder presents position (3 minutes each)
- Identify common ground
- Surface conflicts
- Form working groups if needed

### Phase 3: Negotiation (45 minutes)
- Breakout negotiations on specific issues
- Trade-offs and compromises
- Coalition facilitator guides process
- Draft agreement language

### Phase 4: Final Coalition Position (20 minutes)
- Present draft coalition position
- Vote on final agreement (consensus preferred)
- Dissenting opinions recorded if needed
- Finalize coalition statement

### Phase 5: Debrief (15 minutes)
- Reflect on process
- Discuss coalition dynamics
- Identify successful strategies
- Analyze breakdowns and resolutions

## Deliverables

**Individual** (before simulation):
- Stakeholder position paper (2 pages)
- Negotiating strategy

**Team** (during simulation):
- Active participation in negotiation
- Coalition building efforts

**Coalition** (end of simulation):
- Final coalition position statement
- Program framework agreed to
- List of participating organizations

**Individual** (after simulation):
- Reflection paper (2-3 pages):
  - What worked in building coalition?
  - What were major challenges?
  - How did you compromise?
  - What would you do differently?
  - Lessons for real-world coalitions?

## Instructor Notes

**Role of Instructor**: Neutral facilitator, keep process moving, enforce time limits

**Success Criteria**: Coalition reaches agreement (even if imperfect)

**If Coalition Fails**: Still valuable learning experience; debrief on why and what could have worked

**Variations**:
- Introduce crisis mid-simulation (opposition coalition, champion withdraws)
- Add "consultant" role to advise coalition
- Require supermajority or unanimous consent

## Learning Objectives

Through this simulation, students will:
- Experience coalition dynamics firsthand
- Practice negotiation and compromise
- Understand stakeholder perspectives
- Navigate conflicting interests
- Develop consensus-building skills
- Appreciate complexity of real coalitions

---

*Real coalitions face these challenges continuously. This simulation builds empathy, skills, and appreciation for coalition work.*
