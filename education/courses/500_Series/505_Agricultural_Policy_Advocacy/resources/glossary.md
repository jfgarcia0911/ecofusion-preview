# Agricultural Policy & Advocacy Glossary

## A

**Administrative Procedure Act (APA)**: Federal law governing how federal agencies develop and issue regulations, requiring notice-and-comment rulemaking.

**Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR)**: Minimum wage that must be paid to H-2A temporary agricultural workers to prevent adverse effect on similarly employed U.S. workers.

**AFRI (Agriculture and Food Research Initiative)**: USDA's flagship competitive grants program for agricultural research, administered by NIFA.

**Amendment**: Change to a bill's text proposed during committee markup or floor consideration.

**AMS (Agricultural Marketing Service)**: USDA agency responsible for marketing programs, including organic certification and agricultural marketing orders.

**APHIS (Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service)**: USDA agency regulating plant and animal health, including biotechnology.

**Appropriations**: Annual funding provided by Congress for discretionary programs and agencies.

**Arbitrary and Capricious**: Legal standard for challenging agency actions; must not be unreasonable, without consideration of relevant factors, or contrary to evidence.

**ARS (Agricultural Research Service)**: USDA's internal research agency conducting fundamental agricultural research.

**Astroturf**: Manufactured appearance of grassroots support, rather than authentic citizen engagement.

**Authorization**: Legislative permission for a program to exist and receive funding, though funding must still be appropriated.

## B

**Benefit-Cost Ratio**: Total benefits divided by total costs in cost-benefit analysis; ratio >1 indicates benefits exceed costs.

**Bioengineered Food**: FDA term for genetically modified organisms (GMOs); mandatory disclosure required since 2022.

**Bridging**: Communication technique of redirecting conversation to key messages.

**Budget Reconciliation**: Special legislative process allowing expedited consideration of budget-related legislation with limited debate.

## C

**CAFO (Concentrated Animal Feeding Operation)**: Large livestock operation subject to Clean Water Act permitting requirements.

**Cap-and-Trade**: Market-based approach to reducing emissions by setting cap and allowing trading of emission allowances.

**Carbon Offset**: Credit for emissions reductions that can be sold to offset another entity's emissions.

**CEA (Controlled Environment Agriculture)**: Agriculture in controlled structures including greenhouses, vertical farms, and indoor operations.

**CFR (Code of Federal Regulations)**: Codification of federal regulations organized by topic; Title 7 is Agriculture.

**Champion**: Legislator who leads advocacy for a bill, serving as primary sponsor or advocate.

**Clean Water Act (CWA)**: Federal law regulating water pollution, including agricultural impacts.

**Cloture**: Senate procedure to end debate, requiring 60 votes.

**Coalition**: Alliance of diverse organizations working toward shared policy objectives.

**Co-Sponsor**: Member of Congress who adds their support to a bill introduced by another member.

**Conference Committee**: Joint House-Senate committee formed to resolve differences between versions of legislation passed by each chamber.

**Conservation Compliance**: Requirement to follow conservation practices to receive farm program benefits.

**Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)**: Systematic comparison of total costs and total benefits of a policy, typically in monetary terms.

**Crop Insurance**: Federally subsidized insurance protecting farmers against yield and revenue losses.

**CRISPR**: Gene editing technology allowing precise modifications to DNA.

## D

**Direct Lobbying**: Communications directly with legislators or staff to influence legislation.

**Discount Rate**: Rate for converting future values to present value in cost-benefit analysis.

**Docket**: Collection of documents related to a specific rulemaking.

## E

**Earned Media**: News coverage obtained through public relations rather than paid advertising.

**Elasticity**: Measure of responsiveness of quantity to price changes; most food has inelastic demand.

**Endangered Species Act (ESA)**: Federal law protecting threatened and endangered species and their habitats.

**EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program)**: USDA conservation cost-share program for working lands.

**Equity**: Fair distribution of costs and benefits across groups or individuals.

**E-Verify**: Electronic employment eligibility verification system.

**Ex Parte Communication**: One-way communication with decision-makers that must be disclosed in rulemaking.

## F

**Farm Bill**: Comprehensive agricultural legislation reauthorized approximately every 5 years, covering commodities, conservation, nutrition, and more.

**Federal Register**: Official daily publication of federal regulations, proposed rules, and notices.

**FIFRA (Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act)**: Federal law governing pesticide registration and use.

**Filibuster**: Extended Senate debate to delay or prevent vote on legislation.

**FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act)**: Federal law on minimum wage, overtime, and child labor (with agricultural exemptions).

**FSIS (Food Safety and Inspection Service)**: USDA agency responsible for meat, poultry, and egg product inspection.

**FSMA (Food Safety Modernization Act)**: 2011 law reforming FDA food safety regulation with focus on prevention.

## G

**Germaneness**: Requirement (in House) that amendments relate to the bill's subject matter.

**GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe)**: FDA classification for food ingredients not requiring pre-market approval.

**Grassroots**: Broad-based citizen advocacy and mobilization.

**Grasstops**: High-level influencer and decision-maker advocacy.

**Greenhouse Gas (GHG)**: Gases that trap heat in atmosphere, including carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide.

## H

**H-2A**: Temporary agricultural worker visa program allowing foreign workers for seasonal agricultural jobs.

**HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points)**: Systematic preventive approach to food safety.

**Horizontal Equity**: Fair treatment of similar situations similarly.

## I

**IMPLAN**: Economic impact modeling software used to estimate direct, indirect, and induced economic impacts.

**Incidence**: Who ultimately bears costs or receives benefits of a policy.

## L

**Land-Grant University**: Institution established under Morrill Acts to teach agriculture and mechanical arts; includes 1862, 1890 (HBCUs), and 1994 (Tribal) institutions.

**LDA (Lobbying Disclosure Act)**: Federal law requiring registration and reporting by lobbyists.

**Lobbying**: Communications with covered officials to influence legislation, regulations, or government actions.

## M

**Mandatory Spending**: Federal spending authorized by law not requiring annual appropriations (e.g., SNAP, crop insurance).

**Markup**: Committee session to amend and vote on legislation.

**MCDA (Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis)**: Systematic comparison of alternatives using multiple weighted criteria.

**MFN (Most Favored Nation)**: Trade principle requiring non-discrimination among trading partners.

**MSPA (Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act)**: Federal law protecting migrant farmworkers.

## N

**NAFTA (North American Free Trade Agreement)**: Former trade agreement between U.S., Canada, and Mexico, replaced by USMCA in 2020.

**Net Present Value (NPV)**: Discounted value of future costs and benefits; NPV >0 indicates benefits exceed costs.

**NIFA (National Institute of Food and Agriculture)**: USDA agency administering competitive research grants and supporting land-grant universities.

**NOP (National Organic Program)**: USDA program establishing organic certification standards and oversight.

**NOSB (National Organic Standards Board)**: Advisory board of 15 members providing recommendations on organic standards.

**NPRM (Notice of Proposed Rulemaking)**: Publication of proposed regulation in Federal Register, opening public comment period.

**NPDES (National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System)**: Clean Water Act permit program for point source discharges.

## O

**OIRA (Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs)**: OMB office reviewing economically significant federal regulations.

**Organic**: Agricultural production system avoiding synthetic inputs, certified under USDA National Organic Program.

**OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)**: Federal agency regulating workplace safety, including agriculture.

**Owned Media**: Communications channels controlled by organization (website, blog, social media accounts).

## P

**PAC (Political Action Committee)**: Organization raising and spending money for political campaigns, subject to contribution limits.

**PCQI (Preventive Controls Qualified Individual)**: Person with training to develop and implement food safety plan under FSMA.

**PDR (Purchase of Development Rights)**: Farmland preservation program where government buys development rights, keeping land in agriculture.

**Policy Entrepreneur**: Individual or organization identifying policy opportunities and advancing solutions through political process.

**Policy Window**: Opportunity for policy change when problem, policy, and political streams align.

**Precedent**: Past decision or action serving as guide for future similar situations.

**Prior Appropriation**: Western water rights system based on "first in time, first in right."

## R

**Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA)**: Economic analysis of regulation's costs and benefits required for significant rules.

**Revolving Door**: Movement of individuals between government and lobbying/industry positions.

**Rider**: Legislative provision attached to unrelated bill, often added to must-pass legislation.

**Right-to-Farm Law**: State law protecting agricultural operations from nuisance lawsuits if following best practices.

**RMA (Risk Management Agency)**: USDA agency administering federal crop insurance program.

**RPS (Renewable Portfolio Standard)**: State requirement for percentage of electricity from renewable sources.

## S

**SBIR/STTR (Small Business Innovation Research/Small Business Technology Transfer)**: Federal programs supporting R&D in small businesses.

**SCRI (Specialty Crop Research Initiative)**: USDA competitive grants program for fruits, vegetables, tree nuts, and nursery crops research.

**SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program)**: Federal food assistance program (formerly food stamps), largest component of Farm Bill.

**SPS (Sanitary and Phytosanitary)**: Trade agreement provisions on food safety, animal health, and plant health standards.

**Stakeholder**: Individual or organization with interest in policy issue.

**Steering Committee**: Governing body of coalition making decisions and providing direction.

## T

**TBT (Technical Barriers to Trade)**: WTO agreement on technical regulations and standards affecting trade.

**Theory of Change**: Logical framework connecting advocacy activities to outcomes and impacts.

**TMDL (Total Maximum Daily Load)**: Maximum amount of pollutant a water body can receive while meeting water quality standards.

**TPA (Trade Promotion Authority)**: Congressional authorization for president to negotiate trade agreements subject to expedited up-or-down vote.

**TRQ (Tariff Rate Quota)**: Two-tier tariff system with lower rate up to quota and higher rate above quota.

## U

**USMCA (United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement)**: Trade agreement replacing NAFTA, implemented 2020.

**USDA (United States Department of Agriculture)**: Federal department overseeing agricultural programs, food safety, rural development, and nutrition assistance.

**USTR (United States Trade Representative)**: Federal agency responsible for developing and coordinating U.S. trade policy.

## V

**Vertical Equity**: Fair treatment based on differences in circumstances or ability to pay.

**Veto**: Presidential rejection of legislation; can be overridden by 2/3 vote of both chambers.

## W

**Whip Count**: Vote counting process to determine support, opposition, and undecided members.

**WOTUS (Waters of the United States)**: Definition of federal Clean Water Act jurisdiction, subject of ongoing regulatory and legal battles.

**WPS (Worker Protection Standard)**: EPA pesticide regulation protecting agricultural workers.

**WTO (World Trade Organization)**: International organization governing trade rules among member nations.
