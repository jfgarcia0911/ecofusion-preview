# Legislative Process Quick Reference Guide

## How a Bill Becomes Law - Federal Process

### Step 1: Introduction
- **House**: H.R. 1, H.R. 2, etc.
- **Senate**: S. 1, S. 2, etc.
- Primary sponsor + co-sponsors
- Referred to committee(s)

### Step 2: Committee Action
- Subcommittee referral (often)
- Hearings (testimony, build record)
- Markup (amend, vote)
- Report to floor (if approved)

### Step 3: Floor Consideration

**House**:
- Rules Committee sets debate terms
- Limited debate time
- Structured amendments
- Majority vote to pass (218 if all present)

**Senate**:
- Unlimited debate (filibuster possible)
- Cloture requires 60 votes
- Amendments freely offered
- Majority vote to pass

### Step 4: Other Chamber
- Same process in other chamber
- May pass different version

### Step 5: Resolve Differences
- Conference committee OR
- Amendments between chambers
- Identical version needed

### Step 6: Presidential Action
- **Sign**: Becomes law
- **Veto**: Returns to Congress (override needs 2/3)
- **No action**: Becomes law after 10 days (if in session)
- **Pocket veto**: Dies if Congress adjourns within 10 days

## Key Congressional Committees

### House Agriculture Committee
- Jurisdiction: USDA programs, farm policy, nutrition, rural development
- 50+ members
- 5 subcommittees

### Senate Agriculture, Nutrition & Forestry Committee
- Similar jurisdiction to House
- ~20 members
- 4 subcommittees

### Appropriations Subcommittees (House & Senate)
- Agriculture, Rural Development, FDA appropriations
- Annual funding levels

## Critical Numbers

| Action | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| Pass bill (House) | Majority of members voting (~218) |
| Pass bill (Senate) | Majority of members voting (51) |
| Cloture (end debate) | 60 senators |
| Override veto | 2/3 of both chambers |
| Conference report | Cannot be amended |

## Legislative Timeline

### Congressional Calendar
- **Two-year cycle**: Congress (e.g., 118th Congress 2023-2024)
- **First session**: Odd year, more ambitious legislation
- **Second session**: Even year, election year, shorter
- **Bills die**: End of Congress unless enacted

### Annual Cycle
- January: Session begins
- Spring: Committee hearings and markups
- Summer: Floor action, appropriations
- Fall: Final push, continuing resolutions
- December: Lame duck (post-election)

---

## Farm Bill Cycle

**Typical Process** (~5 years):
- Year 1-2: Hearings, stakeholder input
- Year 2-3: Committee development, markup
- Year 3: Floor passage, conference
- Year 4-5: Implementation, extension if needed

**2018 Farm Bill**: Expires 2023, reauthorization process begins 2022-2023

## Lobbying Strategy Quick Tips

### Before Introduction
- Develop legislative proposal
- Identify potential sponsors
- Build coalition support
- Prepare materials

### Committee Stage
- Testify at hearings
- Meet with committee members and staff
- Provide technical assistance
- Work on amendments

### Floor Stage
- Whip count (vote counting)
- Amendment strategy
- Member education
- Grassroots mobilization

### Conference
- Lobby conferees
- Provide side-by-side comparisons
- Generate stakeholder pressure

### Implementation
- Agency engagement on regulations
- Appropriations follow-up
- Oversight

## Effective Meeting Checklist

**Before Meeting**:
- [ ] Research legislator (district, priorities, record)
- [ ] Prepare talking points (3-5 key messages)
- [ ] Bring materials (1-pager, business card)
- [ ] Confirm appointment
- [ ] Identify your ask (specific request)

**During Meeting**:
- [ ] Thank for time
- [ ] Introduce yourself and connection to district
- [ ] Make your case (brief, compelling)
- [ ] Listen and respond
- [ ] Make specific ask
- [ ] Offer to be resource

**After Meeting**:
- [ ] Send thank you
- [ ] Provide any requested information
- [ ] Follow up on commitments
- [ ] Document meeting and outcome

## Common Legislative Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Amendment** | Change to bill text |
| **Cloture** | Vote to end debate (Senate, 60 votes) |
| **Conference** | Committee to resolve House-Senate differences |
| **Co-sponsor** | Member adding support to bill |
| **Filibuster** | Extended debate to delay/prevent vote |
| **Germaneness** | Amendment must relate to bill |
| **Markup** | Committee session to amend and vote |
| **Rider** | Unrelated provision attached to bill |
| **TPA** | Trade Promotion Authority (fast track) |
| **Whip** | Party leader counting votes |
