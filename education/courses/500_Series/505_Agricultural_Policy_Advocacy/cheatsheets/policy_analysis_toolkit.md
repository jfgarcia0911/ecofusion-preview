# Policy Analysis Toolkit - Quick Reference

## Cost-Benefit Analysis Formula

**Net Present Value (NPV)**:
```
NPV = Σ [(Benefits_t - Costs_t) / (1 + r)^t]
```
- t = year
- r = discount rate (typically 3% and 7%)
- If NPV > 0, policy economically justified

**Benefit-Cost Ratio**:
```
B/C Ratio = Total Benefits / Total Costs
```
- If B/C > 1, benefits exceed costs

## The Five E's of Policy Evaluation

1. **Effectiveness**: Does it achieve objectives?
2. **Efficiency**: Best use of resources?
3. **Equity**: Fair distribution of costs/benefits?
4. **Environmental Sustainability**: Protects natural resources?
5. **Political Feasibility**: Can it pass and be implemented?

## Eight-Step Policy Analysis Process

1. Define the problem
2. Establish evaluation criteria
3. Identify alternative policies
4. Evaluate alternatives
5. Compare alternatives
6. Make recommendations
7. Consider implementation
8. Monitor and evaluate

## Elasticity Quick Guide

**Price Elasticity of Demand** = % Change in Quantity / % Change in Price

- **Elastic** (>1): Quantity very responsive to price
- **Inelastic** (<1): Quantity not very responsive (most food)
- **Unit Elastic** (=1): Proportional response

**Agricultural products typically inelastic**: People need to eat regardless of price.

## Political Feasibility Checklist

- [ ] Legislative champion identified
- [ ] Broad stakeholder support
- [ ] Public opinion favorable or neutral
- [ ] Administrative capacity to implement
- [ ] Budget neutral or funded
- [ ] Timing aligned with political calendar
- [ ] Coalition assembled
- [ ] Opposition manageable

## Equity Analysis Questions

**Horizontal Equity**: Are similar situations treated similarly?

**Vertical Equity**: Are different situations treated appropriately differently?

**Distribution**:
- Who pays costs?
- Who receives benefits?
- Are vulnerable populations protected?
- Geographic equity?

---

## Common Analytical Pitfalls

- Counting same benefit multiple times
- Ignoring distributional impacts
- Using wrong discount rate
- Comparing apples and oranges
- Ignoring unintended consequences
- Selection bias in evidence
- Overstating certainty
- Failure to consider alternatives
