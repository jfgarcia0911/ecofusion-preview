# Lobbying Compliance Checklist

## Federal Registration Requirements

**Do you need to register?**
- [ ] Organization spends $12,500+ per quarter on lobbying, OR
- [ ] Individual spends 20%+ of time lobbying for single client

**If YES, you must register within 45 days**

---

## Registration Process (LD-1)

- [ ] Complete LD-1 form online
- [ ] Identify all covered officials contacted
- [ ] List lobbyists engaged in activities
- [ ] Specify general lobbying issues
- [ ] Submit electronically
- [ ] Update within 30 days of changes

---

## Quarterly Reporting (LD-2)

**Due**: 20 days after quarter ends (Q1: Apr 20, Q2: Jul 20, Q3: Oct 20, Q4: Jan 20)

- [ ] Calculate total lobbying expenses (rounded to $10,000)
- [ ] List specific issues lobbied
- [ ] Identify Houses of Congress and agencies contacted
- [ ] List lobbyists who worked on each issue
- [ ] Report contributions (semi-annual)
- [ ] Submit electronically by deadline

---

## Gift Rules

**Federal Officials - Generally NO GIFTS**

**Limited Exceptions**:
- [ ] Widely attended events (250+ attendees, official invited)
- [ ] Campaign contributions (within legal limits)
- [ ] Food/beverage under $50 for meetings (check current rules)

**Document all**:
- [ ] Date and amount
- [ ] Recipient
- [ ] Purpose
- [ ] Legal basis for exception

---

## State Lobbying Compliance

**Varies by state - Check each state where you lobby**

- [ ] Determine if registration required (thresholds vary)
- [ ] Register within state deadline
- [ ] File periodic reports (monthly, quarterly, or annual)
- [ ] Understand state gift rules (some zero tolerance)
- [ ] Track campaign contributions (enhanced disclosure)
- [ ] Comply with cooling-off periods

---

## 501(c)(3) Nonprofit Lobbying Limits

**Substantial Part Test** (default):
- Lobbying cannot be "substantial part" of activities
- No specific limit, facts and circumstances
- Risk: Loss of tax-exempt status

**501(h) Election** (optional safe harbor):
- Specific dollar limits based on organization budget
- 20% of first $500K, declining for larger budgets
- Distinguish direct vs. grassroots lobbying
- Exceed limit = excise tax
- File Form 5768 to elect

**Calculate lobbying annually and track throughout year**

---

## 501(c)(6) Trade Association Compliance

**Unlimited lobbying allowed (if related to mission)**

**Member disclosure required**:
- [ ] Estimate % of dues used for lobbying
- [ ] Notify members (dues non-deductible to that extent)
- [ ] Calculate using reasonable allocation method

---

## Time Tracking

**Track time spent on**:
- [ ] Direct lobbying (communications with officials)
- [ ] Supporting activities (research, preparation)
- [ ] Non-lobbying activities

**Methods**:
- Contemporaneous time logs
- Reasonable estimates if contemporaneous not feasible
- Consistent methodology

---

## Expense Tracking

**Lobbying expenses include**:
- [ ] Lobbyist compensation (salary, contract fees)
- [ ] Travel for lobbying purposes
- [ ] Meals with officials (if exception applies)
- [ ] Event costs (if lobbying focus)
- [ ] Allocated overhead

**Non-lobbying expenses** (do NOT count):
- Research and analysis for general public
- Responding to requests for information
- Self-defense lobbying (some cases)
- Non-lobbying portions of travel/events

---

## PAC Compliance (if applicable)

**Separate Segregated Fund**:
- [ ] Separate from corporate/union treasury
- [ ] Voluntary contributions only
- [ ] Restricted class (employees, members, shareholders)
- [ ] Contribution limits ($5,000 per candidate per election)
- [ ] File FEC reports (monthly or quarterly)
- [ ] Maintain detailed records

---

## Ethics Best Practices

- [ ] Never lie or mislead policymakers
- [ ] Disclose who you represent
- [ ] Manage conflicts of interest
- [ ] Respect confidential information
- [ ] Comply with all laws and regulations
- [ ] Document lobbying activities
- [ ] Train staff on compliance
- [ ] Conduct periodic audits
- [ ] Seek legal advice when uncertain

---

## Red Flags - Seek Legal Counsel

- Uncertain if activity counts as lobbying
- Approaching spending thresholds
- Complex gift or contribution situations
- State with unfamiliar laws
- Potential conflicts of interest
- Former government employee situations
- Foreign entity involvement (FARA)

---

## Annual Compliance Review

**At year-end**:
- [ ] Review all registrations current
- [ ] All reports filed timely and accurately
- [ ] Lobbying calculations correct
- [ ] Gift and contribution records complete
- [ ] Staff trained on policies
- [ ] Policies updated for law changes
- [ ] Identify improvements for next year
