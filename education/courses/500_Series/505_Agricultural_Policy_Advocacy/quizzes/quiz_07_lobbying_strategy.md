## Questions

### Question 1
What is the federal threshold for registering as a lobbyist under the Lobbying Disclosure Act (LDA)?

A) $5,000 per quarter in lobbying expenses
B) $12,500 per quarter in lobbying expenses
C) $25,000 per quarter in lobbying expenses
D) $50,000 per quarter in lobbying expenses

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Under the LDA, organizations must register if they spend more than $12,500 per quarter on lobbying activities or if an individual lobbyist spends 20% or more of their time on lobbying for a client.

---

### Question 2
Who is considered a "covered official" under federal lobbying laws?

A) Only members of Congress
B) Only executive branch officials
C) Members of Congress, their staff, and senior executive branch officials
D) State legislators only

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Covered officials include members of Congress, congressional staff, senior executive branch officials, and certain other government employees who may be targets of lobbying activities.

---

### Question 3
How often must registered lobbyists file LD-2 disclosure reports?

A) Monthly
B) Bi-monthly
C) Quarterly
D) Annually

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Registered lobbyists must file LD-2 quarterly disclosure reports detailing their lobbying activities, clients, issues, and expenditures.

---

### Question 4
What did the Honest Leadership and Open Government Act (HLOGA) of 2007 add to lobbying regulations?

A) Campaign contribution limits
B) Gift restrictions and longer cooling-off periods for former members
C) Lower registration thresholds
D) State lobbying requirements

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** HLOGA strengthened lobbying rules by adding stricter gift restrictions, extending cooling-off periods (revolving door restrictions) for former members and staff, and increasing disclosure requirements.

---

### Question 5
What restrictions apply to 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations regarding lobbying?

A) Unlimited lobbying is permitted
B) Limited lobbying permitted; must not be "substantial part" of activities
C) No lobbying permitted under any circumstances
D) Only grassroots lobbying permitted

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** 501(c)(3) organizations can engage in limited lobbying activity as long as it's not a "substantial part" of their activities. They can elect the 501(h) expenditure test for more specific limits.

---

### Question 6
What type of organization is a 501(c)(6)?

A) Religious organization
B) Private foundation
C) Trade association or business league
D) Political action committee

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** 501(c)(6) organizations are trade associations, chambers of commerce, or business leagues that can engage in unlimited lobbying as long as it furthers their exempt purpose.

---

### Question 7
What is the "revolving door" restriction for former U.S. Senators?

A) 1-year ban on lobbying former colleagues
B) 2-year ban on lobbying former colleagues
C) 5-year ban on lobbying former colleagues
D) Lifetime ban on lobbying

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Former U.S. Senators face a 2-year cooling-off period before they can lobby their former colleagues. Former House members face a 1-year restriction.

---

### Question 8
What is the maximum contribution a PAC can make to a candidate per election?

A) $5,000 per candidate per election
B) $10,000 per candidate per election
C) $25,000 per candidate per election
D) Unlimited contributions

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Under federal law, PACs can contribute up to $5,000 per candidate per election (primary and general elections count separately).

---

### Question 9
In what year was the Lobbying Disclosure Act (LDA) enacted?

A) 1985
B) 1990
C) 1995
D) 2000

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Lobbying Disclosure Act was enacted in 1995 to provide transparency in federal lobbying activities and require registration and reporting.

---

### Question 10
What is "grassroots lobbying"?

A) Lobbying agricultural issues only
B) Direct contact with legislators
C) Lobbying at the state level
D) Encouraging the general public to contact government officials about legislation

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Grassroots lobbying involves mobilizing the public to contact their elected officials about specific legislation, as opposed to direct lobbying where lobbyists directly communicate with officials.
