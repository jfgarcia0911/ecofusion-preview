# Master Grower Terminology Glossary

## Course 510: Master Grower Certification

---

## A

**Abiotic Stress**: Plant stress caused by non-living environmental factors such as temperature extremes, drought, salinity, or nutrient deficiency. Contrast with biotic stress.

**Acclimation**: The physiological adjustment of a plant to gradual environmental changes, allowing it to maintain performance under new conditions.

**Aeroponic System**: A growing system where plant roots are suspended in air and periodically misted with nutrient solution, providing maximum oxygen availability.

**Andragogy**: The method and practice of teaching adult learners, emphasizing self-direction, experience, and practical application.

**Anthocyanin**: Red, purple, or blue pigments in plants responsible for coloration; often increased by environmental stress (UV light, cool temperatures, nutrient stress).

**Aphid**: Small, soft-bodied sap-sucking insects that reproduce rapidly and can transmit plant viruses; common CEA pest managed through biological control.

**Aquaponics**: An integrated system combining aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics, where fish waste provides nutrients for plants and plants filter water for fish.

---

## B

**Beneficial Insects**: Organisms used in biological pest control, including predators (ladybugs, lacewings), parasitoids (parasitic wasps), and pollinators.

**Biocontrol**: The use of living organisms (beneficial insects, microbes) to control pests and diseases; cornerstone of sustainable IPM programs.

**Biological Control**: See Biocontrol.

**Biotic Stress**: Plant stress caused by living organisms including pests, diseases, and competing plants. Contrast with abiotic stress.

**Blossom End Rot (BER)**: Physiological disorder in fruiting vegetables (tomatoes, peppers) caused by calcium deficiency at the fruit blossom end, often due to inconsistent watering or high humidity reducing transpiration.

**Bolting**: Premature flowering and seed production in crops grown for leaves or roots (lettuce, spinach), often triggered by heat stress, long photoperiods, or plant maturity.

**Botrytis**: Gray mold fungal disease favored by cool, humid conditions with poor air circulation; managed through environmental control and sanitation.

---

## C

**Canopy**: The upper layer of foliage in a crop; managing canopy density, uniformity, and light interception is critical for yield optimization.

**Carbon Partitioning**: The distribution of photosynthetically fixed carbon between different plant organs (roots, shoots, fruits); influenced by environmental conditions and plant growth regulators.

**Certified Crop Advisor (CCA)**: Professional certification for agricultural advisors specializing in nutrient management and agronomy.

**Chelated Micronutrients**: Nutrients (especially iron) bound to organic molecules (EDTA, DTPA, EDDHA) to improve stability and plant availability in nutrient solutions.

**Chlorosis**: Yellowing of plant tissue due to chlorophyll loss or reduced synthesis; can indicate nutrient deficiency, disease, or environmental stress.

**Circadian Rhythm**: The internal biological clock in plants (and animals) that regulates daily cycles; most plants benefit from dark periods rather than continuous light.

**Circular Economy**: Economic system aimed at eliminating waste through resource reuse, recycling, and regeneration; applicable to CEA through waste valorization and resource recovery.

**CO₂ Enrichment**: Increasing carbon dioxide concentration above ambient levels (typically to 800-1200 ppm) to enhance photosynthesis and growth in controlled environments.

**Coco Coir**: Substrate made from coconut husk fibers; renewable, with good water retention and aeration properties.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)**: Direct costs to produce crops including seeds, nutrients, substrate, energy, and direct labor; excluding overhead and operating expenses.

**Continuous Improvement**: Ongoing effort to improve products, services, or processes through incremental gains (Kaizen) or breakthrough innovations.

**Control Chart**: Statistical process control tool plotting data over time with upper and lower control limits to distinguish normal variation from special causes requiring intervention.

**Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA)**: Food production in structures with varying degrees of environmental control (greenhouses, indoor vertical farms, high tunnels).

**Crop Cycle Time**: Duration from seeding/transplanting to harvest; influenced by variety, environmental conditions, and production goals.

**Cryptochromes**: Blue light photoreceptors in plants that regulate photomorphogenesis, circadian rhythm, and flowering.

**Cultivar**: A plant variety that has been produced through selective breeding or genetic modification; short for "cultivated variety."

---

## D

**Daily Light Integral (DLI)**: The total amount of photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) received per day, measured in moles of photons per square meter per day (mol/m²/day); primary determinant of photosynthesis and yield.

**Deep Water Culture (DWC)**: Hydroponic system where plant roots are suspended in aerated nutrient solution; also called raft system in commercial applications.

**Deficiency**: Insufficient nutrient availability causing symptoms such as chlorosis, necrosis, or stunted growth; diagnosed through visual symptoms, tissue analysis, and solution testing.

**Dehumidification**: Removal of water vapor from air; critical in CEA to control humidity, manage VPD, and prevent disease.

**DIF (Day-Night Temperature Difference)**: The difference between daytime and nighttime temperatures; positive DIF promotes stem elongation, negative DIF produces compact growth.

**Drain-to-Waste**: Irrigation method where runoff solution is discarded rather than recirculated; simpler management but lower resource efficiency than recirculating systems.

**DROP Technique**: Brief temperature reduction (5-10°C for 30-60 minutes) at sunrise to promote compact growth without maintaining full negative DIF all night.

---

## E

**EC (Electrical Conductivity)**: Measure of dissolved salts in solution (mS/cm or dS/m), indicating total fertilizer strength; managed based on crop type and growth stage.

**Entomopathogenic**: Organisms (fungi, bacteria, nematodes) that infect and kill insects; used in biological pest control (e.g., *Beauveria bassiana*).

**Environmental Control System**: Integrated systems managing temperature, humidity, light, CO₂, and air circulation in CEA facilities; typically SCADA or PLC-based.

**Etiolation**: Abnormal plant growth characterized by elongated, weak stems and pale leaves due to insufficient light.

**Evapotranspiration (ET)**: Combined water loss from plant transpiration and evaporation from surfaces; basis for irrigation scheduling in advanced systems.

---

## F

**Far-Red Light (FR)**: Light in 700-800nm range; high FR:R ratios trigger shade avoidance responses (stem elongation), low ratios promote compactness.

**Fertigation**: Application of fertilizers through irrigation system; allows precise nutrient delivery and adjustment based on crop needs.

**Fishbone Diagram**: Problem-solving tool organizing potential causes into categories (Ishikawa diagram); used in root cause analysis.

**FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis)**: Systematic method for proactively identifying potential failures, their causes, and risk mitigation strategies.

**FSMA (Food Safety Modernization Act)**: U.S. law establishing produce safety standards including water quality, sanitation, and traceability requirements.

**Fusarium**: Fungal pathogen causing wilt, root rot, and damping-off; managed through resistant varieties, substrate disposal, and biocontrol.

---

## G

**GAP (Good Agricultural Practices)**: USDA voluntary audit program certifying food safety practices; often required by retailers and food service buyers.

**Generative Growth**: Plant development focused on flowering and fruiting rather than vegetative growth; promoted by specific environmental and nutritional strategies.

**Germination Rate**: Percentage of seeds that successfully germinate under defined conditions; critical for production planning and seed quality assessment.

**GLOBALG.A.P.**: International farm assurance certification covering food safety, sustainability, and worker welfare; required by many international buyers.

**Greenhouse**: Structure with transparent or translucent walls and roof providing controlled environment using natural and supplemental light.

**Growing Degree Days (GDD)**: Accumulated heat units above a base temperature used to predict crop development and harvest timing; more accurate than calendar days.

**GROW Model**: Coaching framework: Goal, Reality, Options, Will (or Way Forward); used in performance coaching conversations.

---

## H

**HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points)**: Systematic approach to food safety identifying, evaluating, and controlling hazards; required for many processing operations.

**Head Space**: Distance between top of plant canopy and light fixtures; affects light intensity, heat accumulation, and working accessibility.

**Heat Shock Proteins**: Proteins produced by plants in response to heat stress; protect cellular structures but divert energy from growth.

**HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning)**: Integrated system controlling temperature and air movement in enclosed facilities.

**Hydroponics**: Soilless growing method where plant roots receive nutrients from water-based solution; includes NFT, DWC, drip systems, and others.

**Hypocotyl**: Stem segment below cotyledons (seed leaves) in seedlings; elongated hypocotyls indicate insufficient light during germination.

---

## I

**Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**: Holistic approach to pest control emphasizing prevention, monitoring, and intervention using biological, cultural, and chemical methods in that priority order.

**Internode**: Stem segment between two nodes; internode length affected by DIF, light quality (R:FR ratio), and growth regulators.

**Iron Chlorosis**: Yellowing of young leaves due to iron deficiency, typically caused by high pH (>6.5) reducing iron availability rather than true deficiency.

**Irrigation**: Application of water to crops; methods include drip, NFT, ebb-and-flow, and overhead; precision irrigation based on plant demand improves efficiency.

---

## K

**Kaizen**: Japanese philosophy of continuous improvement through small, incremental changes; emphasizes employee involvement and data-driven decisions.

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**: Measurable value demonstrating effectiveness in achieving objectives; examples include yield per square meter, defect rate, and labor productivity.

---

## L

**Leaf Area Index (LAI)**: Ratio of total leaf area to ground area; higher LAI intercepts more light but increases disease pressure and requires more air circulation.

**LED (Light Emitting Diode)**: Energy-efficient lighting technology allowing precise spectrum control; typical efficacy 2.5-3.2 μmol/J in modern horticultural fixtures.

**LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)**: Green building certification program assessing sustainability in design, construction, and operation.

**Liebig's Law of the Minimum**: Principle that growth is limited by the scarcest resource (limiting factor), not total resources; guides prioritization of improvements.

---

## M

**Macronutrients**: Nutrients required in large quantities: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), and sulfur (S).

**Master Grower**: Senior technical authority responsible for all aspects of crop production in CEA facility; integrates technical expertise, operational management, and team leadership.

**Micronutrients**: Nutrients required in small quantities: iron (Fe), manganese (Mn), zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), boron (B), molybdenum (Mo), chlorine (Cl).

**Mode of Action (MOA)**: Mechanism by which a pesticide affects target pests; rotating MOA prevents resistance development.

**Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP)**: Packaging with altered gas composition (reduced O₂, increased CO₂) to extend shelf life.

---

## N

**Necrosis**: Death of plant tissue; appears as brown or black spots or margins; can indicate nutrient deficiency, disease, or toxic stress.

**NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)**: Hydroponic system where a thin film of nutrient solution continuously flows through channels containing plant roots.

**Node**: Point on stem where leaves, branches, or flowers attach; node count affects plant architecture and production strategies.

**Nutrient Use Efficiency (NUE)**: Ratio of nutrients in harvested crops to total nutrients applied; recirculating systems achieve 70-90% vs. 30-60% for drain-to-waste.

---

## O

**Organic Certification**: USDA NOP (National Organic Program) certification requiring approved inputs, specific practices, and prohibition of synthetic pesticides/GMOs; complex for hydroponic systems.

**Orius insidiosus**: Minute pirate bug; effective generalist predator for thrips, aphids, and spider mites in biological control programs.

---

## P

**PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)**: Light in 400-700nm range used by plants for photosynthesis; measured as PPFD (photosynthetic photon flux density).

**PDCA Cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act)**: Iterative problem-solving and continuous improvement framework; also known as Deming Cycle.

**Pest Threshold**: Population level at which pest damage justifies intervention cost; basis for threshold-based IPM rather than zero-tolerance.

**pH**: Measure of acidity/alkalinity on 0-14 scale; affects nutrient availability, with hydroponics optimal at 5.5-6.5 for most crops.

**Photoperiod**: Duration of light period in 24-hour cycle; affects flowering in photoperiod-sensitive crops and may influence quality in day-neutral crops.

**Photomorphogenesis**: Light-regulated plant development including stem elongation, leaf expansion, and flowering; controlled through spectrum, intensity, and photoperiod.

**Photorespiration**: Wasteful process where Rubisco fixes oxygen instead of CO₂, occurring at high temperatures; C3 crops benefit from CO₂ enrichment and temperature management.

**Photosynthesis**: Process converting light energy, CO₂, and water into sugars and oxygen; foundation of all crop production.

**Phytochrome**: Red/far-red light photoreceptor regulating shade avoidance, flowering, and seed germination; critical for morphology control.

**Phytoseiulus persimilis**: Specialist predatory mite for two-spotted spider mite; highly effective in biological control but cannot survive without prey.

**PLC (Programmable Logic Controller)**: Industrial computer controlling environmental systems in CEA facilities; often integrated with SCADA for facility-wide management.

**Poka-Yoke**: Mistake-proofing technique designing systems to prevent errors; applies to agriculture through automation, sensors, and process controls.

**Post-Harvest**: Period and practices after harvest including cooling, washing, packaging, and storage; critical for quality maintenance and shelf life.

**PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)**: Instantaneous measurement of PAR (μmol/m²/s); integrate over photoperiod to calculate DLI.

**Precision Agriculture**: Technology-enabled approach using data, sensors, and automation to optimize inputs and outputs.

**Preventive Maintenance**: Scheduled maintenance to prevent equipment failure; more cost-effective than reactive repairs and reduces downtime.

**Pythium**: Oomycete pathogen ("water mold") causing root rot and damping-off; favored by overwatering, poor aeration, and warm temperatures.

---

## Q

**Quality Assurance (QA)**: Systematic processes ensuring products meet defined quality standards; includes monitoring, measurement, and corrective actions.

---

## R

**Recirculating System**: Hydroponic system capturing and reusing runoff solution; achieves 70-90% water and nutrient efficiency vs. 30-60% drain-to-waste.

**Resilience**: Ability of system or organism to withstand and recover from stress or disturbances; goal in both plant production and business operations.

**Rockwool**: Substrate made from melted rock spun into fibers; sterile, good water retention and aeration, but not easily biodegradable.

**ROI (Return on Investment)**: Financial metric comparing gain from investment to its cost; critical for evaluating technology adoption and improvement projects.

**Root Cause Analysis**: Problem-solving methodology identifying underlying causes rather than treating symptoms; techniques include 5 Whys and Fishbone diagrams.

**Root Zone**: Area containing plant roots; conditions include temperature, oxygen, moisture, pH, and EC directly affect plant health and productivity.

---

## S

**Salinity**: Concentration of dissolved salts; high salinity (high EC) causes osmotic stress reducing water uptake and potentially ion toxicity.

**Sanitation**: Practices maintaining cleanliness and reducing pathogen loads; includes cleaning, disinfection, and biosecurity protocols.

**SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)**: Computer system monitoring and controlling industrial processes; used in CEA for environmental management and data logging.

**Shelf Life**: Duration product maintains acceptable quality in storage; extended through breeding, pre-harvest management, and post-harvest handling.

**SMART Goals**: Framework for effective goal-setting: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

**SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)**: Documented step-by-step instructions for routine operations; ensures consistency, facilitates training, and maintains quality.

**Source-Sink Relationship**: Balance between photosynthetic capacity (source: mature leaves) and demand for photosynthate (sink: growing tissues, fruits); optimized through pruning, environmental control, and nutrition.

**Spider Mites**: Tiny arachnid pests causing stippling damage; reproduce rapidly in hot, dry conditions; managed through humidity control and predatory mites.

**Statistical Process Control (SPC)**: Use of statistical methods (control charts) to monitor and control processes; distinguishes normal variation from special causes requiring intervention.

**Substrate**: Growing medium supporting plant roots; includes rockwool, coco coir, peat, perlite, or combinations; affects water/air balance and buffering.

**Succession Planting**: Staggered planting schedule enabling continuous harvest; requires planning based on crop cycle time and harvest frequency goals.

**Sustainability**: Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet their needs; includes environmental, economic, and social dimensions.

---

## T

**Theory of Constraints**: Management philosophy that system performance is limited by single bottleneck; focus efforts on identifying and managing constraint.

**Thrips**: Tiny slender insects causing silvering/scarring damage and virus transmission; managed through biological control (predatory mites, minute pirate bugs) and monitoring.

**Tip Burn**: Physiological disorder causing necrosis of leaf margins, especially lettuce; caused by calcium deficiency at growing tips due to insufficient transpiration (high humidity).

**Tissue Analysis**: Laboratory analysis of plant tissue nutrient content; diagnostic tool for confirming deficiencies or excesses and refining fertigation programs.

**Toxicity**: Excessive nutrient levels causing symptoms such as necrosis, chlorosis, or stunted growth; managed by reducing EC, flushing substrate, or adjusting formulation.

**Traceability**: Ability to track products through production chain from seed to sale; required for food safety, quality control, and rapid recall if needed.

**Transpiration**: Water loss from plants through stomata; driven by VPD, necessary for cooling and nutrient transport, but excessive transpiration causes water stress.

**Trichome**: Hair-like structures on plant surfaces; can deter pests, reduce water loss, or produce essential oils; density affects crop characteristics.

---

## U

**UV Light**: Ultraviolet light (<400nm); UV-A (315-400nm) increases secondary metabolites and anthocyanins but can reduce yield if excessive.

---

## V

**Value Stream Mapping**: Lean manufacturing technique visualizing all steps in process to identify waste and improvement opportunities.

**Variety Trial**: Systematic evaluation of multiple cultivars under controlled conditions to determine best performers for specific market and environmental conditions.

**Vegetative Growth**: Plant development focused on roots, stems, and leaves rather than flowering and fruiting; promoted by specific environmental and nutritional strategies.

**Vermiculture**: Cultivation of earthworms for composting organic waste; produces high-value vermicompost (worm castings) from plant waste.

**Vertical Farm**: Indoor facility using vertical growing systems (stacked tiers) and artificial lighting to maximize production per square foot of building footprint.

**VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)**: Device controlling motor speed in pumps and fans; improves energy efficiency by matching output to demand.

**VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)**: Difference between saturated and actual vapor pressure at given temperature; drives transpiration and is more meaningful than relative humidity alone for crop management.

---

## W

**Water Use Efficiency**: Crop production per unit water consumed (kg produce per liter water); excellent CEA systems achieve 10-20 L/kg for leafy greens vs. 100-250+ L/kg conventional.

**Whitefly**: Small white flying insects feeding on phloem and excreting honeydew; transmit viruses; managed through biological control (parasitic wasps) and yellow sticky traps.

---

## Y

**Yield**: Quantity of marketable crop produced per unit area or time; affected by genetics, environment, nutrition, management, and pest/disease pressure.

---

## Z

**Zero Defects**: Quality philosophy aiming for perfect output; in practice, CEA targets <2-5% defect rates through prevention and consistent processes.
