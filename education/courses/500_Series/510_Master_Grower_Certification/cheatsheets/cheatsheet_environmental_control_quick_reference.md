# Environmental Control Quick Reference

## Master Grower Cheatsheet #1

---

## Temperature Management

### Optimal Ranges by Crop Type
```
Cool-Season Crops (Lettuce, Spinach, Greens):
Day: 18-22°C (64-72°F)
Night: 16-18°C (61-64°F)

Moderate Crops (Basil, Herbs):
Day: 22-25°C (72-77°F)
Night: 18-22°C (64-72°F)

Warm-Season Crops (Tomato, Pepper, Cucumber):
Day: 24-28°C (75-82°F)
Night: 18-22°C (64-72°F)
```

### DIF (Day-Night Temperature Difference)
- **Positive DIF** (+4 to +6°C): Standard growth, moderate elongation
- **Zero DIF** (0°C): Compact growth
- **Negative DIF** (-2 to -4°C): Very compact, quality enhancement
- **DROP Technique**: 5-10°C drop for 30-60 min at sunrise = compactness

## Humidity & VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)

### VPD Calculation (Simplified)
```
VPD (kPa) = (1 - RH/100) × SVP
Where SVP = Saturated Vapor Pressure at temperature
```

### Target VPD by Growth Stage
```
Propagation/Seedlings:    0.4-0.8 kPa  (high humidity, gentle)
Vegetative Growth:        0.8-1.2 kPa  (active transpiration)
Flowering/Fruiting:       1.0-1.4 kPa  (nutrient delivery)
Pre-Harvest:              0.8-1.0 kPa  (controlled, quality)
```

### VPD Effects
- **Low VPD (<0.8)**: Reduced transpiration, tip burn risk, disease pressure
- **Optimal VPD (0.8-1.4)**: Balanced growth, good nutrient uptake
- **High VPD (>1.5)**: Water stress, stomatal closure, wilting

## Lighting

### Daily Light Integral (DLI) Targets
```
Microgreens:          6-12 mol/m²/day
Leafy Greens:        12-17 mol/m²/day
Herbs:               15-20 mol/m²/day
Fruiting Vegetables: 20-30+ mol/m²/day
```

### DLI Calculation
```
DLI = PPFD (μmol/m²/s) × Photoperiod (hours) × 3.6 / 1000

Example: 300 μmol/m²/s × 16 hrs × 3.6 / 1000 = 17.3 mol/m²/day
```

### Spectrum Effects
- **Blue (20-30%)**: Compactness, chlorophyll, stomatal opening
- **Red (60-75%)**: Photosynthesis, biomass, efficiency
- **Green (5-15%)**: Canopy penetration, understory
- **Far-Red (0-10%)**: Elongation, flowering (use selectively)
- **UV-A (0-5%)**: Secondary metabolites, color, compactness

## CO₂ Enrichment

### Optimal Concentrations
```
Ambient:            400-420 ppm (baseline, no enrichment)
Optimal CEA:        800-1200 ppm (30-50% photosynthesis increase)
Diminishing Returns: >1200 ppm (minimal additional benefit)
Negative Effects:    >2000 ppm (avoid)
```

### CO₂ Schedule
```
Night (Lights OFF):  400 ppm (no enrichment, respiration only)
Lights ON:          Ramp to 1000 ppm (over 30 min)
Peak Light:         1000-1200 ppm (maintain)
Pre-Sunset:         Reduce to 800 ppm (1 hr before lights off)
Ventilation Events: Stop injection (prevent waste)
```

## Irrigation & Fertigation

### EC (Electrical Conductivity) Targets
```
Lettuce/Leafy Greens:    1.2-1.8 mS/cm
Herbs (Basil, Cilantro): 1.5-2.2 mS/cm
Tomato (Vegetative):     2.0-2.5 mS/cm
Tomato (Generative):     2.5-3.5 mS/cm
Cucumber:                2.0-2.8 mS/cm
Pepper:                  2.2-3.0 mS/cm
Strawberry:              1.0-1.5 mS/cm
```

### pH Targets
```
Hydroponic (Rockwool, NFT, DWC): 5.5-6.5 (optimal 5.8-6.2)
Coco Coir:                       5.8-6.2
Peat-based:                      5.5-6.0
Aquaponic:                       6.5-7.0 (compromise)
```

### Substrate Moisture
```
Rockwool:       70-80% moisture content
Coco Coir:      60-70% moisture content
Peat Mix:       50-60% moisture content
Tension:        5-10 kPa (slight tension, readily available)
```

## Nutrient Deficiency Quick ID

### Mobile Nutrients (Older Leaves First)
- **N (Nitrogen)**: Uniform yellowing, stunted growth
- **P (Phosphorus)**: Purple/dark coloration, dark green leaves
- **K (Potassium)**: Marginal necrosis (leaf edges burn)
- **Mg (Magnesium)**: Interveinal chlorosis (veins stay green)

### Immobile Nutrients (Younger Leaves First)
- **Ca (Calcium)**: Tip burn, blossom end rot, growing point death
- **Fe (Iron)**: Interveinal chlorosis on young leaves (check pH!)
- **S (Sulfur)**: Uniform yellowing of young leaves
- **B (Boron)**: Growing point death, poor fruit set

### Quick Fix
1. Check pH first (most deficiencies are pH-related availability issues)
2. Review EC (too high or too low?)
3. Adjust nutrient formulation if confirmed deficiency
4. Monitor transpiration (Ca/B require water movement)

## Common Environmental Problems

### Problem: Tip Burn in Lettuce
- **Cause**: Calcium deficiency (usually high humidity reducing transpiration)
- **Fix**: Reduce humidity to 60-70% RH (VPD 0.8-1.2 kPa), increase air circulation
- **Prevention**: Monitor VPD, ensure adequate Ca in solution (100-150 ppm)

### Problem: Leggy, Elongated Plants
- **Cause**: High far-red ratio, high temperature DIF, low light
- **Fix**: Reduce far-red in spectrum, use negative/zero DIF, increase light intensity
- **Prevention**: Proper spectrum selection, DIF management

### Problem: Slow Growth
- **Cause**: Low light (DLI), low temperature, low CO₂, nutrient deficiency
- **Fix**: Increase DLI, raise temp to optimal, enrich CO₂, check/adjust nutrition
- **Prevention**: Monitor all environmental parameters daily

### Problem: Pest Outbreak
- **Cause**: Prevention failure (exclusion, monitoring, beneficial insects)
- **Fix**: Identify pest, apply threshold-based intervention (biological or chemical)
- **Prevention**: 80% effort on prevention (screens, sanitation, beneficial releases)

## Daily Environmental Checklist

**Morning (First 30 minutes)**:
- [ ] Check all zone temperatures (actual vs. setpoint)
- [ ] Check humidity/VPD (in target range?)
- [ ] Verify lighting on/off schedule
- [ ] Check CO₂ levels during photoperiod
- [ ] Review overnight alerts/alarms

**Midday**:
- [ ] Spot-check PPFD at canopy level (3-5 locations per zone)
- [ ] Verify HVAC systems running properly
- [ ] Check irrigation timing and substrate moisture

**Evening**:
- [ ] Review day's environmental data (trends, anomalies)
- [ ] Check dehumidification performance (night humidity)
- [ ] Plan any setpoint adjustments

**Weekly**:
- [ ] Download and analyze environmental data
- [ ] Calibrate sensors (EC, pH, moisture)
- [ ] Clean light fixtures (if needed for intensity maintenance)

---

## Emergency Response

### Power Loss
1. Open vents (if safe) for air circulation
2. Start backup generator if available
3. Manual watering if extended outage (priority crops)
4. Monitor crop conditions closely

### HVAC Failure
1. Reduce light intensity (reduce heat load)
2. Misting for evaporative cooling (if hot)
3. Emergency repair call
4. Document for insurance/analysis

### Temperature Extreme
1. Immediate action (cooling/heating)
2. Monitor crops for stress symptoms
3. Adjust watering if needed (heat stress = more water)
4. Assess damage and recovery plan

## Quick Reference Numbers

```
Temperature Conversions:
°F = (°C × 1.8) + 32
°C = (°F - 32) / 1.8

Light Conversions:
1 μmol/m²/s × 3.6 seconds/hour = 3.6 μmol/m²/hour
× photoperiod hours / 1000 = DLI (mol/m²/day)

VPD Quick Estimates (at 20°C, SVP ≈ 2.34 kPa):
50% RH = 1.17 kPa
60% RH = 0.94 kPa
70% RH = 0.70 kPa
80% RH = 0.47 kPa
```
