# Course 515 Glossary: Capstone Practicum Terminology

## Academic and Research Terms

### Abstract
A concise summary (typically 250-300 words) of a research project or capstone report, including problem, methods, findings, and implications. Written to enable quick assessment of relevance and quality.

### Action Research
Research methodology combining investigation with practical action to solve real-world problems, common in consulting and implementation projects.

### Alternative Hypothesis
In statistical testing, the hypothesis proposing a relationship or difference exists (opposite of null hypothesis).

### Analysis
Systematic examination of data or information to identify patterns, relationships, meanings, or insights.

### Appendix (pl. Appendices)
Supplementary material at end of document providing supporting detail without interrupting main narrative flow (e.g., survey instruments, detailed tables, technical specifications).

### APA Format
American Psychological Association citation and formatting style, standard for business and social science writing. Includes specific rules for in-text citations, reference lists, and document formatting.

## Project Management Terms

### Baseline
Approved version of scope, schedule, or budget against which performance is measured and changes are managed.

### Buffer
Additional time built into schedule to accommodate unexpected delays or challenges, typically 15-20% of task estimates.

### Contingency Plan
Predetermined course of action to be implemented if specific risk occurs or plan fails.

### Critical Path
Longest sequence of dependent tasks determining minimum project duration; delays to critical path tasks delay entire project.

### Deliverable
Tangible output or product required by project (e.g., report, presentation, analysis, model).

### Dependency
Relationship where one task cannot start or finish until another task starts or finishes.

### Float (or Slack)
Amount of time a task can be delayed without affecting project completion date; tasks on critical path have zero float.

### Gantt Chart
Visual timeline showing project tasks, durations, dependencies, milestones, and progress over time.

### Milestone
Significant point or event in project representing completion of major phase or deliverable. Zero-duration markers in project schedule.

### RACI Matrix
Responsibility assignment matrix defining who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for each project activity.

### Risk
Uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has positive or negative effect on project objectives.

### Risk Mitigation
Actions taken to reduce probability or impact of threats, or increase probability or impact of opportunities.

### Scope
Work required to deliver project outputs and outcomes; defines what is included and excluded from project.

### Scope Creep
Uncontrolled expansion of project scope without adjustments to time, cost, or resources; major cause of project failure.

### Stakeholder
Individual, group, or organization with interest in, influence over, or impact from project.

### Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
Hierarchical decomposition of total project scope into manageable work packages and tasks.

## Research Methodology Terms

### Case Study
In-depth investigation of specific instance, organization, or phenomenon to understand complex issues in real-world context.

### Coding
Process of labeling and organizing qualitative data into categories or themes for analysis.

### Concurrent Mixed Methods
Research design collecting and analyzing quantitative and qualitative data simultaneously for comparison or convergence.

### Construct Validity
Extent to which measurement actually represents theoretical construct it claims to measure.

### Convenience Sampling
Non-probability sampling where participants selected based on availability and accessibility rather than representativeness.

### Cross-Tabulation
Statistical table showing frequency distribution of two or more variables simultaneously to examine relationships.

### Data Saturation
Point in qualitative research where new data no longer provides new insights or themes; indicates sufficient data collected.

### Deductive Approach
Starting with theory or hypothesis and testing through data collection; moving from general to specific.

### Dependent Variable
Variable being measured or predicted; outcome affected by independent variable.

### Descriptive Statistics
Methods for summarizing and presenting data (mean, median, mode, standard deviation, etc.).

### Empirical
Based on observation, experience, or experiment rather than theory or logic alone.

### Epistemology
Theory of knowledge; philosophical assumptions about what knowledge is and how it is acquired.

### Ethnography
Qualitative research method involving immersive observation and participation in culture or setting being studied.

### External Validity
Extent to which research findings can be generalized beyond specific study to other populations, settings, or times.

### Face Validity
Superficial assessment of whether measurement appears to measure what it claims (least rigorous form of validity).

### Focus Group
Qualitative research method where small group discusses topic under guidance of moderator to explore perceptions and attitudes.

### Generalizability
Extent to which findings from sample can be applied to broader population.

### Grounded Theory
Qualitative methodology where theory is developed from systematic analysis of data rather than testing pre-existing theory.

### Hypothesis
Testable prediction about relationship between variables, typically derived from theory.

### Independent Variable
Variable manipulated or categorized to examine its effect on dependent variable; the presumed cause.

### Inductive Approach
Starting with observations and developing theory from patterns in data; moving from specific to general.

### Inferential Statistics
Statistical methods for drawing conclusions about populations based on sample data (t-tests, ANOVA, regression, etc.).

### Internal Validity
Extent to which study establishes trustworthy cause-and-effect relationship between variables.

### Inter-Rater Reliability
Degree of agreement among multiple coders or raters analyzing same data; measure of consistency.

### Likert Scale
Rating scale typically with 5 or 7 points measuring agreement, frequency, or intensity (e.g., Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree).

### Literature Review
Comprehensive survey of existing research and writing on topic, identifying knowledge, gaps, and contextualizing current study.

### Mean
Arithmetic average; sum of values divided by number of values.

### Median
Middle value when data ordered from lowest to highest; better than mean for skewed distributions.

### Methodology
Systematic approach and rationale for conducting research; includes methods, procedures, and analytical strategies.

### Mixed Methods
Research approach combining quantitative and qualitative methods to provide more comprehensive understanding.

### Mode
Most frequently occurring value in dataset.

### Null Hypothesis
Hypothesis proposing no relationship or difference exists; what statistical tests attempt to reject.

### Operational Definition
Precise, measurable definition of abstract concept or variable for research purposes.

### Participant Observation
Qualitative method where researcher actively participates in setting being studied while observing and recording.

### Population
Entire group of individuals or cases about which researcher wants to draw conclusions.

### Primary Research
Original research collecting new data directly (surveys, interviews, experiments, observations).

### Probability Sampling
Sampling where every member of population has known, non-zero probability of selection (enables generalization).

### P-Value
Probability that observed results occurred by chance if null hypothesis is true; p<0.05 typically considered statistically significant.

### Purposive Sampling
Non-probability sampling where participants deliberately selected based on characteristics relevant to research questions.

### Qualitative Research
Research exploring meanings, experiences, and perspectives through non-numerical data (interviews, observations, documents).

### Quantitative Research
Research examining relationships and patterns through numerical data and statistical analysis.

### Quasi-Experimental Design
Research design comparing groups without random assignment; approximates experiment when randomization not feasible.

### Random Sampling
Probability sampling where each population member has equal chance of selection.

### Regression Analysis
Statistical method examining relationship between dependent variable and one or more independent variables.

### Reliability
Consistency and repeatability of measurements; extent to which same results obtained if study repeated.

### Research Design
Overall strategy and framework for conducting research, including methodology, data collection, and analysis approaches.

### Research Question
Clear, focused question research aims to answer; guides methodology and analysis.

### Response Rate
Percentage of invited participants who complete survey or participate in research.

### Sample
Subset of population selected for study; used to draw inferences about entire population.

### Sample Size
Number of participants or observations in study; affects statistical power and precision.

### Sampling Frame
List or source from which sample is drawn; should represent target population.

### Saturation
See Data Saturation.

### Secondary Research
Research analyzing existing data collected by others (published studies, industry reports, government statistics).

### Semi-Structured Interview
Interview with predetermined questions but flexibility to explore responses in depth; balances consistency and flexibility.

### Sequential Mixed Methods
Research design where one method follows another, with second phase informed by first (qual→quant or quant→qual).

### Significance (Statistical)
Likelihood that observed relationship or difference is not due to chance; typically p<0.05.

### Snowball Sampling
Non-probability sampling where initial participants refer additional participants, useful for hard-to-reach populations.

### Standard Deviation
Measure of variability; how much values typically differ from mean.

### Statistical Power
Probability of detecting real effect if it exists; affected by sample size, effect size, and significance level.

### Stratified Sampling
Probability sampling dividing population into subgroups (strata) and randomly sampling within each.

### Survey
Research method collecting data from sample through standardized questions, typically for quantitative analysis.

### Synthesis
Integration of findings from multiple sources or methods to create comprehensive understanding.

### Thematic Analysis
Qualitative analytical method identifying, analyzing, and reporting patterns (themes) within data.

### Theoretical Framework
Conceptual structure of theories, concepts, and assumptions guiding research and interpretation.

### Thick Description
Rich, detailed description of context, participants, and phenomena providing depth for understanding and transferability.

### Transferability
Qualitative equivalent of generalizability; extent to which findings transfer to other contexts.

### Triangulation
Using multiple methods, sources, or perspectives to validate findings and increase credibility.

### Trustworthiness
Qualitative research quality criteria including credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability.

### Validity
Extent to which measurement or study accurately represents what it claims to study.

### Variable
Characteristic or attribute that can take different values (e.g., age, revenue, satisfaction level).

## Business and Strategy Terms

### Business Case
Document providing justification for investment or initiative, including strategic rationale, costs, benefits, and risks.

### Business Model
Framework describing how organization creates, delivers, and captures value; includes value proposition, customers, resources, and revenue.

### Business Model Canvas
Visual framework for developing and documenting business models, with nine key components.

### Competitive Advantage
Attribute enabling organization to outperform competitors (lower costs, differentiation, better customer experience, etc.).

### Feasibility Study
Assessment of practicality and viability of proposed project, examining technical, financial, market, and organizational feasibility.

### Go-to-Market Strategy
Plan for bringing product or service to customers, including positioning, channels, sales approach, and market entry.

### Implementation Plan
Detailed roadmap for executing strategy or initiative, including phasing, resources, timeline, and success metrics.

### Key Performance Indicator (KPI)
Measurable value demonstrating how effectively organization achieves business objectives.

### Return on Investment (ROI)
Financial metric measuring gain or loss from investment relative to cost; (Gain - Cost) / Cost.

### SMART Objectives
Goal-setting framework: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

### Stakeholder Analysis
Systematic identification and assessment of stakeholders based on interest, influence, and other factors.

### Strategic Planning
Process of defining direction and making decisions on allocating resources to pursue strategy.

### SWOT Analysis
Strategic planning tool examining Strengths, Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities, Threats (external).

### Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)
Comprehensive assessment of all costs associated with acquiring, implementing, and operating solution over its lifetime.

### Value Proposition
Clear statement of tangible benefits customer receives from product or service; why customer should buy.

## Presentation and Communication Terms

### Call to Action
Statement telling audience what action they should take based on presentation; clear next steps.

### Deck
Presentation slide set (from "deck of slides").

### Elevator Pitch
Concise 30-60 second summary of project, business, or idea that can be delivered in elevator ride.

### Engagement
Degree to which audience is actively attending, participating, and connecting with presentation.

### Executive Summary
Concise summary of longer document (typically 3-5 pages) enabling executives to understand essentials without reading full report.

### Hook
Opening statement or story designed to capture audience attention and interest.

### Key Message
Core point or takeaway audience should remember from presentation or communication.

### Presenter Notes
Hidden notes in presentation software visible only to presenter, providing reminders, talking points, or additional detail.

### Slide Master
Template defining default formatting, fonts, and layouts for presentation slides.

### Storytelling
Presentation technique using narrative structure and examples to make data and concepts more engaging and memorable.

### Transition
Connecting statement or phrase linking one topic or section to next, maintaining flow.

### Visual Hierarchy
Arrangement of elements to show importance through size, color, position, and emphasis.

## Capstone-Specific Terms

### Capstone Defense
Formal presentation and question-and-answer session where student presents and defends capstone work before faculty panel.

### Capstone Practicum
Applied master's project demonstrating synthesis of learning and professional competence through significant real-world project.

### Capstone Proposal
Comprehensive document outlining planned project, submitted for approval before beginning execution.

### Defense Panel
Faculty committee evaluating capstone work through review of deliverables and formal defense session.

### Faculty Advisor
Professor assigned to guide student through capstone process, providing consultation, feedback, and evaluation.

### Portfolio
Collection of work samples and professional materials showcasing competencies, achievements, and career readiness.

### Professional Development
Activities and experiences contributing to career readiness and advancement (skills, knowledge, networks, credentials).

### Reflection Essay
Written critical reflection on learning, growth, and professional development through capstone experience.

### Subject Matter Expert (SME)
Individual with deep expertise in specific domain, often consulted for specialized knowledge or validation.

## Tips for Using This Glossary

1. **Reference During Reading**: Look up unfamiliar terms as you encounter them in lessons or assignments.

2. **Study Aid**: Review relevant terms before quizzes, exams, or major deliverables.

3. **Professional Communication**: Use precise terminology when communicating with advisors, panel members, and stakeholders.

4. **Writing Quality**: Proper use of technical vocabulary demonstrates expertise and professionalism in written deliverables.

5. **Defense Preparation**: Ensure you can define and explain all key terms related to your project methodology and approach.

6. **Build Your Vocabulary**: Add project-specific terms not included here to your personal glossary.
