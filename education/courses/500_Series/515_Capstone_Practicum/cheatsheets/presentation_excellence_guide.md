# Capstone Presentation Excellence Guide

## Quick Reference for High-Impact Presentations

---

## 1. Presentation Structure (30-40 min)

### Classic Opening (3-5 min)
```
Hook (30 sec) → Introduce Self (30 sec) → Agenda (1 min)
    → Significance (2 min) → Roadmap (1 min)
```

**Hook Examples:**
- Surprising statistic: "70% of vertical farms fail financially—here's why"
- Provocative question: "What if I told you we've been optimizing for the wrong metric?"
- Brief story: "Last year, a farm operator told me..."
- Bold statement: "The vertical farming industry has a profitability problem, but not the one you think"

---

### Main Body (22-30 min)
```
Context (3-5 min)
    ↓
Approach (3-5 min)
    ↓
Key Findings (10-12 min) ← HEART OF PRESENTATION
    ↓
Recommendations (8-10 min)
```

**Findings Organization:**
Finding 1 (3-4 min) → Finding 2 (3-4 min) → Finding 3 (3-4 min)

**Each Finding:**
- State finding clearly (1 slide)
- Show evidence (1-2 slides with data/quotes)
- Explain significance (verbal)
- Transition to next

---

### Strong Closing (2-3 min)
```
Summary → Implications → Call to Action → Thanks
```

**Closing Formula:**
1. "In summary, we found..."
2. "This means that..."
3. "I recommend that..."
4. "Thank you. I welcome your questions."

---

## 2. Slide Design Principles

### The 6x6 Rule
```
Maximum 6 bullet points per slide
Maximum 6 words per bullet point
```

**Better Yet: 1-1-1 Rule**
- 1 main idea per slide
- 1 key visual or data point
- 1 supporting message

---

### Visual Hierarchy
```
LARGEST/BOLDEST = Most Important (Title, Key Message)
    ↓
Medium = Supporting (Sub-points)
    ↓
Smallest = Details (Data, sources)
```

---

### Color Psychology
- **Blue**: Trust, professionalism, stability
- **Green**: Growth, sustainability, nature
- **Orange/Yellow**: Energy, optimism, innovation
- **Red**: Urgency, importance (use sparingly)
- **Gray**: Neutral, background

**Formula:**
- Primary color: 60% (backgrounds, main elements)
- Secondary color: 30% (supporting elements)
- Accent color: 10% (highlights, calls to attention)

---

### Font Selection
**Do:**
- Sans-serif for slides (Arial, Calibri, Helvetica)
- Minimum 24pt for body text
- Minimum 36pt for headings
- Maximum 2 fonts per presentation

**Don't:**
- Serif fonts (harder to read on screen)
- Decorative or script fonts
- Fonts smaller than 24pt
- All caps (except short headings)

## 4. Delivery Excellence

### Voice Control
**Volume:** Loud enough for back row
**Pace:** 120-150 words/minute (conversational)
**Pauses:** Strategic silence for emphasis
**Inflection:** Vary pitch to maintain interest
**Clarity:** Enunciate, especially technical terms

**Practice Drill:**
Record yourself → Listen critically → Identify issues
    → Practice fixes → Record again → Improve

---

### Body Language
```
POSTURE:  Stand tall, feet shoulder-width, weight balanced
GESTURES: Natural hand movements, purposeful
EYE CONTACT: 2-3 seconds per person, scan room
MOVEMENT: Move with purpose, not pacing
FACE: Smile when appropriate, show enthusiasm
```

**The Triangle:**
```
      Panel/Audience
        /    \
       /      \
   Screen    You
```
Face audience 80% of time, screen 20%

---

### Managing Nerves
**Before:**
- Deep breathing (4 counts in, hold 4, out 4)
- Power poses (2 minutes)
- Positive visualization
- "Nervousness is excitement"

**During:**
- Focus on one person at a time
- Pause and breathe when needed
- Have water available
- Remember: they want you to succeed

## 5. Question Handling Framework

### The L.A.P. Method
**L**isten: Full attention, don't interrupt
**A**ssess: Understand what's really being asked
**P**ause: Brief moment to think

### Response Structure
```
1. "That's a great question"
2. [Pause to think]
3. Restate or clarify if needed
4. Answer directly
5. Provide supporting evidence
6. Check: "Does that address your question?"
```

---

### Question Types & Responses

**Clarifying:**
*"How did you select your sample?"*
- Direct factual answer
- Reference methodology
- Brief and clear

**Probing:**
*"Why that approach over alternatives?"*
- Explain reasoning
- Acknowledge trade-offs
- Justify choice

**Challenging:**
*"Your sample size seems small..."*
- Acknowledge concern
- Provide context
- Explain mitigation
- Stand by work

**Hypothetical:**
*"What if you had more time/budget?"*
- Engage thoughtfully
- Don't undermine what you did
- Show broader thinking

**Don't Know:**
- "That's a great question I don't have data for"
- "Outside my current scope, but interesting"
- Better honest than bluffing

## 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Slide Sins
❌ Reading slides verbatim
❌ Too much text
❌ Tiny fonts
❌ Cluttered layouts
❌ Cheesy clip art
❌ Inconsistent formatting
❌ 3D charts (distort data)
❌ Distracting animations

### Delivery Disasters
❌ Turning back to audience
❌ Apologizing ("Sorry this is boring...")
❌ Running over time
❌ Monotone voice
❌ Fidgeting or pacing
❌ Reading from notes
❌ No eye contact
❌ Defensive responses

## 8. Practice Strategy

### Multi-Level Practice
```
Week 4: Solo practice (content, flow)
    ↓
Week 3: Timed practice (refine, cut/add)
    ↓
Week 2: Peer practice (get feedback)
    ↓
Week 1: Mock defense (full simulation)
    ↓
Day Before: Final polish (smooth edges)
```

### Practice Drill
1. **Full Run-Through** (4-6 times minimum)
   - Start to finish, no stopping
   - Time yourself
   - Record if possible

2. **Section Practice** (Problem areas)
   - Repeat difficult transitions
   - Perfect critical slides
   - Smooth rough spots

3. **Q&A Practice**
   - List 20 potential questions
   - Practice answers
   - Role-play with peers

## 9. Time Management

### The 30-Minute Breakdown
```
Opening:         3-5 min  (10-15%)
Context:         3-5 min  (10-15%)
Methodology:     3-5 min  (10-15%)
Findings:       10-12 min (33-40%) ← LARGEST BLOCK
Recommendations: 8-10 min (25-30%)
Closing:         2-3 min  (7-10%)
_________________________________________
Total:          30-40 min
```

### Time Check Points
```
Slide 5:  5 minutes elapsed ✓
Slide 10: 15 minutes elapsed ✓
Slide 15: 25 minutes elapsed ✓
Slide 20: 35 minutes elapsed ✓
```

**If Running Long:**
- Skip example slides (keep data)
- Condense sections (don't rush)
- Cut nice-to-have content

**If Running Short:**
- Expand on key findings
- Add examples
- Elaborate on implications
- Slow pace (don't pad)

## 10. Handling Difficult Situations

### Technical Failure
**Plan B:** Have printed handouts or be able to present from memory
**Stay Calm:** "Let me tell you about this while we sort the tech..."
**Humor Helps:** Brief, light comment, then move on

### Hostile Question
**Stay Professional:** Don't take personally
**Find the Kernel:** Look for legitimate concern
**Respond Calmly:** Address substance, ignore tone
**Don't Engage Battle:** Panel will notice and appreciate your poise

### Drawing a Blank
**Pause:** "Let me think about that for a moment..."
**Rephrase:** "Can you rephrase the question?"
**Honest:** "I'm not recalling the specific data, but the pattern was..."
**Move On:** Don't dwell on what you can't answer

### Running Over
**Acknowledge:** "I see we're at time..."
**Offer:** "I can either wrap up quickly or continue if time permits"
**Defer:** "I'll cover remaining points in Q&A if they come up"
**Don't:** Panic or rush through critical content

## 11. Engagement Techniques

### Rhetorical Questions
"What if we could reduce energy costs by 30%?"
(Pause, let them think)
"Here's how..."

### Data Storytelling
❌ "70% of farms are unprofitable"
✅ "Meet Sarah. She grows beautiful lettuce but loses money on every head..."

### Contrast and Comparison
"Traditional approach yields X... Our approach yields 2X"

### "You" Language
❌ "The research shows..."
✅ "You might be wondering why... Here's what we found..."

### Strategic Pauses
Bold statement → [Pause 2-3 seconds] → Continue

## 12. Virtual Presentation Adaptations

### Camera Presence
- Eye level camera (stack books if needed)
- Look at camera, not screen
- Ring light or good lighting
- Professional background
- Frame: Head and shoulders visible

### Engagement Strategies
- More frequent verbal signposting
- Poll or chat questions
- Encourage video on
- Monitor chat for questions
- More visual variety (images, charts)

### Technical Prep
- Test audio/video extensively
- Hardwired internet if possible
- Close all other applications
- Have phone backup
- Test screen share

## 14. The Closer Arsenal

### Summary Close
"In summary, we found X, Y, Z, which means..."

"Based on these findings, I recommend three immediate actions..."

### Future Vision Close

"As [authority] said, '...' and this research proves it"

## 15. Confidence Builders

### Pre-Presentation Ritual
1. Review notes (30 min before)
2. Power pose (2 min)
3. Deep breathing (5 min)
4. Positive affirmations
5. Light physical movement
6. Arrive early, set up
7. Chat with friendly face
8. "Showtime" mindset

### Mid-Presentation Resets
- **Pause and breathe**: If flustered
- **Take a sip**: If voice shaky
- **Make eye contact**: Find friendly face
- **Slow down**: If rushing
- **Smile**: Releases endorphins
