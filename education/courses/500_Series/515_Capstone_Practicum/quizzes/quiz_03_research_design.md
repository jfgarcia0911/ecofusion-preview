# Quiz 3: Research Design and Methodology

## Course 515: Capstone Practicum

**Module**: 3 - Research Design and Methodology
**Time Limit**: 15 minutes
**Passing Score**: 80% (8/10 correct)

---

## Questions

### Question 1
What distinguishes qualitative from quantitative research methods?

A) No difference exists
B) Qualitative explores meaning/themes; quantitative measures numerical data statistically
C) Qualitative uses numbers; quantitative uses words
D) They cannot be combined

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Qualitative research explores experiences, meanings, and themes through interviews/observations. Quantitative research measures variables numerically with statistical analysis.

---

### Question 2
What is a "literature review" and why is it essential for capstone projects?

A) Reading fiction books
B) Systematic examination of existing research to establish context and identify gaps
C) Copying others' work
D) Unnecessary step

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Literature reviews establish what's known, identify gaps, provide theoretical framework, and position your work within existing knowledge.

---

### Question 3
What is "triangulation" in research methodology?

A) Geometric calculations only
B) Using multiple data sources or methods to validate findings and increase credibility
C) Three researchers working together
D) Triangle-shaped charts

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Triangulation strengthens research by corroborating findings through multiple sources, methods, or perspectives, increasing validity.

---

### Question 4
When conducting interviews for capstone research, what makes questions effective?

A) Leading questions suggesting desired answers
B) Open-ended, neutral questions that elicit detailed responses
C) Yes/no questions only
D) Trick questions

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Open-ended, neutral questions encourage participants to share detailed perspectives without bias from question framing.

---

### Question 5
What is "sampling" and why does methodology matter?

A) Testing food products
B) Selecting subset of population; methodology affects representativeness and validity
C) Random guessing
D) Including everyone always

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Sampling strategy (random, stratified, purposive) determines whether findings can be generalized and affects research validity.

---

### Question 6
What is the difference between primary and secondary data?

A) No difference
B) Primary is collected firsthand for specific research; secondary is existing data from other sources
C) Primary is less valuable
D) Secondary is always better

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Primary data (surveys, interviews, experiments) is collected for your research. Secondary data (reports, databases) exists from prior work.

---

### Question 7
What does "validity" mean in research design?

A) Legal approval
B) Whether research measures what it intends to measure and conclusions are sound
C) Expiration dates
D) Computer programming

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Validity ensures research instruments measure intended constructs and conclusions accurately reflect what was studied.

---

### Question 8
What is "reliability" in research and how is it ensured?

A) Trustworthy people only
B) Consistency of measurements; ensured through standardized protocols and instruments
C) Random variation encouraged
D) Not important

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Reliability means consistent results under similar conditions, achieved through standardized procedures and well-designed instruments.

---

### Question 9
Why is documenting research methodology important in capstone work?

A) Unnecessary paperwork
B) Enables evaluation of rigor, replication by others, and transparency
C) To make document longer
D) Only for formality

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Clear methodology documentation allows assessment of research quality, enables replication, and demonstrates scholarly rigor.

---

### Question 10
What are "research ethics" and why do they matter?

A) Optional guidelines
B) Principles ensuring participant protection, informed consent, and research integrity
C) Slowing down research
D) Unnecessary restrictions

**Correct Answer**: B

**Explanation**: Research ethics protect participants, ensure informed consent, maintain confidentiality, and uphold research integrity.

---

9. B - Enables rigor evaluation and replication
