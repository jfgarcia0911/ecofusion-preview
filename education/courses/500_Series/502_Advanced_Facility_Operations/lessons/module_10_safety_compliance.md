# Module 10: Safety and Compliance Programs

## Master Level - Course 502: Advanced Facility Operations

### Module Overview

Comprehensive safety management systems, OSHA compliance, food safety regulations, environmental permits, and audit preparation for CEA operations.

**Duration:** 4 hours

### Learning Objectives

1. Implement comprehensive safety management systems
2. Achieve and maintain OSHA compliance
3. Manage food safety and traceability requirements
4. Navigate environmental regulations and permits
5. Conduct effective safety training programs
6. Prepare for regulatory audits and inspections
7. Develop safety culture and accountability

#### OSHA Compliance

```
OSHA REQUIREMENTS FOR CEA
==========================

General Industry Standards (29 CFR 1910):

Subpart D - Walking-Working Surfaces
├── Slip, trip, fall hazards
├── Ladder safety
├── Elevated platforms
└── Floor markings and maintenance

Subpart E - Exit Routes and Emergency Planning
├── Emergency action plan
├── Fire prevention plan
├── Exit route requirements
└── Emergency evacuation procedures

Subpart H - Hazardous Materials
├── Hazard communication (HazCom)
├── Safety Data Sheets (SDS)
├── Chemical labeling (GHS)
├── Employee training
└── Written program required

Subpart I - Personal Protective Equipment
├── PPE hazard assessment
├── PPE selection and use
├── Eye and face protection
├── Respiratory protection
├── Hand protection
└── Hearing conservation

Subpart J - General Environmental Controls
├── Sanitation requirements
├── Potable water
├── Toilet facilities
├── Washing facilities
└── Eating and drinking areas

Subpart S - Electrical Safety
├── Electrical safety program
├── LOTO procedures
├── Arc flash protection
├── Ground fault protection
└── Qualified persons

Record Keeping Requirements:
├── OSHA 300 Log (injury/illness record)
├── OSHA 301 (incident report)
├── OSHA 300A (annual summary - post Feb 1-Apr 30)
├── Retention: 5 years
└── Electronic submission if 100+ employees

Inspection Preparedness:
├── Self-audits quarterly
├── Mock OSHA inspections
├── Document organization
├── Employee interview preparation
├── Corrective action follow-up
└── Management walk-throughs
```

├── Traceback to source

├── 24-hour traceback capability

### Audit Preparation

```
REGULATORY AUDIT READINESS
===========================

Pre-Audit Preparation:
├── Self-assessment against standards
├── Document review and organization
├── Facility inspection and cleanup
├── Employee interview preparation
├── Mock audits
└── Corrective actions completed

Document Organization:
├── Policies and procedures
├── Training records
├── Inspection records
├── Maintenance logs
├── Incident reports
├── Meeting minutes
├── Certifications
└── Index for quick retrieval

Facility Readiness:
├── 5S implemented
├── Signage current
├── Equipment properly guarded
├── PPE available and in use
├── Emergency equipment accessible
├── Records visible and up-to-date
└── Professional appearance

During the Audit:
├── Designate point person
├── Professional and cooperative
├── Answer questions honestly
├── Don't volunteer extra information
├── Request clarification if needed
├── Take notes
├── Ask for findings in writing
└── Don't commit to unrealistic timelines

Post-Audit:
├── Review findings
├── Develop corrective action plan
├── Assign responsibilities
├── Set deadlines
├── Implement corrections
├── Verify effectiveness
├── Document completion
└── Follow-up audit if needed
```

### Practical Application

**Safety Program ROI:**

```
BUSINESS CASE FOR SAFETY
=========================

Costs of Poor Safety:
├── Direct costs
│   ├── Medical expenses
│   ├── Workers' compensation
│   ├── Legal fees
│   ├── OSHA fines
│   └── Property damage
│
└── Indirect costs (4-10x direct)
    ├── Lost productivity
    ├── Replacement labor
    ├── Training new workers
    ├── Investigation time
    ├── Morale impact
    ├── Reputation damage
    └── Insurance premium increases

Example:
Lost-time injury: 1 employee, 30 days
├── Direct costs: $25,000
│   ├── Medical: $15,000
│   ├── Workers' comp: $8,000
│   └── Legal: $2,000
│
└── Indirect costs: $125,000
    ├── Productivity loss: $50,000
    ├── Replacement worker: $30,000
    ├── Investigation/admin: $15,000
    ├── Training: $10,000
    ├── Overtime coverage: $15,000
    └── Other: $5,000

Total cost: $150,000 per incident

Safety Program Investment:
├── Safety manager: $80,000/year
├── Training: $20,000/year
├── PPE and equipment: $15,000/year
├── Audits and consultants: $10,000/year
└── Total: $125,000/year

ROI: Prevent 1 major incident = break even
     Typical reduction: 3-5 incidents/year
     Net savings: $300K-$600K/year
     ROI: 240-380%
```

### Assessment Questions

1. What are the core elements of a comprehensive safety management system?
2. Describe OSHA record-keeping requirements and posting rules
3. What are the key requirements of FSMA Preventive Controls?
4. How do you prepare for a regulatory audit?
5. What is the business case for investing in safety programs?

### Key Takeaways

1. **Safety First is a Commitment:** Leadership sets the tone for safety culture
2. **Compliance is Minimum:** Exceed regulations to truly protect workers
3. **Prevent, Don't React:** Proactive programs prevent incidents
4. **Train Continuously:** Competency requires ongoing education
5. **Document Everything:** Records prove compliance and drive improvement
6. **Engage Employees:** Workers are the best source of safety insights
7. **Measure and Improve:** Track metrics and continuously enhance programs
