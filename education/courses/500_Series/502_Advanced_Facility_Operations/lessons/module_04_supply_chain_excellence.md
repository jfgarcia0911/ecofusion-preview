# Module 4: Supply Chain Excellence

## Master Level - Course 502: Advanced Facility Operations

### Module Overview

This module explores end-to-end supply chain management for CEA operations, from procurement and vendor management to logistics and distribution. Students will learn how to design resilient supply chains, optimize inventory, manage supplier relationships, and create competitive advantage through supply chain excellence.

**Duration:** 5 hours
**Difficulty:** Master

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Design end-to-end supply chain strategies for CEA operations
2. Optimize procurement processes and vendor relationships
3. Implement advanced inventory management techniques
4. Develop efficient logistics and distribution systems
5. Manage supply chain risk and build resilience
6. Use supply chain analytics for decision-making
7. Create sustainable and circular supply chains

**Inventory Optimization Models:**

```
ECONOMIC ORDER QUANTITY (EOQ)
==============================

Basic EOQ Formula:
EOQ = √(2DS/H)

Where:
D = Annual demand
S = Order cost per order
H = Holding cost per unit per year

Example: Growing Media
D = 50,000 cubic feet/year
S = $150 per order
H = $2 per cubic foot/year

EOQ = √(2 × 50,000 × 150 / 2)
    = √7,500,000
    = 2,738 cubic feet per order

Orders per year = 50,000 / 2,738 = 18 orders
Order frequency = 365 / 18 = Every 20 days

Total Annual Cost:
Ordering cost = (D/EOQ) × S = $2,250
Holding cost = (EOQ/2) × H = $2,738
Total = $4,988

Reorder Point (ROP):
ROP = (Daily demand × Lead time) + Safety stock

Example:
Daily demand = 137 cubic feet
Lead time = 7 days
Safety stock = 200 cubic feet (for variability)

ROP = (137 × 7) + 200 = 1,159 cubic feet

When inventory drops to 1,159 cf, order 2,738 cf
```

**Safety Stock Calculation:**

```
SAFETY STOCK DETERMINATION
===========================

Method 1: Basic Formula
SS = Z × σ × √LT

Where:
Z = Service level factor (Z-score)
σ = Standard deviation of demand
LT = Lead time in same units as demand

Service Level Table:
90% → Z = 1.28
95% → Z = 1.65
98% → Z = 2.05
99% → Z = 2.33

Example: Nutrient Solution
Daily demand: avg 100L, std dev 20L
Lead time: 5 days
Service level: 98%

SS = 2.05 × 20 × √5
   = 2.05 × 20 × 2.24
   = 92 liters

Method 2: Demand and Lead Time Variability
SS = Z × √[(Avg LT × σD²) + (Avg D² × σLT²)]

Where:
σD = Std dev of demand
σLT = Std dev of lead time

More accurate when both vary significantly

Inventory Metrics:
├── Inventory turnover = COGS / Avg inventory
├── Days of supply = (Avg inventory / Avg daily usage)
├── Fill rate % = Orders filled / Total orders
├── Stockout frequency = Stockouts / Total periods
└── Carrying cost % = Annual carrying cost / Avg inventory value
```

#### 4. Logistics and Distribution

**Distribution Network Design:**

```
DISTRIBUTION MODELS
===================

Model 1: Direct Distribution
[Facility] → [Customer]

Advantages:
+ Lowest cost (no intermediaries)
+ Fastest delivery
+ Best freshness
+ Direct customer relationship

Disadvantages:
- Limited by facility location
- Higher transportation costs
- Minimum order quantities
- Route density required

Best for:
- Local markets (<50 miles)
- Large volume customers
- High-value products

Model 2: Distribution Center
[Facility] → [DC] → [Customers]

Advantages:
+ Extended reach
+ Consolidation benefits
+ Better route optimization
+ Cross-docking opportunities

Disadvantages:
- Additional handling
- Extra inventory holding
- DC operating costs
- Longer lead times

Best for:
- Regional expansion
- Multi-facility networks
- Mixed product portfolios

Model 3: Hybrid Model
[Facility] → [Direct] (local/large)
          ↘ [DC] → [Customers] (regional/small)

Optimizes based on:
├── Customer location
├── Order size
├── Service requirements
└── Cost to serve

Model 4: Third-Party Logistics (3PL)
[Facility] → [3PL] → [Customers]

Advantages:
+ Leverage 3PL infrastructure
+ Variable cost structure
+ Expertise in logistics
+ Scalability

Disadvantages:
- Loss of control
- Service variability
- Hidden costs
- Customer interface concerns

Best for:
- Rapid geographic expansion
- Seasonal volume spikes
- Focus on core operations
```

### Practical Application

#### Case Study: Supply Chain Redesign

**Scenario:**
GreenLeaf Farms operates 3 facilities in different states, each with independent supply chains. Seeking to reduce costs by 15% and improve service levels.

**Current State Analysis:**

```
Facility A (California):
├── Annual spend: $2.5M
├── Suppliers: 85
├── Inventory turns: 8x
├── Fill rate: 92%
└── Logistics cost: $350K

Facility B (Texas):
├── Annual spend: $1.8M
├── Suppliers: 72
├── Inventory turns: 7x
├── Fill rate: 89%
└── Logistics cost: $280K

Facility C (Florida):
├── Annual spend: $2.1M
├── Suppliers: 79
├── Inventory turns: 6x
├── Fill rate: 90%
└── Logistics cost: $320K

Total Spend: $6.4M
Total Suppliers: 236 (with overlap)
Avg Fill Rate: 90.3%
Total Logistics: $950K
```

**Improvement Initiatives:**

```
1. Supplier Consolidation
   ├── Consolidate to 45 suppliers
   ├── Volume leverage → 12% price reduction
   ├── Preferred supplier tiers
   └── Savings: $425K

2. Centralized Procurement
   ├── Single procurement team
   ├── Standardized processes
   ├── E-procurement platform
   ├── Contract management
   └── Savings: $180K (efficiency)

3. Inventory Optimization
   ├── Centralized planning
   ├── Safety stock reduction
   ├── Improved forecasting
   ├── Cross-facility sharing
   └── Savings: $95K (carrying cost)

4. Logistics Network Optimization
   ├── Regional distribution center
   ├── Consolidated shipments
   ├── Route optimization
   ├── 3PL for long-haul
   └── Savings: $165K

Total Savings: $865K (13.5% of spend)
Service Improvement: 90% → 96% fill rate
Implementation Cost: $245K
Payback: 3.4 months
```

### Key Takeaways

1. **Align Strategy:** Match supply chain strategy (efficient vs. responsive) to product and market characteristics

2. **Manage Relationships:** Segment suppliers and invest in strategic partnerships

3. **Optimize Inventory:** Use data-driven approaches to balance service levels and working capital

4. **Design for Efficiency:** Network design dramatically impacts cost and service

5. **Manage Risk:** Identify, assess, and mitigate supply chain risks proactively

6. **Measure Performance:** Track KPIs and continuously improve

7. **Embrace Technology:** Leverage technology for visibility, optimization, and automation
