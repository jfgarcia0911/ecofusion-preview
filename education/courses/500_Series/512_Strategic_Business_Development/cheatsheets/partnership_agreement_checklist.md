# Partnership Agreement Checklist

## Course 512: Strategic Business Development

---

## Essential Partnership Agreement Components

Use this checklist when negotiating and documenting strategic partnerships to ensure all critical elements are addressed.

---

## 1. Parties and Structure

### Basic Information
- [ ] Full legal names of all parties
- [ ] Legal addresses and jurisdictions
- [ ] Type of legal entity for each party
- [ ] Partnership structure type (alliance, JV, licensing, etc.)
- [ ] Effective date and execution date

### New Entity (if JV)
- [ ] Name and legal structure
- [ ] Jurisdiction of incorporation
- [ ] Registered agent and address
- [ ] Initial capitalization
- [ ] Ownership percentages

---

## 2. Purpose and Scope

### Strategic Objectives
- [ ] Clear statement of partnership purpose
- [ ] Strategic goals and objectives
- [ ] Success criteria defined
- [ ] Timeline and milestones

### Scope Definition
- [ ] Products/services covered
- [ ] Geographic scope
- [ ] Customer segments included/excluded
- [ ] Specific activities included
- [ ] Explicit exclusions

### Example:
"The parties will collaborate to develop and commercialize vertical farming technology for the Asian market, specifically targeting Japan, South Korea, and Singapore for leafy greens and herbs production."

---

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### Party A Responsibilities
- [ ] Specific deliverables
- [ ] Resources to be provided
- [ ] Performance standards
- [ ] Timeline for each

### Party B Responsibilities
- [ ] Specific deliverables
- [ ] Resources to be provided
- [ ] Performance standards
- [ ] Timeline for each

### Shared Responsibilities
- [ ] Joint activities defined
- [ ] Collaboration mechanisms
- [ ] Communication protocols

---

## 4. Financial Terms

### Capital Contributions (if applicable)
- [ ] Initial contributions by each party
- [ ] Form (cash, assets, services)
- [ ] Timing of contributions
- [ ] Additional capital call provisions
- [ ] Consequences of non-contribution

### Revenue and Cost Sharing
- [ ] Revenue allocation methodology
- [ ] Cost sharing formula
- [ ] Expense categories covered
- [ ] Payment terms and timing
- [ ] Invoicing and reconciliation process

### Pricing (if supply agreement)
- [ ] Base pricing structure
- [ ] Volume-based pricing tiers
- [ ] Price adjustment mechanisms
- [ ] Payment terms
- [ ] Minimum purchase commitments

### Example Pricing Schedule:
```
Tier 1: 0-10,000 lbs/month = $3.50/lb
Tier 2: 10,001-25,000 lbs/month = $3.25/lb
Tier 3: 25,000+ lbs/month = $3.00/lb
Annual CPI adjustment, max 3%
Payment: Net 30
```

---

## 5. Governance and Decision-Making

### Governance Structure
- [ ] Steering committee composition
- [ ] Management team structure
- [ ] Working group/task forces
- [ ] Meeting frequency and protocols
- [ ] Quorum requirements

### Decision Rights
- [ ] Major decisions requiring unanimous approval
- [ ] Strategic decisions (define threshold)
- [ ] Operational decisions (management authority)
- [ ] Emergency decision protocols
- [ ] Deadlock resolution mechanism

### Major Decisions Typically Include:
- Capital expenditures over $__________
- New market or product launches
- Hiring/firing key personnel
- Material contracts over $__________
- Changes to business plan
- Dissolution or sale of JV
- Addition of new partners

---

## 6. Intellectual Property

### Existing IP
- [ ] Party A IP listed and defined
- [ ] Party B IP listed and defined
- [ ] License grants (if any)
- [ ] Restrictions on use
- [ ] Ownership confirmation

### New IP Created
- [ ] Ownership of new IP (joint, by creator, to entity)
- [ ] License back provisions
- [ ] Improvements to existing IP
- [ ] Use rights after termination
- [ ] Patent filing responsibilities and costs

### IP Protection
- [ ] Confidentiality obligations
- [ ] Non-disclosure of trade secrets
- [ ] Protection mechanisms
- [ ] Breach remedies

### Example IP Provision:
"All IP created jointly in the partnership shall be jointly owned. Each party grants the other a perpetual, royalty-free license to use jointly created IP within their respective fields of use. Party A field: Vertical farming systems. Party B field: Controlled environment agriculture."

---

## 7. Performance Standards and SLAs

### Quality Standards
- [ ] Product/service specifications
- [ ] Quality control processes
- [ ] Inspection and testing protocols
- [ ] Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Rejection and remedy procedures

### Service Level Agreements
- [ ] On-time delivery (e.g., 99%)
- [ ] Quality metrics (e.g., defect rate <0.5%)
- [ ] Response times
- [ ] Availability commitments
- [ ] Reporting requirements

### Performance Consequences
- [ ] Bonuses for exceeding standards
- [ ] Penalties for missing standards
- [ ] Cure periods and remedies
- [ ] Termination thresholds

---

## 8. Term and Termination

### Term
- [ ] Initial term length
- [ ] Renewal provisions (automatic or opt-in)
- [ ] Renewal terms and conditions
- [ ] Notice period for non-renewal

### Termination Rights

**Termination for Cause:**
- [ ] Material breach definition
- [ ] Cure period (e.g., 30 days)
- [ ] Notice requirements
- [ ] Effective date of termination

**Termination for Convenience:**
- [ ] Conditions (if allowed)
- [ ] Notice period (e.g., 90-180 days)
- [ ] Termination fee (if any)
- [ ] Limitations on timing

**Automatic Termination:**
- [ ] Bankruptcy or insolvency
- [ ] Change of control (if specified)
- [ ] Regulatory prohibition
- [ ] Force majeure (extended)

### Post-Termination
- [ ] Wind-down period and obligations
- [ ] Return of property and materials
- [ ] Final accounting and payments
- [ ] Surviving obligations
- [ ] Non-compete (if applicable)
- [ ] Customer transition plan

---

## 9. Exclusivity and Non-Compete

### Exclusivity Provisions
- [ ] Exclusive or non-exclusive
- [ ] Scope of exclusivity:
  - [ ] Geographic
  - [ ] Product/service
  - [ ] Customer segment
  - [ ] Channel
- [ ] Duration of exclusivity
- [ ] Performance requirements to maintain
- [ ] Exceptions to exclusivity

### Non-Compete (if applicable)
- [ ] Restricted activities
- [ ] Geographic scope
- [ ] Duration (during term and post-term)
- [ ] Exceptions
- [ ] Enforceability considerations

### Example:
"Partner A grants Partner B exclusive rights to distribute Products in Japan for 3 years, provided Partner B achieves minimum annual sales of $2M. Exclusivity automatically ends if minimum not met for two consecutive years."

---

## 10. Confidentiality

### Confidential Information Definition
- [ ] What constitutes confidential information
- [ ] Exclusions (public domain, independently developed, etc.)
- [ ] Marking requirements

### Obligations
- [ ] Standard of care (e.g., same as own confidential info)
- [ ] Use restrictions (only for partnership purposes)
- [ ] Disclosure limitations (need-to-know basis)
- [ ] Return or destruction upon termination

### Duration
- [ ] During term of agreement
- [ ] Post-termination period (e.g., 3-5 years)
- [ ] Trade secrets (indefinite)

### Exceptions
- [ ] Required by law or regulation
- [ ] Court order (with notice)
- [ ] Permitted disclosures (specify)

---

## 11. Representations and Warranties

### Each Party Represents:
- [ ] Authority to enter agreement
- [ ] No conflicts with other obligations
- [ ] Ownership of contributed IP/assets
- [ ] Compliance with laws
- [ ] Financial capability to perform
- [ ] No pending litigation affecting ability to perform

### Product/Service Warranties (if applicable):
- [ ] Performance specifications
- [ ] Freedom from defects
- [ ] Compliance with regulations
- [ ] Non-infringement of third-party rights

### Warranty Duration and Remedies
- [ ] Warranty period
- [ ] Notice of breach
- [ ] Cure rights
- [ ] Remedies (repair, replace, refund)
- [ ] Limitations on warranties
- [ ] Disclaimer of implied warranties

---

## 12. Indemnification and Liability

### Indemnification
**Party A Indemnifies Party B for:**
- [ ] Breach of representations/warranties
- [ ] IP infringement claims
- [ ] Negligence or willful misconduct
- [ ] Violation of laws
- [ ] [Specific liabilities]

**Party B Indemnifies Party A for:**
- [ ] Same categories as above

### Indemnification Process
- [ ] Notice requirements
- [ ] Defense control
- [ ] Settlement approval
- [ ] Cooperation obligations

### Liability Limitations
- [ ] Cap on liability (e.g., 12 months fees, $X amount)
- [ ] Exclusions from cap (willful misconduct, IP, confidentiality)
- [ ] Disclaimer of consequential damages
- [ ] Time limit for claims

### Insurance
- [ ] Required coverage types
- [ ] Minimum coverage amounts
- [ ] Certificate of insurance
- [ ] Additional insured requirements

---

## 13. Dispute Resolution

### Escalation Process
**Level 1:** Working level managers (5 business days)
**Level 2:** Executive management (10 business days)
**Level 3:** Mediation or arbitration

### Mediation
- [ ] Mediation required before arbitration/litigation
- [ ] Mediator selection process
- [ ] Cost sharing
- [ ] Timeline

### Arbitration vs. Litigation
- [ ] Binding arbitration (specify rules, e.g., AAA)
- [ ] Number of arbitrators
- [ ] Location
- [ ] Governing law
- [ ] OR: Litigation (jurisdiction and venue)

### Continued Performance
- [ ] Parties continue performing during dispute
- [ ] Exceptions (if any)

---

## 14. General Provisions

### Governing Law and Jurisdiction
- [ ] Governing law (state/country)
- [ ] Jurisdiction for disputes
- [ ] Venue
- [ ] Waiver of jury trial (if applicable)

### Notices
- [ ] Notice addresses for each party
- [ ] Acceptable delivery methods
- [ ] When deemed received
- [ ] Update procedures

### Assignment
- [ ] Prohibition on assignment without consent
- [ ] Exceptions (affiliates, change of control)
- [ ] Conditions for consent

### Force Majeure
- [ ] Definition of force majeure events
- [ ] Notice requirements
- [ ] Suspension of obligations
- [ ] Termination right if extended period

### Entire Agreement
- [ ] Supersedes prior agreements
- [ ] Amendment process (written, signed)
- [ ] No oral modifications

### Severability
- [ ] Invalid provisions severed
- [ ] Remainder remains in effect

### Counterparts
- [ ] Electronic signatures valid
- [ ] Multiple counterparts constitute one agreement

### Relationship of Parties
- [ ] Independent contractors (not partnership, agency, etc.)
- [ ] No authority to bind other party
- [ ] Exceptions (if any)

---

## 15. Special Provisions for CEA Partnerships

### Food Safety and Quality
- [ ] Food safety certification requirements (GFSI, HACCP)
- [ ] Quality control protocols
- [ ] Traceability requirements
- [ ] Recall procedures and responsibilities
- [ ] Audit rights

### Regulatory Compliance
- [ ] FDA, USDA compliance
- [ ] Organic certification (if applicable)
- [ ] State/local regulations
- [ ] Labeling requirements
- [ ] Responsibility for compliance

### Sustainability and ESG
- [ ] Sustainability commitments
- [ ] Reporting requirements
- [ ] Certification requirements (B Corp, etc.)
- [ ] Carbon footprint tracking

### Supply Chain
- [ ] Forecasting and ordering process
- [ ] Minimum order quantities
- [ ] Lead times
- [ ] Inventory management
- [ ] Logistics and delivery terms
- [ ] Risk of loss

## Negotiation Tips

### Must-Haves (Non-Negotiable)
Identify your critical terms before negotiating:
- Deal-breakers
- Core economic terms
- Essential protections

### Nice-to-Haves (Negotiable)
Items you can trade:
- Extended payment terms
- Marketing support
- Exclusivity scope
- Governance participation

### Be Prepared to Walk Away
Know your BATNA and reservation point.

## Review and Execution Checklist

### Before Signing
- [ ] All terms match negotiated deal
- [ ] All blanks filled in
- [ ] All exhibits attached
- [ ] No handwritten changes (or properly initialed)
- [ ] Legal review completed
- [ ] Executive approval obtained
- [ ] Board approval (if required)

### Execution
- [ ] All parties sign
- [ ] Dates included
- [ ] Notarization (if required)
- [ ] Copies distributed to all parties
- [ ] Filed/stored properly

### Post-Execution
- [ ] Implementation plan activated
- [ ] Key stakeholders notified
- [ ] Systems updated (CRM, finance, etc.)
- [ ] Initial meetings scheduled
- [ ] Contract management system updated

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

**Vague Terms:**
- "Best efforts" without definition
- "Reasonable" without standards
- "As needed" without parameters

**One-Sided Terms:**
- All risk on one party
- Unilateral termination rights
- Excessive indemnification

**Missing Provisions:**
- No dispute resolution
- Unclear IP ownership
- No termination rights
- Vague performance standards

**Unreasonable Terms:**
- Perpetual non-compete
- Unlimited liability
- Unilateral price changes
- Indefinite exclusivity without performance requirements
