## Preparation Phase

### Pre-Negotiation Checklist

**Know Your Position:**
- [ ] BATNA defined and realistic
- [ ] Reservation price/walkaway point
- [ ] Target outcome (aspiration)
- [ ] Value creation opportunities identified
- [ ] Authority and approval limits understood

**Know Their Position:**
- [ ] Likely interests and priorities
- [ ] Possible BATNA
- [ ] Decision-making process
- [ ] Key stakeholders and influencers
- [ ] Constraints and pressures

**Know the Deal:**
- [ ] Issues to negotiate ranked by importance
- [ ] Trade-off analysis completed
- [ ] Package options prepared
- [ ] Objective criteria identified
- [ ] Draft terms prepared

## Opening Moves

### Anchoring Strategy
**When to Anchor First:**
- You have strong information advantage
- Market has clear benchmarks
- You want to frame the discussion

**Anchoring Technique:**
- Start ambitious but defensible
- Provide rational justification
- Leave room for concessions
- Watch for reaction

**Counter-Anchoring:**
- Don't immediately accept their frame
- Reframe with objective criteria
- Introduce alternative metrics
- Propose package deals

### Creating Collaborative Tone
**Opening Statements:**
- Express mutual benefit goal
- Acknowledge their interests
- Propose process and ground rules
- Build rapport authentically

## Core Tactics

### Value Creation Tactics

**1. Logrolling (Trading Issues)**
- Identify issues where you value differently
- Trade what's cheap for you but valuable to them
- Example: "We'll extend payment terms if you commit to volume"

**2. Contingent Agreements**
- Address uncertainty with conditional terms
- Share risk based on outcomes
- Example: "Lower base price with performance bonuses"

**3. Post-Settlement Settlements**
- Reach agreement then explore improvements
- Reduces pressure, enables creativity
- "We have a deal, but let's see if we can improve it for both sides"

**4. Expand the Pie**
- Bring in additional resources or opportunities
- Find creative solutions beyond original scope
- Example: "What if we included marketing co-op funds?"

### Value Claiming Tactics

**5. Anchoring and Adjustment**
- Set initial reference point
- Make them adjust toward you
- Justify with objective criteria

**6. Making Concessions Strategically**
- Concede slowly and diminishingly
- Extract something for each concession
- Signal approaching your limit
- Example: "This is really stretching us..."

**7. Good Cop/Bad Cop**
- One negotiator tough, one reasonable
- Creates relief when "good cop" intervenes
- Use sparingly; can backfire if transparent

**8. Deadline Pressure**
- Real deadlines create urgency
- Artificial deadlines often backfire
- Be prepared to walk away

### Information Tactics

**9. Active Listening and Probing**
- Ask open-ended questions
- Listen for interests behind positions
- Confirm understanding
- "Help me understand why that's important to you..."

**10. Strategic Disclosure**
- Share information that strengthens your position
- Withhold information that weakens it
- Test reactions with trial balloons
- Build trust selectively

**11. Silence**
- Don't fill every pause
- Let them elaborate
- Creates pressure to offer more
- Shows confidence

## Specific Situations

### When They Make Extreme Demand
**Don't:**
- Get angry or walk out immediately
- Accept or counter with equal extreme

**Do:**
- Stay calm and professional
- Ask for justification
- Reframe with objective criteria
- Make clear what's realistic
- "That's outside market norms. Let's look at comparable deals..."

### When Negotiations Stall
**Diagnose the Block:**
- Lack of authority?
- Missing information?
- Process issue?
- Relationship problem?
- Substantive impasse?

**Solutions:**
- Take a break
- Bring in fresh perspectives
- Park difficult issues temporarily
- Build momentum on easier points
- Escalate if appropriate

### When They Say "Take It or Leave It"
**Assess:**
- Is it really final?
- What's their BATNA?
- Can you improve your BATNA?

**Respond:**
- Test it: "If we could address X, is there flexibility?"
- Reframe: "I understand that's your position on price. Let's discuss terms..."
- Walk away if truly unacceptable

### When You Need to Say No
**Deliver Clearly but Diplomatically:**
- "I understand that's important to you..."
- "Unfortunately, we can't go there because..."
- "What we can do is..."
- "If circumstances changed (specify how), we could revisit..."

## Deal Structure Options

### Pricing Models

**1. Fixed Price**
- Simple and predictable
- Works when costs are known
- Clear and easy to administer

**2. Cost-Plus**
- Transparent cost basis
- Plus agreed margin/markup
- Suitable when costs uncertain

**3. Volume-Based Pricing**
- Tiered pricing by volume
- Incentivizes larger commitments
- Common in supply agreements

**4. Value-Based/Performance-Based**
- Tied to value delivered
- Shares risk and reward
- Requires measurable metrics

**5. Hybrid Models**
- Base fee + variable component
- Minimum guarantees + upside
- Balances predictability and flexibility

### Payment Terms

**Standard Terms:**
- Net 30, 60, 90
- 50% upfront, 50% on delivery
- Monthly/quarterly billing

**Strategic Terms:**
- Longer terms = financing for buyer
- Shorter terms = working capital for seller
- Progress payments for large projects
- Milestone-based payments

### Risk Allocation

**Common Risk Issues:**
- Performance risk (what if it doesn't work?)
- Volume risk (what if demand varies?)
- Market risk (what if market changes?)
- Regulatory risk (what if regulations change?)

**Risk Allocation Tools:**
- Warranties and guarantees
- Indemnification clauses
- Insurance requirements
- Force majeure provisions
- Termination rights and conditions

### Exclusivity Provisions

**Types:**
- Geographic exclusivity
- Product/service exclusivity
- Customer/vertical exclusivity
- Time-limited exclusivity

**Considerations:**
- Exclusivity is valuable - demand something in return
- Shorter terms preferable (renewable)
- Performance requirements (lose exclusivity if not met)
- Clearly defined scope

## Closing Techniques

### Trial Close
"If we could agree on X, are we ready to move forward?"

### Summary Close
Recap benefits and agreement points, then ask for commitment.

### Assumptive Close
Act as if agreement is settled, move to implementation details.

### Alternative Choice Close
"Would you prefer option A or option B?"

### Urgency Close
"This pricing is available if we can finalize by month-end."

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Strategic Mistakes:**
- [ ] Not preparing BATNA
- [ ] Negotiating without authority
- [ ] Focusing only on price
- [ ] Accepting first offer
- [ ] Making unilateral concessions

**Tactical Mistakes:**
- [ ] Revealing walkaway point
- [ ] Getting emotional
- [ ] Lying or misleading
- [ ] Rushing to close
- [ ] Not documenting agreements

**Relationship Mistakes:**
- [ ] Burning bridges
- [ ] Extracting all value (leaving nothing for them)
- [ ] Being inflexible
- [ ] Ignoring their constraints
- [ ] Failing to follow through

## Negotiation Ethics

### Never Do:
- Outright lie about facts
- Make commitments you can't keep
- Mislead about authority
- Create false competition
- Exploit personal information

### Gray Areas (Use Judgment):
- Bluffing about reservation price
- Exaggerating enthusiasm
- Claiming "best and final" (when not)
- Strategic ambiguity
- Selective disclosure

### Always Do:
- Honor commitments
- Be honest about deal-breakers
- Disclose material facts
- Respect confidentiality
- Treat counterparties professionally

## Quick Decision Matrix

### Should You Make the First Offer?

**YES if:**
- Strong information advantage
- Clear market benchmarks
- Want to anchor discussion
- Confident in your position

**NO if:**
- Significant information gaps
- Uncertain market value
- Want to understand their position first
- Risk of anchoring too low

### Should You Accept Their First Offer?

**Almost Never**
- Even if acceptable, negotiate to:
  - Ensure you're not leaving value
  - Establish credibility
  - Build relationship through exchange
  - Understand priorities

**Exception:**
- Exploding offer with strong rationale
- Need to move extremely fast
- Offer exceeds your aspiration significantly

## Negotiation Styles

**Competing (Win-Lose):**
- When: One-time transaction, zero-sum
- Risk: Damages relationship

**Collaborating (Win-Win):**
- When: Ongoing relationship, value creation possible
- Best for: Strategic partnerships

**Compromising (Split the Difference):**
- When: Time pressure, equal power
- Risk: Suboptimal for both

**Accommodating (Lose-Win):**
- When: Relationship more important than deal
- Risk: Being taken advantage of

**Avoiding (Lose-Lose):**
- When: BATNA better than any possible deal
- Right move: Walk away

**Best Practice**: Default to Collaborating, use others situationally
