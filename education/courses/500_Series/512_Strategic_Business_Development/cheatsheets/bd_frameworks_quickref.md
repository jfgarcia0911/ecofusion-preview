## Strategic Frameworks

### Three Horizons of Growth
- **Horizon 1 (0-1 year)**: Defend and extend core business
- **Horizon 2 (1-3 years)**: Build emerging businesses
- **Horizon 3 (3-5 years)**: Create transformational opportunities

### Ansoff Growth Matrix
| | Existing Products | New Products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing Markets** | Market Penetration | Product Development |
| **New Markets** | Market Development | Diversification |

### Porter's Five Forces
1. Competitive Rivalry (HIGH in CEA)
2. Threat of New Entrants (MEDIUM)
3. Bargaining Power of Suppliers (MEDIUM)
4. Bargaining Power of Buyers (HIGH)
5. Threat of Substitutes (HIGH)

### Porter's Generic Strategies
- **Cost Leadership**: Lowest cost producer
- **Differentiation**: Unique value, premium price
- **Focus**: Serve specific niche exceptionally well

## Market Intelligence

### Market Sizing Formula
```
TAM (Total Addressable Market)
  ↓
SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market) = TAM × % we can serve
  ↓
SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market) = SAM × realistic market share
```

### Research Methods
- **Primary**: Interviews, surveys, focus groups, observation
- **Secondary**: Industry reports, databases, publications, public data

### Customer Segmentation Dimensions
1. Demographic (size, industry, location)
2. Psychographic (values, risk tolerance)
3. Behavioral (purchase patterns, decision process)
4. Needs-based (pain points, jobs-to-be-done)

## Partnership Development

### Partnership Types
1. **Supply Chain**: Retailers, distributors, logistics
2. **Technology**: Equipment, software, automation
3. **R&D**: Universities, research institutions
4. **Co-Marketing**: Brand partnerships, distribution
5. **Financial**: Investors, joint ventures

### Partnership Structures
- **Supply Agreement**: Buyer-seller relationship
- **Strategic Alliance**: Collaborative, independent entities
- **Joint Venture**: Separate legal entity, shared ownership
- **Licensing**: IP usage rights for fees
- **Equity Investment**: Strategic investor takes stake

### Partnership Qualification Criteria
- Strategic fit and alignment
- Complementary capabilities
- Cultural compatibility
- Resource commitment
- Risk level acceptable

## Strategic Selling

### MEDDPICC Framework
- **M**etrics: Quantifiable value
- **E**conomic Buyer: Budget authority
- **D**ecision Criteria: How they evaluate
- **D**ecision Process: Steps and timeline
- **P**aper Process: Legal/procurement
- **I**dentify Pain: Critical problems
- **C**hampion: Internal advocate
- **C**ompetition: Alternatives considered

### Value-Based Selling
**ROI Formula:**
```
ROI = (Gain - Investment) / Investment × 100%

Target: >300% ROI (3:1 return)
```

### Solution Selling Process
1. Discovery (understand problems)
2. Diagnose (identify root causes)
3. Design (custom solution)
4. Deliver (implement)
5. Document (prove value)

## Negotiation

### Principled Negotiation (Getting to Yes)
1. **Separate** people from problem
2. Focus on **interests**, not positions
3. Generate **options** for mutual gain
4. Use **objective criteria**

### BATNA
**Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement**
- Your walkaway power
- Improves your negotiating position
- Must be realistic and attractive

### Deal Structure Components
- Value sharing (fixed, variable, revenue-share)
- Risk allocation (who bears what risks)
- Control and governance
- Exclusivity provisions
- IP and confidentiality
- Term and termination

## Metrics & KPIs

### Core BD Metrics
**Pipeline:**
- Pipeline value
- Pipeline velocity
- Conversion rates by stage
- Win rate

**Financial:**
- Revenue (bookings vs. revenue)
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
- LTV (Lifetime Value)
- LTV:CAC ratio (target >3:1)

**Activity:**
- Qualified leads generated
- Meetings/demos conducted
- Proposals submitted
- Deals closed

### Performance Targets
- Win rate: 25-40% (complex B2B)
- Sales cycle: 90-180 days (average)
- Average deal size: Growing year-over-year
- Revenue attainment: 100%+ of quota

## Quick Decision Trees

### Build, Buy, or Partner?
**Build** if:
- Core competency
- Competitive advantage
- Long-term control needed

**Buy** if:
- Speed critical
- Proven acquisition target
- Financial capacity available

**Partner** if:
- Complementary capabilities
- Shared risk desirable
- Faster/cheaper than build/buy

### Direct vs. Indirect Channel?
**Direct** if:
- Complex solutions
- High touch required
- Premium positioning
- Limited target accounts

**Indirect** if:
- Standard offerings
- Broad market reach needed
- Limited sales resources
- Proven channel partners

## Red Flags to Watch

**Partnerships:**
- Misaligned objectives or values
- Cultural incompatibility
- One-sided value creation
- Poor communication
- Lack of executive sponsorship

**Sales:**
- No economic buyer identified
- No champion or coach
- Unclear decision process
- Budget not confirmed
- Timeline keeps slipping

**Accounts:**
- Declining health scores
- Executive relationships weakening
- Competitive activity increasing
- Payment delays
- Reduced engagement
