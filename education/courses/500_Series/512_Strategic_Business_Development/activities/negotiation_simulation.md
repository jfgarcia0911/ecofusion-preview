# Activity: Multi-Party Negotiation Simulation

## Course 512: Strategic Business Development

---

## Simulation Overview

**Objective**: Apply negotiation strategies and tactics in a realistic multi-party business development scenario

**Duration**: 3 hours (2-hour preparation + 1-hour negotiation + debrief)

**Format**: Role-play simulation with 6-8 participants per negotiation

**Weight**: 15% of final grade

---

## Scenario: CEA Supply Agreement Negotiation

### Background

**Date**: Present day
**Location**: Virtual negotiation (simulating COVID-era reality)

Three parties are negotiating a strategic supply agreement for locally-grown produce:

1. **FreshGrow Vertical Farms** - Regional vertical farming company
2. **GreenGrocer Market** - Mid-size grocery chain (30 stores)
3. **SustainPack Solutions** - Sustainable packaging provider

The parties have been in preliminary discussions and now meet to negotiate a comprehensive three-way agreement.

---

## The Parties

### Party 1: FreshGrow Vertical Farms

**Company Profile:**
- 2 vertical farms in the region
- 150,000 sq ft total growing space
- Production: Leafy greens, herbs, microgreens
- Current capacity: 50,000 lbs/week
- Expansion possible: Up to 100,000 lbs/week with $5M investment
- Revenue: $8M annually
- Profitable at current scale
- Looking for growth capital and market access

**Key People:**
- **CEO**: Sarah Martinez (visionary, sustainability-focused)
- **CFO**: David Chen (financially conservative, ROI-focused)

**Objectives:**
1. Secure long-term supply agreement with GreenGrocer (3-5 years)
2. Achieve $3.50/lb average price (current market: $3.00-4.00/lb)
3. Minimum commitment of 30,000 lbs/week to justify expansion
4. Co-marketing support from GreenGrocer
5. Sustainable packaging (aligns with brand values)

**Constraints:**
- Need 6-month lead time for expansion
- Cash flow tight until expansion funded
- Can't go below $3.00/lb and remain profitable
- Want to avoid exclusivity if possible (other opportunities)

**BATNA:**
- Continue current small-scale supply to 5 smaller retailers
- Projected revenue: $9M next year (slow growth)
- Less risky but limited growth

---

### Party 2: GreenGrocer Market

**Company Profile:**
- 30-store grocery chain (regional)
- Revenue: $250M annually
- Positioning: "Fresh, Local, Sustainable"
- Produce is 18% of revenue, 25% of foot traffic driver
- Competitive pressure from national chains and online
- Customer base: Health-conscious, willing to pay premium for quality/local

**Key People:**
- **VP Procurement**: Janet Williams (deal-focused, practical)
- **Director of Sustainability**: Marcus Johnson (mission-driven, brand guardian)

**Objectives:**
1. Secure reliable local supply for differentiation
2. Achieve 25% gross margin on produce (requires buying at $2.80/lb or less)
3. Exclusive local supply in region (competitive advantage)
4. Sustainable packaging (customer and brand expectation)
5. Flexibility in volume as demand varies seasonally

**Constraints:**
- Budget pressure from competition
- Can't exceed $3.00/lb average (price point is $4.99 retail)
- Customers expect visible sustainability efforts
- Need supply within 3 months (competitive pressure)

**BATNA:**
- Continue with current mix of traditional suppliers
- Some local from small farms (inconsistent quality)
- California organic (not truly local, higher transport cost)
- Projected: Slower growth, continued margin pressure

---

### Party 3: SustainPack Solutions

**Company Profile:**
- Sustainable packaging manufacturer
- Compostable and recyclable produce packaging
- Revenue: $15M annually
- Premium pricing (25-40% over conventional)
- Growing market but still niche
- Need scale to reduce costs and compete

**Key People:**
- **Business Development Manager**: Alex Rivera (growth-focused, creative)
- **Operations Manager**: Kim Park (cost-conscious, practical)

**Objectives:**
1. Sign 3-year agreement with guaranteed volume
2. Achieve $0.40/unit average price (cost: $0.28/unit)
3. Co-branding opportunity (showcase sustainability)
4. Potential for geographic expansion with GreenGrocer
5. Case study and reference customer

**Constraints:**
- Need minimum 25,000 units/week to justify dedicated production
- Current capacity: 50,000 units/week (can expand)
- Price floor: $0.32/unit (must cover costs + minimum margin)
- Competitive pressure from conventional packaging

**BATNA:**
- Continue current small customer base
- Slower growth
- Difficulty achieving scale for cost reduction
- Projected: $16M revenue next year (7% growth)

---

## The Opportunity

### Potential Three-Way Agreement

**Structure:**
FreshGrow supplies produce to GreenGrocer, packaged in SustainPack packaging, with:
- Long-term commitments (3-5 years)
- Volume commitments providing scale for all
- Co-marketing highlighting local + sustainable story
- Shared investment in market development

**Potential Benefits:**
- **FreshGrow**: Guaranteed market, expansion justification, brand alignment
- **GreenGrocer**: Differentiated local/sustainable offering, customer loyalty, competitive advantage
- **SustainPack**: Scale, showcase customer, geographic expansion potential

**Challenges to Negotiate:**
- Pricing that works for all three parties
- Volume commitments and flexibility
- Exclusivity provisions
- Cost/benefit of sustainable packaging
- Co-marketing investment and approach
- Risk sharing for expansion
- Term and termination provisions

---

## Negotiation Issues and Ranges

### Issue 1: Produce Pricing (FreshGrow to GreenGrocer)

**FreshGrow's Position:**
- Target: $3.50/lb
- Minimum: $3.00/lb
- Justification: Cost of production, expansion investment, quality premium

**GreenGrocer's Position:**
- Target: $2.80/lb
- Maximum: $3.00/lb
- Justification: Margin requirements, competitive pricing, volume commitment

**Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA):** $3.00/lb (barely)

**Creative Options:**
- Volume-based pricing tiers
- Seasonal adjustments
- Co-marketing credit against price
- Performance bonuses for quality/delivery
- Risk-sharing on expansion investment

---

### Issue 2: Packaging Pricing (SustainPack to FreshGrow/GreenGrocer)

**SustainPack's Position:**
- Target: $0.40/unit
- Minimum: $0.32/unit
- Justification: Premium product, environmental benefits, smaller scale

**Buyer's Position (who pays?):**
- FreshGrow: Wants GreenGrocer to pay (or split)
- GreenGrocer: Wants sustainable packaging but doesn't want cost premium
- Target: $0.30/unit (comparable to conventional)

**Zone of Possible Agreement:** Need to find creative solution (narrow or no ZOPA)

**Creative Options:**
- Three-way cost sharing
- GreenGrocer pays premium, gets co-branding value
- Volume commitments to lower SustainPack's unit costs
- Phase in over time (scale-based pricing)
- Consumer pricing premium to fund

---

### Issue 3: Volume Commitments

**FreshGrow Needs:** 30,000 lbs/week minimum to justify expansion

**GreenGrocer Needs:** Flexibility (seasonal demand varies 20,000-50,000 lbs/week)

**SustainPack Needs:** 25,000 units/week minimum (roughly equivalent to 25,000 lbs)

**Negotiation Points:**
- Minimum commitments vs. flexibility
- Forecasting and planning process
- Penalties for under-commitment?
- How to handle seasonality?
- Excess capacity utilization?

**Creative Options:**
- Graduated commitments (year 1 lower, increasing)
- Quarterly minimums with flexibility within
- Other customers to absorb capacity (no exclusivity)
- Inventory management approaches
- Financial penalties vs. operational solutions

---

### Issue 4: Exclusivity

**GreenGrocer Wants:** Exclusive local supplier in region (competitive advantage)

**FreshGrow Position:** Prefers non-exclusive (other opportunities, risk mitigation)

**SustainPack Position:** Exclusive to GreenGrocer in region OK (not preventing other regions/customers)

**Negotiation Points:**
- Geographic scope of exclusivity
- Product/category scope
- Duration of exclusivity
- Performance requirements to maintain
- Compensation for exclusivity

**Creative Options:**
- Limited exclusivity (certain products exclusive, others not)
- Exclusivity with performance requirements
- Right of first refusal rather than exclusivity
- Financial compensation for exclusivity
- Phase: Non-exclusive year 1, exclusive thereafter

---

### Issue 5: Co-Marketing Investment

**All Parties Want:** Successful market launch and consumer awareness

**Questions:**
- Who invests how much?
- What activities?
- Who controls messaging and brand?
- How to measure success?

**Potential Investments:**
- In-store displays and signage
- Consumer education
- Digital marketing
- PR and media
- Events and demos

**Creative Options:**
- Proportional investment based on benefit
- GreenGrocer provides space/access, others provide materials
- Shared fund with governance
- Performance-based additional investment
- Phased approach (test and scale)

---

### Issue 6: Term and Termination

**Alignment:** All want long enough to justify investment

**FreshGrow:** 5 years minimum (expansion investment payback)

**GreenGrocer:** 3 years (flexibility, prove model)

**SustainPack:** 3-5 years (production investment, scale achievement)

**Negotiation Points:**
- Initial term length
- Renewal provisions
- Termination conditions and notice
- Exit provisions if performance fails
- Buyout or transition provisions

## Simulation Structure

### Pre-Negotiation (Week before, 2 hours)

**Individual/Team Preparation:**
1. Read and understand your role thoroughly
2. Develop negotiation strategy:
   - Prioritize your objectives
   - Determine BATNA and reservation points
   - Identify potential trades and creative options
   - Plan opening moves and tactics
3. Prepare for your role play:
   - Research your character
   - Prepare negotiation materials (spreadsheets, proposals, etc.)
   - Coordinate with your partner (if two people per party)

**Submit Pre-Negotiation Plan** (one page):
- Your key objectives (prioritized)
- Your BATNA and reservation points
- Your opening strategy
- Potential creative options you'll propose

---

### Negotiation Session (1.5 hours)

**Opening (15 minutes):**
- Introductions and relationship building
- Agenda setting
- Ground rules
- Opening statements from each party

**Negotiation (60 minutes):**
- Issue-by-issue or package negotiation
- Bilateral and trilateral discussions
- Break-out sessions allowed
- Document emerging agreements

**Closing (15 minutes):**
- Summarize agreements reached
- Document open issues
- Determine next steps (if time expires)
- OR: Close with handshake/signature

**Observers:** Other class members observe and take notes

---

### Debrief (30 minutes)

**Immediate Debrief:**
- Each party shares their perspective
- What worked well?
- What was challenging?
- What would you do differently?

**Observer Feedback:**
- Effective tactics observed
- Missed opportunities
- Interesting dynamics

**Instructor Analysis:**
- Negotiation principles demonstrated
- Learning points
- Connection to course concepts
- Real-world applications

**Written Reflection** (due 48 hours later):
- 2-3 page analysis of the negotiation
- Your performance and learnings
- Application of course concepts
- What you'll do differently in future negotiations

## Negotiation Tips

### Preparation is Key
- Know your priorities
- Understand your BATNA
- Research the other parties
- Prepare creative options

### Build Relationships
- Start with rapport building
- Find common ground
- Demonstrate respect
- Create collaborative tone

### Listen Actively
- Ask questions
- Understand their interests
- Look for creative trades
- Validate their concerns

### Think Value Creation
- Not just value claiming
- Look for win-win options
- Bundle issues for trades
- Think long-term relationship

### Stay Calm and Professional
- Don't get emotional
- Take breaks if needed
- Maintain respectful tone
- Remember it's a simulation (learn)

### Document Agreements
- Clarify and confirm
- Write down key terms
- Avoid future misunderstandings
- Get verbal commitments

## Possible Outcomes

### Great Outcome (Three-Way Agreement)
All parties reach comprehensive agreement addressing all major issues with creative value creation. Everyone wins.

### Good Outcome (Partial Agreement)
Core agreement reached with some issues for future resolution. Foundation established for ongoing relationship.

### Impasse
No agreement reached. Parties walk away to pursue BATNAs. (This is OK - sometimes no deal is better than a bad deal)

### Two-Party Agreement
Two parties agree, third is left out. (Realistic outcome - not all potential deals come together)

## Variations and Advanced Options

**For experienced negotiators:**
- Add time pressure (deal must close today)
- Add competing offer (BATNA improvement mid-negotiation)
- Add external stakeholder (board member, investor calling)
- Add crisis (food safety issue, press inquiry)

**For beginners:**
- Provide more structured agenda
- Instructor facilitation during negotiation
- Pause for coaching and feedback
- Less time pressure

## Real-World Application

This simulation mirrors real strategic business development negotiations:
- Multiple parties with interdependencies
- Complex issues requiring creative solutions
- Value creation AND value claiming
- Long-term relationship orientation
- Strategic implications beyond immediate deal
