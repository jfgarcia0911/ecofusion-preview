# Business Development Glossary

## Course 512: Strategic Business Development

---

## A

**Account-Based Marketing (ABM)**
Strategic approach to marketing that focuses on specific target accounts rather than broad segments, coordinating personalized campaigns across multiple channels.

**Acquisition**
Purchase of one company by another, where the acquiring company obtains controlling interest.

**Addressable Market**
The portion of the total market that a company can realistically serve given its product, geography, and business model. See TAM, SAM, SOM.

**Advance Rate**
In financing, the percentage of collateral value that a lender will provide as a loan.

**Alliance**
Collaborative relationship between organizations that remain independent while pursuing shared objectives. See Strategic Alliance.

**Anchor (Anchoring)**
Negotiation tactic of setting an initial reference point that influences subsequent negotiations.

**Ansoff Matrix**
Strategic planning framework with four growth strategies: market penetration, market development, product development, and diversification.

**ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)**
Predictable and recurring revenue components of a subscription business, normalized for a single year.

---

## B

**B2B (Business-to-Business)**
Business model where a company sells products or services to other businesses rather than consumers.

**B2G (Business-to-Government)**
Business model focused on selling to government entities.

**BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement)**
Your best option if negotiations fail; knowing your BATNA strengthens your negotiating position.

**Benchmark**
Standard or point of reference for comparison, often used to measure performance against competitors or industry standards.

**Blue Ocean Strategy**
Strategy approach that creates uncontested market space rather than competing in existing markets (red oceans).

**Bookings**
Total value of contracts signed in a given period, regardless of when revenue is recognized.

**Breakeven**
Point at which total costs equal total revenue, resulting in neither profit nor loss.

**Business Case**
Document presenting the justification and expected return on investment for a proposed project or initiative.

**Business Development (BD)**
Function responsible for creating long-term value through customers, markets, and relationships; broader and more strategic than sales.

**Buying Center**
Group of individuals involved in a purchase decision, including users, influencers, buyers, decision-makers, and gatekeepers.

---

## C

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**
Total cost of acquiring a new customer, including sales and marketing expenses divided by number of customers acquired.

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)**
Year-over-year growth rate over a specified period, accounting for compounding.

**Capex (Capital Expenditure)**
Funds used to acquire or upgrade physical assets such as property, equipment, or technology.

**Category Management**
Retail strategy of managing product categories as individual business units, optimizing assortment, pricing, and merchandising.

**Champion**
Internal advocate within a customer organization who supports your solution and helps navigate the buying process.

**Channel**
Path through which products or services flow from producer to end customer; can be direct or indirect.

**Channel Conflict**
Disagreement or competition between different channels or channel partners, such as between direct sales and distributors.

**Churn (Churn Rate)**
Rate at which customers stop doing business with a company, typically expressed as percentage per year.

**Closing**
Final stage of a sales process where agreement is reached and contract is signed.

**Co-Marketing**
Collaborative marketing arrangement between two or more companies to promote products or services jointly.

**Competitive Intelligence**
Systematic gathering and analysis of information about competitors, markets, and industry trends.

**Consultative Selling**
Sales approach focused on understanding customer needs and providing tailored solutions rather than pushing products.

**Contribution Margin**
Revenue minus variable costs; the amount that contributes to covering fixed costs and generating profit.

**Conversion Rate**
Percentage of prospects that take a desired action, such as moving to the next stage in the sales funnel.

**Corporate Development**
Function responsible for M&A, strategic investments, joint ventures, and other corporate transactions.

**CRM (Customer Relationship Management)**
System for managing interactions with customers and prospects, tracking relationships, opportunities, and activities.

**Cross-Sell**
Selling additional, complementary products or services to existing customers.

---

## D

**Dashboard**
Visual display of key metrics and KPIs, providing real-time or periodic performance insights.

**Deal Desk**
Internal team that supports deal structuring, pricing, contract negotiation, and approval processes.

**Deal Registration**
Process where channel partners register opportunities to protect their deal flow and earn preferential treatment.

**Decision Criteria**
Factors a customer uses to evaluate and compare alternatives when making a purchase decision.

**Decision-Maker**
Individual with authority to approve a purchase and commit budget.

**Discovery**
Phase of sales process focused on understanding customer needs, challenges, and objectives through questioning and listening.

**Distributor**
Intermediary that purchases products from manufacturers and resells to retailers or end customers.

**Diversification**
Growth strategy involving new products in new markets; highest risk strategy in Ansoff Matrix.

**Due Diligence**
Comprehensive investigation and analysis of a company or opportunity before committing to a transaction or partnership.

---

## E

**EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization)**
Measure of operating profitability before accounting for capital structure, taxes, and non-cash charges.

**Economic Buyer**
Individual who controls budget and has authority to spend money; may differ from decision-maker or user.

**Ecosystem**
Network of organizations, including partners, suppliers, competitors, and customers, that interact and influence each other.

**Enablement**
Process of providing salespeople or partners with content, tools, training, and support to sell effectively.

**Enterprise Value (EV)**
Total value of a company, calculated as market capitalization plus debt minus cash.

**Exclusivity**
Agreement limiting a party's ability to work with others, such as exclusive distribution rights in a territory.

**Executive Sponsor**
Senior leader who champions a strategic initiative, partnership, or key account relationship.

---

## F

**Five Forces**
Michael Porter's framework for analyzing competitive intensity: rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, supplier power, buyer power.

**Flywheel**
Business model concept where initial success generates momentum that fuels further growth in a self-reinforcing cycle.

**Force Majeure**
Contract clause that frees parties from liability due to extraordinary events beyond their control.

**Forecast**
Prediction of future revenue based on pipeline analysis, historical trends, and market conditions.

**Four Actions Framework**
Blue Ocean Strategy tool examining what to eliminate, reduce, raise, and create compared to industry norms.

**Freemium**
Business model offering basic services free while charging for premium features.

---

## G

**Gatekeeper**
Person who controls access to decision-makers or information, such as an executive assistant.

**Generic Strategies**
Porter's three broad strategies: cost leadership, differentiation, and focus.

**Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy**
Plan for delivering products or services to customers, including channels, pricing, and positioning.

**Gross Margin**
Revenue minus cost of goods sold, expressed as percentage of revenue; measures profitability before operating expenses.

**Growth Hacking**
Marketing approach focused on rapid experimentation and scalable growth tactics, common in startups.

---

## H

**Horizon Planning**
Strategic framework organizing initiatives into three time horizons: defend/extend core, build emerging, create transformational.

**Horizontal Integration**
Acquisition or merger with competitors at the same point in the value chain.

---

## I

**ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)**
Description of the type of company or customer that derives maximum value from your offering.

**Indemnification**
Contractual obligation for one party to compensate the other for certain losses or damages.

**Influencer**
Individual who shapes opinions and decisions but may not have formal authority; often technical experts or thought leaders.

**IP (Intellectual Property)**
Intangible assets such as patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets.

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return)**
Discount rate at which the net present value of an investment equals zero; measure of investment profitability.

---

## J

**Joint Venture (JV)**
Separate legal entity created by two or more parties for a specific purpose, with shared ownership and control.

---

## K

**KAM (Key Account Management)**
Strategic approach to managing most important customer relationships with dedicated resources and planning.

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**
Measurable value that indicates how effectively objectives are being achieved.

---

## L

**Land and Expand**
Sales strategy of securing initial entry with small footprint, then expanding within the account over time.

**Lead**
Potential customer who has expressed interest or been identified as fitting target criteria.

**Letter of Intent (LOI)**
Non-binding document outlining key terms of a proposed agreement, used to guide formal contract negotiations.

**Licensing**
Agreement allowing one party to use another's intellectual property in exchange for royalties or fees.

**Lifetime Value (LTV)**
Total revenue expected from a customer over the entire relationship duration.

**Logrolling**
Negotiation tactic of trading issues where parties value things differently.

**LTV:CAC Ratio**
Lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost; healthy ratio is typically 3:1 or higher.

---

## M

**M&A (Mergers and Acquisitions)**
Transactions involving consolidation of companies through mergers, acquisitions, or other corporate combinations.

**Market Development**
Growth strategy focused on entering new markets with existing products.

**Market Penetration**
Growth strategy focused on increasing sales of existing products in existing markets.

**Market Segmentation**
Dividing a market into distinct groups of customers with similar needs or characteristics.

**MDF (Market Development Funds)**
Budget provided to channel partners to support marketing activities.

**MEDDPICC**
Sales qualification framework: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition.

**MOU (Memorandum of Understanding)**
Document outlining terms and details of an agreement between parties, often preliminary to a formal contract.

**MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)**
Lead that marketing has deemed likely to become a customer based on engagement and fit criteria.

**MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)**
Predictable and recurring revenue components normalized for a single month.

---

## N

**Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**
Percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers, including expansions, minus churn and contraction.

**Non-Compete**
Agreement restricting a party from competing in certain markets or activities for a specified period.

**NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**
Legal contract establishing confidential relationship and protecting shared proprietary information.

**NPS (Net Promoter Score)**
Customer loyalty metric based on likelihood to recommend, calculated as % promoters minus % detractors.

---

## O

**Objection Handling**
Sales skill of addressing customer concerns or hesitations during the buying process.

**Opex (Operating Expenditure)**
Ongoing costs for running business operations, such as salaries, utilities, and rent.

**Opportunity**
Qualified prospect with defined need, budget, and timeline; potential deal in the sales pipeline.

---

## P

**Pain Point**
Specific problem, challenge, or frustration that customers experience and seek to solve.

**Partner Ecosystem**
Network of organizations that collaborate to deliver value to end customers.

**Payback Period**
Time required to recover an investment through cash flows or savings it generates.

**Penetration Pricing**
Strategy of setting low initial prices to gain market share quickly.

**Pipeline**
Collection of all active sales opportunities at various stages, representing potential future revenue.

**Pipeline Velocity**
Speed at which opportunities move through the pipeline; calculated as (# opportunities × average deal size × win rate) / average sales cycle length.

**Pitch Deck**
Presentation used to communicate value proposition and opportunity to prospects, partners, or investors.

**P&L (Profit & Loss Statement)**
Financial statement summarizing revenues, costs, and expenses during a specific period.

**POC (Proof of Concept)**
Demonstration or pilot project to verify that an idea or solution works in practice.

**Porter's Five Forces**
See Five Forces.

**Positioning**
How you differentiate your offering in customers' minds relative to competitors.

**Post-Settlement Settlement**
Negotiation tactic of reaching agreement then exploring further improvements.

**Product-Market Fit**
Stage where a product satisfies strong market demand; customers actively seek and adopt the product.

**Proof Point**
Evidence or example demonstrating claims, such as case studies, testimonials, or metrics.

---

## Q

**QBR (Quarterly Business Review)**
Regular strategic meeting with key accounts to review performance, discuss trends, and plan future initiatives.

**Qualified Lead**
Prospect who meets defined criteria for budget, authority, need, and timeline (BANT) or similar qualification framework.

**Quota**
Revenue or unit target assigned to a salesperson or team for a specific period.

---

## R

**Red Ocean**
Existing market space with intense competition, contrasted with uncontested blue oceans.

**Referral**
Introduction to a potential customer provided by an existing customer or contact.

**RFI (Request for Information)**
Preliminary document from buyer seeking general information about products or services.

**RFP (Request for Proposal)**
Formal document from buyer soliciting detailed proposals from potential suppliers.

**RFQ (Request for Quote)**
Document from buyer requesting price quotes for specific products or services.

**ROI (Return on Investment)**
Measure of profitability calculated as (gain minus investment) divided by investment, expressed as percentage.

**Royalty**
Payment made by licensee to licensor based on usage, sales, or other agreed metrics.

---

## S

**SaaS (Software as a Service)**
Business model delivering software via subscription rather than perpetual licenses.

**Sales Cycle**
Time from initial contact with a prospect to closed deal; also refers to the process itself.

**Sales Enablement**
Function providing sales teams with content, tools, training, and processes to sell more effectively.

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**
Portion of total addressable market that your company can serve given current business model and constraints.

**Scope Creep**
Uncontrolled expansion of project scope without corresponding adjustments to time, costs, or resources.

**Segmentation**
See Market Segmentation.

**SLA (Service Level Agreement)**
Contract defining expected service levels, often including metrics, monitoring, and penalties for non-performance.

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**
Portion of SAM that you can realistically capture; your target market share.

**Solution Selling**
Sales methodology focused on understanding customer problems and designing customized solutions.

**SPIF (Sales Performance Incentive Fund)**
Short-term incentive program to motivate specific sales behaviors or outcomes.

**Stakeholder**
Individual or group with interest in or influence over a decision or initiative.

**Strategic Account**
Customer of highest strategic value receiving dedicated resources and relationship management.

**Strategic Alliance**
See Alliance.

**SWOT Analysis**
Strategic planning tool examining Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.

---

## T

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**
Total revenue opportunity available if 100% market share were achieved, without constraints.

**Target Account**
Specific company identified as priority prospect fitting ideal customer profile.

**Territory**
Geographic area, industry segment, or account set assigned to a salesperson or partner.

**Term Sheet**
Summary of key terms and conditions for a proposed agreement, used as basis for formal contract.

**Three Horizons**
See Horizon Planning.

**Touchpoint**
Point of interaction between company and customer, such as website visit, email, call, or meeting.

**Trade-off**
Choosing between competing alternatives; fundamental to strategy where choices must be made.

---

## U

**Upsell**
Selling higher-value version or larger quantity to existing customer.

**Use Case**
Specific situation or scenario where product or service is applied to solve a problem or achieve an outcome.

---

## V

**Value Chain**
Set of activities a company performs to deliver valuable products or services to customers.

**Value Proposition**
Clear statement of tangible benefits customers receive from your offering, and why it's superior to alternatives.

**Value-Based Pricing**
Pricing strategy based on customer's perceived value rather than costs or competition.

**Value-Based Selling**
Sales approach quantifying and demonstrating ROI and business value rather than features or price.

**Vertical Integration**
Company expansion into different stages of the value chain, such as a manufacturer acquiring a distributor.

**VUCA**
Acronym describing business environment: Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, Ambiguity.

---

## W

**Walkaway**
Point at which negotiation terms are no longer acceptable; related to BATNA and reservation price.

**Waterfall**
Chart visualizing how pipeline opportunities progress through stages and convert (or are lost) over time.

**Weighted Pipeline**
Pipeline value adjusted by probability of closing based on stage; used for more accurate forecasting.

**Win Rate**
Percentage of opportunities that result in closed-won deals; key metric for sales effectiveness.

**Win-Loss Analysis**
Systematic review of won and lost deals to understand factors driving outcomes and improve performance.

---

## X

**X-Factor**
Unique or unexpected element that differentiates an offering or provides competitive advantage.

---

## Y

**Yield**
Return or output generated by an investment, process, or activity.

---

## Z

**Zero-Sum Game**
Situation where one party's gain equals another's loss; total value is fixed. Contrasts with value creation where total value grows.

**ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)**
Range between each party's reservation prices where a negotiated agreement is possible; overlapping area of acceptable outcomes.

---
