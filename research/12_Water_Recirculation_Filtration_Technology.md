# Water Recirculation & Filtration Technology
## Advanced Water Management Systems for Controlled Environment Agriculture

---

## Executive Summary

Water is the lifeblood of aquaponics and hydroponic systems. Effective water recirculation and filtration technology enables EcoFusion to achieve extraordinary resource efficiency—using up to 95% less water than conventional agriculture while maintaining optimal growing conditions. This comprehensive research document explores the science, engineering, and operational practices required for world-class water management in controlled environment agriculture, from mechanical filtration to advanced biological treatment and emerging purification technologies.

## Part 2: Mechanical Filtration Systems

### 2.1 Principles of Mechanical Filtration

**Particle Removal Mechanisms**

```
MECHANICAL FILTRATION MECHANISMS

STRAINING (Surface capture)
├── Particles larger than pore/mesh size
├── Surface accumulation
├── Requires regular cleaning
└── Primary mechanism for most filters

DEPTH FILTRATION (Matrix capture)
├── Particles trapped throughout media
├── Higher capacity before bypass
├── Used in sand, bead filters
└── Gradual pressure increase

SETTLING/SEDIMENTATION
├── Gravity-driven particle removal
├── Dependent on particle density and size
├── Low energy, large footprint
└── Effective for heavy solids

CENTRIFUGAL SEPARATION
├── Rotational force separates particles
├── Continuous operation possible
├── Effective for dense particles
└── Higher energy consumption
```

## Part 3: Biological Filtration

### 3.1 Nitrification Process

**The Nitrogen Cycle**

```
NITRIFICATION IN AQUAPONIC SYSTEMS

FISH WASTE                                    PLANT UPTAKE
    │                                              ▲
    ▼                                              │
┌─────────┐         ┌─────────┐         ┌─────────┐
│ AMMONIA │────────→│ NITRITE │────────→│ NITRATE │
│  NH₃    │         │  NO₂⁻   │         │  NO₃⁻   │
│         │         │         │         │         │
│ TOXIC   │         │ TOXIC   │         │ LESS    │
│ to fish │         │ to fish │         │ TOXIC   │
└─────────┘         └─────────┘         └─────────┘
     │                   │                   │
     │                   │                   │
  Nitrosomonas       Nitrobacter/          Primary
  bacteria           Nitrospira            plant N
                     bacteria              source

ENVIRONMENTAL REQUIREMENTS:
- Temperature: 77-86°F optimal (25-30°C)
- pH: 7.0-8.0 optimal for bacteria
- Dissolved oxygen: >4 mg/L, preferably >6 mg/L
- Alkalinity: >50 mg/L CaCO₃ (consumed during nitrification)
- No inhibitors: chlorine, heavy metals, antibiotics
```

**Nitrification Chemistry**

| Reaction | Equation | Alkalinity Consumed |
|----------|----------|---------------------|
| Ammonia → Nitrite | NH₄⁺ + 1.5O₂ → NO₂⁻ + 2H⁺ + H₂O | 3.57 mg CaCO₃/mg NH₄⁺-N |
| Nitrite → Nitrate | NO₂⁻ + 0.5O₂ → NO₃⁻ | None |
| **Total** | NH₄⁺ + 2O₂ → NO₃⁻ + 2H⁺ + H₂O | **7.14 mg CaCO₃/mg NH₄⁺-N** |

### 3.3 Biofilter Sizing

**Biofilter Sizing Methodology**

```
BIOFILTER SIZING CALCULATION

Step 1: Determine ammonia production rate
─────────────────────────────────────────
TAN production (g/day) = Fish biomass (kg) × Feed rate (% body wt) ×
                         Feed N content (%) × 0.03 (conversion factor)

Example:
- 500 kg fish
- 2% feed rate (10 kg feed/day)
- 35% protein feed (5.6% N)
TAN = 500 × 0.02 × 0.056 × 0.03 × 1000 = 16.8 g TAN/day

Step 2: Calculate required surface area
─────────────────────────────────────────
Surface area (m²) = TAN production (g/day) / TAN removal rate (g/m²/day)

TAN removal rates (typical):
- Submerged media: 0.3-0.5 g/m²/day
- Trickling filter: 0.5-1.0 g/m²/day
- MBBR: 0.6-1.2 g/m²/day
- Fluidized bed: 1.0-3.0 g/m²/day

Example (MBBR at 0.8 g/m²/day):
Surface area = 16.8 / 0.8 = 21 m²

Step 3: Calculate media volume
─────────────────────────────────────────
Media volume = Surface area / Specific surface area of media

Example (K1 media at 500 m²/m³):
Volume = 21 / 500 = 0.042 m³ = 42 liters of media

Step 4: Size reactor vessel
─────────────────────────────────────────
Reactor volume = Media volume / Fill fraction

Example (50% fill):
Reactor = 0.042 / 0.50 = 0.084 m³ = 84 liters (22 gallons)

Add safety factor (1.5-2x) for growth and variability:
FINAL SIZE: 150-170 liters (40-45 gallons)
```

### 3.4 Denitrification

**Denitrification Process**

```
DENITRIFICATION PATHWAY

Nitrate → Nitrite → Nitric Oxide → Nitrous Oxide → Nitrogen Gas
NO₃⁻    → NO₂⁻    → NO           → N₂O           → N₂ ↑

CONDITIONS REQUIRED:
├── Anoxic (low/no dissolved oxygen): <0.5 mg/L O₂
├── Carbon source: Organic matter, methanol, etc.
├── Denitrifying bacteria: Pseudomonas, Paracoccus, etc.
├── pH: 6.5-8.5 (7.0-7.5 optimal)
└── Temperature: 50-95°F (higher = faster)

BENEFITS:
├── Nitrate removal (prevents accumulation)
├── Alkalinity recovery (50% of consumed)
└── Complete nitrogen cycle closure
```

## Part 6: Water Chemistry Management

### 6.1 pH Management

**pH Stabilization Strategies**

```
pH MANAGEMENT IN RECIRCULATING SYSTEMS

FACTORS AFFECTING pH:
─────────────────────
↓ pH Decreasing:
├── Nitrification (consumes alkalinity)
├── Fish respiration (CO₂ production)
├── Organic acid production
└── Acid nutrient additions

↑ pH Increasing:
├── Photosynthesis (CO₂ consumption)
├── Denitrification (produces alkalinity)
├── Degassing of CO₂
└── Basic nutrient additions

BUFFERING STRATEGIES:
─────────────────────
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                         │
│   PRIMARY: Maintain adequate alkalinity                 │
│   ├── Target: 50-150 mg/L as CaCO₃                     │
```

### 8.2 Troubleshooting Guide

**Common Water Quality Issues**

```
TROUBLESHOOTING DECISION TREE

HIGH AMMONIA
├── Check: Biofilter operation
│   └── Is air on? Media present? Not clogged?
├── Check: Overfeeding?
│   └── Reduce feed, remove uneaten food
├── Check: Dead fish?
│   └── Remove immediately
├── Check: Recent changes?
│   └── New fish, medication, cleaning?
└── Action: Reduce feed, increase aeration, partial water change

LOW DISSOLVED OXYGEN
├── Check: Aeration equipment
│   └── Blowers, diffusers working?
├── Check: Water temperature
│   └── High temp = low saturation
├── Check: Organic load
│   └── Excess waste consuming O₃
├── Check: Stocking density
│   └── Too many fish?
└── Action: Increase aeration, reduce feeding, partial water change

pH DROPPING
├── Check: Alkalinity level
│   └── <40 mg/L needs buffering
├── Check: Nitrification rate
│   └── High ammonia conversion = pH drop
├── Check: CO₂ levels
│   └── Poor degassing?
└── Action: Add buffer (CaCO₃, KOH), improve degassing

CLOUDY WATER
├── Check: Mechanical filtration
│   └── Clogged? Bypassing?
├── Check: Bacterial bloom
│   └── New system? Overfeeding?
├── Check: Algae
│   └── Light exposure? Nutrients?
└── Action: Improve filtration, reduce feed, UV treatment
```

### 8.3 Emergency Procedures

**Critical Failure Response**

| Emergency | Immediate Action | Secondary Action | Prevention |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Power failure | Activate backup generator | Manual aeration if needed | UPS, generator maintenance |
| Pump failure | Switch to backup pump | Diagnose primary pump | Redundant pumps, spares |
| Pipe burst | Shut isolation valves | Contain flooding | Regular inspection |
| High ammonia | Stop feeding, water change | Check biofilter | Monitoring, alarms |
| Low DO | Emergency aeration | Reduce biomass | DO monitoring, alarms |
| pH crash | Emergency buffer addition | Investigate cause | Alkalinity monitoring |
| Fish die-off | Remove dead fish, test water | Quarantine if disease | Biosecurity, monitoring |

## Conclusion

Water recirculation and filtration technology forms the backbone of sustainable controlled environment agriculture. EcoFusion's commitment to water stewardship—achieving <1 gallon per pound of produce versus 20-50 gallons for field agriculture—demonstrates that food production and environmental responsibility can go hand in hand.

Key principles for water system success:

1. **Redundancy**: Critical systems need backup
2. **Monitoring**: Continuous measurement enables proactive management
3. **Maintenance**: Preventive care prevents failures
4. **Integration**: Treatment components work together as a system
5. **Optimization**: Continuous improvement based on data

By implementing the technologies, designs, and operational practices outlined in this document, EcoFusion can maintain exceptional water quality while minimizing resource consumption and environmental impact—key differentiators in the sustainable food marketplace.
