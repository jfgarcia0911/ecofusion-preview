## 1. Introduction to Anaerobic Digestion <a name="introduction"></a>

### 1.1 Definition and Significance

Anaerobic digestion (AD) is a biological process in which microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas (primarily methane and carbon dioxide) and digestate (nutrient-rich residual material). This technology represents one of the most effective methods for:

- Converting organic waste into renewable energy
- Reducing greenhouse gas emissions
- Creating valuable soil amendments
- Closing the loop in sustainable agriculture systems

### 1.2 Historical Development

- **Ancient origins**: Chinese and Indian civilizations used covered pits for waste decomposition
- **1859**: First anaerobic digester in India for treating leper colony waste
- **1895**: First municipal AD plant in Exeter, UK
- **1970s-present**: Modern high-rate digesters, co-digestion techniques, biogas upgrading

### 1.3 EcoFusion Biodigestion Vision

The EcoFusion biodigestion system will transform organic waste streams from aquaponics operations, food processing, and restaurant activities into:

- **330,000+ cubic meters** of methane annually
- **800-1,000 tons** of high-quality compost
- **Thermal energy** for facility heating
- **Electrical power** for operational needs

## 2. Biochemistry of Methanogenesis <a name="biochemistry"></a>

### 2.1 The Four Stages of Anaerobic Digestion

#### Stage 1: Hydrolysis
Complex organic polymers are broken down into simpler monomers:

```
Carbohydrates → Sugars (glucose, xylose)
Proteins → Amino acids
Lipids → Long-chain fatty acids + glycerol
```

**Key Enzymes:**
- Cellulases, xylanases (carbohydrate breakdown)
- Proteases (protein breakdown)
- Lipases (fat breakdown)

**Rate-Limiting Factors:**
- Particle size of feedstock
- Temperature
- pH (optimal: 6.0-7.0)
- Presence of lignin (inhibitory)

#### Stage 2: Acidogenesis
Monomers are converted to volatile fatty acids (VFAs):

```
Sugars → Acetate, Propionate, Butyrate + H2 + CO2
Amino acids → VFAs + NH3 + H2S
Fatty acids → VFAs + H2
```

**Critical Parameters:**
- pH must remain above 5.0 to prevent acidification
- Hydrogen partial pressure affects product distribution
- Temperature influences bacterial growth rates

#### Stage 3: Acetogenesis
VFAs are converted to acetate, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide:

```
Propionate → Acetate + CO2 + H2
Butyrate → Acetate + H2
Ethanol → Acetate + H2
```

**Syntrophic Relationships:**
Acetogenic bacteria only function when hydrogen-consuming methanogens are active, creating obligate mutualism.

#### Stage 4: Methanogenesis
Final stage producing methane through two pathways:

**Aceticlastic Methanogenesis (70% of CH4):**
```
CH3COOH → CH4 + CO2
(Methanosaeta, Methanosarcina)
```

**Hydrogenotrophic Methanogenesis (30% of CH4):**
```
4H2 + CO2 → CH4 + 2H2O
(Methanobacterium, Methanobrevibacter)
```

### 2.2 Microbial Ecology

**Dominant Methanogenic Genera:**
| Genus | Pathway | Optimal pH | Optimal Temp |
|-------|---------|------------|--------------|
| Methanosaeta | Aceticlastic | 6.8-7.4 | 35-40°C |
| Methanosarcina | Both | 6.5-7.5 | 35-40°C |
| Methanobacterium | Hydrogenotrophic | 7.0-8.0 | 35-55°C |
| Methanothermobacter | Hydrogenotrophic | 7.0-8.0 | 55-65°C |

### 2.3 Inhibition Factors

| Inhibitor | Threshold | Source | Mitigation |
|-----------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Ammonia (NH3) | >1,500 mg/L | Protein-rich waste | Dilution, C:N adjustment |
| Hydrogen sulfide | >200 ppm | Sulfur compounds | Iron addition |
| Long-chain fatty acids | >1,000 mg/L | Fats and oils | Gradual loading |
| Heavy metals | Variable | Industrial waste | Source control |
| pH extremes | <6.0 or >8.5 | VFA accumulation | Buffering, reduced loading |
