## 2. The Science of Phytochemistry <a name="phytochemistry"></a>

### 2.1 Understanding Plant Secondary Metabolites

Plants produce thousands of secondary metabolites that provide flavor, aroma, color, and medicinal properties:

**Major Classes:**

| Class | Examples | Function | Herbs |
|-------|----------|----------|-------|
| Terpenoids | Menthol, limonene | Aroma, antimicrobial | Mint, basil |
| Phenolics | Rosmarinic acid | Antioxidant | Rosemary, oregano |
| Alkaloids | Caffeine, berberine | Stimulant, antimicrobial | Coffee, goldenseal |
| Glucosinolates | Sinigrin | Flavor, anticancer | Mustard, wasabi |
| Flavonoids | Quercetin, apigenin | Antioxidant | Chamomile, parsley |

### 2.2 Factors Affecting Phytochemical Content

**Light Intensity and Spectrum:**
- Higher light = more secondary metabolites (stress response)
- UV exposure increases phenolic compounds
- Red/blue LED ratios affect specific pathways
- Optimal PPFD: 400-600 μmol/m²/s for most herbs

**Temperature:**
- Moderate stress (temperature variations) can enhance oils
- Night temperature drops improve essential oil content
- Species-specific optima (see individual profiles)

**Nutrient Availability:**
- Slight nitrogen limitation increases essential oils
- Potassium enhances oil quality
- Phosphorus affects flowering and seed production

**Water Stress:**
- Controlled deficit increases oil concentration
- Aquaponics allows precise water management
- Timing of stress is critical (pre-harvest)

**Key Compounds in EcoFusion Herbs:**

## 3. High-Value Culinary Herbs <a name="culinary-herbs"></a>

### 3.1 Tier 1: Premium Culinary Herbs

#### 3.1.1 Genovese Basil (Ocimum basilicum)

**Cultivation Profile:**
| Parameter | Optimal Range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Temperature | 70-85°F |
| pH | 5.5-6.5 |
| EC | 1.0-1.6 mS/cm |
| Light | 14-16 hours, 400+ PPFD |
| Harvest Cycle | 21-28 days (cut-and-come) |

**Production Metrics:**
- Yield: 4-6 lbs/ft²/year
- Market price: $8-15/lb (conventional), $15-25/lb (premium living)

**Value-Added Products:**
- Fresh pesto (premium pricing)
- Basil-infused oils
- Dried basil flakes
- Basil essential oil (steam distillation)

#### 3.1.2 Cilantro/Coriander (Coriandrum sativum)

**Cultivation Challenges:**
- Bolts quickly in warm temperatures
- Requires succession planting every 2 weeks

| pH | 6.0-6.8 |
| EC | 1.2-1.8 mS/cm |

## 5. Aquaponic Herb Cultivation Optimization <a name="aquaponic-cultivation"></a>

### 5.1 System Selection by Herb Type

| Herb Category | Recommended System | Rationale |
|---------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Fast-growing (basil) | NFT or DWC | Rapid nutrient uptake |
| Perennial woody (rosemary) | Dutch bucket/media bed | Root structure support |
| Spreading (mint) | Contained NFT | Prevent takeover |
| Root crops (ginger) | Deep media bed | Room for rhizome expansion |
| Microgreens | Shallow trays with flood/drain | Quick turnover |

### 5.2 Nutrient Management for Herbs

**Aquaponics Nutrient Considerations:**

Unlike hydroponic systems where nutrients are precisely controlled, aquaponics relies on fish waste conversion:

**Supplementation Needs:**
| Nutrient | Typical Aquaponics Level | Herb Requirement | Supplement |
|----------|-------------------------|------------------|------------|
| Nitrogen | Adequate | Moderate | None needed |
| Phosphorus | Often adequate | Moderate | Occasional |
| Potassium | Often limiting | High (especially flowering) | Regular |
| Calcium | Variable | Moderate-High | As pH buffer |
| Iron | Limiting | Critical | Chelated Fe |

**Iron Supplementation Protocol:**
- Sequestrene 330 (DTPA chelate): 2 mg/L target
- Apply weekly based on testing
- Critical for preventing chlorosis

### 5.3 Environmental Optimization

**Temperature Zones:**
EcoFusion can establish distinct zones for different herb categories:

| Zone | Temperature | Humidity | Crops |
|------|-------------|----------|-------|
| Mediterranean | 70-80°F | 40-50% | Rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage |
| Tropical | 75-85°F | 60-70% | Basil varieties, lemongrass |
| Temperate | 60-70°F | 50-60% | Mint, cilantro, parsley |

### 5.4 Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

**Biological Controls:**
| Pest | Beneficial Organism | Application |
|------|---------------------|-------------|
| Aphids | Aphidius colemani | Preventive release |
| Thrips | Orius insidiosus | Reactive release |
| Fungus gnats | Stratiolaelaps scimitus | Soil drench |
| Spider mites | Phytoseiulus persimilis | Leaf application |
| Whitefly | Encarsia formosa | Preventive release |

**Cultural Practices:**
- Maintain optimal plant spacing (air circulation)
- Remove lower leaves (reduce splash zone)
- Quarantine new plant material
- Regular scouting and early intervention

## 6. Harvest and Post-Harvest Processing <a name="post-harvest"></a>

### 6.1 Harvest Timing Optimization

**Optimal Harvest Times:**
| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| Time of day | Early morning (highest oil content) |
| Growth stage | Just before flowering (most herbs) |
| Weather | After dry period (for drying) |
| Moon phase | Consider biodynamic practices (market positioning) |

### 6.2 Harvest Techniques

**Cut-and-Come-Again Crops:**
- Basil: Cut above node, leaving 2-4 leaves
- Mint: Cut to 1/3 of plant height
- Parsley: Cut outer stems first

**Single Harvest Crops:**
- Cilantro (before bolting)
- Dill (at desired stage)
- Chamomile flowers

### 6.3 Post-Harvest Handling

**Fresh Herb Handling:**
1. Harvest into clean containers
2. Remove damaged or discolored material
3. Hydrate immediately (stems in water)
4. Pre-cool to 35-40°F within 1 hour
5. Package in breathable containers
6. Maintain cold chain to customer

**Shelf Life Optimization:**
| Herb | Optimal Storage | Expected Life |
|------|-----------------|---------------|
| Basil | 55°F, high humidity | 7-10 days |
| Cilantro | 32-36°F, high humidity | 14-21 days |
| Mint | 32-36°F, high humidity | 14-21 days |
| Rosemary | 32-36°F, moderate humidity | 21-30 days |

### 6.4 Value-Added Processing

#### 6.4.1 Drying Methods

| Method | Equipment | Quality | Throughput | Cost |
|--------|-----------|---------|------------|------|
| Air drying | Racks, dehumidifier | Good | Low | Low |
| Forced air | Commercial dryer | Better | Medium | Medium |
| Freeze drying | Freeze dryer | Best | Medium | High |

**Optimal Drying Parameters:**
- Temperature: 95-115°F (lower preserves oils)
- Humidity: <40%
- Airflow: Gentle, consistent
- Duration: 24-72 hours depending on method

#### 6.4.2 Essential Oil Extraction

**Steam Distillation:**
- Equipment: Distillation apparatus ($3,000-15,000)
- Yield: 0.5-2% (varies by herb)
- Market value: $50-500/oz depending on herb

**Cold Press/Expression:**
- For citrus and certain herbs
- Higher yield, different compound profile

#### 6.4.3 Tincture and Extract Production

**Basic Tincture Protocol:**
1. Macerate fresh or dried herb in alcohol (40-60%)
2. Ratio: 1:5 (dried) or 1:2 (fresh)
3. Store in dark container 4-6 weeks
4. Strain and bottle

**Glycerite Production:**
- Alcohol-free option
- Uses vegetable glycerin
- Suitable for children's products

## 7. Quality Control and Standardization <a name="quality-control"></a>

### 7.1 Quality Parameters

**Physical Quality:**
- Appearance (color, form, size)
- Moisture content (<12% for dried)
- Foreign matter (<2%)
- Ash content (species-specific)

**Chemical Quality:**
- Essential oil content (species-specific minimums)
- Active compound assays
- Heavy metals (<10 ppm lead, etc.)
- Pesticide residues (none for aquaponic)

**Microbiological Quality:**
- Total plate count (<100,000 CFU/g)
- Yeast and mold (<10,000 CFU/g)
- E. coli (absent)
- Salmonella (absent in 25g)

### 7.2 Testing Protocols

**In-House Testing:**
- Moisture analysis (moisture meter)
- Visual inspection and grading
- Organoleptic evaluation (aroma, taste)
- Basic contamination screening

**Third-Party Laboratory:**
- Heavy metals (ICP-MS)
- Pesticide screening (LC-MS/MS, GC-MS)
- Microbiology (standard plate count)
- Identity verification (TLC, HPTLC)
- Potency assays (HPLC)

### 7.3 Documentation and Traceability

**Batch Records:**
- Planting date and seed lot
- Growing conditions log
- Nutrient and treatment records
- Harvest date and conditions
- Processing records
- Testing results
- Distribution records

**Certifications to Pursue:**
- USDA Organic (through certification body)
- Good Agricultural Practices (GAP)
- Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) for processed products
- Kosher/Halal (market expansion)

## 9. Regulatory Considerations <a name="regulatory"></a>

### 9.1 FDA Regulations

**Foods:**
- Herbs sold as food are regulated under FDCA
- Must meet food safety standards
- Labeling requirements apply

**Dietary Supplements:**
- Subject to DSHEA (1994)
- Structure/function claims allowed
- No disease claims without FDA approval
- GMP requirements (21 CFR 111)

**Cosmetics:**
- Herb-infused skincare products
- Labeling requirements
- No FDA pre-approval needed

### 9.2 State Regulations

- Maryland Department of Agriculture licensing
- Food processing facility permits
- Cottage food exemptions (limited scale)
- Business licensing requirements

### 9.3 Organic Certification

**NOP Standards for Aquaponics:**
- Currently evolving regulatory landscape
- Some certifiers accept aquaponic products
- Document fish feed sources carefully
- Maintain detailed production records
