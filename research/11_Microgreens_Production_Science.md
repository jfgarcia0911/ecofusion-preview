# Microgreens Production Science
## Advanced Cultivation, Nutrition, and Commercial Production Strategies

---

## Executive Summary

Microgreens represent one of the highest-value crops in controlled environment agriculture, commanding premium prices while offering exceptional nutritional density. This comprehensive research document explores the science of microgreen production—from seed selection and germination physiology to harvest optimization and nutritional enhancement strategies. For EcoFusion, microgreens offer rapid crop cycles, high margins, and compelling market differentiation as a nutrient-dense superfood.

## Part 1: Microgreens Fundamentals

### 1.1 Definition and Classification

**What Are Microgreens?**

Microgreens are young vegetable greens harvested after the cotyledon leaves have developed, typically with one set of true leaves emerging. They occupy a unique position between sprouts and baby greens:

```
GROWTH STAGE COMPARISON

SPROUTS               MICROGREENS           BABY GREENS
──────────────        ──────────────        ──────────────
Days 3-7              Days 7-21             Days 21-40
Seed + root + hypocotyl   Cotyledons + 1st true leaves   Multiple true leaves
Eaten whole (including root)   Stem + leaves only        Stem + leaves
Grown in water/jar    Grown on media        Grown in media/soil
No light needed       Light required        Light required
Lowest cost           Medium cost           Higher cost
```

**Microgreen Categories**

| Category | Examples | Flavor Profile | Days to Harvest |
|----------|----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Brassicas | Broccoli, kale, radish, arugula | Peppery, pungent | 7-12 |
| Alliums | Onion, leek, garlic chives | Savory, mild onion | 12-18 |
| Amaranth family | Amaranth, beet, chard | Earthy, mild | 10-14 |
| Asteraceae | Sunflower, lettuce | Nutty, crisp | 8-14 |
| Legumes | Pea shoots, lentils, fenugreek | Sweet, vegetal | 10-16 |
| Umbellifers | Cilantro, dill, fennel | Aromatic, distinctive | 14-21 |
| Mucilaginous | Basil, chia, flax | Variable | 12-18 |

## Part 2: Seed Science and Selection

### 2.1 Seed Quality Parameters

**Critical Seed Specifications**

| Parameter | Specification | Test Method | Importance |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Germination rate | >85% (ideally >90%) | Standard germination test | Yield consistency |
| Seed vigor | High | Accelerated aging test | Uniform emergence |
| Seed purity | >98% | Purity analysis | No off-types |
| Seed health | Pathogen-free | PCR testing | Disease prevention |
| Seed moisture | 8-12% | Oven dry method | Storage stability |
| Seed size | Uniform | Screen separation | Even planting density |

**Seed Treatment Considerations**

```
SEED TREATMENT OPTIONS

UNTREATED (Preferred for microgreens)
├── Organic certification possible
├── No chemical residues
├── Higher pathogen risk
└── May need sanitization before planting

TREATED (Conventional)
├── Fungicide coated
├── Better germination in some conditions
├── Not suitable for organic markets
└── May affect seed coat color

SANITIZATION OPTIONS (For untreated seeds)
├── Hot water treatment (50°C for 20-30 min)
├── Hydrogen peroxide soak (3% for 5-10 min)
├── Chlorine dioxide (careful with dose)
└── Calcium hypochlorite (0.5-2% for 10 min)
```

**Density Effects on Quality**

```
SEEDING DENSITY TRADE-OFFS

TOO LOW                    OPTIMAL                   TOO HIGH
──────────────            ──────────────            ──────────────
• Poor tray coverage      • Full, even canopy       • Stretched, weak stems
• Wasted space            • Good stem strength      • Disease pressure
• Thick stems             • Optimal yield           • Mold issues
• Low yield/tray          • Best color              • Poor air circulation
• Uneconomical            • Efficient               • Lower quality

CALIBRATION METHOD:
1. Plant test trays at 3 densities (50%, 100%, 150% of baseline)
2. Track emergence, growth, yield, and quality
3. Select density with best overall performance
4. Adjust seasonally as needed
```

## Part 3: Germination and Growing Protocols

### 3.1 Germination Physiology

**Stages of Germination**

```
GERMINATION PROCESS

STAGE 1: IMBIBITION (Water uptake)
├── Dry seed absorbs water (50-150% of dry weight)
├── Seed coat softens
├── Metabolic enzymes activate
└── Duration: 4-24 hours

STAGE 2: LAG PHASE (Metabolic activation)
├── Respiration increases
├── Reserve mobilization begins
├── Protein synthesis initiates
├── Cell elongation prepares
└── Duration: 12-48 hours

STAGE 3: RADICLE EMERGENCE (Root protrusion)
├── Root tip breaks through seed coat
├── First visible sign of germination
├── Geotropism orients root downward
└── Duration: 24-72 hours after imbibition

STAGE 4: HYPOCOTYL EMERGENCE (Stem growth)
├── Hook-shaped hypocotyl emerges
├── Pushes cotyledons above media
├── Light triggers de-etiolation
└── Duration: 1-3 days after radicle
```

**Factors Affecting Germination**

| Factor | Optimal Range | Effect of Suboptimal |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|
| Temperature | 65-75°F (18-24°C) | Too cold: slow, uneven; Too hot: poor germination |
| Moisture | Consistent, not saturated | Too dry: fails; Too wet: rot, disease |
| Oxygen | Adequate air exchange | Anaerobic: fails, pathogens |
| Light | Species dependent | Some need dark, others light |
| Seed quality | High vigor | Low vigor: poor uniformity |

### 3.2 Soaking and Pre-Treatment

**Soaking Protocols by Seed Type**

| Seed Type | Soaking Required | Duration | Temperature | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|----------|-------------|-------|
| Large seeds (pea, sunflower) | Yes | 8-12 hours | Room temp | Rinse 2-3x |
| Medium seeds (radish, beet) | Optional | 4-8 hours | Room temp | Can improve uniformity |
| Small seeds (broccoli, kale) | No | N/A | N/A | Direct sow |
| Mucilaginous (basil, chia) | No | N/A | N/A | Gel when wet |

**Soaking Procedure**

```
STANDARD SOAKING PROTOCOL

1. MEASURE seeds (record weight)
2. RINSE in clean water
3. SOAK in 3x volume of water
   • Temperature: 65-75°F (18-24°C)
   • Duration: per species guidelines
   • Container: food-safe bucket or tray
4. DRAIN and RINSE thoroughly
5. INSPECT for quality
   • Remove floaters (dead seeds)
   • Check for swelling
6. PLANT immediately or hold briefly
   • Holding: spread thin, cool location
   • Maximum hold: 4-6 hours
```

### 3.4 Blackout and Germination Phase

**Blackout Protocol**

```
BLACKOUT/GERMINATION PHASE

PURPOSE:
• Encourage hypocotyl elongation
• Build stem strength through weight resistance
• Ensure uniform emergence
• Reduce water loss

PROCEDURE:
1. SEED trays evenly on prepared media
2. MIST thoroughly (not soaking)
3. COVER with humidity dome or second tray (inverted, weighted)
4. WEIGHT: 2-5 lbs on top (species dependent)
5. STACK if using multiple trays
6. STORE in dark area at 65-75°F
7. MONITOR daily
   • Check moisture (mist if dry)
   • Look for emergence
8. DURATION: 2-5 days depending on species

EMERGENCE INDICATORS:
• Lifting of cover tray
• Yellow/white shoots visible
• Roots anchoring in media
```

## Part 4: Watering and Nutrition

### 4.1 Watering Strategies

**Irrigation Methods**

| Method | Description | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|--------|-------------|------|------|----------|
| Bottom watering | Tray sits in water bath | Uniform, prevents foliar disease | Slow, infrastructure needed | Production scale |
| Top misting | Fine mist applied from above | Quick, flexible | Foliar disease risk, uneven | Small scale |
| Flood and drain | Periodic flooding of tray bottoms | Automated, consistent | Infrastructure cost | Large operations |
| Capillary mat | Wicking from mat below | Hands-off, consistent | Mat management, algae | Medium scale |

**Watering Schedule**

```
WATERING PROTOCOL

BLACKOUT PHASE (Days 1-3)
├── Initial: Heavy mist at seeding
├── Daily: Check, light mist if surface dry
├── Method: Top mist only
└── Quantity: Just enough to maintain moisture

LIGHT PHASE (Days 4-harvest)
├── Frequency: 1-2x daily (environment dependent)
├── Method: Bottom water preferred
├── Quantity: 0.5-1 L per 10"x20" tray per watering
├── Timing: Morning preferred, avoid evening
└── Check: Media should be moist, not saturated

SIGNS OF WATER STRESS:
• Under-watering: Wilting, dry media, slow growth
• Over-watering: Yellow leaves, root rot, mold
```

## Part 5: Pest and Disease Management

### 5.1 Common Microgreen Diseases

**Disease Identification and Management**

```
DISEASE QUICK REFERENCE

DAMPING OFF (Pythium, Fusarium, Rhizoctonia)
├── Symptoms: Seedlings collapse at soil line
├── Conditions: High moisture, poor air flow
├── Prevention: Sterile media, good drainage, air flow
└── Treatment: Remove affected trays, improve conditions

GRAY MOLD (Botrytis cinerea)
├── Symptoms: Gray fuzzy growth on leaves/stems
├── Conditions: High humidity, cool temps, poor air
├── Prevention: Humidity control, air circulation
└── Treatment: Remove affected areas, reduce humidity

WHITE MOLD (Sclerotinia)
├── Symptoms: White cottony growth, stem rot
├── Conditions: High humidity, dense planting
├── Prevention: Proper spacing, air movement
└── Treatment: Remove affected trays, sanitize

POWDERY MILDEW
├── Symptoms: White powdery coating on leaves
├── Conditions: High humidity, poor air circulation
├── Prevention: Air flow, moderate humidity
└── Treatment: Potassium bicarbonate spray, remove affected
```

**Environmental Disease Prevention**

| Factor | Target Range | Disease Prevention Mechanism |
|--------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Humidity | 50-65% | Reduces fungal spore germination |
| Temperature | 65-72°F | Optimal plant health, suboptimal for many pathogens |
| Air flow | 0.5-1 m/s gentle | Prevents moisture accumulation, strengthens stems |
| Seeding density | Species-specific | Prevents microclimate conducive to disease |
| Sanitation | Trays, tools, surfaces | Eliminates pathogen reservoirs |

**Manual Harvest Protocol**

```
HARVEST PROCEDURE

PRE-HARVEST:
□ Verify harvest readiness (visual, height, taste)
□ Stop watering 12-24 hours before (reduces moisture)
□ Prepare clean harvest containers
□ Ensure cool storage ready
□ Clean and sanitize harvest tools

HARVEST:
□ Position tray at comfortable angle
□ Grip stems gently, cut above media line
□ Cut in sections, avoid crushing
□ Transfer to harvest container immediately
□ Avoid contact with media
□ Work efficiently (quality degrades with handling)

POST-HARVEST:
□ Move to cooling area immediately
□ Do not wash unless necessary
□ Package promptly
□ Record yield data
□ Clean and sanitize work area
```

### 6.4 Shelf Life Optimization

**Factors Affecting Shelf Life**

| Factor | Optimal Condition | Effect on Shelf Life |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Temperature | 36-40°F (2-4°C) | Each 10°F above optimal halves shelf life |
| Humidity | 90-95% RH | Too low = wilting; Too high = rot |
| Ethylene exposure | Minimal | Accelerates senescence |
| Physical damage | None | Damage = rapid decay |
| Initial quality | High | Starting quality determines end quality |

Microgreens represent a strategic crop category for EcoFusion, offering high margins, rapid cycles, and exceptional nutritional value that aligns with the company's mission of sustainable, healthy food production. - **Seed quality and proper sourcing**
- **Optimized germination and growing protocols**

- **Efficient harvest and cold chain management**
