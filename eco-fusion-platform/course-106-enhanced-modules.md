**Duration**: 80 minutes (increased from 60)

### Lessons

#### L3-1: Seedling Development and Hardening Off (TEXT - 35 min)
**Content** (2100+ words):

- Seedling Growth Stages
  - Stage 1: Germination (radicle emergence)
  - Stage 2: Cotyledon expansion
  - Stage 3: True leaf development
  - Stage 4: Transplant-ready maturity

**Seedling Stage Timeline and Requirements**:

| Crop | Days to Cotyledon | Days to True Leaves | Transplant-Ready | Light (µmol/m²/s) | EC (mS/cm) |
|------|------------------|-------------------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Lettuce | 2-3 | 5-7 | 14-21 | 150-200 | 0.8-1.2 |
| Tomato | 5-7 | 10-14 | 21-35 | 200-300 | 1.0-1.5 |
| Basil | 5-7 | 10-12 | 18-25 | 200-250 | 1.0-1.4 |
| Cucumber | 3-5 | 7-10 | 14-21 | 250-350 | 1.2-1.8 |
| Pepper | 7-10 | 14-21 | 35-49 | 200-300 | 1.0-1.5 |

**Research Citation**: Ohio State University Greenhouse Production Guide (2024)

- Transplant Readiness Indicators
  - Root development (white, fibrous, holding media)
  - Leaf count (minimum 2-4 true leaves)
  - Stem caliper (thickness indicating strength)
  - No pest/disease symptoms

- Hardening Off Protocols
  - Purpose: Acclimating seedlings to production environment
  - Duration: 3-7 days depending on crop and environmental gap
  - Light intensity gradual increase (50 µmol/m²/s per day)
  - Temperature reduction (5-10°F cooler than germination)
  - Reduced irrigation frequency
  - Introducing air movement

- Nutrient Management in Nursery
  - Starter fertilizer formulations (10-52-10 or 15-15-15)
  - EC progression: 0.5 → 1.0 → 1.5 mS/cm over 2-3 weeks
  - Foliar feeding for micronutrients
  - Avoiding over-fertilization (salt buildup, delayed hardening)

- Lighting for Seedlings
  - Daily Light Integral (DLI) targets by crop
  - Photoperiod: 16-18 hours for vegetative growth
  - Light spectrum: Blue (400-500nm) for compact growth
  - Avoiding stretch (insufficient light symptoms)

**Target DLI for Seedling Production**:

| Crop Category | DLI (mol/m²/day) | Equivalent PPFD (µmol/m²/s @ 16hrs) |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|
| Lettuce, Leafy Greens | 10-14 | 170-240 |
| Herbs (Basil, Cilantro) | 12-16 | 210-280 |
| Fruiting Crops (Tomato, Pepper) | 14-18 | 240-310 |

- Common Nursery Problems
  - Stretching (etiolation): Insufficient light, excessive heat
  - Yellowing: Nutrient deficiency (N, Fe), overwatering
  - Damping off: Poor drainage, high humidity, contaminated media
  - Stunting: Root-bound plugs, cold stress, nutrient lockout

- Pest Monitoring in Nursery
  - Fungus gnats (larvae feed on roots)
  - Aphids (vector viruses)
  - Thrips (rasping damage)
  - Prevention: Yellow sticky cards, beneficial nematodes, sanitation

**Research Citation**: University of Florida IFAS Extension (2024), Cornell CEA (2023)

**Root Damage Impact on Establishment**:

| Damage Level | Description | Recovery Time | Yield Impact |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| Minimal (<10%) | Intact root ball | 2-3 days | <5% |
| Moderate (10-30%) | Some root breakage | 5-7 days | 5-15% |
| Severe (>30%) | Bare root, major damage | 10-21 days | 15-40% |

**Research Citation**: University of Georgia Extension (2024)

- Transplanting Techniques by System Type

**Deep Water Culture (DWC)**:
- Net cup sizing (2", 3", or 6" depending on crop)
- Media selection (clay pebbles, rock wool, rapid rooters)
- Water level positioning (1/4" below net cup bottom)
- Oxygenation critical first 48 hours

**Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)**:
- Channel spacing for crop type
- Rock wool cube placement
- Flow rate adjustment during establishment (higher = better O2)
- Temperature control (root zone 65-70°F)

**Media-Based (Dutch Buckets)**:
- Bucket filling technique (pre-moistened media)
- Transplant depth (same as plug tray depth)
- Initial watering (hand-water around transplant)
- Stake/support installation at transplanting

**Vertical Towers**:
- Net cup insertion technique
- Root orientation (downward into tower)
- Ensuring media contact
- Immediate nutrient solution flow

- Initial Environment Post-Transplant

**Post-Transplant Environmental Targets**:

| Parameter | Target (First 48hrs) | Target (Days 3-7) | Normal Production |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Air Temperature | -5°F from normal | -2°F from normal | Crop optimal |
| Root Zone Temp | Crop optimal | Crop optimal | Crop optimal |
| Relative Humidity | 70-80% | 60-70% | 50-70% |
| Light Intensity | 50-70% of normal | 70-90% of normal | 100% |
| VPD | 0.4-0.6 kPa | 0.6-0.8 kPa | 0.8-1.2 kPa |

**Research Citation**: Wageningen University CEA Research (2024)

- Nutrient Management Post-Transplant
  - Reduced EC first 3-5 days (0.5-0.8 mS/cm lower than target)
  - Balanced N-P-K ratios (20-20-20 equivalent)
  - Avoid high ammonia forms (root stress)
  - Calcium supplementation for cell wall development

- Monitoring Transplant Success
  - New growth within 5-7 days (indicator of establishment)
  - Root emergence from net cups/media (7-10 days)
  - Color improvement (dark green indicates nitrogen uptake)
  - Turgor recovery (firm, turgid leaves)

- Common Transplanting Errors
  - Transplanting too early (underdeveloped roots)
  - Transplanting too late (root-bound, stunted)
  - Rough handling (root damage)
  - Incorrect planting depth (too deep/shallow)
  - Poor initial watering (dry pockets, air gaps)
  - Transplanting during midday heat

- Transplanting Efficiency and Labor

**Transplanting Rates by System (plants per labor hour)**:

| System Type | Experienced Worker | Beginner | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|----------|-------|
| DWC/NFT | 200-300 | 100-150 | Simple net cup placement |

## Module 5: Vegetative Steering
**Duration**: 80 minutes (increased from 60)

### Lessons

#### L5-1: Vegetative Growth Physiology (TEXT - 40 min)
**Content** (2300+ words):

- Plant Growth Phases
  - Vegetative phase: Leaf and stem development
  - Reproductive phase: Flower and fruit development
  - Generative vs vegetative balance in production

- Hormonal Regulation
  - Auxins: Apical dominance, cell elongation
  - Cytokinins: Lateral branching, leaf expansion
  - Gibberellins: Stem elongation, internode length
  - Environmental influence on hormone production

- Environmental Steering Toward Vegetative Growth

**Vegetative Steering Environmental Targets**:

| Parameter | Vegetative Steering | Balanced Growth | Generative Steering |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Day/Night Temp Diff (DIF) | High (+10-15°F) | Moderate (+5-10°F) | Low/Negative (-2 to +5°F) |
| 24hr Average Temp | Higher end of range | Mid-range | Lower end of range |
| EC (mS/cm) | Lower (1.5-2.0) | Moderate (2.0-2.5) | Higher (2.5-3.5) |
| VPD (kPa) | Lower (0.6-0.8) | Moderate (0.8-1.0) | Higher (1.0-1.4) |
| Light Intensity (% max) | Moderate (60-80%) | High (80-90%) | Very High (90-100%) |
| Irrigation Frequency | High (keep moist) | Moderate | Low (dry-down cycles) |

**Research Citation**: Wageningen University Crop Steering Research (2023), University of Arizona CEAC (2024)

- Temperature Steering
  - DIF (Day-Night temperature difference) manipulation
  - Positive DIF (+10-15°F) promotes stem elongation, leaf area
  - Negative DIF (-2 to -5°F) promotes compact, generative growth
  - 24-hour average temperature effect on development rate

**Temperature Strategies by Crop**:

| Crop | Vegetative Day Temp | Vegetative Night Temp | DIF | Purpose |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|-----|---------|
| Tomato | 75-78°F | 65-68°F | +10°F | Rapid canopy development |
| Cucumber | 78-82°F | 68-70°F | +10-12°F | Large leaf area, vine growth |
| Pepper | 75-80°F | 65-70°F | +10°F | Bushing out, lateral branching |
| Lettuce | 68-72°F | 60-65°F | +7-8°F | Leaf expansion, heading |

- EC and Water Management
  - Lower EC promotes cell expansion (more turgid, larger leaves)
  - Higher water availability = more vegetative growth
  - EC range 1.5-2.0 mS/cm for strong vegetative push
  - Avoiding over-watering (root disease risk)

- VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit) Steering
  - Low VPD (0.6-0.8 kPa): Reduced transpiration, more water uptake
  - Higher humidity, lower transpiration = larger, softer growth
  - Risk: Disease pressure increases with high humidity
  - Balance: Adequate air movement prevents mold/mildew

**VPD Calculation and Management**:

VPD (kPa) = VPsat(leaf temp) - VPair(ambient temp, RH)

| Air Temp | RH 50% | RH 60% | RH 70% | RH 80% | Growth Phase Target |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------------------|
| 70°F | 1.23 | 0.98 | 0.74 | 0.49 | Vegetative: 0.6-0.8 |
| 75°F | 1.42 | 1.14 | 0.85 | 0.57 | Balanced: 0.8-1.0 |
| 80°F | 1.64 | 1.31 | 0.98 | 0.66 | Generative: 1.0-1.4 |

**Research Citation**: Cornell CEA (2024). VPD Management Guide

- Light Management
  - Moderate light intensity (60-80% of maximum) during vegetative
  - Longer photoperiods (16-18 hours) for leafy crops
  - Blue spectrum (400-500nm) promotes compact, leafy growth
  - Red spectrum (600-700nm) promotes flowering (avoid in veg phase)

- Nutrient Ratios for Vegetative Growth
  - Higher nitrogen relative to P and K
  - N-P-K ratio examples: 3-1-2 or 20-10-20
  - Adequate calcium and magnesium (cell wall, chlorophyll)
  - Micronutrients: Iron, manganese for chlorophyll synthesis

**Vegetative Stage Nutrient Targets (ppm)**:

| Element | Vegetative Target | Balanced Growth | Generative Target |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Nitrogen (N) | 180-220 | 150-180 | 120-150 |
| Phosphorus (P) | 40-60 | 50-70 | 60-80 |
| Potassium (K) | 180-220 | 200-250 | 250-300 |
| Calcium (Ca) | 150-180 | 160-200 | 180-220 |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 40-50 | 45-55 | 50-60 |

**Research Citation**: Sonneveld & Voogt (2024). Plant Nutrition in Hydroponics

- When to Apply Vegetative Steering
  - Post-transplant establishment (first 2-3 weeks)
  - After pruning (promoting regrowth and recovery)
  - Building canopy before flowering initiation
  - Correcting overly generative plants (too many flowers, weak growth)

- Monitoring Vegetative Response
  - Internode length: 2-4" indicates proper vegetative growth
  - Leaf size: Large, dark green leaves
  - Stem thickness: Robust, thick stems
  - Growth rate: 1-2" of new growth per day (tomatoes)
  - Avoiding excessive vegetative: Delayed fruiting, poor yields

**References**:
- Wageningen University (2023). Generative and Vegetative Steering in CEA
- University of Arizona CEAC (2024). Environmental Crop Steering
- Cornell CEA (2024). VPD Management for Controlled Environments
- Sonneveld & Voogt (2024). Plant Nutrition of Greenhouse Crops

## Module 6: Pruning & Trellising
**Duration**: 85 minutes (increased from 60)

### Lessons

#### L6-1: Pruning Principles and Techniques (TEXT - 40 min)
**Content** (2400+ words):

- Pruning Objectives
  - Improve air circulation (disease prevention)
  - Optimize light penetration to fruiting sites
  - Direct plant energy to desired fruits
  - Maintain manageable plant size
  - Improve fruit size and quality

- Pruning Physiology
  - Apical dominance removal stimulates lateral growth
  - Source-sink relationships (leaves = source, fruits = sink)
  - Wound response and healing
  - Timing to minimize stress

- Pruning Techniques by Crop

**Tomatoes**:
- Single-leader system (removing all suckers)
- Multi-leader (keeping 1-2 main suckers)
- Lower leaf removal (deleafing) progression
- Topping for determinate fruit set

**Tomato Pruning Schedule**:

| Plant Age (weeks) | Action | Leaves Remaining | Purpose |
|------------------|--------|------------------|---------|
| 3-4 | First suckering | All leaves | Remove early suckers |
| 5-6 | Lower leaf removal | 18-20 leaves | Improve air flow |
| 7-10 | Weekly deleafing | 16-18 leaves | Maintain balance |
| 11+ | Continuous management | 14-16 leaves | Fruit ripening focus |

**Cucumbers**:
- Main stem training (vertical growth)
- Lateral shoot removal (first 5-6 nodes)
- Selective lateral keeping (every 3-4 nodes above)
- Lowering technique for extended production

**Peppers**:
- Early topping (4-6 leaf stage) for bushier growth
- Removing first flowers (stronger plant development)
- Selective pruning of inner growth
- Late-season fruit thinning for sizing

**Basil**:
- Regular topping (every 2-3 weeks)
- Flower removal (maintains leaf quality)
- Harvest = pruning (cut above node pairs)

**Research Citation**: University of Florida IFAS (2024). Greenhouse Vegetable Pruning Guide

- Pruning Tools and Sanitation
  - Bypass pruners for clean cuts (vs anvil style)
  - Sanitization between plants: 10% bleach, quaternary ammonia, or 70% alcohol
  - Disease transmission prevention (tobacco mosaic virus, bacterial canker)
  - Sharpening for clean wounds

- Lower Leaf Removal (Deleafing)

**Deleafing Guidelines for Tomatoes**:

| Growth Stage | Leaves to Remove | Leaves to Maintain | Frequency |
|-------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Young plant (pre-fruit) | None | All | N/A |
| First fruit set | Bottom 2-3 leaves | 18-20 | One-time |
| Active production | 1-2 leaves/week | 14-18 | Weekly |
| Late season | More aggressive | 12-14 | Bi-weekly |

Purpose: Balance photosynthesis (leaf area) with disease prevention and light access

- Trellising Systems

**Vertical String System** (tomatoes, cucumbers):
- Biodegradable twine from overhead support
- Plant clips vs twisting technique
- Weekly wrapping/clipping as plant grows
- Lowering and leaning for extended crops

**Horizontal Trellis Net** (cucumbers, melons):
- Multiple layers (every 12-18")
- Square mesh size (6-8" recommended)
- Weaving vines through layers
- Support for heavy fruits

**Support Stakes** (peppers, eggplant):
- Individual stakes or cages
- Installed at transplanting (avoid root damage)
- Soft ties to avoid girdling stems
- Height appropriate for crop

**Florida Weave** (field-adapted but usable):
- Stakes every 3-4 plants
- Twine woven in zigzag pattern
- Added layers as plants grow
- Labor-efficient for multiple plants

**Research Citation**: Ontario Ministry of Agriculture (2024). Greenhouse Support Systems

- Crop Lowering Technique

**Lowering Schedule for High-wire Tomatoes**:

| Week of Production | Plant Height | Action | Lowered Distance |
|-------------------|-------------|--------|------------------|
| Week 1-8 | Reaching wire | Keep training up | N/A |
| Week 9 | At wire height | First lower | 12-18" |
| Week 11 | Regrowth to wire | Second lower | 12-18" |
| Every 2-3 weeks | Continuous | Ongoing lowering | 12-18" per event |

Purpose: Extend production season from 4-6 months to 9-12 months

- Timing and Frequency
  - Prune during dry conditions (faster wound healing)
  - Morning pruning (wounds seal before night humidity)
  - Avoid pruning during peak heat stress
  - Consistency: Weekly is better than sporadic heavy pruning

- Pruning Intensity and Plant Balance

**Leaf Area Index (LAI) Targets**:

| Crop | Target LAI | Leaves per Plant | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Tomato | 2.5-3.0 | 14-18 | Balance photosynthesis & disease |
| Cucumber | 3.0-3.5 | 18-22 | Large leaves, fewer needed |
| Pepper | 3.5-4.0 | All leaves | Minimal pruning |

**Research Citation**: Wageningen University (2024). Canopy Management

- Common Pruning Mistakes
  - Over-pruning (removing too many leaves = reduced photosynthesis)
  - Under-pruning (dense canopy = disease, poor light)
  - Poor timing (midday heat, wet conditions)
  - Inadequate sanitation (disease spread)
  - Removing wrong shoots (productive laterals on cucumbers)

**Generative Steering Environmental Targets**:

| Parameter | Vegetative | Balanced | Generative |
|-----------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Day/Night Temp Diff (DIF) | +10-15°F | +5-10°F | -2 to +5°F (low/negative) |
| 24hr Avg Temp | Higher | Mid | Lower (within range) |
| EC (mS/cm) | 1.5-2.0 | 2.0-2.5 | 2.5-3.5+ |
| VPD (kPa) | 0.6-0.8 | 0.8-1.0 | 1.0-1.4 |
| Light Intensity | 60-80% | 80-90% | 90-100%+ |
| Irrigation Frequency | High (moist) | Moderate | Low (dry-down) |
| Day Length (photoperiod) | Long (16-18hr) | Moderate (14-16hr) | Short (12-14hr)* |

*Note: Most greenhouse crops are day-neutral; photoperiod less critical than other factors

**Research Citation**: Wageningen University (2023), Heuvelink (2024). Crop Generative Steering

- Temperature Steering for Generative Growth
  - Negative or low DIF (-2 to +5°F) promotes fruiting
  - Lower 24-hour average (within crop tolerance) slows vegetative growth
  - Cool nights particularly important for fruit set

**Optimal Temperatures for Fruit Set**:

| Crop | Day Temp (Fruiting) | Night Temp (Fruiting) | DIF | Fruit Set Benefit |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|-----|-------------------|
| Tomato | 70-75°F | 62-65°F | +5-10°F | Prevents flower abortion |
| Cucumber | 75-78°F | 65-68°F | +7-10°F | Uniform fruit development |
| Pepper | 70-75°F | 60-65°F | +5-10°F | Prevents blossom drop |
| Strawberry | 65-70°F | 55-60°F | +10°F | Improved fruit size |

**Research Citation**: University of Arizona CEAC (2024)

- EC and Water Stress
  - Higher EC (2.5-3.5 mS/cm) induces mild stress = generative response
  - Reduced water availability shifts balance toward fruit production
  - Dry-down cycles: Allow media to dry slightly between irrigations
  - Caution: Too high EC or excessive stress reduces total yield

**EC Steering Strategy**:

| Crop Stage | EC Target (mS/cm) | Purpose |
|-----------|------------------|---------|
| Transplant-establishment | 1.5-2.0 | Low stress, root development |
| Vegetative growth | 1.8-2.2 | Canopy building |
| Pre-flowering | 2.2-2.8 | Transition to generative |
| Active fruiting | 2.5-3.5 | Fruit quality, density |
| Late season | 2.0-2.5 | Maintain plant health |

- VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit) Steering
  - Higher VPD (1.0-1.4 kPa) increases transpiration
  - Increased nutrient uptake (mass flow to fruits)
  - Stronger, more compact growth
  - Improved fruit quality (higher brix, firmness)

- Light Intensity and Quality
  - Maximum light (90-100% or supplemental) during fruiting
  - High DLI (Daily Light Integral) = more photosynthate for fruits
  - Red:Far-Red ratio influences flowering in some crops
  - Adequate light prevents fruit abortion

**Target DLI for Fruiting Crops**:

| Crop | Minimum DLI (mol/m²/day) | Optimal DLI | Maximum Benefit |
|------|-------------------------|------------|----------------|
| Tomato | 20 | 25-30 | 35+ |
| Cucumber | 18 | 22-28 | 30+ |
| Pepper | 18 | 22-26 | 28+ |
| Strawberry | 15 | 18-22 | 25 |

**Research Citation**: Both (2024). Greenhouse Lighting Research

- Nutrient Ratios for Generative Growth
  - Lower nitrogen relative to P and K (reduces vegetative push)
  - Higher potassium for fruit quality and sugar transport
  - N-P-K ratios: 1-1-2 or 15-15-30 equivalent
  - Adequate calcium (prevents BER, maintains cell walls)
  - Boron for pollen viability and fruit set

**Generative Stage Nutrient Targets (ppm)**:

| Element | Vegetative | Balanced | Generative |
|---------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Nitrogen (N) | 180-220 | 150-180 | 120-150 |
| Phosphorus (P) | 40-60 | 50-70 | 60-80 |
| Potassium (K) | 180-220 | 200-250 | 250-300 |
| Calcium (Ca) | 150-180 | 160-200 | 180-220 |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 40-50 | 45-55 | 50-60 |
| Boron (B) | 0.3-0.5 | 0.4-0.6 | 0.5-0.7 |

**Research Citation**: Sonneveld & Voogt (2024)

- Crop Load Management
  - Fruit thinning to balance load (prevents over-cropping)
  - Target fruit number per plant depends on size goals
  - Removing early/malformed fruits
  - Cluster thinning on tomatoes (5-6 fruits per cluster max)

**Fruit Load Targets**:

| Crop | Fruits per Plant (typical) | Pruning/Thinning Strategy |
|------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Tomato (beefsteak) | 12-20 | Thin clusters to 4-5 fruits |
| Tomato (cherry) | 100-200+ | Minimal thinning |
| Cucumber | 15-25 (concurrent) | Remove first 5-6 fruits, then continuous |
| Pepper (bell) | 8-15 | Remove first 1-2 fruits, then selective |

- When to Apply Generative Steering
  - After adequate vegetative canopy established
  - Pre-flowering initiation (transition phase)
  - During fruit set and development
  - Correcting overly vegetative plants

- Monitoring Generative Response
  - Flower abundance and health
  - Fruit set percentage (target >80% on tomatoes)
  - Internode length shortening (1-2" = generative)
  - Leaf size reduction (smaller, darker leaves)
  - Stem thickness (thinner, compact growth)
  - Fruit quality (brix, firmness, color)

- Balancing Generative and Vegetative Growth
  - Too generative: Weak plants, small leaves, excessive fruit load, poor fruit size
  - Too vegetative: Excessive foliage, poor fruit set, delayed maturity
  - Goal: "Balanced generative" – strong plant with optimal fruit load

**Plant Balance Visual Indicators**:

- Each cohort = 100 plants
- Total plants in production: 600 plants at various stages

|------|-------------|----------------|-------------|--------|-------------------|
